# Response to Referee — R2 Major Revision

**Paper:** Frequent Losers and Cover Bidding in Public Procurement
**Authors:** Genicolo-Martins & Azevedo (Insper)
**Journal:** International Journal of Industrial Organization
**Date:** April 2026

---

Dear Editor,

We thank the referee for a careful, constructive, and intellectually honest report. The referee's central assessment — that the paper "has a real path to publication" as a screening paper — is exactly the outcome we aimed for, and the four conditions for acceptance are clear and actionable. We have implemented all four. Below we describe each change in detail.

---

## Major Point 1: Consolidate screening framing

**Referee:** "Assume fully the identity of a screening paper."

**Response:** We have done so throughout the manuscript.

- **Abstract** now opens with the detection problem and closes with "a flag is a starting point for investigation, not a verdict." No causal claims, no welfare numbers, no structural markup.
- **Introduction** ¶5 states: "The contribution is primarily *diagnostic*: FL presence identifies procurement environments systematically associated with higher prices and with known cartel activity." The word "diagnostic" appears 5 times in the introduction and 2 times in the conclusion.
- **Contributions** subsection frames the three contributions as: (1) a firm-level diagnostic tool, (2) a robust empirical regularity (3.6–7.7%), and (3) evidence *consistent with* cover bidding, "assembled with explicit acknowledgment that observational data cannot close the identification."
- The bridge sentence "Comparability, however, is not identification" (intro ¶4) prepares the reader for the caveat before the results paragraph.
- The conclusion opens with conceptual framing ("A simple participation anomaly turns out to be a reliable marker") rather than with numbers, and closes with "Whether frequent losers are unlucky competitors or cartel operatives, their presence marks markets that deserve a closer look."

---

## Major Point 2: Cut remaining mechanism language

**Referee:** "Cut what remains of language that is too strong about mechanism."

**Response:** We conducted a body-wide audit. Specific changes:

| Before | After | Location |
|--------|-------|----------|
| "hard to explain without some form of strategic coordination" | "most informative heterogeneity result...more naturally read through a cover-bidding lens, though selection cannot be excluded" | Intro ¶7 |
| "We lean toward the first reading" (pre-trends) | "We cannot adjudicate between the two readings" | sec_identification |
| "The evidence above establishes that FL presence marks" | "The evidence above suggests that FL presence marks" | sec7_results H1→H2 transition |
| "confirms this: the voluntary deployment premium" | "supports this reading: the voluntary deployment premium" | sec_mechanisms (convite) |
| "consistent with cover-bidder roles turning over as cartels reorganize" | "could reflect cartel reorganization, firm exit, or simply noise" | sec4_data_fl (persistence) |
| "narrow the space of non-collusive explanations" | "form a coherent pattern, though each admits non-collusive readings" | Intro, contribution 3 |

Zero occurrences remain of: "hard to explain," "quasi-experiment," "lean toward," "best guess," "uncontaminated," "4–6%," "preferred estimate" (except the disclaimer), "smoking gun," "proves," "demonstrates that" (in causal context).

The **convite variation** (7.6% voluntary deployment premium) is now described as "the most informative heterogeneity result" and explicitly hedged: "selection within the non-binding subsample cannot be excluded."

The **structural model** was compressed by 53 lines (385 → 332): the Testable Predictions subsection was replaced by a compact 5-row table, and the Regime 2 variance derivation and Proposition 4 economic foundation were moved to the appendix. The text now says: "The structural model does not independently identify the price effect" and frames the model as "a rationalization that clarifies when dispersion-based screens may fail."

The **bid rotation / bid inflation** subsection retains its evidence but the Mechanisms section now opens with: "Building on the mechanism diagnostics in the previous section, we now test three additional implications and examine competing explanations" — framing them as complements, not proof.

## Major Point 3: Reduce welfare weight

**Referee:** "Push almost everything to appendix."

**Response:** Done.

- **Conclusion:** No welfare numbers. The Magnitudes paragraph now says: "Illustrative welfare calculations assuming full causality — which the paper does not establish — are in Appendix."
- **Robustness section:** The welfare paragraph now says: "Appendix X reports back-of-the-envelope welfare calculations... These figures assume full causality, which the paper does not establish, and should be read as rough benchmarks rather than policy estimates." No dollar amounts in the body.
- **Appendix** (sec_counterfactual): Opens with: "The counterfactuals below are illustrative. They take the price coefficients at face value as causal parameters; to the extent the estimates reflect selection rather than treatment, the welfare figures overstate the gains."

---

## Major Point 4: No preferred estimate

**Referee:** "The range 3.6–7.7% is sufficient."

**Response:** The manuscript consistently reports the range 3.6–7.7% without designating any specification as preferred. The decomposition coefficient (0.043) is described as "an intermediate estimate" that "does not resolve the selection within market that affects both [OLS and cross-fit]." The text says explicitly: "we do not elevate the decomposition to a preferred estimate: it clarifies the source of the OLS–cross-fit gap but does not resolve within-market selection." Zero occurrences of "best guess," "uncontaminated association," or "4–6% likely range."

## Additional Points

### Comparability result (overlap 0.978)
The result (raw gap 2.43 → 0.050 after item FE, overlap 0.978) now appears in Introduction ¶4, immediately after the price results: "After absorbing item fixed effects the raw gap collapses from 2.43 to 0.050 log points (overlap 0.978), so the regressions compare like with like."

### Unrestricted sample
The Results section (OLS paragraph) now states: "The unrestricted sample (including items that never attract FL firms) yields a nearly identical coefficient, so the item-type restriction does not inflate the baseline." The Data section already noted that the restriction "conditions on the treatment" and that the unrestricted sample produces a similar coefficient.

### FL classification framing
The Data section now says: "FL status should not be read as a stable firm type; it serves better as an aggregate marker of suspicious tender-level environments than as a label for individual firms with a persistent collusive role." The low persistence (10%) and zero-win sensitivity are both discussed as features that reinforce the aggregate-marker interpretation.

### Event study pre-trends
Section sec_identification now says: "We cannot adjudicate between the two readings, but two pieces of evidence limit the scope of the second." The two interpretations (strategic selection vs. selection bias) are presented symmetrically, and the closing sentence says: "we regard the pre-trends as an unresolved ambiguity — one more reason to interpret the estimates as conditional associations rather than causal effects."

### Paper length
Body text was reduced by approximately 21% through polishing, compression of the structural model, removal of welfare numbers from the body, and tightening of the literature review, mechanisms section, and conclusion. The manuscript is now 73 pages including appendix.

---

We believe these changes fully address the referee's four conditions. The paper is now unambiguously a screening paper: it documents a robust empirical regularity, validates it against external enforcement data, and assembles mechanism diagnostics that are consistent with cover bidding — without claiming to identify causation or to classify individual firms as cartel members. We are grateful for the referee's guidance.
