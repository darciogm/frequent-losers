Referee Report — Revision 1

Journal: International Journal of Industrial Organization (IJIO)
Paper: Frequent Losers as Cover Bidders in Public Procurement
Authors: Darcio Genicolo-Martins & Paulo Furquim de Azevedo (INSPER)
Date: 2026-03-15
Minor Revision

1. Summary

This paper proposes “frequent losers” (FL)—firms with a zero win rate that participate in abnormally many public procurement tenders—as a firm-level screening marker for cover bidding. Using 4.5M tender-items and 40M bids from São Paulo’s BEC platform (2009–2019), the authors identify 2,735 FL firms. FL-present tenders exhibit 4–9% higher prices (OLS). External validation against CADE convictions shows 3.5x the participation-adjusted co-occurrence rate. A leave-one-out IV yields a 21% LATE (F=396), now presented as supplementary evidence. Bajari–Ye tests reject exchangeability, though the conditional independence result is attenuated under tender FE—now honestly presented in the main text.

The revision adds: conditional descriptive statistics (FE-residualized gap = 0.05 log points), a horse-race regression against Imhof-style screens (FL persists at 0.084, correlation = 0.06), alternative mechanism tests (firm age, geography, financial distress, minimum-bidder compliance), FL subgroup observable characteristics, an enriched Bajari–Ye first stage, and sensitivity-adjusted welfare bounds.

2. Overall Assessment

The revision substantially improves the paper. The three conditions for IJIO acceptance are addressed:

✓ Condition 1 (Reframe H2): Fully met. H2 is now consistently presented as “suggestive.” Bajari–Ye tender-FE reversal in main text. IV reframed as supplementary.
✓ Condition 2 (Benchmark screens): Substantially met. Horse-race shows FL persists (0.084) after Imhof control; correlation = 0.06. Demonstrates incremental value.
~ Condition 3 (Network split): Partially met. Ambiguity acknowledged; observable similarity reported. But no compelling theoretical explanation for why concentrated-market FL shows zero price effect.

The paper is now a solid candidate for IJIO. Four minor items remain (M1–M4), none requiring new estimation.

3. Major Comments

[M1] Prediction 5 should be revised or dropped

Problem: Prediction 5 (Section 3.3) states FL residuals should show “magnitude larger than for non-FL residuals.” Section 6.3 now honestly shows the opposite under tender FE (FL 0.38 < non-FL 0.86). The prediction should match the finding.

Fix: Revise Prediction 5 to predict exchangeability violation without the magnitude comparison. Or add a discussion of why the prediction fails.

[M2] Balance test discussion internally contradictory

Problem: Section 5.2 (Threat 2) honestly notes “the pattern of significant imbalance across all four observables is more concerning” while Section 6.2 dismisses imbalance as “well inside negligible.”

Fix: Harmonize. The honest version (5.2) is better. Section 6.2 should reference it and acknowledge the tension.

[M3] Enriched Bajari-Ye first stage not reported in text

Problem: The enriched first stage (firm age + CNAE sector, R² 0.776→0.803, KS unchanged) is an important robustness finding but does not appear in the manuscript text.

Fix: Add a paragraph in Section 6.3 noting that enriching the first stage does not change results. Correct Section 5.4 description.

[M4] Welfare figure caption inconsistent

Problem: Figure D.2 caption still says “OLS (lower bound) and IV (upper bound),” contradicting the text’s “back-of-the-envelope” framing.

Fix: Update the figure caption.

4. Minor Comments

  1. REVISION-V5 tags: ~20 LaTeX comments visible. Remove before submission.
  2. Section 5.4: Text describes first stage as including “reference price” but Table B.10 only has porte. Correct the text (ref price is tender-level, absorbed by item FE).
  3. Section 7.4 (Learning): Note that a one-year age difference could reflect firm entry timing across product markets, not just learning.
  4. Table 7 notes: Explain economic rationale for HHI median + repeat partners ≥2 cutoffs.
  5. Section 8.3: Note that the Imhof proxy (CV median split) is coarser than the full Imhof (2017) implementation which uses multiple features and a trained classifier.
  6. Section 4.2: The IQR footnote is improved but could frame the threshold more economically: “participation frequency so extreme it is difficult to rationalize as competitive optimization.”
  7. Conditional descriptive statistics: The 0.050 residualized gap finding is important. Consider mentioning it in the Introduction when presenting the OLS result.
  8. Section 6.2: LATE interpretation paragraph is standard and takes more space than needed given the IV is now supplementary. Consider compressing.

5. Specific Comments on Identification

5.1 OLS (H1)

The OLS baseline is the paper’s strongest result. The revision strengthens it with: (a) conditional descriptive statistics showing FE absorb 98% of the unconditional gap; (b) horse-race showing FL persists after Imhof control. Together with matching, sensitivity analysis, and alternative FL definitions, the OLS evidence for H1 is strong. No further work needed.

5.2 Leave-One-Out IV (H2)

Appropriately reframed as supplementary. The honest treatment of exclusion restriction threats (Section 5.2) is a significant improvement over the original. The internal contradiction between Sections 5.2 and 6.2 should be resolved (M2). No new estimation needed.

5.3 Bajari–Ye Tests

Substantially improved. Tender FE results in main text with honest dual-interpretation is the most important change from v4. Enriched first stage (firm age + CNAE) showing R² barely changes is a useful robustness finding. Remaining issue: Prediction 5 should be revised (M1). The Bajari–Ye contribution is now properly framed as “descriptive evidence of bid heterogeneity.”

5.4 Alternative Mechanisms

New Section 7.4 usefully tests four non-collusive explanations. None can individually account for the 6–9% price effect. The minimum-bidder compliance test is particularly compelling: the FL effect is larger in pregão (no minimum requirement) than convite. The learning test is honest about directional consistency. This section meaningfully narrows the space of alternatives.

6. Assessment of Contribution

Contribution 1: FL as Screening Marker (H1)

Assessment: Strong

Significantly strengthened by horse-race regression (FL orthogonal to Imhof screens, corr=0.06) and conditional descriptive statistics. Now well-established for IJIO.

Contribution 2: Suggestive Evidence for Cover Bidding (H2)

Assessment: Appropriately Calibrated

Correctly reframed as suggestive. Convergence of IV, network, and bid-level evidence presented honestly. Alternative mechanisms narrow non-collusive explanations. Sufficient as secondary contribution.

Contribution 3: Bajari–Ye with FL Partition

Assessment: Improved

Honest tender-FE presentation. Enriched first stage rules out omitted firm controls. Framed as descriptive evidence. Prediction 5 needs revision (M1).

Overall Novelty

The FL concept is now accurately positioned: a data-parsimonious, firm-level screen that captures information orthogonal to bid-level tools. The horse-race provides empirical evidence for complementarity. Solid applied IO contribution.

7. Conclusion & Recommendation

I recommend Minor Revision. The paper has substantially improved. The three conditions for IJIO acceptance are largely met. Four remaining items:

  1. Revise Prediction 5 to match tender-FE findings (M1)
  2. Harmonize balance test discussion between Sections 5.2 and 6.2 (M2)
  3. Add enriched Bajari–Ye results to manuscript text (M3)
  4. Fix welfare figure caption (M4)

These are minor changes requiring no new estimation. The paper is ready for publication at IJIO after this final pass.
