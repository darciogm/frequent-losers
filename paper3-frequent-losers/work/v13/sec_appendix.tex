\section{Cover-Bidding Framework and Proofs}\label{app:proofs}
\noindent\textit{(Online Appendix.)}\medskip

This appendix formalizes the cover-bidding framework summarized
in the main text and derives the four propositions referenced
there. The framework is intended as an organizing device for
the empirical patterns; it is not the source of identification.

\paragraph{Setup.}
A cartel of $n$ firms in a procurement market controls a
designated winner with bid $b^*$ and deploys $m \geq 0$ cover
bidders, each submitting a bid $b_\ell > b^*$. Per-bidder
deployment cost is $c_1 > 0$; the cartel faces a detection
probability $\theta_k \in (0,1)$ that depends on oversight
capacity in market $k$ (smaller, less-supervised buyers have
lower $\theta_k$). The procurement modality $\tau$ imposes a
minimum-bidder constraint $n + m \geq \underline{n}(\tau)$,
with $\underline{n}(\text{convite}) = 3$ and
$\underline{n}(\text{preg\~ao}) = 1$. The cartel maximizes
expected surplus $\pi(m, \theta_k, c_1; \tau)$, the difference
between cartel rents and the sum of cover-bidder costs and
expected detection penalties. We impose three standard
assumptions:

\begin{description}
\item[A1 (Profit positivity).] $\partial \pi / \partial m \big|_{m=0} > 0$:
the first cover bidder generates positive marginal cartel
surplus, otherwise no cartel deploys cover.
\item[A2 (Diminishing returns).] $\partial^2 \pi / \partial m^2 < 0$:
the surplus function is strictly concave in $m$, so an interior
optimum exists.
\item[A3 (HHI-cover complementarity).] $\partial^2 \pi /
\partial m \, \partial \mathrm{HHI} < 0$: cover bidders are
more valuable in less-concentrated (more-competitive) markets,
because genuine competition exerts downward price pressure that
cover bidders are deployed to neutralize.
\end{description}

\paragraph{Predictions.}
Under A1--A3, the framework yields four testable predictions
derived in the proofs below. Proposition~\ref{prop:optimal_m}
characterizes $m^*$ and its comparative statics
($\partial m^*/\partial \theta_k < 0$,
$\partial m^*/\partial c_1 < 0$, $m^*$ weakly larger under
convite). Proposition~\ref{prop:regime1} characterizes the
maximum-entropy cover-bid distribution when the cartel maximizes
detectability-evasion subject to a support constraint
(complementary regime, uniform bids above $b^*$, higher
dispersion than genuine bids). Proposition~\ref{prop:regime2}
characterizes the truncated-normal cover-bid distribution when
the cartel maximizes price-anchor proximity (coordinated
regime, bids clustered near $b^*$, lower dispersion than
genuine bids). Proposition~\ref{prop:market_selection} states
the market-selection result: $\partial m^*/\partial \mathrm{HHI} < 0$,
so cover bidding concentrates in competitive markets.

\begin{proof}[\textbf{Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:optimal_m}
(Optimal number of cover bidders)}]
Relaxing the integer constraint, the cartel's first-order condition
in $m$ is
\begin{equation}
  \frac{\partial \pi}{\partial m} = c_1 + \theta_k \phi_0,
  \label{eq:foc_m}
\end{equation}
where $\phi_0 > 0$ is the marginal expected penalty per cover
bidder evaluated at the optimum. By A2,
$\partial^2 \pi / \partial m^2 < 0$, so the implicit interior
solution $\tilde{m}$ defined by~\eqref{eq:foc_m} is unique. When
the minimum-bidder constraint $n + m \geq \underline{n}(\tau)$
binds---i.e., $\underline{n}(\tau) - n > \tilde{m}$---the optimum
is the corner $m^* = \underline{n}(\tau) - n$; otherwise
$m^* = \lfloor \tilde{m} \rfloor$. Implicit differentiation of
the FOC yields
$\partial m^*/\partial \theta_k =
-\phi_0 / (\partial^2 \pi / \partial m^2) < 0$ and
$\partial m^*/\partial c_1 =
-1 / (\partial^2 \pi / \partial m^2) < 0$ at the interior
solution. Property (iii) follows because
$\underline{n}(\text{convite}) > \underline{n}(\text{preg\~ao})$
weakly raises the corner solution; whenever the corner binds,
$m^*$ is strictly larger under convite. \qed
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[\textbf{Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:regime1}
(Regime 1: Complementary cover bidding)}]
Suppose the cartel selects the cover-bid distribution $G$ on
support $[b^*, b^* + \delta]$ to maximize entropy
$H(G) = -\int g \log g$ subject to $\mathrm{supp}(G) \subseteq
[b^*, b^* + \delta]$. The maximum-entropy distribution on a
compact interval is uniform: by Gibbs' inequality,
$D_{\mathrm{KL}}(G \,\|\, U) = -H(G) - \log \delta \geq 0$
with equality iff $G = U$ almost everywhere on
$[b^*, b^* + \delta]$. Hence $G_1 = U[b^*, b^* + \delta]$.
The variance of $G_1$ is $\delta^2/12$. For the cartel that
chooses $\delta$ such that $\delta^2/12 > \sigma_g^2$ (cover
bids more dispersed than genuine bids),
$\mathrm{Var}(b_\ell) > \sigma_g^2$, the higher-dispersion
prediction the regime is named for. \qed
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[\textbf{Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:regime2}
(Regime 2: Coordinated cover bidding)}]
Suppose cover bids follow
$b_\ell \sim \mathrm{TruncNormal}(\mu_c, \sigma_c^2, b^*, \infty)$
with $\mu_c = b^* + \epsilon$, $\epsilon > 0$. Truncation at
$b^*$ ensures $\Pr(b_\ell > b^*) = 1$ by construction. Let
$\alpha \equiv (b^* - \mu_c)/\sigma_c = -\epsilon/\sigma_c < 0$
and $\lambda(\alpha) \equiv \phi(\alpha)/(1 - \Phi(\alpha))$,
the inverse Mills ratio. Standard truncated-normal moments
\citep[][p.\ 156]{imbens2015causal} give
\[
  \mathrm{Var}(b_\ell \mid b_\ell > b^*) =
  \sigma_c^2 \bigl[1 - \delta(\alpha)\bigr], \qquad
  \delta(\alpha) = \lambda(\alpha)\bigl[\lambda(\alpha) - \alpha\bigr] > 0
\]
for all finite $\alpha$. Hence
$\mathrm{Var}(b_\ell \mid b_\ell > b^*) < \sigma_c^2$ strictly.
If the cartel sets $\sigma_c$ no greater than the genuine-bid
standard deviation $\sigma_g$, left-truncation compresses
observed dispersion below $\sigma_g$, yielding the
lower-dispersion prediction the regime is named for. The
empirical $\hat{\sigma}_c / \hat{\sigma}_g = 0.72$ reported in
\ref{app:structural_id} is consistent with $\sigma_c \leq \sigma_g$
and $\alpha < 0$, so the data select Regime 2 over Regime 1
under the BIC criterion in \ref{app:structural_id}. \qed
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[\textbf{Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:market_selection}
(Market-selection prediction)}]
From the interior FOC \eqref{eq:foc_m},
$\partial \pi / \partial m = c_1 + \theta_k \phi_0$. The
right-hand side does not depend on $\mathrm{HHI}$. Implicit
differentiation of the FOC with respect to $\mathrm{HHI}$ yields
\[
  \frac{\partial^2 \pi}{\partial m^2} \cdot
  \frac{\partial m^*}{\partial \mathrm{HHI}}
  + \frac{\partial^2 \pi}{\partial m \, \partial \mathrm{HHI}} = 0
  \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
  \frac{\partial m^*}{\partial \mathrm{HHI}} =
  -\frac{\partial^2 \pi / \partial m \, \partial \mathrm{HHI}}
        {\partial^2 \pi / \partial m^2}.
\]
By A2 the denominator is negative; by A3 the numerator is
negative. Hence $\partial m^*/\partial \mathrm{HHI} < 0$:
optimal cover-bidder deployment falls with market
concentration. \qed
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Linearized cumulative exposure]\label{prop:exposure_stmt}
Suppose firm $i$ is a cover bidder of type $C$ deployed by a cartel
in market $k$ at per-period rate $\lambda_C(\theta_k, c_1)$ and
tenure $\tau_i$. Under A1--A3 and a stationary
deployment process,
\[
  \mathbb{E}\!\left[\,\text{tenders\_count}_i \mid \text{type}=C,\
                                                  \tau_i\right]
  \;=\; \lambda_C(\theta_k, c_1)\,\tau_i.
\]
For genuine competitive entrants of type $G$,
$\mathbb{E}[\text{tenders\_count}_i \mid \text{type}=G, \tau_i]
= \lambda_G\,\tau_i$ with $\lambda_G < \lambda_C$ in any
cartel-targeted market: by Proposition~\ref{prop:optimal_m},
$m^* > 0$ implies positive equilibrium deployment of cover
bidders, while genuine entry does not respond to cartel demand
for participation slots. The log-statistic
$\log(1+\text{tenders\_count}_i)$ is therefore monotone in
$\lambda$ given tenure, and---under monotone likelihood ratio
in the Poisson approximation
$T_i \sim \mathrm{Poisson}(\lambda \tau_i)$---the
posterior $\Pr(\text{type}=C \mid \text{tenders\_count}_i = t)$
is non-decreasing in $t$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[\textbf{Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:exposure_stmt}}]
The expectation result is immediate from the law of large
numbers applied to the deployment counting process given
constant rate $\lambda$. The monotonicity claim follows from
Bayes' rule:
\[
  \Pr(C \mid t) =
  \frac{\Pr(t \mid C)\,\pi_C}{\Pr(t \mid C)\,\pi_C
                              + \Pr(t \mid G)\,(1-\pi_C)},
\]
which is non-decreasing in $t$ if and only if the likelihood
ratio $\Pr(t \mid C)/\Pr(t \mid G)$ is non-decreasing in $t$.
For Poisson likelihoods with rates $\lambda_C > \lambda_G$,
this MLR property is standard. Hence
$\log(1+\text{tenders\_count})$ is a sufficient ranking
statistic for the cover-bidder type, and the binary indicator
$\mathbf{1}[\text{tenders\_count} > k]$ is its
information-coarsening operational form. \qed
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Wins-zero separating equilibrium]\label{lem:wins_zero_stmt}
Suppose cover bidders draw their bid $b_C$ from a strategy that
satisfies (a) $b_C > b^*$ in equilibrium (cartel-allocation
constraint) and (b) winning triggers either capacity revelation
(undermining the cover identity in subsequent rounds) or
internal cartel sanctions on the deviating firm. Genuine
entrants of type $G$ face no such constraint and accept any
$b_G < b^*$ that yields positive expected payoff.
Then in the separating equilibrium, cover bidders satisfy
$\Pr(\text{win} \mid C) = 0$, i.e., the bright-line filter
$\text{wins}=0$ is the equilibrium choice of type $C$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}[\textbf{Proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:wins_zero_stmt}}]
Write the cover-bidder per-period payoff as
$u_C(b) = -c_1 - \theta_k\phi_0 \cdot \mathbf{1}[\text{win}]
       - \kappa\cdot\mathbf{1}[\text{win}]$, where $\kappa>0$
captures expected cartel-internal sanction or capacity
revelation cost. Choosing $b_C > b^*$ delivers
$\Pr(\text{win}|b_C, b^*) = 0$ in any equilibrium where the
designated winner submits $b^*$, so $u_C = -c_1$. Any deviation
to $b_C' < b^*$ delivers $u_C' = -c_1 - \theta_k\phi_0 - \kappa
+ \mathrm{rents}$, where $\mathrm{rents}$ is the deviation gain
from winning. Under the cartel's allocation rule
$\mathrm{rents} < \kappa$ for any cover bidder (otherwise the
allocation would not be incentive-compatible). Hence
$u_C > u_C'$, and the equilibrium strategy is $b_C > b^*$ with
$\Pr(\text{win}|C) = 0$. By contrast, type $G$ has no $\kappa$
penalty and accepts $b_G < b^*$ when expected payoff is
positive. Hence the empirical filter $\text{wins} = 0$
identifies the type-$C$ subset, conditional on the cartel
deploying its allocation rule. \qed
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Bayesian implication: the construct as a sufficient screen.}
Combining Proposition~\ref{prop:exposure_stmt} and
Lemma~\ref{lem:wins_zero_stmt}, the joint statistic
$(\text{tenders\_count}, \mathbf{1}[\text{wins}=0])$ is a
sufficient screen for cover-bidder type given award-record
data: the wins-zero filter selects the cover-bidder pool sharply
at the equilibrium type boundary, and the tenders-count statistic
ranks within that pool by posterior cover-bidder probability.
This is the formal sense in which the construct is theoretically
derived rather than data-mined: the binary $\valFL$ rule is the
information-coarsening of the latent posterior under the
framework's separating equilibrium. The continuous score is the
substantive object; the binary rule is its administrative
implementation.

% JLEO-R2: empirical tension with A3 acknowledged honestly
\paragraph{Empirical tension with A3.}
Proposition~\ref{prop:market_selection} predicts
$\partial m^*/\partial \mathrm{HHI} < 0$ under A3---i.e., that
cover-bidder deployment concentrates in less-concentrated
markets. The empirical decomposition reported in
Table~\ref{tab:unified_mechanism} is partially consistent with
this prediction in the dominant cell (Low HHI $\times$ Low pairs,
$+\valMechQLLLCoef\%$), but the High HHI $\times$ High pairs
cell returns a \emph{negative} coefficient
($\valMechQLHHCoef\%$, $p=\valMechQLHHP$).
We do not interpret this as falsification of A3 in isolation,
because the negative cell is small ($\valMechQLHHN$ items) and is
plausibly a selection cell---environments under or already
targeted by CADE enforcement, where cartel activity has been
deterred or prosecuted. We acknowledge, however, that this
leaves the mechanism partially unidentified: the framework
guides the interpretation of the broader empirical patterns
but the data do not deliver a single canonical mechanism, and
the coefficient sign in the High HHI $\times$ High pairs cell
remains an open puzzle. We treat the empirical patterns as
risk heterogeneity rather than mechanism evidence.

\paragraph{Economic content of A3.}
A3 states that cover bidders are more valuable in less-concentrated
markets: when $\mathrm{HHI}$ is low, genuine bidders exert
downward price pressure, and cover bidders create noise, satisfy
minimum-bidder rules, and obscure the cartel's bid pattern from
forensic screens. When $\mathrm{HHI}$ is high, the cartel sustains
prices through market power alone and the marginal return to
manufactured competition falls.

\section{Structural Model: Identification, Estimation, and Specification Tests}
\label{app:structural_id}
\noindent\textit{(Online Appendix.)}\medskip

This appendix specifies the bid equation, identifies the
structural parameters, reports two-stage maximum-likelihood
estimates, selects between the cover-bidding regimes of
\ref{app:proofs} via BIC, and presents three out-of-sample
specification tests on moments not used in estimation. The
exercise is a calibration of the cover-bidding mechanism and the
distribution of FL bids; we do not claim independent
identification of the markup or the causal price effect.

\subsection{Bid Equation and Estimation}

The bid-level data comprise \valStatusBidsTotal non-FL losing bids
and \valItemsCount FL losing bids on \valStatusBidsTotal tender-items with
valid prices and item-year fixed-effect coverage (after
restricting to losing bids per
Section~\ref{sec:supporting_setup}). The bid equation is
\begin{equation}
  \log b_{jt} = \mathbf{x}_j' \boldsymbol{\beta}
  + \alpha_i + \lambda_t + u_{jt},
  \label{eq:by_first_stage}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{x}_j$ contains firm size, firm age, and CNAE
sector dummies; $\alpha_i$ is the item fixed effect; $\lambda_t$
the year fixed effect; and $u_{jt}$ a bid-level residual. The
first stage absorbs item-year structural variation in genuine
costs and markups; the residuals carry the FL/non-FL signal
exploited by the structural likelihood and the Bajari--Ye tests
of \S\ref{sec:results_bajari}.

We estimate the cover-bid mixture by maximum likelihood in two
stages. \emph{Stage 1}: estimate
$(\boldsymbol{\beta}, \sigma_g^2)$ from the non-FL losing bids
under a log-normal specification, which reduces to OLS of
$\log b_{jt}$ on $\mathbf{x}_j$ with item and year fixed
effects; the residual variance gives $\hat{\sigma}_g^2$.
\emph{Stage 2}: conditional on $b^*_t$ observed in each tender,
estimate cover-bid parameters from FL bids under each regime
specification of \ref{app:proofs}. Under Regime~1, the support
parameter $\delta$ is identified from the range of FL bids
above $b^*_t$; under Regime~2, the location and scale
$(\epsilon, \sigma_c)$ are identified from the mean and
variance of $\log b_{\ell t} - \log b^*_t$ among FL bids.
Standard errors come from a parametric bootstrap (500
replications, tender-level resampling).

\subsection{Identifying Assumptions}

\begin{description}
\item[B1 (Genuine-bid exclusion).] FL status does not enter the
genuine-bid distribution $F_{\mathrm{genuine}}$: conditional on
observables and item-year fixed effects, a genuine bidder's cost
draw and markup are independent of whether FL firms participate
in the same tender. \emph{Test:} comparing $\hat{\sigma}_g^2$ on
genuine-only subsamples that do vs.\ do not include FL bids in
the same tender yields a difference of $\valBidIVraw$ (NS), consistent
with the exclusion restriction.

\item[B2 (Conditional cover-bid identification).] $b^*_t$ is
observed in each tender (it is the realized winning bid), so
cover-bid parameters are identified conditional on $b^*_t$
without further restriction. The assumption is data-driven: we
do not need to model the cartel's choice of $b^*_t$ to identify
the distribution of $b_{\ell t}$ above it.

\item[B3 (Detection probability bounded, not point-identified).]
$\theta_k$ is not point-identified from bid data alone. We
bound it using cross-PBU variation in oversight capacity (PBU
size as a proxy for $\theta_k$) and test the comparative static
$\partial m^*/\partial \theta_k < 0$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:optimal_m})
in the oversight-heterogeneity analysis of
\S\ref{sec:robustness}: the FL coefficient varies $0.214 \to
0.017$ across PBU-size quartiles Q1--Q4, monotonically and in
the predicted direction.

\item[B4 (Markup not separately identified).] The cartel markup
$b^*_t - c_{j^*t}$ requires observing the cartel's true cost
$c_{j^*t}$, which the data do not provide. The structural
model therefore characterizes the cover-bid \emph{mechanism}
(distribution and regime selection), not the cartel
\emph{markup}. The implied markup we report in
Table~\ref{tab:structural_params} is a calibration that
mechanically applies the cover-bid distribution to the
reduced-form coefficients; it is not an independent estimate
of the price effect.
\end{description}

\subsection{Regime Selection and Specification Tests}

\paragraph{BIC selects Regime 2.}
The two-stage likelihood evaluated under each regime
specification yields BIC values that select Regime 2
(coordinated cover bidding) by a margin of $\Delta\mathrm{BIC} =
\valCVStructPre$ (Table~\ref{tab:structural_params}). The fitted
ratio $\hat{\sigma}_c / \hat{\sigma}_g = 0.72$ is consistent
with $\sigma_c \leq \sigma_g$ and left-truncation at $b^*$, the
sufficient condition for the lower-dispersion prediction of
Proposition~\ref{prop:regime2}. The BIC margin is large but is
itself a within-sample selection criterion; the three
out-of-sample tests below provide independent confirmation.

\paragraph{Out-of-sample test 1: Corner vs.\ interior solution.}
Proposition~\ref{prop:optimal_m} predicts that the
minimum-bidder constraint forces $m \geq 3$ when no genuine
bidders enter ($n_{\mathrm{genuine}} = 0$, corner solution) and
that the interior solution is achieved when at least two
genuine bidders are present. The data: mean FL count is $\valFLcountMean$
under $n_{\mathrm{genuine}} = 0$ and $\approx 2$ for
$n_{\mathrm{genuine}} \geq 2$. The corner-vs-interior gap of
roughly five-fold matches the sign and order of magnitude the
proposition predicts and is computed on a moment not used in
estimation.

\paragraph{Out-of-sample test 2: Within-tender bid dispersion.}
Proposition~\ref{prop:regime2} predicts that FL bids are less
dispersed than non-FL bids \emph{within} a tender, conditional
on the winning bid. In the \valFLBidEvent tenders with at least two
FL and at least two non-FL bidders, the FL bid CV is $\valFLBidCV$
versus $\valNonFLBidCV$ for non-FL bids; FL dispersion is lower in
$\valPctEightytwosix$ of tenders (paired $t = -104$, $p < 0.001$). This is
the sharpest validation of Regime 2 at the tender level: it
holds within-tender, controlling for the focal-point variation
that drives the BIC selection.

\paragraph{Out-of-sample test 3: $m$--$\theta_k$ comparative
static.}
Proposition~\ref{prop:optimal_m} predicts $\partial m^* /
\partial \theta_k < 0$: cover-bidder deployment falls with
oversight capacity. Using PBU size as a proxy for $\theta_k$
(larger buyers face more administrative scrutiny), mean FL
firms per tender decreases monotonically across the four
PBU-size quartiles: $\valPBUQone$ (Q1, smallest) $\to 0.39$ (Q2) $\to
0.34$ (Q3) $\to 0.31$ (Q4, largest). The prediction is
qualitative; the monotonic decline across quartiles is the
out-of-sample bite. Combined with the FL-coefficient gradient
of $0.214 \to 0.017$ across the same quartiles
(\S\ref{sec:robustness}), the test confirms the comparative
static in both quantities Proposition~\ref{prop:optimal_m}
constrains.

\paragraph{What the structural exercise does and does not deliver.}
The exercise selects a regime, calibrates the cover-bid
distribution within it, and confirms three out-of-sample
predictions. It does not identify the cartel markup or the
causal effect of FL participation on prices. The implied
markup we report ($\approx \valPctSixfour$) is by construction
mechanically tied to the reduced-form coefficient and serves
as a consistency check, not as an independent estimate. The
welfare calculations in \ref{app:extensions} apply the
reduced-form coefficient to FL-present spending and inherit the
same caveat.

The version of this comparison reported in earlier drafts of the
paper used the within-tender coefficient of variation alone as
the bid-distribution benchmark, which yields AUC $\valAUCImhofCV$ in our
data and is not a representative implementation of the screening
literature \citep{imhof2019detecting, huber2019machine,

discrimination at substantially lower data cost}, not
displacement of the bid-distribution literature. The full Imhof
pipeline requires per-bid offer values for every tender; the
participation-only screen requires only the participant list
and the win flag in each item. Brazilian Tribunais de Contas,
the Controladoria Geral da Uni\~ao, and CADE investigators
triaging tips routinely face data envelopes in which the latter
are available and the former are not. The data-cost wedge is
the operational contribution.

classifier that uses both the participation-only feature and
the five bid-distribution features reaches AUC
$\valAUCImhofPlusFL$; replacing the binary rule with the
continuous loss-intensity score yields $\valAUCImhofPlusTC$.
The combined model gains roughly ten AUC points over the rich
benchmark alone, indicating that participation-only and
bid-distribution information sources are not measuring the same
underlying object. Where bid microdata are available, our
recommendation is to combine the two; where they are not, the
participation-only construct remains deployable at sharply lower
data cost.

\section{Staggered Designs We Attempted and Rejected}
\label{app:did}
\noindent\textit{(Online Appendix; reported for transparency.
Designs declared invalid for this setting in
\S\ref{sec:robustness}; do not interpret estimates below as
robustness checks for the headline coefficient.)}\medskip

This appendix documents three staggered event-study designs we
implemented and the diagnostics that led us to reject them. The
designs treat first FL entry into a PBU $\times$ item market as
the treatment of interest. We report them so a reader
considering similar designs in a comparable setting can see
both the implementation and the rejection criteria.

\paragraph{Sample and estimators.}
The market-level panel (PBU $\times$ item-group $\times$ year)
restricted to markets with at least three pre- and three
post-treatment observations contains $\valDiDmarketYear$ market-year
observations across $\valDiDmarkets$ markets ($\valDiDtreated$ treated,
$\valDiDneverTreated$ never-treated). We estimate three specifications:
the TWFE event study around first FL entry; the
\citet{callaway2021difference} doubly-robust estimator using
never-treated units as the comparison group; and the
\citet{cengiz2019effect} stacked regression with cohort-specific
market and year fixed effects (avoiding the
``bad-comparison'' bias of standard TWFE under heterogeneous
treatment effects). Outcomes are log negotiated price and
log non-FL firm count.

\paragraph{Why the designs fail.}
The estimating assumption common to all three is parallel
pre-trends in the absence of treatment. The TWFE event study
(Figure~\ref{fig:event_study}) yields pre-event coefficients
at $t = -2$ and $t = -3$ that are positive and similar in
magnitude to the post-event coefficient. Markets that
eventually receive FL entry already had higher prices two and
three years before. Two readings of the pre-trend are
observationally equivalent: cartels select into already-high-price
markets and then deploy cover bidders, or unobserved
within-market dynamics predate FL arrival. Either reading
violates the parallel-trends assumption the staggered
estimators require, and \citet{rambachan2023more} sensitivity
bounds confirm that the maximum pre-trend slope under which
the conclusion would survive is implausibly small.

\paragraph{Estimates that follow are diagnostics, not robustness.}
\citet{callaway2021difference} doubly-robust ATT $= 0.014$
(SE $\valCSseverr$); \citet{cengiz2019effect} stacked ATT $= -0.006$
(SE $\valCSlimit$). The stacked $95\%$ CI $(-0.034, 0.022)$ excludes
the OLS baseline of $\valThreshOneFivex$. The discrepancy between staggered
and OLS is mechanical: under violated parallel trends the
estimators identify a different object (the within-cohort
deviation from a counterfactual that depends on never-treated
trajectories), and that object is not the conditional
association the OLS specification estimates. The right reading
is that staggered DiD identification requires exogenous
treatment timing the FL setting does not provide. The
conditional-association range we report in
\S\ref{sec:results_ols} is robust to this constraint, not
despite it: the estimands differ, and the staggered designs
target the wrong one.

\paragraph{Tables and figure.}
Table~\ref{tab:did_revised} reports the TWFE and C\&S
estimates with pre-trend coefficients and Rambachan--Roth
sensitivity bounds. Figure~\ref{fig:event_study} plots the
TWFE event study coefficients. Table~\ref{tab:stacked_did}
reports the stacked regression with cohort-specific fixed
effects.

\section{Illustrative Welfare Calculations}
\label{app:extensions}
\noindent\textit{(Online Appendix. The calculations below assume
causality the paper does not establish; read as rough benchmarks,
not policy estimates.)}\medskip

This appendix reports back-of-the-envelope welfare calculations
that mechanically apply the headline FL price coefficients to
FL-present procurement spending. The exercise quantifies an
upper bound on the overcharge that would be implied \emph{if}
the conditional-association coefficients were causal. The
identification of \S\ref{sec:empirical} does not deliver that
causality, so the figures below are illustrative.

\paragraph{Computation.}
Let $\beta$ denote the price coefficient on FL presence, $s$
the share of BEC procurement spending in FL-present tenders,
and $S$ total BEC procurement spending. The implied overcharge
under the assumption of causal $\beta$ is
$W = \beta \cdot s \cdot S$. We compute $s = 0.048$
(FL-present share of tender-items, weighted by spending; from
Table~\ref{tab:descstats}) and report $W/S$ across four
coefficient choices:

\begin{itemize}
\item OLS baseline ($\beta = 0.064$): $W/S \approx \valWelfareTinyTwo$
\item Matching, CEM ($\beta = 0.077$): $W/S \approx \valWelfareTinyThree$
\item Cross-fit ($\beta = 0.036$): $W/S \approx \valWelfareTinyOne$
\item IV diagnostic ($\beta = 0.194$): $W/S \approx \valWelfareTinyFour$
\end{itemize}

The OECD cartel-overcharge benchmark range
(\citealt{connor2007price}, \citealt{oecd2019government}: 5--30\%
of contract value in established cartels) is roughly an order
of magnitude higher than the FL-spending-weighted figures.
The discrepancy reflects two facts: FL-flagged tenders are
\valCartelMarkup of all tender-items, so even a \valPctSixfour price effect on
FL-flagged tenders dilutes to a small share of total
procurement spending; and the headline conditional association
is below the typical cartel overcharge documented in
post-conviction record reviews. Detailed bounds and
year-by-year decompositions appear in
Table~\ref{tab:welfare_bounds} and
Figure~\ref{fig:welfare}.

\paragraph{Why these are not causal estimates.}
The mechanical application of $\beta$ to FL-present spending
\emph{assumes} that $\beta$ would persist if FL participation
were exogenously removed. The conditional-association design
of \S\ref{sec:reduced_form} does not establish this
counterfactual: pre-event coefficients in the FL-entry event
study at $t = -2, -3$ remain positive, indicating cartel
selection into already-high-price markets that the FL flag
does not by itself remove. A causal welfare quantification
requires a setting with exogenous variation in FL participation;
the 2021 Brazilian reform (Lei 14.133/2021) is the natural
candidate, and replication on post-2021 BEC data---if and when
the data become available---would exploit the reform's
restriction of convite as a policy-driven first-stage on FL
deployment.

\section{Heterogeneity and FL Characteristics}
\label{app:heterogeneity}
\noindent\textit{(Online Appendix.)}\medskip

This appendix reports the network-based heterogeneity decomposition
that supports the network-split classification of \S\ref{sec:empirical_network}
and the descriptive statistics on the FL co-bidding network. Two
results structure the appendix: \emph{(i)} the FL price
coefficient is concentrated among FL firms operating in
competitive markets (low winner HHI), confirming the
market-selection prediction of Proposition~\ref{prop:market_selection};
\emph{(ii)} the winner-HHI distribution among FL firms is
right-skewed, with a mode in the 0.20--0.40 range characteristic
of moderately diversified loser-winner relationships.

\paragraph{Network-based summary statistics.}
Table~\ref{tab:fl_network_summary} reports the network metrics
underlying the concentrated-vs-competitive classification. The
median winner HHI among FL firms is 0.178 (14.3 unique
winners), substantially below the non-FL always-loser median
of 0.303 (5.0 winners; $p < 0.001$). FL firms co-bid with a
broader set of winners than other always-losers, consistent
with rotation of designated winners across competitive markets
(\S\ref{sec:mechanisms}, M5 cartel-ecosystem reading). The
network-based concentrated-FL classification combines two
necessary conditions for sustained coordination: winner HHI
above the sample median, and at least two repeat co-bidding
partners.

\paragraph{Network-split price coefficient.}
Figure~\ref{fig:network_split} displays the FL price
coefficient separately for the concentrated and competitive
subgroups under the PBU FE specification. The competitive-market
subgroup yields $\valCompFLcoef$ ($p < 0.01$); the concentrated-market
subgroup yields $\valConcMarketcoef$ (insignificant). The competitive-FL
subgroup drives the entire price effect, the empirical
counterpart of Proposition~\ref{prop:market_selection}'s
prediction that cover bidding is most profitable where
genuine competition is otherwise active. Three competing
readings of the concentrated-market null are discussed in
\S\ref{sec:results_network} and remain unresolved with
cross-sectional data.

\paragraph{Winner-HHI distribution.}
Figure~\ref{fig:network_hhi} plots the empirical distribution
of winner HHI across FL firms. The distribution is right-skewed
with a mode in the 0.20--0.40 range; 70\% of FL firms have
winner HHI below 0.40 (diversified losing partnerships), and
the right tail above 0.60 captures the concentrated-market
subgroup that sustains repeat coordination with a small set of
winners. The distributional shape is consistent with a cartel
ecosystem in which most FL firms are deployed across
heterogeneous tender pools---generating the diversified
winner-pattern signature the network-split test exploits.
