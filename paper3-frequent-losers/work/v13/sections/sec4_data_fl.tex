\section{Data and Frequent Losers Definition}
\label{sec:data}

\subsection{BEC Platform}
\label{sec:bec}

The data come from the Bolsa Eletr\^onica de Compras (BEC), S\~ao
Paulo's centralized electronic procurement platform. BEC serves 1,308
purchasing units (PBUs) and handles two modalities:
\emph{convite} (sealed-bid, minimum three bidders, Lei~8.666/93) and
\emph{preg\~ao} (electronic reverse auction, no minimum-bidder rule).
The convite minimum-bidder rule creates a direct incentive for cover
bidding; under preg\~ao, cover bidders inflate apparent competition.
The sample spans 2009--2019: 4.5 million tender-items,
40 million bids, 41,000 firms
(Table~\ref{tab:modality_year}, \ref{app:competition}).

\subsection{Frequent Losers Definition}
\label{sec:fl_definition}

\paragraph{Behavioral anchor.} Sustained participation with a zero
win rate is rationally dominated under standard entry models. With
positive per-bid cost $c > 0$, expected profit on a bid by a firm
that has lost $n$ consecutive prior tenders is
$E[\pi_{n+1}] = \pi_w \cdot \bar{V} \cdot m - c$, where $\pi_w$ is
the firm's posterior win probability, $\bar{V}$ the mean
procurement-order value, and $m$ the gross margin. A Bayesian
learner with a uniform prior updates $\pi_w$ toward $1/(n+2)$
after $n$ zero-win trials. At BEC parameters ($\bar{V} = $~R\$$86{,}000$,
$m = 0.10$), the posterior expected gain per bid is
$\bar{V} m / (n+2)$, falling below per-bid cost $c$ once
$n > \bar{V} m / c - 2$. For $c = $~R\$$500$ this threshold is
$n \approx 15$; for $c = $~R\$$200$ it is $n \approx 41$; for the
lower bound $c = $~R\$$50$ it is $n \approx 170$. At the FL
threshold of 14 zero-win participations, expected profit is already
negative under any plausible $c \geq $~R\$$200$ (which captures
documented BEC bid-preparation costs of 1--2 staff-hours plus
documentation compliance); at the 90th-percentile FL participation
of 50+ zero-win tenders, expected profit is negative under any $c$
above R\$$167$ and the cumulative direct cost reaches R\$$10{,}000$
or more. Sustained participation in this regime reveals either
(i) a non-pecuniary objective (cartel side-payment, market
signaling), (ii) systematically incorrect beliefs (a firm that
fails to update on its own losing record), or (iii) zero-win
participation outside BEC offset by wins in unobserved markets.
The screen exploits (i): firms pursuing collusive side-payments
rationally accept negative direct expected profit because the
lateral transfer makes total profit positive. Under any of
(ii)--(iii), the screen produces false positives the validation
exercise should detect.

We define frequent losers (FL) in two steps:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Always-losers}: firms with a zero win rate across all
    2009--2019 tenders. 16,843 firms ($\sim$41\% of all
    BEC participants) never win a single tender. The high always-loser
    rate likely reflects several forces: small firms bidding beyond
    their capacity, BEC's low participation cost (electronic,
    no travel), and---for convite---PBU officials inviting firms to
    meet the three-bidder minimum. FL firms are a small, extreme
    subset of this heterogeneous population.
  \item \textbf{IQR threshold}: among always-losers, we identify those
    with abnormally high participation counts using a median + 1.5
    $\times$ IQR threshold on the distribution of tender
    participations. The primary justification is economic: at the
    resulting threshold of 14 or more tenders with a zero win rate,
    expected profit is strictly negative for any positive bidding cost
    ($E[\pi] = 0 \times \overline{V} \times \text{margin} - c < 0$;
    see Section~\ref{sec:rationality}). Sustained participation at this
    intensity is difficult to rationalize as competitive entry and
    in line with the side-payment arrangements documented in prosecuted
    cartels \citep{asker2010leniency, marshall2012economics}. The
    threshold was fixed \emph{a priori} at 1.5$\times$IQR based on
    this economic rationale, before examining CADE data.%
    \footnote{We use median + 1.5 $\times$ IQR rather than the standard
    Tukey rule (Q3 + 1.5 $\times$ IQR) because the participation
    distribution is highly right-skewed.}
    Post hoc, the ROC analysis in
    Section~\ref{sec:detection_validation} shows that the
    data-driven optimal threshold (Youden's $J = 0.84$) corresponds
    to a multiplier of 1.45$\times$IQR---close to the 1.5$\times$
    rule we chose on economic grounds. This convergence is reassuring
    but was not used to select the threshold; robustness to the
    multiplier is reported in Section~\ref{sec:robustness}.
    The definition yields 2,735 FL firms.
\end{enumerate}

The treatment variable $\text{losers}_{igt}$ equals one if tender-item
$i$ in item group $g$ at time $t$ has at least one FL firm among its
participants, and zero otherwise.

The FL classification is static: it pools participation over the full
2009--2019 window. Classifying FL separately in 2009--2013 and
2014--2019, only 10\% of early-period FL firms reappear as FL in the
second half (111 of 1,095). This turnover could reflect cartel
reorganization, firm exit, or simply noise in the classification. The low persistence means the static classification is noisy and that
FL status should not be read as a stable firm type; it serves better
as an aggregate marker of suspicious tender-level environments than
as a label for individual firms with a persistent collusive role. The
FL phenomenon is not an artifact of a fixed pool of chronic losers
--- the identities rotate, even if the pattern endures. Diagnostics
of the classification---continuous measures, permutation placebos, and
zero-win sensitivity---are reported alongside the price results in
Section~\ref{sec:class_diagnostics}.

\subsection{Sample Construction}
\label{sec:sample}

The participation distribution among always-losers is heavily
right-skewed (Figures~\ref{fig:losses}--\ref{fig:iqr},
\ref{app:robustness}).
The regression sample retains convite and preg\~ao modalities with a
recorded winner and item types with at least one FL-present tender.
This last restriction conditions on the treatment: items that never
attract FL firms are excluded. It ensures controls come from item
markets where FL firms could plausibly participate, but may overstate
the association if FL firms self-select into inherently expensive
item types. The unrestricted sample
(\ref{app:online_robustness}) yields a nearly identical
coefficient, so the restriction is empirically innocuous.
The resulting sample contains 1,654,447 tender-items across 18,783
item types (defined at the finest product-code level available in BEC,
not aggregated to two-digit groups), of which 15,101 (82\%) have both
FL-present and FL-absent tenders and thus contribute to within-item
identification. FL firms
participate in 4.8\% of tender-items.

\subsection{Structural Estimation Sample}
\label{sec:structural_sample}

The structural model requires bid-level data: 40 million bids from
41,000 firms, with bid amount, winning price, firm identity, and FL
classification. FL losing bids exhibit a mean log-spread of 0.83
(SD = 1.19) above the winning price, versus a genuine-bid SD of 1.64.
The ratio $\hat{\sigma}_c / \hat{\sigma}_g = 0.72$ is coherent with Regime~2
(coordinated cover bidding).

\subsection{CADE Sample}
\label{sec:cade_sample}

The CADE consistency check is partially prospective: FL status is
defined on 2009--2019 BEC participation, and validated against 12
CADE cartel cases (65 firm-defendants) with final adjudications
spanning 2015--2025. Eight of the 12 cases were decided after the
end of the BEC sample, so the test asks whether 2009--2019
participation patterns anticipate later enforcement outcomes. Of
the 65 firm-defendants, 47 match BEC participants via CNPJ. Among
2,735 FL firms, 193 (7.1\%) co-participate with at least one of
these 47 firms in the same tender-item---3.5 times the
participation-stratified baseline ($p < 0.001$;
Section~\ref{sec:cade}).

A formal cover-bidding framework---summarized in
\ref{app:proofs} (Online)---organizes the mechanism
diagnostics. The empirical strategy proceeds in
Section~\ref{sec:empirical}.
