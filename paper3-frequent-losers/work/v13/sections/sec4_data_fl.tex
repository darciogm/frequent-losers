\section{Data and Frequent Losers Definition}
\label{sec:data}

\subsection{BEC Platform}
\label{sec:bec}

The data come from the Bolsa Eletr\^onica de Compras (BEC),
São Paulo's centralized electronic procurement platform
operated by the State Treasury since 2002. BEC serves 1,308
purchasing units (PBUs)---state agencies, public universities,
hospitals, schools---and clears two procurement modalities
during our sample: \emph{convite} (sealed-bid, statutory
minimum three bidders, Lei \valLeiOriginal) and \emph{preg\~ao}
(electronic reverse auction, no minimum-bidder rule, Lei
10.520/2002). The convite minimum-bidder rule creates the
regulatory cover-bidding incentive we analyze; preg\~ao serves
as a within-platform comparator. Our 2009--2019 window
comprises \valDataItemAvgVar{,}754 contract-record items
across 22 calendar semesters, generated from \valDataNbidsMean{,}357
individual bids placed by \valBECfirms distinct firms
(Table~\ref{tab:modality_year}, \ref{app:competition}). The
contract-record layer preserves participants, winners,
negotiated prices, reference prices, and procurement metadata;
the bid-by-bid log is preserved in operational systems but
exported to analytical warehouses only on request.

\subsection{Frequent Losers Definition}
\label{sec:fl_definition}

\paragraph{Behavioral anchor.}
Sustained participation with a zero win rate is rationally
dominated under standard entry models. With positive per-bid
cost $c > 0$, expected profit on a bid by a firm that has lost
$n$ consecutive prior tenders is
\[
  E[\pi_{n+1}] = \pi_w \cdot \bar{V} \cdot m - c,
\]
where $\pi_w$ is the firm's posterior win probability, $\bar{V}$
the mean procurement-order value, and $m$ the gross margin. A
Bayesian learner with a uniform prior updates $\pi_w$ toward
$1/(n+2)$ after $n$ zero-win trials, so the expected gain per
bid is $\bar{V} m / (n+2)$ and falls below cost $c$ once
$n > \bar{V} m / c - 2$. At BEC parameters
($\bar{V} = $~R\$$\valDataItemAvgPay$ from the sample mean, $m = 0.10$
from documented procurement-supplier margins), this threshold is
$n \approx 15$ for $c = $~R\$$500$, $n \approx 41$ for
$c = $~R\$$200$, and $n \approx 170$ for the lower bound
$c = $~R\$$50$. The plausible cost range is anchored on
documented BEC bid-preparation effort (1--2 staff-hours plus
documentation compliance, $c \geq $~R\$$200$); at the FL
threshold of 14 zero-win participations expected profit is
already negative under that anchor. At the 90th-percentile FL
participation of 50+ zero-win tenders, expected profit is
negative under any $c$ above R\$$167$ and cumulative direct
cost exceeds R\$$\valPermFirms$. Sustained participation past this
point reveals (i) a non-pecuniary objective---cartel
side-payment, signaling, eligibility maintenance---(ii)
systematically incorrect beliefs that fail to update on the
loss record, or (iii) zero-win participation in BEC offset by
wins in unobserved tenders elsewhere. The screen exploits (i);
under (ii) and (iii) the validation exercise should detect
false positives.

\paragraph{Two-step classification.}
We define frequent losers (FL) as the intersection of:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Always-losers}: firms with a zero win rate across
all 2009--2019 BEC tenders. The pool comprises \valAlwaysLosers firms
(\valPctFortyone of all BEC participants), reflecting low electronic
participation costs, small firms bidding beyond their
operational capacity, and---for convite---PBU officials
inviting firms primarily to meet the three-bidder minimum. FL
firms are a small extreme subset of this population.

\item \textbf{IQR threshold}: among always-losers, those with
participation counts above $\text{median} + 1.5 \times \text{IQR}$
of the always-loser participation distribution.\footnote{We
use median + $1.5 \times$ IQR rather than the standard Tukey rule
(Q3 + $1.5 \times$ IQR) because the participation distribution
is highly right-skewed; the rule we use yields a stricter
threshold and fewer false positives. Robustness to the multiplier
choice is reported in Section~\ref{sec:robustness}.} The
threshold yields 14 or more tenders with zero wins, the level
above which expected profit is negative under any plausible
$c \geq $~R\$$200$. The threshold was fixed \emph{a priori} on
this economic rationale, before any examination of CADE data;
the data-driven optimal threshold from a CADE-based ROC
exercise (Youden's $J = 0.84$) corresponds to a multiplier of
1.45$\times$IQR, close to the rule chosen
\emph{ex ante}. The definition yields \valFL FL firms.
\end{enumerate}

The treatment variable $\text{losers}_{igt}$ equals one if
tender-item $i$ in item group $g$ at time $t$ has at least one
FL firm among its participants, and zero otherwise.

\paragraph{Static classification and rotation.}
The classification pools participation over the full 2009--2019
window. Splitting the sample, only 10\% of FL firms classified
on 2009--2013 data reappear as FL on 2014--2019 data (111 of
\valBoundaryFLone). This is the \emph{environment-marker} reading we use
throughout the paper: FL flags procurement
\emph{environments}---the PBU $\times$ item $\times$ year cells
where regulatory cover bidding operates---rather than firm
\emph{types}. Identities rotate; the patterns endure. The
rotation has three plausible drivers, each consistent with the
environment-marker reading: (a) cartel reorganization (firms
exit and replacement firms take their place); (b) firm exit (FL
firms close, are acquired, or stop bidding); (c) classification
noise near the threshold. Diagnostics distinguishing these
drivers---continuous-measure regressions, permutation placebos,
zero-win sensitivity, market-level persistence---are reported
in Section~\ref{sec:class_diagnostics}.

\subsection{Sample Construction}
\label{sec:sample}

The participation distribution among always-losers is heavily
right-skewed (Figures~\ref{fig:losses}--\ref{fig:iqr},
\ref{app:robustness}). The regression sample retains convite
and preg\~ao tenders with a recorded winner and item types with
at least one FL-present tender during the sample window. The
last restriction conditions on the treatment: items that never
attract FL firms during 2009--2019 are excluded. The
restriction ensures controls come from item markets where FL
firms could plausibly participate; it could overstate the
association if FL firms self-select into inherently
expensive item types. The unrestricted sample
(\ref{app:online_robustness}) yields a coefficient
within 0.5 percentage points of the restricted-sample baseline,
so the restriction is empirically innocuous.

The resulting regression sample contains 1,654,447
tender-items across \valFirmCount item types, defined at the finest
product-code level available in BEC (not aggregated to
two-digit groups). Of the \valFirmCount item types, \valDataFirmMean (82\%)
have both FL-present and FL-absent tenders within sample,
contributing to within-item identification under the
fixed-effects specification of
Equation~\ref{eq:ols_main}. FL firms participate in \valCartelMarkup of
tender-items.

Table~\ref{tab:descstats} reports unconditional descriptive
statistics by FL presence. The unconditional negotiated-price
ratio between FL-present and FL-absent tenders---R\$$\valOCcount$
versus R\$$\valDataItemMean$, a factor of $11\times$---reflects the
sorting of FL firms toward larger contracts: FL-present tenders
have on average $\valDataMedianWinPart$ bidders versus $\valDataMeanWinPart$ in FL-absent
tenders, indicating that FL firms appear in tenders that
attract bidder pools roughly twice the size. Under
within-item, within-year, within-PBU fixed effects, the raw
gap of 2.43 log points compresses to 0.050 log points (overlap
0.978; Table~\ref{tab:conditional_descstats},
\ref{app:competition}); the regressions
compare similar tenders within tight product categories.
Table~\ref{tab:descstats} establishes the magnitude of the
selection problem the fixed effects are designed to absorb.

\subsection{CADE Sample}
\label{sec:cade_sample}

The CADE consistency check is partially prospective. FL status
is defined on 2009--2019 BEC participation; ground truth is
co-bidding with the 47 BEC-active firm-defendants from CADE's
12 procurement-cartel adjudications spanning 2015--2025.
Eight of the 12 cases were decided after the BEC sample window
closes, so the test asks whether 2009--2019 participation
patterns anticipated firms whose collusive role enforcement
confirmed years later. Of the 65 firm-defendants, 47 match BEC
participants via CNPJ. Among \valFL FL firms, 193 (\valCobidShareFL)
co-participate with at least one of these 47 firms in the same
tender-item---3.5 times the participation-stratified baseline
of \valCADEbase ($p < 0.001$; Section~\ref{sec:cade}).

The bid-level structural exercises and Bajari--Ye coordination
tests (Section~\ref{sec:supporting_setup}) are estimated on the
40-million-bid log from BEC, which preserves bid amount,
winning price, firm identity, and the FL classification at the
firm level. The full bid-level setup is reported in
\ref{app:structural_id} (Online); the structural likelihood
sample comprises 23.2 million genuine (non-FL) losing bids and
\valItemsCount FL losing bids after restricting to losing bids with
valid prices and item-year fixed-effect coverage.

A formal cover-bidding framework---summarized in
\ref{app:proofs} (Online)---organizes the mechanism diagnostics.
The empirical strategy proceeds in Section~\ref{sec:empirical}.
