\subsection{Illustrative Policy Counterfactuals}
\label{sec:counterfactual}

The counterfactuals below are illustrative. They take the price
coefficients at face value as causal parameters; to the extent the
estimates reflect selection rather than treatment, the welfare
figures overstate the gains. They should be read as
order-of-magnitude benchmarks, not precise policy estimates.

The structural model generates counterfactual predictions under three
policy levers (Table~\ref{tab:counterfactual_welfare}).

\paragraph{CF1: Removing the minimum-bidder requirement.}
Under convite, \valPctThirtyninetwo of FL-present tenders have $n_{\text{genuine}}
< 3$, forcing cover bidder deployment (corner solution,
Proposition~\ref{prop:optimal_m}); \valPctTwentythreefive have $n = 0$ (pure cover
bidding). Removing the rule yields welfare gains of \valWelfareOLS (OLS) to
\valCounterMaxImp (IV-adjusted) over 2009--2019---a costless institutional
reform.

\paragraph{CF2: Welfare-maximizing screening threshold.}
We embed the ROC curve in a welfare function
$W(t) = \delta \cdot \hat{\beta} \cdot S_{\text{FL}}(t) -
c_{\text{inv}} \cdot N_{\text{flagged}}(t)$,
where $\delta$ is the deterrence rate and $c_{\text{inv}}$ the
per-firm investigation cost. At \valCounterFLfineK/investigation with
$\delta = 0.50$, the optimal threshold is $t^* = 1.6\times$IQR
(net welfare \valCounterAvgImp; 2,537 flagged firms)---close to the paper's
baseline 1.5$\times$ and the Youden-optimal 1.45$\times$
(Figure~\ref{fig:counterfactual_screening}). Screening becomes
welfare-negative at $c_{\text{inv}} > \text{\valCounterCapMaxK}$; a full
threshold $\times$ cost surface is in
Figure~\ref{fig:welfare_heatmap}
(\ref{app:extensions}).

\paragraph{CF3: Increasing detection probability.}
Using PBU size as proxy for $\theta$, mean FL per tender decreases
from Q1 (0.41) to Q4 (0.31). A 10\% increase in detection probability
yields annual savings of \valCounterStartCost (OLS) to \valCounterMinImp (IV).
