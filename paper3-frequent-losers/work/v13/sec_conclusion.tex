\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

The convite minimum-bidder rule of Lei~8.666/93, intended to protect
procedural competition, generated a population of firms whose
sustained zero-win participation cannot be rationalized under
standard entry models. That population is detectable from the
data every procurement registry maintains: contract-award records.
We propose a participation-only screen that operationalizes this
behavioral residue, validate it against CADE's cartel adjudication
portfolio, and document the price patterns associated with
FL-flagged tenders.

The empirical record is clear about what the screen does and what
it does not. FL-flagged environments have 3.6--7.7\% higher
negotiated prices in within-item, within-year, within-purchasing-unit
comparisons. The screen achieves AUC~$=0.94$ against CADE
co-bidding ground truth, and three of the 47 BEC-active CADE
firm-defendants are themselves classified as FL---a 2.6$\times$
enrichment over the population base rate among always-losers.
Eight of the twelve cartel cases were adjudicated after the
sample window, so part of the validation is prospective: the
screen anticipated firms that subsequent enforcement confirmed.

The price gap is a conditional association, not a causal estimate.
Within-market selection remains unresolved, the event-study
pre-trends at $t = -2$ and $t = -3$ preclude a causal reading,
and the FL classification's low temporal persistence (10\%
across sample halves) marks it as an environment indicator
rather than a firm type. We are explicit about these limits
(Section~\ref{sec:limitations}). Welfare quantification requires
a causal interpretation this design does not provide; an
illustrative calculation appears in \ref{app:extensions}.

\paragraph{Policy implementation.}
The screen's contribution is operational, not theoretical. It is
designed to be deployable by oversight bodies whose data
infrastructure cannot support tender-level forensic analysis.
We propose a three-stage implementation pathway:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Screen.} Apply the FL rule to participation
records: count tenders per firm in a defined window, flag
always-losers with participation above the median + $1.5 \times$
IQR threshold. The computation requires no data transformation
beyond contract-award tallies and runs in seconds at the scale of
millions of contracts.

\item \textbf{Triage.} Re-score FL-flagged markets (not firms:
the 10\% firm-level persistence across our sample halves means
the flag is a marker for procurement \emph{environments} rather
than for individual operatives) by winner-HHI and repeated
co-bidding pairs, prioritizing markets with diversified-winner
patterns and concentrated repeat partnerships. Our heterogeneity
results show the price effect concentrates in the
competitive-market FL subgroup; the network-based triage operationalizes
this finding for case selection.

\item \textbf{Investigate.} On the triaged set, deploy bid-level
forensic tools---Bajari--Ye exchangeability and conditional
independence tests, Imhof-style dispersion screens, document
discovery, and (where available) leniency-driven testimony.
Bid-level analysis is expensive; the screen narrows the caseload
to where it is most likely to pay off.
\end{enumerate}

For TCE-SP, CGE, and analogous oversight bodies, the screen is
implementable with existing data and existing personnel. For
CADE, it offers an automated triage layer for tip-driven
investigations where bid-level microdata are not yet available.
The screen's data parsimony makes it portable: the same
participation-only rule applies in any procurement system whose
records distinguish participants from winners.

\paragraph{Open questions and replication.}
Whether the FL--price association is causal remains open. The
2021 Brazilian reform (Lei~14.133/2021) replaces Lei~8.666 for
new contracts, restricts convite, and tightens habilitation; if
post-2021 BEC data become available, a difference-in-differences
exploiting the modality transition would test whether reducing
the regulatory cover-bidding incentive moves prices in the
direction the FL diagnosis predicts. Replication in other
Brazilian states and OECD jurisdictions with comparable
contract-record archives is the most pressing next step.
Whether frequent losers are unlucky competitors or cartel
operatives, their presence marks markets that deserve a closer
look.
