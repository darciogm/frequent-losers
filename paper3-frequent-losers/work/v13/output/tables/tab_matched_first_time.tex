\begin{tablenotes}
\small
\item \textit{Notes:} Outcome is $\log(b_i / w_i)$, the log of
firm $i$'s first-tender bid relative to that item's winning
bid. Sample restricted to always-loser firms with at least
two bids in the same item, trimmed at $|\log(b/w)| \leq 5$ to
drop bid-entry errors (the untrimmed unconditional coefficient
is $+0.20$, dominated by a small number of extreme outliers
that survive in both treatment and control). Unconditional
specification uses item and year fixed effects with
item-clustered SEs. CEM matches on 4-digit item group, year,
modality, and quartiles of $\log(p_{\text{winner}})$, with
$k=1$ pairing. PS matching uses the same covariates as a
1-NN nearest neighbor on the propensity score. The
coefficient attenuates by approximately $40\%$ under PS matching
and loses statistical significance, indicating that the
unconditional first-tender pattern reflects observable
item-selection rather than behavioral selection by FL firms
on first appearance. We do not interpret the residual
coefficient as evidence of FL-specific bidding behavior.
\item \textit{Source:} \texttt{scripts/30\_first\_time\_fl\_matching.R}.
\end{tablenotes}
