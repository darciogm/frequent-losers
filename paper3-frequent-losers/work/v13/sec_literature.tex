\section{Related Literature}
\label{sec:literature}

Four literatures bear on the screening problem we address. Each
faces a constraint that has shaped its empirical reach. Our
contribution is the conjunction: a screening rule that resolves
or relaxes the binding constraint in each.

\paragraph{Bid-coordination tests cannot deliver their own
input.} \citet{bajari2003deciding} establish the canonical
econometric framework for detecting collusion through
exchangeability of bid functions and conditional independence of
bid residuals; the tests achieve discriminating power against
known cartels but require the researcher to first partition
bidders into suspected colluders and a competitive fringe. The
literature that builds on Bajari--Ye inherits this constraint:
\citet{porter1993detection} and \citet{porter1999ohio} detect bid
rotation conditional on assumed cartel membership;
\citet{baldwin1997bidder} document collusion in timber auctions
identified by post-prosecution records;
\citet{conley2016detecting} infer bidder groups from co-bidding
patterns---the closest precedent in data parsimony, but
recovering group structure rather than individual flags;
\citet{clark2021collusion} and \citet{schurter2020identification}
study collusion dynamics in long-lived cartels with
ex-post-known cartel membership. The partition is constructed
\emph{ex post} or by assumption in every case. We close that
gap: the FL classifier delivers an \emph{ex ante} firm-level
partition computable from contract-award records alone, and we
show in Section~\ref{sec:results_bajari} that Bajari--Ye tests
applied with the FL partition reject exchangeability
($D = 0.15$, $p < 0.001$) and conditional independence
($t = 81.0$, $p < 0.001$).

\paragraph{Proactive screens require microdata that procurement
systems do not always preserve.} \citet{imhof2019detecting},
\citet{imhof2018screening}, \citet{huber2019machine}, and
\citet{wallimann2023machine} train machine-learning classifiers
on tender-level bid statistics (within-tender CV, kurtosis,
skewness, normalized spread); the most accurate implementations
achieve AUC $\geq 0.85$ against ground-truth cartel cases in
European procurement.\footnote{Our Imhof comparison uses a
coefficient-of-variation median split, a coarser proxy for the
full implementation; the AUC of $0.79$ we report against the
$0.94$ of the FL screen is therefore conservative.}
\citet{abrantesmetz2006a} pioneer the variance screen;
\citet{chassang2022robust} provide robust theoretical
foundations; \citet{harrington2008detecting} surveys.
Every implementation operates at the \emph{tender} level and
requires per-bidder bid amounts. Many real procurement systems
do not preserve this layer: electronic platforms, including BEC
during much of our sample, archive winning prices and
participant lists at the contract-record layer but not bid-by-bid.
The gap is operational, not theoretical: what cartel screens
\emph{can} do (with full microdata) is structurally larger than
what oversight bodies \emph{do} have (winner + participants).
The FL screen runs on the second.

\paragraph{Cover-bidding identification has been retrospective.}
\citet{porter1999ohio}, \citet{pesendorfer2000study},
\citet{asker2010leniency}, and \citet{marshall2012economics}
document cover bidders in prosecuted cartels with rich case
records (the Ohio milk cartel, the Italian highway cartel, the
NYC stamp cartel, and a series of cases reviewed by
Marshall--Marx). \citet{athey2011comparing} compare cover-bidding
incentives across auction formats theoretically. In every
empirical case the cover bidders are identified through
leniency testimony, post-conviction records, or judicial
discovery---\emph{ex post}, after the cartel is broken.
\emph{Ex ante} prospective identification of cover bidders has
not been attempted in the empirical procurement literature, to
our knowledge. We invert the retrospective workflow: by flagging
firms whose participation patterns are behaviorally coherent
with cover bidding, the post-conviction observation becomes a
pre-investigation screen. Eight of the twelve CADE cases in our
validation portfolio were adjudicated after the BEC sample
window closes (Section~\ref{sec:cade}), so part of the
validation operates as a strict prospective test: the screen
anticipated firms whose collusive role enforcement confirmed
years later.

\paragraph{Procurement enforcement is hardest where data are
scarcest.} The bid-rigging literature is dominated by
developed-economy settings where data infrastructure is mature
and procurement spending shares of GDP are 5--10\% of the
economy. Developing economies, where shares can exceed 12\% of
GDP \citep{oecd2019government} and oversight resources are
thinner, are simultaneously more vulnerable to bid rigging and
less equipped to deploy the existing tools.
\citet{sanchezgraells2019screening} surveys the limits of
existing screens in low-resource settings. BEC is a useful test
bed: a centralized electronic platform (1{,}308 purchasing
units, 4.5 million tender-items, 40 million bids, 2009--2019),
two auction formats with different cover-bidding incentives,
and a cartel adjudication portfolio at CADE (12 cases, 65
firm-defendants, 47 BEC-active). The contribution generalizes
in principle wherever a procurement system records winners and
participants, regardless of bid-level archiving---the layer
preserved, in our experience and the cited surveys, more
consistently than bid-by-bid logs.

\paragraph{Synthesis.} The four literatures share a structural
constraint: each requires more information than typical
oversight bodies have, or works only after collusion is
established. Bid-coordination tests need the partition;
proactive screens need the microdata; cover-bidding
identification needs the post-conviction record; the developing-economy
literature has flagged the resource gap but not closed it.
The FL screen addresses the conjunction. It produces the
partition that Bajari--Ye-class tests need, runs on the
contract-award records that Imhof-class screens cannot use,
operates prospectively rather than retrospectively, and is
deployable in low-resource settings. The empirical contribution
is not a more powerful test against any one of these
literatures' benchmarks; it is a test that can be run in
settings where the existing tests cannot, and that produces
inputs the existing tests then use.
