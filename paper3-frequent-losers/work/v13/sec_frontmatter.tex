\begin{abstract}
We propose a participation-only screen for bid-rigging cartels
that runs on the contract-award records virtually every
electronic procurement system maintains: no bid microdata, no
structural assumptions, no enforcement records. The screen
identifies \emph{frequent losers} (FL)---firms that never win
yet participate in abnormally many tenders, the empirical
signature of regulatory cover bidding under minimum-bidder
rules. Applied to 4.5 million tender-items from São Paulo's
electronic procurement platform (2009--2019), the screen
achieves AUC $= 0.94$ against CADE adjudications ($95\%$
DeLong CI $[0.932, 0.946]$), well above the $0.79$ of
bid-level alternatives. Detection generalizes across ten
product sectors at mean AUC $0.954$. FL-flagged environments
show 3.6--7.7\% higher prices in within-item comparisons and
$3.5\times$ the baseline rate of co-participation with
CADE-defendant firms; eight of twelve cartel cases were
adjudicated after the sample window closes, so the bulk of the
validation is prospective. The institutional design identifies
a 7.6\% premium under voluntary cover bidding within convite,
flipping negative when the minimum-bidder rule binds. The
price gap is a conditional association, not a causal estimate;
sensitivity analysis yields $RV_{q=1} = 17.5\%$. The
contribution is operational: a screening rule deployable where
tender-level forensic analysis is not. A flag is a starting
point, not a verdict.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Highlights:}
\begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{1pt}
\item A participation-only cartel screen using only contract-award records (winner, participants).
\item AUC $= 0.94$ against CADE adjudications; mean cross-sector AUC $0.954$ (SD $0.034$).
\item Prospective validation: 8 of 12 cartel cases adjudicated after the 2009--2019 sample.
\item FL premium of 7.6\% under voluntary cover bidding within convite, identified institutionally.
\item Three-stage policy pathway (Screen $\to$ Triage $\to$ Investigate) for procurement oversight bodies.
\end{itemize}
