\begin{abstract}
Existing bid-rigging screens in the
\citet{porter1993detection}--\citet{bajari2003deciding}--\citet{imhof2019detecting}
tradition deliver high discrimination but require granular
bid microdata that most public procurement systems do not
preserve or release. We propose a complementary,
participation-only construct---\emph{loser-side concentration}:
the share of tender-items in which the same set of
zero-win firms keeps participating---and an operational
implementation we call \emph{frequent losers} (FL). The screen
runs on contract-award records alone: winner identity,
participant identity, item identifier. Applied to 4.5~million
tender-items from São Paulo's electronic procurement
platform (BEC, \valYearStart--\valYearEnd), the screen prioritizes
environments enriched in firms later associated with
adjudicated bid-rigging cartels: AUC $=\valAUCFLfirm$
($95\%$ CI $[0.898, 0.925]$) against \valCobidders CADE-defendant
co-bidders within the always-loser pool, comparable to a full
\citeauthor{imhof2019detecting}-style bid-distribution pipeline
(AUC $=\valAUCImhofFull$, CI $[0.865, 0.912]$) at substantially lower
data cost; combining the two pipelines reaches AUC $=\valAUCImhofPlusFL$.
We are explicit about scope. The screen does not flag the
\valDirectCADE\ direct CADE defendants in the universe of BEC firms above
chance (AUC $= 0.491$): direct defendants tend to be frequent
\emph{winners}, not losers, and the construct is targeted at
the loser-side of the bidding pool by design. Within the screen
output, FL-flagged environments associate with $\approx\!\valApproxSixPct$
higher negotiated prices in within-item, within-year
comparisons; the association is not causal, attenuates under
matching, and is heterogeneous across network quadrants in
ways that do not match a clean cartel-rotation signature. We
make explicit the asymmetric data-cost wedge that motivates
the construct: the screen is a triage tool for oversight bodies
that hold contract-award records but not bid microdata, not a
substitute for forensic bid-distribution analysis where
microdata are available.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Highlights:}
\begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{1pt}
\item Loser-side concentration: a participation-only construct for procurement-risk screening.
\item Frequent losers (FL) implements the construct using award records alone.
\item AUC $=\valAUCFLfirm$ against \valCobidders CADE-defendant co-bidders; comparable to full Imhof at lower data cost.
\item Combining FL with bid-distribution features reaches AUC $=\valAUCImhofPlusFL$.
\item Construct does not flag \valDirectCADE\ direct CADE defendants above chance (AUC $= 0.49$); scope is explicit.
\item Three-stage administrative pathway (Screen $\to$ Triage $\to$ Forensic) for oversight bodies without microdata.
\end{itemize}
