\begin{abstract}
Existing bid-rigging screens in the
\citet{porter1993detection}--\citet{bajari2003deciding}--\citet{imhof2019detecting}
tradition deliver high discrimination but require granular
bid microdata that most public procurement systems do not
preserve or release. We propose a complementary,
participation-only construct---\emph{loser-side concentration}:
the share of tender-items in which the same set of
zero-win firms keeps participating---and an operational
implementation we call \emph{frequent losers} (FL). The screen
runs on contract-award records alone: winner identity,
participant identity, item identifier. Applied to 4.5~million
tender-items from São Paulo's electronic procurement
platform (BEC, \valYearStart--\valYearEnd), the screen
prioritizes risk \emph{within the always-loser stratum}: AUC
$=\valAUCFLfirm$ ($95\%$ CI $\valAUCFLfirmCI$) against
\valCobidders CADE-defendant co-bidders, versus
$\valAUClogtc$ ($\valAUClogtcCI$) for the underlying continuous
score. On the same 11{,}676-firm comparison universe, a full
\citeauthor{imhof2019detecting}-style bid-distribution pipeline
reaches AUC $=\valAUCImhofFull$ ($\valAUCImhofFullCI$), the FL
binary alone reaches $\valAUCFLalone$, and the combination
reaches $\valAUCImhofPlusFL$. Freezing the threshold on
2009--2016 weakens the binary screen to AUC
$=\valAUCStrictFirmFL$ at the firm level and to
$\valAUCStrictItemFL$ on 2017--2019 items, while the continuous
item score retains AUC $=\valAUCStrictItemTC$. We are explicit
about scope. The screen does not flag the
\valDirectCADE\ direct CADE defendants in the universe of BEC firms above
chance (AUC $= 0.491$): direct defendants tend to be frequent
\emph{winners}, not losers, and the construct is targeted at
the loser-side of the bidding pool by design. Within the screen
output, FL-flagged environments are associated with
$\valMatchBaselineCoef$ higher negotiated prices in the baseline
within-item specification, but the sign turns negative under
strict overlap matching; the price evidence is therefore
descriptive, not causal. The asymmetric data-cost wedge is the
paper's main point: loser-side concentration is a low-cost
triage device for oversight bodies that hold contract-award
records but not bid microdata, not a general cartelist
detector and not a substitute for forensic bid-distribution
analysis where microdata are available.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Highlights:}
\begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{1pt}
\item Loser-side concentration: a participation-only construct for procurement-risk screening.
\item Frequent losers (FL) implements the construct using award records alone.
\item AUC $=\valAUCFLfirm$ against \valCobidders CADE-defendant co-bidders \emph{within the always-loser stratum}; strict holdout is weaker.
\item Continuous loss intensity is the construct of substantive interest; the FL binary is the deployable discretization. Neither uniformly dominates: the continuous score wins at the item level, the binary rule is more robust to firm-level truncation. % JLEO-R2: nuance
\item Combining FL with bid-distribution features reaches AUC $=\valAUCImhofPlusFL$ on the same firm universe.
\item Construct does not flag \valDirectCADE\ direct CADE defendants above chance (AUC $= 0.49$); scope is explicit and narrow.
\item Three-stage administrative pathway (Screen $\to$ Triage $\to$ Forensic) for oversight bodies without microdata.
\end{itemize}
