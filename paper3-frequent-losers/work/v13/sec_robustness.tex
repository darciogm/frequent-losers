\section{Robustness and Extensions}
\label{sec:robustness}

% JLEO-R1: add strict holdout, threshold audit, overlap sign-flip, and remove welfare from the main robustness narrative
We subject the conditional-association baselines and the
institutional-identification estimates to five groups of checks:
FL-definition variants (IQR multiplier, win-rate cutoff, temporal
window); sample and specification variants (unrestricted sample,
homogeneous subsamples, item$\times$year FE, matching);
alternative clustering; sensitivity to unobservables
(\citealt{cinelli2020making, oster2019unobservable}); and
oversight heterogeneity. We also report two designs whose
identifying assumptions fail in this setting and explain why we
treat them as transparency artifacts rather than as estimates.
Table~\ref{tab:robustness_summary} collects the point
estimates with the assumption each estimator imposes.

\paragraph{Threshold sensitivity.}
% JLEO-R2: continuous-vs-binary nuance — neither dominates uniformly
The revised threshold exercise clarifies what the binary rule is
and is not doing. Inside the always-loser stratum, the paper
rule (median $+ 1.5\times$IQR) yields \valFL flagged firms and
AUC $=\valAUCFLfirm$; the looser median $+ 1.0\times$IQR rule
falls to lower discrimination, while the referee's standard
Tukey alternative ($Q_3 + 1.5\times$IQR) drops to
$\valAUCQThreeIQR$ and flags only \valFLQThreeIQR firms. The
threshold is therefore not arbitrary, but neither is it
theory-privileged. We retain the binary rule because it is
auditable and deployable, not because the data isolate a
uniquely correct cutoff.

\paragraph{Continuous vs.\ binary: trade-off, not dominance.}
% JLEO-R2: explicit nuance on continuous-vs-binary trade-off
Whether the continuous loss-intensity score uniformly dominates
the binary $\valFL$ rule depends on the holdout structure. In
the in-sample firm-level evaluation (full $2009$--$\valYearEnd$
participation observable for both score construction and
labeling), the continuous score wins:
$\valAUClogtc$ vs.\ $\valAUCFLfirm$
(DeLong $Z=\valDeLongZ$, $p=\valDeLongP$). In the strict
train-period-only firm-level holdout, where the threshold is
calibrated on $\valYearStart$--$2016$ and evaluated on the
same firms with their full participation, the binary rule
ranks marginally \emph{above} the continuous score
(AUC $\valAUCStrictFirmFL$ vs.\ $\valAUCStrictFirmTC$). At the
item level, the pattern reverses again: the continuous score
($\valAUCStrictItemTC$) clearly outperforms the binary
($\valAUCStrictItemFL$) in the train-period-only holdout
(Table~\ref{tab:strict_train_threshold}). Reading: the
continuous score captures finer ordering when the full sample
is observable and when the unit of evaluation is the
tender-item; the binary rule is more robust to truncation when
the unit of evaluation is the firm. We therefore treat the
continuous loss-intensity score as the construct of substantive
interest and the binary rule as the deployable form, with
neither uniformly dominant. The construct--rule relationship
is concept--versus--operational rule, not equilibrium--versus--noise.

\paragraph{Sensitivity to unobservables.}
The \citet{cinelli2020making} sensitivity analysis yields
$RV_{q=1} = \valRVqOne$: an unobserved confounder would need to
explain at least $\valRVqOne$ of residual variation in
\emph{both} FL presence and log prices to nullify the
coefficient---stringent given the item, year, and PBU
fixed-effects structure. Sensitivity contour plots appear in
\ref{app:robustness}. The \citet{oster2019unobservable}
bound is uninformative here because adding PBU fixed effects
moves $R^2$ from $\valCScornerLow$ to $\valCScornerHigh$; the denominator of the
Oster formula is near zero and $\hat{\delta} = \valOsterDelta$ is
mechanically large. The Cinelli--Hazlett bound, which does not
depend on the $R^2$ gap, is the more informative robustness
statement for this design.

\paragraph{Oversight heterogeneity.}
The FL price coefficient varies steeply with PBU size: $\valOversightQone$
in the smallest quartile, $\valOversightQtwo$ in Q2, $\valOversightQthree$ in Q3, and
$\valOversightQfour$ in Q4 (Table~\ref{tab:regime_oversight},
\ref{app:robustness}). The 12.5$\times$ gradient between Q1
and Q4 is the single largest source of heterogeneity in the
data---larger than the preg\~ao--convite gap---and runs in the
direction the cover-bidding framework implies
($\partial m^*/\partial \theta_k < 0$, \ref{app:proofs}):
smaller PBUs with less oversight capacity see more FL activity
and higher associated price gaps. The alternative reading is
that smaller PBUs have thinner item--PBU cells, so item FE
absorb less within-market variation and the coefficient picks
up residual heterogeneity. The monotonic gradient across all
four quartiles is hard to generate from pure FE noise, but we
cannot fully rule it out.

\paragraph{Falsification: pregão-only subsample (no minimum-bidder rule).}
% CO-AUTHOR EDIT (Path A+ Pacote 3, 2026-05-01): pregão-only falsification
% directly addresses the "regulatory artifact" alternative reading.
A natural alternative reading of the loser-side concentration
construct is that it merely picks up regulatory artifacts of
the convite minimum-bidder rule: forced quorum filling produces
zero-win participants by statutory necessity, not by
coordination. We falsify this reading on the pregão-only
subsample, which carries no analogous minimum-bidder rule
(Lei~\valLeiPregao). The pregão-only sample contains
$\valFalPregN$ items; the convite-only sample contains
$\valFalConvN$. The within-item price specification of
Equation~\eqref{eq:ols_main} run separately on each modal
subsample yields, for the binary FL marker: $\valFalPregBin$
($\valFalPregBinPSig$) in pregão-only versus $\valFalConvBin$
($\valFalConvBinPSig$) in convite-only. The pregão-only point
estimate is approximately $\valFalRatio\!\!\times$ larger than
the convite-only estimate; both are statistically significant.
The continuous loss-intensity specification yields the same
pattern at finer granularity: $\valFalPregLog$ in pregão versus
$\valFalConvLog$ in convite (Table~\ref{tab:falsification_modal}).
% JLEO-R2: falsification claim disciplined — describe-not-identify
The loser-side construct is therefore not a mechanical
consequence of the convite minimum-bidder rule: the
conditional price association is empirically robust to the
absence of the minimum-bidder rule, supporting the descriptive
interpretation that loser-side concentration discriminates
similar environments across modal regimes. We do not interpret
this as identification of a coordination mechanism; the test
addresses one specific competing reading (regulatory artifact)
and rules it out, leaving the descriptive scope of the
construct intact while the mechanism behind that scope
remains an open question.

\paragraph{Pre-registered diagnostics for an alternative paper formulation.}
% CO-AUTHOR EDIT (Path A+ Action 7+, 2026-04-30): D2 reframed as
% environment heterogeneity rather than failed institutional test;
% D3 leakage audit reported.
Before settling on the descriptive scope of this paper, we
pre-registered four diagnostics whose joint pass would have
authorized an alternative formulation foregrounding a
winner/loser-side institutional reading
(Table~\ref{tab:gate_diagnostics}, \ref{app:robustness}). Three
of the four returned the predicted sign and magnitude:
the harmonized horse race (D1) confirms continuous
loss-intensity dominates the FL14 binary at conventional
significance; the without-FL14 specification (D3) confirms the
loser-side association survives elimination of the threshold;
the winner-heavy distribution of direct CADE defendants (D4)
confirms the structural scope asymmetry the screen exploits.
The fourth diagnostic (D2), comparing the construct's AUC
across modal-primary subsamples of always-loser firms,
returned the \emph{opposite} sign of the institutional
hypothesis: the convite-primary pool yielded AUC $\valAUCConvlogtc$
(small subsample, $N\!\!+\!\!=\!\!6$ cobidders) versus
preg\~ao-primary AUC $\valAUCPreglogtc$ ($N\!\!+\!\!=\!\!186$). We do not
interpret D2 as a falsification of an institutional mechanism
because the test as designed cannot separate effects of
modality from selection of firms into modalities, but neither
do we extract a positive institutional reading from it. The
honest interpretation is that loser-side concentration
discriminates more strongly in modal environments without the
minimum-bidder rule, which is information about the
construct's operating range and not evidence of a coordination
mechanism we can identify with this design. We report D2 in
Table~\ref{tab:gate_diagnostics} for transparency and rely on
the descriptive operational claim made in the body of the
paper.

\paragraph{Anti-leakage audit of the item-level AUC.}
% CO-AUTHOR EDIT (Path A+ Action 7+, 2026-04-30).
The diagnostic D3 reports an item-level AUC of $\valFLpremitemRaw$ for
$\log(1{+}\max\,\text{tc})$ predicting any-cobidder
participation. Cobidders are by construction always-loser
firms with elevated participation counts; an item containing a
cobidder mechanically has elevated max-tenders-count among its
always-loser participants. Part of the in-sample AUC is
therefore tautological. We audit the leakage component along
three dimensions (Table~\ref{tab:leakage_audit},
\ref{app:robustness}). Audit~1 evaluates the same score
against a label that does not share the construction (any
direct CADE defendant in the item): AUC $=\valAUCitemDirect$, random,
confirming the screen's structural scope limit. Audit~2
withholds approximately one fifth of the cobidder firms per
fold, recomputes tenders\_count without their participation,
and predicts items containing held-out cobidders using the
recomputed score; pooling the five out-of-fold predictions
yields AUC $=\valAUCitemCV$ ($95\%$ CI $\valAUCitemCVCI$), a drop of
roughly $\valFLpremtenAttenSE$ from the in-sample reference. Audit~3 is the
strict holdout relevant for deployment: training the threshold
on 2009--2016 and predicting 2017--2019 items yields
$\valAUCStrictItemFL$ for the binary flag and
$\valAUCStrictItemTC$ for the continuous score. The binary
deployment rule is weak out of time; the continuous score
retains some prospective content. We rely on the audited and
strict-holdout numbers in our claims, not on the in-sample
$\valAUCitemRaw$.

\paragraph{Designs we attempted and rejected.}
Two estimators that the recent staggered-DiD literature
recommends fail their identifying assumptions in this setting,
and we report them only to document the failures.
\citet{callaway2021difference} doubly-robust staggered DiD
yields $\mathrm{ATT} = 0.014$ (SE $\valCSseverr$); the
\citet{cengiz2019effect} stacked design yields
$\mathrm{ATT} = -0.006$ (SE $\valCSlimit$;
Table~\ref{tab:stacked_did}, \ref{app:did}). The stacked 95\%
CI $(-0.034, 0.022)$ excludes the OLS baseline of $\valThreshOneFivex$. The
discrepancy is mechanical: positive pre-event coefficients at
$t = -2$ and $t = -3$ (Figure~\ref{fig:event_study},
\ref{app:did}) indicate that FL entry is endogenous to market
conditions, violating the parallel-trends assumption both
estimators require. We declare the designs \emph{invalid} for
this setting, not merely underpowered, and exclude them from
Table~\ref{tab:robustness_summary}. \citet{rambachan2023more}
sensitivity bounds confirm that the maximum pre-trend slope
under which the conclusion would hold is implausibly small.
\citet{oster2019unobservable} $\hat{\delta} = \valOsterDelta$ is
similarly degenerate (above). The right reading of these
exercises is that staggered-DiD identification requires
exogenous treatment timing the FL setting does not provide,
and the conditional-association range we report is robust to
this constraint, not despite it.

\paragraph{Design implication.}
Taken together, the robustness exercises favor a specific
editorial posture. The continuous participation score is the
more stable empirical object; the FL binary is the cheaper and
more transparent deployment rule. The price regressions remain
descriptive because overlap tightening can overturn the sign,
and the detection result remains narrow because the strong AUC
belongs to a within-stratum task. The rereview revisions
therefore reframe the paper around scope, complementarity, and
triage value rather than around causal or welfare claims.
