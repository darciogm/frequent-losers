\section{Supporting Diagnostics and Alternative Explanations}
\label{sec:mechanisms}

The institutional design of Section~\ref{sec:additional_id}
identifies a 7.6\% premium where voluntary cover bidding is
operative under convite and a negative coefficient where the
minimum-bidder rule binds; the cross-sector replication of
Section~\ref{sec:cross_sector} shows detection (AUC) generalizes
uniformly. Five additional diagnostics constrain the
alternative readings the institutional design leaves open.
M1, M4, and M5 test \emph{coherence} with the regime-2
cartel-ecosystem mechanism by examining its non-price
implications. M2 and M3 rule out the most plausible
\emph{competing explanations} for the price gap: that FL
presence proxies for the reference-price anchor (M2) or for
contemporaneous price-chasing entry (M3). A final block
addresses non-cartel alternatives---shell firms, learning
entrants, the preg\~ao--convite asymmetry---and bounds them
against the rationality condition that defines the FL anomaly.
None of these tests identifies the mechanism on its own;
together they narrow the alternative readings.

\paragraph{M1: FL firms do not crowd out genuine competitors.}
If FL firms displace real bidders, FL-present tenders should
attract \emph{fewer} non-FL participants. They attract more:
$+0.143$ non-FL firms ($p < 0.01$), under the same FE
specification as Equation~\eqref{eq:ols_main}. The pattern is
the regime-2 prediction of \ref{app:proofs} (Online), where
cover bidders add manufactured competition without displacing
genuine bidders. The same pattern fits FL gravitation toward
inherently competitive tenders for non-collusive reasons; the
institutional ID separates the readings, since the
convite-voluntary premium of 7.6\% appears precisely where
strategic cartel selection is plausible and not where mechanical
procedural compliance forces FL entry.

\paragraph{M2: Winners in FL-present tenders bid closer to the
reference price.}
BEC reference prices are publicly posted before bidding opens.
Regressing $\log(p_{\text{negot}} / p_{\text{ref}})$ on FL
presence yields $-0.041$ ($p < 0.01$): winners in FL-present
tenders bid 4\% closer to the publicly visible anchor.
The pattern fits \emph{calibrated} cover bidding
\citep{marshall2012economics}: cartelists exploit the public
reference to coordinate on a price near it without revealing
the side-payment structure. Within-item variation in
reference-price accuracy could also generate the result; M2 is
a coherence check, not identification.

\paragraph{M3: Price-chasing is too small to explain the headline.}
Regressing FL entry on lagged within-market prices yields
$0.0021$ (SE $0.0008$): a 10\% price increase raises FL entry
by 0.02 percentage points, against a 4.8\% baseline. Reverse
causality through price-chasing would need to be roughly
30 times larger to account for the headline 6.4\%
FL--price gap. M3 does not rule out selection on
contemporaneous market characteristics that the FE structure
does not absorb; the Cinelli--Hazlett $RV_{q=1} = 17.5\%$
bound (Section~\ref{sec:robustness}) addresses that residual
threat.

\paragraph{Non-cartel alternatives.}
FL firms are roughly one year younger than non-FL firms,
fitting either a shell-company hypothesis or ordinary learning
by younger entrants. A one-year gap is also within the range
that differences in product-market entry timing could
generate, so the age pattern alone does not discriminate.
The preg\~ao--convite asymmetry (9.3\% vs.\ 3.8\% in
Table~\ref{tab:prices}) reflects two forces: preg\~ao's
real-time format facilitates coordinated cover-bid placement,
while convite's minimum-bidder constraint forces some cover
bidding regardless of market conditions, diluting the
strategic signal under convite. The convite--constraint
interaction (Section~\ref{sec:additional_id}) is coherent with
this reading: voluntary deployments yield $+7.6\%$, exceeding
the convite average of $+3.8\%$ that mixes voluntary and
mandatory deployments.

\paragraph{Rationality of FL participation.}
\label{sec:rationality}
With zero wins, expected profit per bid is
$E[\pi] = 0 \cdot \bar{V} \cdot m - c = -c < 0$ for any bidding
cost $c > 0$. BEC is electronic; $c$ captures staff time for bid
preparation and documentation compliance (1--2 hours at
R\$25--50/hour), yielding a conservative range
$c \in [\text{R\$}50, \text{R\$}500]$. Near the FL threshold
(14 tenders), cumulative direct cost is R\$$700$--R\$$7{,}000$,
conceivably explained by persistent bad luck or learning. At
the 90th-percentile FL participation (50+ tenders), cumulative
direct cost reaches R\$$2{,}500$--R\$$25{,}000$.\footnote{A
Bayesian learner with a uniform prior updates
$\Pr(\text{win}) = (0+1)/(n+2)$ after $n$ zero-win trials.
After $n = 50$, $\Pr(\text{win}) = 0.019$; with
$\bar{V} = $~R\$$86{,}000$ and $m = 0.10$, expected gain per
bid is $0.019 \times 86{,}000 \times 0.10 = $~R\$$163$, below
the lower-bound bidding cost. Exit is rational even for the
most optimistic learner.} A standard competitive search model
implies exit after the posterior win probability falls below
this lower bound; observed FL behavior contradicts that
prediction. Win rate zero in BEC does not preclude wins in
unobserved tenders elsewhere, but $50+$ zero-win participations
demand explanation even for firms profitable outside BEC
\citep{asker2010leniency, marshall2012economics}
(Table~\ref{tab:rationality}).

\paragraph{M4: FL firms form repeated co-bidding partnerships.}
If FL firms are random losers, their winner-side partners
should be no more concentrated than those of other always-losers.
A stratified permutation test (1{,}000 iterations preserving
participation-count quartiles) compares observed FL--winner
pair concentration against a null where FL labels are
reassigned within the 16,843 always-loser pool. FL firms
form $4,603$ unique pairs with $\geq 5$ shared tenders
out of $38{,}941$ total pairs, against a permuted mean of
$3,271$ ($p < 0.001$); top-10 pairs average $129.7$
co-occurrences vs.\ a permuted $109.9$ ($p = 0.016$). Stable
high-frequency pairings fit persistent cartel relationships
\citep{clark2021collusion}; the test cannot rule out geographic
or product-market co-location as alternative drivers
(Table~\ref{tab:dyadic_permutation}, \ref{app:robustness}).

\paragraph{M5: Genuine firms in FL-exposed markets survive
longer, not shorter.}
A Cox proportional-hazard model estimates whether non-FL firms
in FL-heavy markets exit BEC faster. FL exposure---the fraction
of a firm's tenders with FL presence---is associated with a
\emph{lower} exit hazard (HR $= 0.60$, $p < 0.01$) after
controlling for participation volume and win rate
(Table~\ref{tab:cox_survival}, \ref{app:robustness}). Firms
in FL-exposed markets survive \emph{longer}, the opposite of
crowding-out. The pattern fits two readings: FL-present markets
are more profitable and attract sustained participation, or the
cartel sustains a stable ecosystem of genuine and cover bidders
over time. The PH assumption is rejected ($p < 0.001$), so the
hazard estimate should be read as descriptive. M1
($+0.143$ non-FL firms) and M5 (longer survival) jointly fit a
cartel ecosystem in which cover bidders add noise rather than
displace competition; ``exit'' here means cessation of BEC
participation, not firm closure.

\paragraph{Strategic adaptation.}
If a cartel reads this paper, the obvious responses---rotating
FL firms across years, allowing occasional wins, staying
below the IQR threshold---would blunt the screen. The threshold
sensitivity already hints at this: the price coefficient
weakens as the threshold rises (Section~\ref{sec:robustness}).
The zero-win condition does heavy lifting; relaxing to
low-win-rate firms attenuates the coefficient substantially
(Table~\ref{tab:fl_lowwinrate}, \ref{app:online_robustness}).
A deploying competition authority would need to update the
threshold periodically and combine the screen with bid-level
tools---the same cat-and-mouse dynamic that applies to all
proactive screens \citep{harrington2008detecting}. We discuss
the implementation details in Section~\ref{sec:conclusion}.
