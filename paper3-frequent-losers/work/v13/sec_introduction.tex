\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

% JLEO-R1: narrow headline claim to within-stratum triage, continuous-vs-binary distinction, and descriptive price scope
Public procurement oversight bodies face an asymmetric
data-cost problem. The bid-rigging screens that have organized
the empirical literature since
\citet{porter1993detection}---bid distributions, second-lowest
distances, within-tender variance, the
\citet{imhof2019detecting} composite, and the machine-learning
extensions in \citet{huber2019machine} and
\citet{wallimann2023machine}---deliver strong discrimination
when bid microdata are available. They demand exactly that:
the per-bidder, per-item monetary offer for every tender. Most
electronic procurement systems do not preserve these records
beyond the award decision, do not release them under freedom-of-information
statutes, or release them in formats that
require expensive forensic processing. Brazilian Tribunais de
Contas, the Controladoria Geral da União, and CADE
investigators triaging tips routinely face a setting in which
they have access to who participated and who won, but not
to what each firm offered. Under that constraint, the screens
that are easiest to defend econometrically are also the screens
that are hardest to deploy administratively.

We propose a complementary construct that operates within the
data envelope a procurement oversight body can be expected to
hold: \emph{loser-side concentration}, the share of
tender-items in which the same firms participate without ever
winning. Where bid-distribution screens diagnose a tender by
the shape of the offers it received, loser-side concentration
diagnoses an environment by the persistence of the
zero-win participation it sustains. The two constructs measure
different objects on different data, target different stages of
oversight, and---we will document below---generate
complementary risk rankings. The paper's central claim is
operational, not universal: loser-side concentration is useful
for low-cost triage inside a narrow scope that we now state
explicitly.

We implement loser-side concentration through a binary marker
we call \emph{frequent losers} (FL). Of the $\valAlwaysLosers$ firms
that participated in São Paulo's electronic procurement
platform (BEC) between 2009 and 2019 without ever winning, we
flag the $\valFL$ that bid in \valThreshold\ or more tenders.
The operational cutoff at \valThreshold\ corresponds to a
statistical threshold of $\valThresholdStat$, the
median plus $\valIQRmul$ interquartile range of the participation
distribution among always-losers, set as a Tukey-style outlier
rule \emph{ex ante} on the participation-count distribution
itself.\footnote{We use median plus $\valIQRmul$ IQR (rather
than the standard Tukey $Q_3 + 1.5\times$ IQR) to identify
participation outliers within an already extreme-tail
sample; \S\ref{sec:robustness} reports cutoff sensitivity.}
The FL marker is the operational object that an oversight
body can compute from award records alone: it requires no
bid-level offers, no enforcement labels, no structural
assumptions. The cutoff itself is not the object of
substantive interest. Continuous loss intensity is the
construct-rich object; FL is the binary deployment rule. This
distinction matters because the continuous score ranks
co-bidder proximity better than the binary rule in sample
($\valAUClogtc$ vs.\ $\valAUCFLfirm$), and generalizes much
better out of time at the item level ($\valAUCStrictItemTC$
vs.\ $\valAUCStrictItemFL$). Neither uniformly dominates: at
the firm level under strict train-period truncation the
binary rule is marginally more robust
(Section~\ref{sec:robustness}).

% JLEO-R2: explicit construct rationale (Bayesian rationality + selection)
The economic logic that motivates the construct is direct:
under positive bid-preparation costs and persistent zero wins,
continued participation past any reasonable Bayesian threshold
is incompatible with expected-profit maximization
(\S\ref{sec:rationality} formalizes the calculation). Firms
that nevertheless persist past such a threshold are doing so
for reasons orthogonal to expected-profit competition---capacity
transfer, market intelligence, instrumental participation in
coordinated arrangements, or systematically misspecified
priors. The construct does not distinguish among these
non-competitive readings; it captures their shared empirical
footprint.

% JLEO-R2-MAJOR1: derivation of the construct from the framework
Appendix~\ref{app:proofs} formalizes this logic in a
two-type framework with cover bidders ($C$) and genuine
entrants ($G$). Under the framework's separating equilibrium,
two derivations make the empirical construct theoretically
grounded rather than data-mined.
Proposition~\ref{prop:exposure_stmt} shows that
$\log(1+\text{tenders\_count})$ is a sufficient ranking
statistic for cover-bidder type given award-record data, under
a Poisson approximation to the cartel's deployment process:
the posterior probability of being a cover bidder is
non-decreasing in tenders count by monotone likelihood ratio.
Lemma~\ref{lem:wins_zero_stmt} shows that the bright-line
filter $\text{wins}=0$ is the equilibrium choice of cover
bidders: winning either reveals capacity, undermining the
cover identity in subsequent rounds, or violates the cartel's
allocation rule and triggers internal sanctions. The joint
statistic $(\text{tenders\_count}, \mathbf{1}[\text{wins}=0])$
is therefore a sufficient screen given the data envelope, and
the binary $\valFL$ rule is the information-coarsening of the
latent posterior. Table~\ref{tab:predictions_findings} maps
the five framework propositions and the lemma to the empirical
findings most directly testing them; four predictions pass,
one inverts in a small cell, one characterizes a regime not
selected by the data. The framework is an organizing device
that yields testable predictions, not a source of causal
identification.

\paragraph{What the screen prioritizes, and what it does not.}
We validate against CADE's adjudicated bid-rigging portfolio
in two ways, and the gap between the two is informative.
Among the $\valAlwaysLosers$ always-losers, $\valCobidders$ co-bid with firms
that CADE later named as bid-rigging defendants. Within this
pool, the FL marker scores AUC $=\valAUCFLfirm$ ($95\%$ CI $\valAUCFLfirmCI$)
against the co-bidder label. A continuous score
based on $\log(1 + $tenders$)$ achieves AUC $=\valAUClogtc$
% JLEO-R2: continuous score is "of substantive interest", not strictly dominant
($\valAUClogtcCI$); the binary FL flag is the discretized
version of an underlying continuous loss-intensity signal,
which is the price of operational simplicity. A full
\citet{imhof2019detecting}-style pipeline using within-tender
bid-distribution features reaches AUC $=\valAUCImhofFull$
($\valAUCImhofFullCI$) on the same 11{,}676-firm comparison
universe; the FL binary alone reaches $\valAUCFLalone$, and the
combined model reaches $\valAUCImhofPlusFL$. The implication is
not that participation data dominate bid microdata in general.
It is that award-record information carries non-redundant
signal and can materially improve low-cost triage, while still
adding value even when rich-data forensic tools are available.

The same validation also identifies where the claim breaks.
Freezing the threshold on 2009--2016 and carrying it to 2017--2019
reduces the firm-level AUC to $\valAUCStrictFirmFL$ and the
item-level binary AUC to $\valAUCStrictItemFL$, while the
continuous item score remains at $\valAUCStrictItemTC$. The
headline $\valAUCFLfirm$ therefore belongs to a
within-stratum, pooled-sample prioritization problem; it
should not be read as a portable cartel-detection number.

The screen does not, however, flag the \valDirectCADE\ direct CADE
defendants in the broader universe of $\valBECfirms$ BEC firms
above chance (AUC $=\valAUCdirectCADE$, $\valAUCdirectCADECI$). This is by
construction, not by accident. Direct defendants in the
adjudicated cartels were typically frequent \emph{winners},
not frequent losers; the screen targets the loser-side of the
bidding pool---the pool that minimum-bidder rules in
\emph{convite} (Lei~\valLeiOriginal, Article~22) and analogous
statutory rules in OECD jurisdictions can fill with deliberately
losing participants \citep{marshall2012economics,
asker2010leniency,porter1993detection}. Loser-side
concentration prioritizes the environments in which a cartel
operates, not the firms that organize it. Conflating the two
would be a category error. We therefore report the
construct as a screen for environments enriched in
adjudicated bid-rigging activity, not as a detector of
cartelist firms; the AUC asymmetry is a feature of what the
construct measures, but it is also a hard limit on what
oversight conclusions follow from a positive flag.

\paragraph{Descriptive associations within the screen output.}
Conditional on the construct being deployed, FL-flagged
environments associate with approximately $\valMatchBaselineCoef$ higher
negotiated unit prices in within-item, within-year, within-PBU
comparisons (Section~\ref{sec:results}). Confidence
intervals are tight under item-clustered standard errors. The
association is not causal: pre-event coefficients in the
event-study at $t = -2, -3$ are not statistically zero, and a
\citet{cinelli2020making} sensitivity analysis bounds the
confounding tolerance at $RV_{q=1} = \valRVqOne$, which is the
order of variation any plausible omitted variable could
mimic. Two design-based identification strategies---a sharp
RDD at the convite/pregão statutory caps (\valCapOldR
pre-Decreto \valDecreto; \valCapNewR post) and a
difference-in-differences using the 2018 cap raise---both
return null first stages and null effects on FL prevalence
under the bandwidths and sample sizes available
(Section~\ref{sec:robustness}). More sharply, when we force
treated and untreated items into the same observed
item-group-year-modality-size cells, the coefficient turns
negative ($\valMatchOverlapCoef$; $\valMatchOverlapRefCoef$
once reference-price bins are added). The screen is therefore a
flag, not an estimate; we read the price gap as a conditional
descriptive statistic, not as a treatment effect.

Heterogeneity along network primitives is also less
disciplined than a clean cartel-rotation signature would
require. Splitting cells (PBU $\times$ item-group $\times$ year)
on winner-side HHI and on the count of repeated FL--winner
pairs, the largest FL premium falls in the
\emph{Low HHI $\times$ Low pairs} cell ($+\valMechQLLLCoef\%$, $p < 10^{-4}$,
$N = \valMechQLLLN$)---the single largest cell, not a cell that
matches the configurations classical cartel models
\citep{harrington2008detecting} predict. The \emph{Low HHI $\times$ High pairs} cell, which
would carry the cleanest cartel-rotation reading, returns a
null coefficient ($+\valMechQLLHCoef\%$, $p = 0.18$). The
\emph{High HHI $\times$ High pairs} cell returns a
\emph{negative} coefficient ($\valMechQLHHCoef\%$, $p = 0.002$), and
the audit of that cell shows the negative sign in both convite
and pregão ($\valNegCellConvCoef\%$ and $\valNegCellPregCoef\%$),
with the largest negative estimates concentrated in the early
period (\valNegCellEarlyCoef\%) and in the largest PBUs
(\valNegCellPBUQFourCoef\%). We discuss the heterogeneity pattern in
Section~\ref{sec:mechanisms} but do not rely on it as
mechanism evidence, because the cell pattern is consistent
with several non-collusive readings and with at least one
selection mechanism.

\paragraph{Contributions.} We make three contributions, of
unequal weight. \emph{First}, we document a participation-only
risk-screening construct that achieves discrimination
comparable to the
\citeauthor{imhof2019detecting}--\citeauthor{huber2019machine}
bid-distribution pipeline against the same ground truth at
substantially lower data cost, and that contributes
non-redundant signal when combined with bid-distribution
features. This is the operational contribution. \emph{Second},
we connect the construct to the institutional design of
minimum-bidder rules in Brazilian procurement law, articulating
how convite-style rules can sustain a population of zero-win
participants whose joint persistence is detectable in
contract-award records but who are not, individually, the
targets of cartel adjudication. This is the institutional
contribution. \emph{Third}, we translate the construct into an
administrative pathway---Screen $\to$ Triage $\to$ Forensic---in
which the participation-only screen narrows the case-load of
oversight bodies before forensic bid-microdata methods are
deployed where data permit. This is the law-and-economics
contribution.

\paragraph{What we do not claim.} The screen is not a
detector of cartelist firms: the direct-defendant AUC of
$\valAUCdirectCADE$ is the appropriate calibration of detection
scope. The price
gap is not an overcharge estimate; welfare quantification
beyond the back-of-the-envelope figures of
\ref{app:extensions} (Online) requires causal identification
this design does not provide. The cutoff at \valThreshold\ tenders
is a deployable rule, not an economically primitive number;
the underlying signal is continuous loss intensity. Mechanism
heterogeneity is reported but is not load-bearing for the
operational claim. The screen is a flag, not a verdict.
