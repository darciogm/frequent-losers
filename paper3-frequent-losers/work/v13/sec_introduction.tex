\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Of the 16,843 firms that bid on São Paulo's electronic
procurement platform between 2009 and 2019 without ever winning,
2,735 placed bids in fourteen or more tenders. At plausible
bid-preparation costs of R\$50--500, sustained zero-win
participation past this threshold is rationally dominated under
any standard entry model. A Bayesian learner with a uniform
prior expects negative profit per bid. Why do thousands of
firms behave this way?

Brazilian procurement law (Lei 8.666/93, Article~22) requires
the sealed-bid \emph{convite} modality to receive at least
three valid proposals before a contract clears. The rule was
designed to protect competition. In practice, when fewer than
three motivated bidders appear, the buyer---or the cartel that
controls the buyer's pool---must produce additional proposals.
Those proposals tend to be deliberately losing, the
configuration competition law calls \emph{cover bidding}
\citep{marshall2012economics, asker2010leniency,
porter1993detection}. The minimum-bidder rule generates, by
construction, a population of firms whose participation pattern
is the empirical signature of regulatory cover bidding:
persistent presence, zero wins. We turn that signature into a
screening rule and validate it against the procurement-cartel
adjudication portfolio of Brazil's competition authority (CADE).

We call these firms \emph{frequent losers} (FL) and define
them in two steps: always-losers (zero win rate) above an
outlier threshold of 14 tenders---median + $1.5 \times$ IQR of
the participation distribution, set \emph{a priori} on
behavioral grounds. The screen needs only contract-award
records: no bid microdata, no structural assumptions, no
enforcement records. It runs on what every procurement
oversight body already has, including the Tribunal de Contas
do Estado, the Controladoria Geral da União, comparable
institutions in OECD countries, and CADE itself when triaging
tips.

\paragraph{Findings.} We make two empirical claims, of unequal
strength. The detection claim is robust. Against CADE-defendant
co-bidding as ground truth, the FL screen achieves AUC
$=0.94$ ($95\%$ DeLong CI $[0.932, 0.946]$), well above the
$0.79$ obtained by an \citet{imhof2019detecting}-style
bid-level composite ($p < 0.001$). The result generalizes
within-platform: across ten BEC product sectors with at least
three CADE positives, the AUC averages $0.954$ (SD $0.034$),
and every sector exceeds $0.90$. Eight of the twelve cartel
cases were adjudicated \emph{after} the BEC sample window
closes, so the bulk of the validation is strictly prospective:
the 2009--2019 FL classification anticipated firms whose
collusive role enforcement confirmed years later. Three of the
47 BEC-active CADE-defendants are themselves FL by the screen,
against a $16\%$ base rate among always-losers---a
$2.6\times$ enrichment.

The price-gap claim is weaker and we treat it as such.
FL-flagged tenders show 3.6--7.7\% higher negotiated prices in
within-item, within-year, within-PBU comparisons, robust across
cross-fit, OLS, and matching. The gap concentrates where the
regulatory cover-bidding incentive is operative. Within
convite, the FL premium is $7.6\%$ when at least three
genuine bidders participate (the rule is slack and cover
bidding is voluntary); the coefficient flips negative when
fewer than three appear (the rule binds and FL participation
is statutorily required). Pregão (Lei 10.520/2002), which has
no minimum-bidder rule, shows a smaller premium than
convite-voluntary. The price gap is a conditional association,
not a causal estimate. Cinelli--Hazlett sensitivity yields
$RV_{q=1} = 17.5\%$, which bounds the scope for omitted
confounders without identifying them; pre-event coefficients
at $t = -2, -3$ in the FL-entry event study preclude a fully
causal reading.

\paragraph{Mechanism evidence.} Five diagnostics constrain the
alternative readings the price gap leaves open. FL-present
tenders contain $0.143$ \emph{more} non-FL firms, not fewer
(no crowding-out, the prediction of legitimate FL competition).
Winners in FL-present tenders bid $4\%$ closer to publicly
posted reference prices, consistent with calibrated cover
bidding. FL entry has elasticity $0.0021$ with respect to
lagged prices, too small to account for the headline by a
factor of thirty. FL firms form $4,603$ unique winner pairs
with at least five shared tenders, against a permuted mean of
$3,271$ ($p < 0.001$). And genuine firms in FL-exposed markets
exit BEC \emph{less} frequently, not more, consistent with a
stable cartel ecosystem rather than displacement. Each test is
individually consistent with non-collusive alternatives;
jointly with the institutional identification, they tighten
the reading.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\emph{First}, we close the partitioning gap in the
bid-coordination literature. \citet{bajari2003deciding},
\citet{conley2016detecting}, and subsequent work require an
\emph{ex post} partition of bidders into suspected colluders
and a competitive fringe. The FL classifier produces the
\emph{ex ante} firm-level partition these tests need.
\emph{Second}, we close an operational gap in the
proactive-screen literature. \citet{imhof2019detecting},
\citet{huber2019machine}, and \citet{wallimann2023machine}
require bid microdata that many electronic procurement systems
do not preserve. The FL screen runs on the contract-award
records virtually every system maintains, lowering the data
threshold for proactive enforcement to participation tallies
and win/loss flags. \emph{Third}, we develop the
law-and-economics framing: the convite minimum-bidder rule,
designed to protect competition, generates a decoy-firm
population that oversight bodies can detect at virtually no
marginal cost. We translate the detection capability into a
three-stage pathway---Screen $\to$ Triage $\to$ Investigate
(Section~\ref{sec:conclusion})---deployable by Brazilian and
OECD oversight bodies.

We are explicit about what the design does not deliver. The
screen does not identify cover bidders firm-by-firm: FL--CADE
overlap conditional on participation count is statistically
indistinguishable from non-FL always-losers ($p = 0.93$),
which makes the FL flag an environment-level marker rather
than a stable firm type. The price gap is a conditional
association, not a causal overcharge. Welfare quantification
beyond the illustrative back-of-the-envelope figures of
\ref{app:extensions} (Online) requires causal identification
this design does not provide. The screen is a flag, not a
verdict.
