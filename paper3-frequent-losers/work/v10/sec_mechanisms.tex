\section{Mechanisms and Alternative Explanations}
\label{sec:mechanisms}

We examine three mechanisms to distinguish the cover-bidding hypothesis
from alternatives (Table~\ref{tab:mechanisms},
Appendix~\ref{app:robustness}).

\paragraph{M1: Strategic selection vs.\ competitive displacement.}
Competitive displacement predicts FL firms crowd out genuine
competitors; cover bidding predicts they are added on top. We regress
genuine (non-FL) competitor count on FL presence: the coefficient is
$+0.143$ ($p < 0.01$). FL-present tenders have \emph{more} genuine
competitors, ruling out crowding-out.

\paragraph{M2: Reference price calibration.}
BEC reference prices are set administratively and publicly observable.
Regressing $\log(p_{\text{negot}} / p_{\text{ref}})$ on FL presence
yields $-0.041$ ($p < 0.01$): winning bids in FL-present tenders are
4\% closer to the reference price, consistent with calibrated cover
bidding \citep{marshall2012economics}.

\paragraph{M3: Reverse causality.}
Regressing FL entry on lagged log prices within market (item $\times$
PBU; 427,474 market-year observations) yields $0.0021$
(SE~$= 0.0008$)---statistically detectable but economically negligible
(implied elasticity $\approx 0.004$). A 10\% price increase raises FL
entry probability by 0.02~pp against a 4.8\% baseline, ruling out
price-chasing selection.

\paragraph{Alternative explanations.}
FL firms are approximately one year younger than non-FL firms---consistent
with recently formed shell firms, though incomplete learning by younger
entrants cannot be excluded. The FL coefficient is larger for preg\~ao (9.3\%) than convite
(3.8\%), which may seem counterintuitive given that convite's
minimum-bidder rule (3 bidders) creates a direct \emph{demand} for
cover bidders. Two factors reconcile this pattern. First, preg\~ao's
real-time format lets cover bidders observe competing bids and
calibrate above the designated winner, facilitating coordinated
cover-bid placement (Regime~2); convite's sealed-bid format makes
such calibration harder. Second, convite's minimum-bidder constraint
creates a corner solution (Proposition~\ref{prop:optimal_m}): cover
bidding is \emph{forced} regardless of market conditions, diluting
the FL signal by mixing constraint-driven deployments (where the
cartel adds cover bidders to meet the rule, not to inflate prices)
with voluntary deployments. The minimum-bidder interaction analysis
(Section~\ref{sec:additional_id}) confirms this: the voluntary
deployment premium (7.6\%) exceeds the constraint-driven effect.

\paragraph{Rationality of FL participation.}
\label{sec:rationality}
FL firms have zero win rate, so $E[\pi] = -c < 0$ for any bidding cost
$c > 0$. Near the threshold (14 tenders), persistent unsuccessful entry
is plausible. The argument strengthens in the tail: FL firms at the
90th percentile participate in 50+ tenders without a single win,
incurring career losses exceeding R\$10{,}000 at $c = \text{R\$200}$---difficult to reconcile with profit maximization and consistent with
side-payment arrangements documented in prosecuted cartels
\citep{asker2010leniency, marshall2012economics}. However, FL firms may
win tenders outside BEC (federal, municipal, other states); win rate
zero in BEC does not imply zero globally
(Table~\ref{tab:rationality}).%
\footnote{If FL firms are profitable outside BEC, the irrationality
argument weakens for firms near the threshold but remains strong for
tail firms: 50+ zero-win BEC participations represent a substantial
fixed cost even if the firm wins elsewhere.}
