\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

We present the reduced-form evidence for FL as a screening marker (H1)
first, then the mechanism and structural evidence for cover bidding
(H2). An important interpretive note: FL firms do not set the winning
price---the cartel does. Cover bidders bid above $b^*$ by construction
(Equation~\ref{eq:cover_above}). The OLS coefficient $\hat{\beta}$
therefore measures the price difference between tenders where cartels
operate (and deploy FL firms) and comparable tenders without cartel
activity. It captures the \emph{markup associated with cartel
operations that use cover bidders}, not the marginal causal effect of
adding one additional cover bidder. We do not claim to identify a
causal effect: the estimates capture conditional associations that are
consistent with---but do not prove---a causal channel from cover
bidding to prices. A natural concern---that FL firms are merely
inefficient competitors---is addressed in
Section~\ref{sec:rationality} ($E[\pi] < 0$ for any positive bidding
cost).

% ── 7.1 Reduced-Form Evidence (H1 core) ─────────────────────────

\subsection{OLS and Matching Results}
\label{sec:results_ols}

Table~\ref{tab:prices} reports the baseline OLS estimates of
Equation~\eqref{eq:ols_main} for log negotiated prices
(Prediction~\ref{pred:price_v6}). Across all four
specifications, FL presence is associated with significantly higher
prices: 6.8\% in the general specification (column~1), 6.4\% with PBU
fixed effects (column~2), 9.3\% for preg\~ao (column~3), and 3.8\% for
convite (column~4). Adding PBU fixed effects barely changes the
coefficient, indicating that FL presence does not proxy for high-cost
purchasing units.

\input{./output/tables/tab_prices}

FL-present tenders have 0.19 log points more non-FL firms
(Prediction~\ref{pred:selection_v6}; PBU FE specification),
contradicting crowding-out and consistent with \emph{strategic
selection}: cartels deploy cover bidders in tenders that already attract
genuine competition
(Table~\ref{tab:nfirms_excl} in Appendix~\ref{app:competition}).

\paragraph{Cross-fit estimate.}
FL status is defined using the same 2009--2019 data on which the price
regression is estimated, potentially creating a mechanical link between
classification and outcomes. To address this, we define FL using only
odd years and estimate on even years, and vice versa. The cross-fit
average is $\hat{\beta}_{\text{CF}} = 0.036$ (3.6\%), attenuated by
44\% relative to full-sample OLS. Three factors contribute:
(i)~fewer FL firms per subsample (1,885--2,153 vs.\ 2,735);
(ii)~classification noise near the threshold; and (iii)~the temporal
split eliminates any within-sample mechanical correlation between
the FL classification rule and the price outcome. Because factor
(iii) removes a potential source of upward bias in the full-sample
OLS, we regard the cross-fit estimate as potentially the more
credible of the two. The OLS--cross-fit range is 3.6--6.4\%, with
3.6\% as the out-of-sample estimate and 6.4\% as the full-sample
baseline; matching estimators (below) confirm the association
under non-parametric reweighting.

\paragraph{Matching estimators.}
Coarsened exact matching (CEM: 0.077, $N = 969{,}751$) and inverse
probability weighting (IPW: 0.055, $N = 830{,}194$) bracket the OLS
and cross-fit estimates, confirming that the FL--price association
survives non-parametric reweighting on observables.

\paragraph{Measurement-error diagnostic (IV).}
The leave-one-out IV estimate (0.194, $p = 0.01$, $F = 396$) exceeds
OLS by a factor of three, consistent with measurement-error
attenuation in the binary FL indicator
(Section~\ref{sec:reduced_form}). However, if cartels operate across
PBUs within the same product market, the instrument may capture cartel
intensity rather than misclassification alone, biasing the IV upward.
We therefore treat the IV as a diagnostic indicating that the OLS is
conservative, not as a primary estimator
(Table~\ref{tab:iv_balance}, Table~\ref{tab:iv_main} in
Appendix~\ref{app:robustness}).

\paragraph{Summary.}
Cross-fit (3.6\%), IPW (5.5\%), OLS (6.4\%), and CEM (7.7\%) yield a
consistent range of 3.6--7.7\%. These estimators share the maintained
assumption of selection on observables (plus fixed effects) but differ
in functional form and reweighting method; the cross-fit further
removes any mechanical link between the FL classification and the price
outcome. The consistency across approaches provides confidence that the
FL--price association is not an artifact of a particular specification.
The IV diagnostic (19.4\%) suggests the OLS is attenuated by
measurement error, but exclusion-restriction concerns preclude treating
it as a primary estimate. Sensitivity analysis
(\citealt{cinelli2020making}, $RV = 17.5\%$) bounds the residual
scope for omitted-variable bias: an unobserved confounder would need
to explain 17.5\% of residual variation in both FL presence and log
prices to nullify the coefficient.

% ── 7.2 Detection Performance (H1 validation) ───────────────────

\subsection{Detection Performance}
\label{sec:results_detection}

Figure~\ref{fig:roc} (Appendix~\ref{app:robustness}) presents the
FL ROC curve, evaluated against CADE cartel co-participation as ground
truth. The FL screen achieves an AUC of 0.94
(Table~\ref{tab:roc_detection} in Appendix~\ref{app:robustness}).
The data-driven optimal threshold (Youden's $J = 0.84$) corresponds
to 1.45$\times$IQR---nearly identical to the 1.5$\times$ baseline
rule---achieving TPR~$= 1.00$ with FPR~$= 0.16$. The AUC may
overstate detection performance because the ROC varies the
participation-count threshold, and higher counts mechanically
increase co-participation opportunities with any firm, including
CADE cartelists. The permutation test
(Section~\ref{sec:cade}), which controls for this by stratifying on
participation quartile, provides an independent confirmation of the
FL--cartel association (3.5$\times$, $p < 0.001$).

\paragraph{Complementarity with Imhof-style screens.}
We compare the FL screen with a \citet{imhof2019detecting}-style
bid-level proxy (CV median split) on the same CADE ground truth. The
FL screen achieves AUC~$= 0.94$ versus the Imhof proxy's 0.79
(DeLong $p < 0.001$; Table~\ref{tab:imhof_comparison},
Figure~\ref{fig:roc_comparison} in Appendix~\ref{app:robustness}).
This comparison is indicative rather than definitive, for three
reasons. First, our Imhof proxy is coarser than the full
\citet{imhof2019detecting} implementation, which combines CV,
kurtosis, spread, and a trained classifier. Second, the two screens
operate at different levels (firm vs.\ tender) and use different
inputs (participation vs.\ bid values), so the AUC difference partly
reflects different task structures. Third, the ground
truth---co-participation with CADE-convicted firms---is constructed
from participation records, which mechanically favors the FL screen:
higher participation counts increase co-participation opportunities.

The FL screen's contribution is \emph{complementarity}, not
dominance: it functions where bid-level records are unavailable and
produces firm-level flags that can triage investigations before
bid-level analysis is applied. Precision is low (3.6\%) given the
0.58\% base rate; the FL screen is a high-recall triage tool, not a
standalone classifier.

\paragraph{Horse-race regression.}
To test whether FL adds incremental information beyond bid-level
screens, we include both FL presence and an Imhof-style CV flag
(within-item-group median split) as regressors in
Equation~\eqref{eq:ols_main}. The FL coefficient is 0.084
($p < 0.01$) and the Imhof flag coefficient is 0.021 ($p < 0.01$),
with a correlation between the two indicators of only 0.060. FL
presence captures a signal largely orthogonal to bid-level
dispersion features, confirming that the two screening approaches
are complementary rather than redundant
(Table~\ref{tab:horse_race}, Appendix~\ref{app:robustness}).

% ── 7.3 Mechanism and Network Evidence (H2) ─────────────────────

\subsection{Network-Split Heterogeneity}
\label{sec:results_network}

Table~\ref{tab:network_split} splits FL firms into
concentrated-market and competitive-market subgroups based on
co-bidding network metrics (Section~\ref{sec:empirical_network}).\footnote{Markets
above the winner-HHI sample median are classified as concentrated;
firms sharing at least two tenders are classified as repeat
co-participants.}
Competitive-market FL firms---operating in markets with diversified
winner patterns (low HHI)---drive the entire price effect: 0.126
($p < 0.01$, PBU FE). Concentrated-market FL firms show no significant
effect ($-0.018$, $p > 0.3$).

This heterogeneity is consistent with Proposition~\ref{prop:market_selection}---cover bidding is most profitable in
competitive markets---but three alternative interpretations merit
consideration. First, cartels in concentrated markets may sustain
supra-competitive prices through market power alone, making cover
bidding unnecessary; FL firms there would serve as redundant insurance
rather than active price inflators. Second, some FL firms in
concentrated markets may be genuinely unsuccessful entrants rather than
cover bidders, implying that the FL classification is noisier in
concentrated markets. Third, item fixed effects may absorb the price
effect in thin markets with few observations per cell.

We cannot distinguish among these interpretations with cross-sectional
data. For screening purposes, the implication is that the FL marker is
most informative in competitive markets---precisely where detection is
most valuable, because cartels in those markets rely on manufactured
competition to sustain prices.

\subsection{Bajari--Ye Tests}
\label{sec:results_bajari}

Having established that the price effect concentrates in competitive
markets, we now test whether FL bids exhibit the statistical
signatures of coordination. We apply the \citet{bajari2003deciding}
framework to test
Prediction~\ref{pred:exchange_v6}
(Table~\ref{tab:bajari_ye_corrected} in Appendix~\ref{app:robustness}).

\paragraph{Exchangeability.}
The KS test rejects the null that FL and non-FL bid residuals share
the same distribution ($D = 0.15$, $p < 0.001$): FL bids are drawn
from a distinct process, as the structural model predicts.

\paragraph{Conditional independence.}
The mean pairwise product of FL residuals is 4.28 ($t = 81.0$,
$p < 0.001$), with the bootstrap FL--non-FL difference
excluding zero, indicating systematically different within-tender
correlation consistent with coordinated bidding. Enriching the
first stage with firm age and CNAE sector dummies alongside firm
size leaves R-squared virtually unchanged (0.770) and does not
alter the KS or pairwise-product results, ruling out the concern
that the exchangeability violation is driven by omitted firm-level
cost shifters (Table~\ref{tab:bajari_ye_corrected}).

\paragraph{Tender FE.}
Under tender FE (Table~\ref{tab:bajari_ye_tender_fe} in
Appendix~\ref{app:robustness}), both products drop substantially
(FL: $5.16 \to 0.38$; non-FL: $2.21 \to 0.86$), with the FL product
falling below non-FL---a reversal predicted by Regime~2. Cover bids
cluster near $b^* + \epsilon$ with low variance; removing the tender
mean eliminates the shared focal-point component. The tender-FE
specification absorbs the within-tender price level that cover bidding
inflates, making it mechanically conservative.

% ── 7.4 Structural Estimation (H2 model) ────────────────────────

\subsection{Structural Estimation Results}
\label{sec:results_structural}

BIC selects Regime~2 (coordinated cover bidding) over Regime~1
($\Delta$BIC~$= -91{,}473$;
Table~\ref{tab:structural_params} in
Appendix~\ref{app:structural_id}). Under Regime~2,
$\hat{\sigma}_c / \hat{\sigma}_g = 1.19/1.64 = 0.72$: cover bids are
\emph{less} dispersed than genuine bids, consistent with coordinated
calibration near a focal point above $b^*$. The ratio is below one but
not near zero, indicating imperfect coordination---cover bidders receive
general instructions rather than precise bid levels. The $n$-conditional
markup (6.4\%) is virtually identical to baseline OLS; this is a
calibration, not an independently identified parameter. The structural
model's contribution is characterizing the cover-bid mechanism (regime
identification and distributional parameters), not providing an
independent price estimate. Specification tests
(Appendix~\ref{app:structural_id}) confirm the corner-vs-interior
solution, within-tender dispersion, and the $m$--$\theta$ comparative
static on non-estimation moments.
