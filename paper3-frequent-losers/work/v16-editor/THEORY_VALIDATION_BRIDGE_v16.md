# Theory–Validation Bridge — v16

**Goal.** Tighten the gap between the *theoretical* object — cover-bidder type / loser-side equilibrium deployment in the framework of Online Appendix~A — and the *empirical validation* object — cobidders inside the always-loser stratum (the 192 firms that participated alongside CADE direct defendants). The bridge in v15 rested on the verbal claim that cobidders are the empirically relevant proxy. This memo replaces that claim with within-stratum descriptive evidence.

**Vehicle.** `scripts/60_theory_validation_bridge.R`, ran 2026-05-02. All numbers from `output/theory_bridge/*.csv` and `work/v13/output/tables/tab_theory_bridge.tex`.

## I. The four operational predictions of the cover-bidder type

Online Appendix~A models the cover bidder as a firm whose participation is generated by the cartel's deployment problem. From this we extract four operational predictions about firm-level descriptive characteristics — predictions that are *non-tautological* given that the cobidder label is constructed only from *adjacency* to direct CADE defendants, not from these characteristics:

| Prediction | Empirical proxy |
|---|---|
| **P1.** Cover bidders deploy at higher intensity than firms that lose for endogenous market reasons. | Higher mean total participations |
| **P2.** Cover bidders are deployed proximally to the cartel's winners. | Higher share of own loss-bids facing a direct CADE defendant |
| **P3.** Cover bidders re-encounter the cartel's winners repeatedly across tenders. | Higher count of repeat-pair encounters with the same winner |
| **P4.** Cover bidders specialize in the cartel's markets, not in broad procurement. | Higher portfolio item-group HHI; fewer distinct item-groups touched |

These four predictions can be tested by comparing cobidders against FL non-cobidders inside the always-loser stratum. This is the within-stratum comparison the validation framing requires: both populations are always-loser firms above the FL threshold; they differ only in adjacency to adjudicated cartel members. If cobidders look systematically more like the modeled cover-bidder type, the bridge tightens. If they look indistinguishable from FL non-cobidders, the bridge collapses.

## II. Empirical results

Five-of-seven measured dimensions move in the direction the cover-bidder theory predicts. Three are strongly so; two are moderate; one is mixed; one is in the wrong direction.

| Dimension | Cobidder | FL non-cobidder | AL non-FL | Other winner | Cohen's $d$ vs FL | Wilcoxon $p$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mean total participations (P1) | $136.5$ | $76.7$ | $12.1$ | $1{,}603.3$ | **$+0.67$** | $<10^{-15}$ |
| Mean unique winners faced | $24.8$ | $13.5$ | $2.4$ | $46.8$ | **$+1.00$** | $<10^{-22}$ |
| Mean share facing direct CADE (P2) | $1.5\%$ | $0.2\%$ | $0.2\%$ | $0.3\%$ | **$+0.46$** | $<10^{-70}$ |
| Mean pairs $\geq 5$ count (P3) | $2.28$ | $1.68$ | $0.04$ | $13.04$ | $+0.19$ | $0.021$ |
| Mean share of bids in repeat-$5$ pairs (P3) | $21.6\%$ | $33.4\%$ | $2.6\%$ | $41.2\%$ | $-0.38$ | $<10^{-6}$ |
| Mean portfolio item-group HHI (P4) | $0.380$ | $0.288$ | $0.719$ | $0.294$ | **$+0.39$** | $<10^{-13}$ |
| Mean number of distinct item-groups (P4) | $7.6$ | $9.5$ | $2.1$ | $17.6$ | **$-0.32$** | $<10^{-6}$ |

### What this shows

1. **P1 (deployment intensity) is supported.** Cobidders participate in $136$ tenders on average, vs $77$ for FL non-cobidders ($+0.67$ standard deviations). Within the FL stratum, cobidders are $\approx 1.8 \times$ more deployed than the comparison group. Stronger still on number of distinct winners faced ($24.8$ vs $13.5$, $d=+1.00$) — cobidders span twice as many winners.
2. **P2 (deployment proximity) is supported.** Cobidders face direct CADE defendants in $1.5\%$ of their loss-bids, an order of magnitude above FL non-cobidders' $0.2\%$. The point estimate is small in absolute terms but the contrast is statistically overwhelming ($d=+0.46$, $p<10^{-70}$). This is partly definitional (cobidders are defined by direct-defendant adjacency), but the *magnitude* of the gap quantifies how distinguishable the populations are on this margin.
3. **P3 (repeat co-bidding) is mixed.** Cobidders have more repeat-pair encounters with the same winners *in absolute count* ($2.28$ vs $1.68$, $d=+0.19$), but a *lower share* of their loss-bids land in repeat-$5$ pairs ($21.6\%$ vs $33.4\%$, $d=-0.38$). The two metrics tell different stories: cobidders span more winners overall (P1), so the share-based metric dilutes; the absolute count goes the right way but only weakly. **P3 is not cleanly supported.**
4. **P4 (specialization) is supported.** Cobidders' item-group HHI is higher ($0.380$ vs $0.288$, $d=+0.39$), and they touch fewer distinct item-groups ($7.6$ vs $9.5$, $d=-0.32$). Both metrics consistent: cobidders concentrate their participation in narrower market slices, as the cartel-specific role would predict.

### Where the comparison populations show the cobidder profile is distinctive

Two background facts make the cobidder–FL non-cobidder gap empirically meaningful:

- **Cobidders are sharply distinguishable from non-FL always-losers** on every dimension. AL non-FL firms participate $\approx 12$ times each, face $2.4$ unique winners, touch $2.1$ item-groups: a thin-participation, narrow-portfolio profile that does not look like the cover-bidder type. This is the largest contrast in the table.
- **Cobidders are not just FL with a CADE label glued on.** On three dimensions (total participations, unique winners, item-group HHI), cobidders are a *non-trivial proper subset* of FL: more concentrated, more proximate, more deployed. The label has empirical content beyond proximity to one of the 47 direct defendants.

## III. What stronger bridge was possible

Three additional bridges, beyond the within-stratum descriptive comparison, were considered and only partially deliverable:

1. **Bid-level price/dispersion behavior.** *Not deliverable.* The bid-level parquet (`bid_level_full.parquet`) does not carry per-bid prices for the universe of bidders; it carries firm × item × OC presence and winner flag only. We cannot test the prediction that cobidders bid less aggressively (higher bid:reference ratio) or with higher within-item dispersion at the firm level. The aggregated tender-item dataset has prices but not per-firm bids. **This is a real bridge that the data layer cannot deliver, and is honestly noted as such in the manuscript.**
2. **Persistence across years.** *Partially deliverable.* The participation-counts are total across the eleven-year window; year-by-year persistence of cobidders alongside specific winners would require disaggregating `firm_tender_map` by year and is feasible in principle but adds little above the repeat-pair measure (P3) which already captures the spirit. Deferred to memo only.
3. **Network-density centrality of cobidders relative to direct defendants.** *Partially deliverable.* This is closely related to P3 (repeat-pair count). The current empirical proxy is the count of pairs of size $\geq 5$ — a thresholded centrality measure. A more refined version would compute eigenvector centrality on the bipartite firm-winner graph, but the additional information is small given the four predictions already evaluated.

## IV. What was not possible

- **Ground-truth sub-classification of cobidders.** The model distinguishes cover bidders ("paid losers") from genuine losers; the validation cannot adjudicate this for individual cobidders. Some of the 192 cobidders may be genuine losers who happened to bid alongside cartel members; the empirical profile shows they look more like the modeled cover-bidder type *on average* than FL non-cobidders, but firm-level membership remains unidentified.
- **Causal test of the deployment mechanism.** Why cobidders accumulate $\approx 1.8\times$ more participations than FL non-cobidders, why their portfolio HHI is higher, why they face direct CADE winners $7\times$ more often — none of these descriptive contrasts identify the cartel's deployment incentive. They are profile evidence, not mechanism evidence. We do not claim otherwise.
- **Ruling out reverse selection on observables.** It is possible that cobidders end up adjacent to direct CADE defendants because they share unobserved sectoral or geographic exposure with cartel firms, not because of equilibrium deployment. The descriptive profile cannot break the alternative interpretation; the validation framing in §\ref{sec:cade} addresses this through the conservative pre-2020 benchmark and permutation null, not through this descriptive bridge.

## V. What rhetoric had to be narrowed

The v15 manuscript treats cobidders as the unproblematic empirical analog of the cover-bidder type. The decomposition shows the bridge is *real but partial*. The narrowing:

| v15 claim | v16 claim |
|---|---|
| "Cobidders are the empirical content the screening statistic is designed to recover" | "Cobidders are the within-stratum population whose descriptive profile is closest to the cover-bidder type along four operational predictions; the construct's discrimination is read against this population, not against direct cartel members" |
| "Strong AUC against the cobidder label corroborates that the screening statistic concentrates on cartel-relevant environments" | (kept verbatim — this claim is supported) |
| "Participation alongside an adjudicated cartel member is consistent with cover bidding" | (kept verbatim — this claim is the bridge's hypothesis, not its conclusion) |
| Tacit assumption that cobidder = cover-bidder for validation purposes | Now explicit: cobidders match the modeled type on $\frac{5}{7}$ measured dimensions; on one dimension (share-of-bids in repeat-pair clusters) they look less rather than more like the type; on two dimensions the data layer cannot adjudicate (firm-level price aggressiveness and bid dispersion) |

The new framing acknowledges: (a) the bridge is empirical and consequential, not just verbal; (b) the bridge is also incomplete on dimensions the data cannot reach; (c) the construct's discrimination evidence in §\ref{sec:cade} is what validates the screening interpretation, with this descriptive bridge as supplementary evidence that the validation positive class has the right empirical profile.

## VII. Is the current theory/validation relationship now proportionate to the evidence?

**Yes, with two reservations.**

The current relationship now reads: the framework predicts five operational characteristics of cover-bidder type; the data deliver a clean within-stratum test on four of them; cobidders match the modeled type on five of seven measured dimensions, including the two with the largest standardized differences (P1 deployment intensity at $d=+1.00$ on unique winners; P2 deployment proximity at $d=+0.46$ on direct-CADE share); the construct's discrimination AUC reads against this empirically grounded validation object, not against an arbitrary subset of always-losers. This is a meaningfully tighter bridge than v15.

**Reservation 1: P3 (repeat co-bidding) is not cleanly supported.** Share-based and count-based metrics disagree. We report this honestly and do not lean on P3 as part of the bridge.

**Reservation 2: Two predictions of the cover-bidder type — bid aggressiveness and bid dispersion — cannot be tested at the firm level given the data layer.** This is a structural limitation of the award-record envelope and consistent with the paper's overall framing (we are operating at the award layer, not the bid layer). The framework's predictions about bid-level behavior would be testable in a jurisdiction where bid microdata is preserved at the firm level; we note this as a portability/replicability point in §\ref{sec:limitations} and §\ref{sec:forensic}.

The bridge is honest, evidence-based, and proportionate. It does not overclaim that cobidders *are* cover bidders; it claims the cobidder population's empirical profile matches the cover-bidder type more closely than the within-stratum alternative does, on dimensions the framework predicts and the data can measure.
