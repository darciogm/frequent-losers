\begin{abstract}
Can cartels be screened without bid-level data? Most
cartel-detection methods read collusion off the distribution of
submitted bids; many enforcement environments preserve only the
contract-award layer. We study what survives that coarsening.
A separating-equilibrium framework identifies
$\log(1+\text{tenders\_count})$ together with the
$\text{wins}=0$ filter as a sufficient ranking statistic for
cover-bidder type given award-record data. In São Paulo's
electronic procurement platform ($\valYearStart$--$\valYearEnd$),
$\valFL$ of $\valAlwaysLosers$ persistent zero-win firms cross
the resulting threshold---\emph{frequent losers}. Their presence
associates with a $\valHeadlineRange$ higher conditional log
negotiated unit price across four estimators. The sign reverses
once the comparison is restricted to items where treated and
control overlap on observables ($\valMatchOverlapCoef$ to
$\valMatchPSCoef$). The framework reads the reversal as a
feature: the overlap restriction strips precisely the
equilibrium sorting the screening statistic is designed to
capture. Against CADE's cartel records, the construct
discriminates cobidders at AUC $\valAUCprePost$ on a strict
pre-$2020$ benchmark and $\valAUCFLfirmTemp$ under temporal
holdout; AUC against direct defendants is $\valAUCdirectStd$,
the design's empirical signature. The result is an award-layer
screening stage that runs ahead of the bid-layer forensic stage
the prior literature requires.
\end{abstract}
