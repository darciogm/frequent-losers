\begin{abstract}
Cartel-detection methods in the empirical industrial-organization
literature read collusion off the distribution of submitted bids.
Most enforcement environments globally preserve only the contract-
award layer. We ask what cartel-screening properties survive that
information coarsening, and we argue that the question reorganizes
how enforcement should be architected. A separating-equilibrium
framework with cover bidders identifies
$\log(1+\text{tenders\_count})$ together with the
$\text{wins}=0$ filter as a sufficient ranking statistic for
cover-bidder type given an award-record envelope; the binary
\emph{frequent-loser} rule is its information-coarsening. In São
Paulo's electronic procurement platform
($\valYearStart$--$\valYearEnd$), the construct discriminates
adjudicated cartel-cobidder firms inside the always-loser stratum
at AUC $\valAUCFLfirmTemp$ under temporal holdout
(train $2009$--$2016$, test $2017$--$2019$) and at
$\valAUCprePost$ on a strict pre-$2020$ benchmark with
participation-stratified permutation null. Against bid-distribution
detectors that require per-bidder bid amounts, the screening
statistic reaches comparable accuracy on a substantially thinner
data envelope (AUC $\valImhofFLBin$ vs.\ $\valImhofFull$ on the same target) and
adds non-redundant signal in same-sample combination
($+\valImhofIncFL$ AUC, DeLong $p=\valImhofIncFLp$). Cobidders
match the modeled cover-bidder type along four of five
operational predictions (Cohen's $d$ up to $\valBridgeUniqWinD$ within the
always-loser stratum). The construct also leaves a
$\valHeadlineRange$ conditional log-price imprint as descriptive
corroboration. The contribution is informational and architectural:
an award-layer screening stage that prioritizes environments for
the bid-layer forensic stage the prior literature requires,
deployable wherever award records are preserved.
\end{abstract}
