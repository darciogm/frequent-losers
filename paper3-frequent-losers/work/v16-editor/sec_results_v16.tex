\section{Main Results}
\label{sec:results}

This section delivers the screening-value evidence in three
moves. First, the broad-sample association $\beta$ across four
estimators (§\ref{sec:results_ols}). Second, the
overlap-restricted association $\beta^{\text{ov}}$ and the sign
reversal as the diagnostic that locates the construct as a
screening-value object rather than a treatment-effect object
(§\ref{sec:results_overlap}). Third, the heterogeneity of the
screening signal across detection regimes
(§\ref{sec:results_oversight}).

\subsection{Broad-Sample Conditional Association \texorpdfstring{($\beta$)}{(beta)}}
\label{sec:results_ols}

Table~\ref{tab:prices} reports OLS estimates of
Equation~\eqref{eq:ols_main}. Frequent-loser presence associates
with a higher log negotiated unit price in all four
specifications: $\valPctSixeight$ within item and year
($\valPctSixfour$ adding procuring-unit fixed effects), and
$\valPctNinethree$ in pregão against $\valPctThreeeight$ in
convite. The comparison set is the $\valSampleN$ tender-items in
the same product-code $\times$ year $\times$ procuring-unit cells
without frequent-loser participants.

\input{./output/tables/tab_prices}

The pregão--convite contrast is informative on whether the
construct's signal could be mechanically driven by the convite
minimum-bidder rule (Lei~$8.666/93$, Art.\ 22, \S\,3): a
quorum-filler reading would predict a sharper imprint in
convite, but the data show the opposite ($\valFalPregBin$ in
pregão vs.~$\valFalConvBin$ in convite;
Table~\ref{tab:falsification_modal}). The signal is therefore
not reducible to the quorum rule. We read the asymmetry as
informativeness across observability regimes
(§\ref{sec:mech_modal}), not as a positive test of any specific
institutional channel.

Cross-fit estimation yields $\valPctThreeSix$, CEM
$\valBidCoefMean$, and IPW $\valBidNonCV$. A leave-one-out IV
returns $\valBidPHigh$ ($p<0.05$, first-stage $F=396$), the
direction expected when the binary indicator coarsens the
underlying continuous loss-intensity statistic
(§\ref{sec:mech_horse_race}). The four broad-sample estimators
jointly bound the conditional range $\valHeadlineRange$.

Under the screening framing of §\ref{sec:emp_estimand}, $\beta$
is the empirical content the construct is designed to recover:
the conditional association at items where frequent losers
deploy, integrated over the cartel's deployment distribution.
The relevant question for §\ref{sec:results_overlap} is whether
the deployment sorting is itself part of the screening-value
content, not whether $\beta$ would survive a counterfactual
that strips the sorting away.

\subsection{Overlap-Restricted Association \texorpdfstring{($\beta^{\text{ov}}$)}{(beta-ov)} and What the Sign Reversal Does and Does Not Tell Us}
\label{sec:results_overlap}

Restricting the comparison to cells in which frequent-loser-present
and -absent items genuinely overlap on observables and reweighting
to the average treatment effect on the treated produces a reversed
conditional coefficient: overlap-cell ATT $\valMatchOverlapCoef$,
propensity-score-trimmed ATT $\valMatchPSCoef$
(Table~\ref{tab:item_level_scope_match}).

\input{./output/tables/tab_item_level_scope_match}

The reversal is real, and we report it. Two interpretive moves,
however, that an earlier version of the paper made are not
supported once the gap is decomposed empirically. We document the
decomposition here so that the reader can see exactly what the
data do and do not say.

\paragraph{The reversal is a reweighting result, not an
overlap-dropping result} The gap from $\valBetaBroad$
($\valBetaBroadN$ items) to $\valBetaOverlapATT$ separates into
two margins: (i) restricting to cells with both treated and
untreated items, and (ii) ATT-reweighting within those cells.
We re-estimated the same regression on the overlap subsample
\emph{without} ATT-weights. The unweighted overlap coefficient is
$\valBetaUnwOverlap$ ($p=\valBetaUnwOverlapP$,
$N=\valBetaOverlapN$). The reversal to $\valBetaOverlapATT$ is
produced by the ATT-weighting step, not by the overlap restriction
itself: only $\valDroppedItems$ of $79{,}452$ treated items
($\valDroppedItemsShare$) lack a within-cell counterfactual and
are strictly dropped. The remaining $99\%$ of treated items
participate in both estimates; the difference is which untreated
counterfactuals each estimator up-weights.

\paragraph{The cell-dropping pattern provides only mixed support
for a deployment-sorting reading} An earlier version of the paper
read the reversal as a diagnostic that overlap restriction strips
out exactly the deployment-sorted, screening-rich environments.
Comparing treated items in dropped versus surviving cells along
six discriminating dimensions
(Table~\ref{tab:sign_reversal_decomp}, Panel C): convite share is
\emph{lower} in dropped cells ($\valDropConviteShare$ vs.\
$\valSurvConviteShare$, $p<10^{-15}$, opposite of the
deployment-targeting prediction); procuring-unit-size quartile
is also lower in dropped cells; direct-CADE-item share differs
trivially and is statistically null
($\valDropDirectShare$ vs.\ $\valSurvDirectShare$,
$p=\valDropDirectP$); cobidder-item share is the one dimension
\emph{consistent} with the deployment reading
($\valDropCobidShare$ vs.\ $\valSurvCobidShare$, $p<10^{-6}$).
Dropped cells are dominated by high-priced specialty items with
many bidders---a thin-market problem distinct from any
deployment-sorting story. We no longer claim that the cell-dropping
pattern confirms the screening-value reading; the empirical
evidence is mixed at best.

\input{./output/tables/tab_sign_reversal_decomp}

\paragraph{What survives inside the overlap sample} Two results
inside the overlap subsample remain informative for the framework.
First, $\widehat\beta^{\text{ov}}$ is statistically null among
items containing direct CADE defendants
($\valBetaCADEDirect$, $p=\valBetaCADEDirectP$), in contrast to
its strongly negative value in non-direct-CADE items
($\valBetaCADENonDirect$, $p<0.001$): on the cells closest to
adjudicated cartel activity, the negative reading does not
survive. Second, in the highest tender-value quartile (Q4)---where
deployment value is most plausible \emph{a priori} on contract
size alone---$\widehat\beta^{\text{ov}}$ is positive and
significant at $\valBetaQfour$ ($p=\valBetaQfourP$). The two
quantities are modest but they are residue, inside the overlap
sample, of what the screening framework would predict; we report
them as such, not as positive identification.

\paragraph{What we read into the reversal, and what we do not}
Under the framework of Online Appendix~A, $\beta$ on the broad
sample picks up the conditional association at items where
frequent losers deploy, integrated over the cartel's deployment
distribution. $\beta^{\text{ov}}$ on the ATT-reweighted overlap
sample is a different empirical object that up-weights cells with
relatively few untreated counterfactuals, and the reweighting can
generate a negative coefficient under data-generating processes
the framework allows (Online Appendix~A,
Proposition~\ref{prop:screening_value}, treated as one
interpretation among several). What we now claim is narrower than
v15 of this paper claimed: (i) the broad-sample positive imprint
\emph{survives} an unweighted overlap restriction at
$\valBetaUnwOverlap$, so it is not built on items the design would
discard; (ii) the cell-dropping pattern offers one of six
empirical dimensions consistent with deployment sorting (cobidder
concentration); (iii) inside the overlap sample,
$\widehat\beta^{\text{ov}}$ is null on direct-CADE items and
positive in the largest tender-value quartile.
What we do \emph{not} claim is that the sign reversal cleanly
identifies the screening-value reading against alternative
data-generating processes. The pairing of $\beta$ and
$\beta^{\text{ov}}$ is reported transparently; we privilege $\beta$
under the screening framing and acknowledge that
$\beta^{\text{ov}}$ would be privileged under a treatment-effect
framing the data do not adjudicate.

The construct's defense, in the rebuilt hierarchy, does not rest
on the sign reversal. The supporting evidence carries: the
discrimination AUCs and the architectural comparison with bid-
distribution detectors (\S\ref{sec:cade}, \S\ref{sec:forensic})
do not depend on overlap weighting. \citet{cinelli2020making}'s
$RV_{q=1}=\valRVqOne$ and \citet{oster2019unobservable}'s
$\hat{\delta}=\valOsterDelta$ bound the broad-sample association
against selection on observables already absorbed by the fixed
effects.

\subsection{Heterogeneity Across Detection Regimes}
\label{sec:results_oversight}

Splitting the sample into quartiles of procuring-unit size and
re-estimating the within-item specification produces a sharp
contrast at the extreme quartiles: $\valOversightQonePct$ at
the smallest-buyer quartile (Q1) and $\valOversightQfourPct$ at
the largest (Q4), a $\valPBUgradientRatio\!\!\times$
extreme-quartile gradient. The two intermediate quartiles---Q2
at $\valOversightQtwoPct$ and Q3 at
$\valOversightQthreePct$---are imprecisely estimated and not
statistically distinguishable from each other or from Q4
(item-clustered standard errors;
Table~\ref{tab:regime_oversight}). We do not claim a clean
monotonic decline across all four quartiles; we claim that the
extremes contrast in the direction the framework predicts and
that the contrast is preserved across alternative size measures
(annual contract volume, cumulative item count, headcount of
distinct purchasers).

The framework in Online Appendix~A predicts a contrast in this
direction through the detection-cost comparative static
$\partial m^*/\partial \theta_k < 0$: cover-bidder deployment
is more aggressive where the principal cost of detection is
lower, so the screening signal is more pronounced in the
small-buyer regime. We read the Q1-versus-Q4 contrast as
heterogeneity in the screening object across the monitoring
environment, not as identification of a causal channel. Buyer
size proxies for several correlated institutional features---procurement-officer
tenure \citep{coviello2017tenure}, internal-audit
infrastructure, item composition, and discretionary procedure
use \citep{decarolis2020rules,decarolis2017corruption}---none of
which can be separated with the variation we have. The
implication is that the screening signal varies systematically
with features of the enforcement environment, in the direction
the framework's deployment problem predicts, with the magnitude
of the variation concentrated at the extremes of the
buyer-size distribution.

\paragraph*{Summary} The three results read jointly. The
broad-sample $\beta$ picks up the participation footprint where
the cartel deploys; the overlap-restricted $\beta^{\text{ov}}$
isolates the within-support comparison; the extreme-quartile
contrast locates the regime where the screening signal is
sharpest. The directions match the framework's predictions
(broad-sample positive, overlap-restricted negative, small-buyer
larger than large-buyer), and the validation in §\ref{sec:cade}
corroborates the reading on an independent dimension. What
remains is the modal contrast and the predictions the data do
not adjudicate (§\ref{sec:mechanisms}).
