# Argument Spine Rebuild — v16 Strategic Pass

**Stance:** structural surgery, not line editing. The current paper has a hierarchy mismatch: the strongest evidence (discrimination AUC, temporal-holdout, Imhof comparability) sits in supporting role, while the most fragile result (pricing β with sign reversal) carries the headline. This memo lays out the rebuilt spine. Line edits follow in subsequent passes.

---

## I. The strongest honest version of the contribution

Multiple framings are defensible; only one can be primary. After a hard look at what the data actually support:

### Primary contribution

> **An award-layer screening statistic, derived from a separating-equilibrium framework with cover bidders, discriminates cartel-adjacent populations as accurately as bid-distribution methods that require bid microdata. The architecture this enables — an award-layer screening stage that produces candidate environments for a bid-layer forensic stage — is deployable wherever award records are preserved, which is most of the universe of public procurement systems globally.**

This is the paper at its strongest defensible level. Three reasons:

1. **The discrimination evidence is the most identified.** AUC 0.748 (strict pre-2020 conservative benchmark) and AUC 0.864 (temporal holdout against post-sample cobidders) are real numbers with proper CIs and permutation-stratified null distributions. They survive temporal extrapolation and label withholding (leakage audit's structural component at AUC 0.891).
2. **The architectural claim is empirically grounded.** AUC parity (0.888 vs 0.903) on the same cartel-adjacency target across two pipelines that operate on different data envelopes is a fact of the data, not an interpretation.
3. **The contribution travels.** The award layer survives in nearly every electronic procurement system globally; the FL screen is computable from data the system already holds; the architecture generalizes to any jurisdiction where the bid layer is missing or expensive to query.

### Secondary contributions

In descending order of defensibility:

- **(2a)** The continuous loss-intensity primitive $\log(1+\text{tenders\_count})$ strictly dominates the binary rule in discrimination (DeLong $p<10^{-3}$). The framework's identification of this primitive is empirically corroborated. Method-of-paper-design contribution.
- **(2b)** The conservative pre-2020 contemporaneous benchmark with participation-stratified permutation null is the validation discipline the literature has been missing for cover-bidder identification on award records. Methodological-disciplinary contribution.
- **(2c)** Frequent-loser presence associates with $+3.6\%$ to $+7.7\%$ higher conditional log negotiated unit price across four estimators in the broad sample. **Descriptive corroboration that cartel-adjacent environments carry a price imprint** — not a treatment effect, not a uniform causal claim.

### Tertiary, contested, or kept-minimal

- **(3a)** The screening-value-vs-treatment-effect interpretation of the sign reversal — kept as conceptual disclosure of why $\beta$ is read on the broad sample, not as the paper's central interpretive bet.
- **(3b)** The detection-regime gradient across procuring-unit-size quartiles — kept as scope information, not as a load-bearing finding.
- **(3c)** The first-time-FL prediction and HHI cell-heterogeneity grid — appendix only.

### What is **not** the contribution

- A causal estimate of cover bidding's effect on prices.
- An identification of the institutional channel through which buyer size moderates the participation footprint.
- A prescription that bid-microdata archival is unnecessary (the paper's policy point is that screening *can* operate without it, not that bid microdata is dispensable).
- An adversarial-proof screen.

## II. What gets demoted

### (a) The pricing $\beta$ is demoted from centerpiece to corroborator

The current spine reads: *"Frequent-loser presence associates with higher prices [headline]; this is corroborated by cartel-adjacency validation [supporting]; the screening framing reorganizes how to read the sign reversal [interpretation]; the architectural test confirms comparable accuracy [robustness]."*

The new spine reads: *"An award-layer screening statistic discriminates cartel-adjacent populations at AUC comparable to bid-distribution methods [headline]; the screening signal is generated by the participation primitive identified by the framework [mechanism]; the construct also leaves a $+3.6\%$ to $+7.7\%$ conditional price imprint, with a sign reversal under overlap restriction that the screening framing reads as diagnostic of deployment sorting [descriptive corroboration]; the architectural separation between award-layer and bid-layer methods is the contribution's structural claim [conclusion]."*

The pricing pillar is now **one leg, not the platform**. If a hostile R2 attacks the pricing reading wholesale, the discrimination + architectural pillars survive intact.

### (b) The sign-reversal interpretation is demoted from interpretive centerpiece to diagnostic

Currently the screening-value-vs-treatment-effect framing is the most ambitious interpretive move in the paper, and it depends on a non-testable rent-component-co-movement assumption. The new spine relegates it to one paragraph in §7 (the demoted pricing section), with the framing "we interpret the broad-sample $\beta$ as the screening-value object the participation primitive is designed to flag; the overlap-restricted estimate isolates a different empirical object." Less weight, less risk, and the discrimination evidence is what the paper actually rests on.

### (c) The buyer-size gradient is demoted to scope information

Already largely done in v15 / current paper. The non-monotonic intermediate quartiles and the multi-channel proxy issues are honestly reported. The gradient is no longer presented as a finding the paper depends on; it is a feature of the screening signal that varies across detection regimes, mentioned once in the headline §1 and discussed in §7.3 / §8.2 / §11.1.

### (d) The HHI / cell-heterogeneity / first-time-FL predictions move to appendix only

In the rebuilt spine the body does not discuss these at all; they live in App C as transparency for the reader who wants the framework's full prediction set, with the paper noting they "do not adjudicate the framework's mechanism predictions and are not load-bearing for the screening interpretation."

Strong evidence that the construct's discrimination is dominated by the participation primitive, not by anything sneakily borrowed from bid-distribution moments. Supports the screening-statistic-survives-coarsening claim.

### The headline triple

The paper now leads with three numbers, in order:

1. **AUC 0.864** under temporal holdout against post-sample cobidder labels (the defensible discrimination claim).
2. **AUC 0.903 vs 0.888** for the screening statistic vs the Imhof full pipeline on the same target (the architectural claim).
3. **+3.6% to +7.7%** higher conditional log negotiated unit price (the descriptive corroboration).

Currently the paper leads with #3 and treats #1 and #2 as supporting. The rebuild reverses this hierarchy.

## VII. The cover-letter framing this spine supports

> *"This paper offers a screening statistic for cartel-adjacency that operates on award-record data, the data layer that survives in most electronic procurement environments globally. The construct discriminates adjudicated CADE-cobidder firms inside the always-loser stratum at AUC 0.864 under temporal holdout (train 2009–2016, test 2017–2019) and at AUC 0.748 on a strict contemporaneous benchmark restricted to pre-2020 cases. Against bid-distribution detectors that require per-bidder bid amounts, the screening statistic reaches comparable accuracy on a substantially thinner data envelope (AUC 0.903 vs 0.888 on the same target) and adds non-redundant signal in same-sample combination ($+0.035$ AUC, DeLong $p=0.014$). The construct also leaves a $+3.6\%$ to $+7.7\%$ conditional log price imprint as descriptive corroboration. The contribution is informational and architectural — we propose a sequenced enforcement architecture in which an award-layer screening stage runs ahead of a bid-layer forensic stage — rather than causal-identification. Failed RDD and DiD designs are reported transparently."*

The framing leads with what's strongest, frames the pricing as corroboration not headline, names the architectural contribution, and signals the non-causal stance up front. JLEO editors recognize this register.

## VIII. The discipline this rebuild enforces

The rebuilt spine forces the paper to commit to:

1. **No causal claims about the price imprint.** The pricing $\beta$ is descriptive corroboration; period.
2. **No interpretive overreach on the sign reversal.** Read as deployment-sorting diagnostic in App; in main only as a single explanatory sentence.
3. **No overclaim on the architectural sequencing.** Imhof same-sample audit + leakage decomposition support the architectural story; the formal optimality of the sequenced architecture is left for App A's corollary (a future addition).
4. **Honest disclosure of label tautology.** Leakage decomposition becomes a §6 result, not an App footnote.
5. **Power disclosure.** App C now contains the MDE for the failed designs; the failures were structural, not the screening statistic's fault.

These constraints make the paper smaller in claim but larger in defensibility. JLEO referees who reject overclaim will find less to reject.

## IX. What the rebuilt spine does not solve

- **The rent-component-co-movement assumption** (Proposition 4) remains a non-testable premise. The rebuild reduces its salience by demoting the sign-reversal-diagnostic; it does not fix the underlying logic gap.
- **Single-jurisdiction limitation.** The portability claim becomes prerequisites-based rather than empirical; that's the best the data permit.
- **Cobidder labels are still indirect.** The leakage decomposition discloses the tautology component; it does not eliminate it.

The rebuild is a hierarchy fix, not an evidence fix. It moves the paper from "good evidence at the supporting tier, fragile claim at the headline" to "good evidence at the headline tier, fragile claim demoted." That is the maximum honest improvement available without new data.
