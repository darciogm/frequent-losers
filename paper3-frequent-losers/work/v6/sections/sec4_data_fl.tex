\section{Data and Frequent Losers Definition}
\label{sec:data}

\subsection{BEC Platform}
\label{sec:bec}

Our data come from the Bolsa Eletr\^onica de Compras (BEC), S\~ao
Paulo's centralized electronic procurement platform serving 1,308
purchasing units (PBUs) across state agencies, universities, hospitals,
and municipal bodies. BEC handles two modalities:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Convite} (sealed-bid): used for purchases up to
    R\$80,000, requires a minimum of three bidders (Lei~8.666/93,
    Art.~22, \S7). Bids are submitted simultaneously and opened together.
  \item \textbf{Preg\~ao} (electronic reverse auction): the default
    modality for standard goods and services. Bidders submit descending
    bids in real time; there is no minimum-bidder requirement.
\end{itemize}

The minimum-bidder rule for convite creates a direct incentive for cover
bidding: if a cartel controls one genuine bidder, it needs at least two
cover bidders to meet the threshold. Under preg\~ao, cover bidders serve
primarily to inflate apparent competition and discourage genuine entry.

Our sample spans 2009--2019 and contains approximately 4.5 million
tender-items, 40 million bids, 41,000 unique firms, and 1,308 PBUs.
Table~\ref{tab:modality_year} shows the evolution of procurement volume
by modality over time.

\subsection{Frequent Losers Definition}
\label{sec:fl_definition}

We define frequent losers (FL) in two steps:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Always-losers}: firms with a zero win rate across all
    2009--2019 tenders. 16,843 firms ($\sim$41\% of all
    BEC participants) never win a single tender.
  \item \textbf{IQR threshold}: among always-losers, we identify those
    with abnormally high participation counts using a median + 1.5
    $\times$ IQR threshold on the distribution of tender participations.%
    \footnote{We use median + 1.5 $\times$ IQR rather than the standard % REVISION-V6: IQR footnote with structural connection
    Tukey rule (Q3 + 1.5 $\times$ IQR) because the participation
    distribution is highly right-skewed. The median-based threshold
    yields a cutoff of approximately 14 participations and identifies
    2,735 FL firms. Economically, this cutoff identifies firms with
    participation frequency so extreme as to be difficult to rationalize
    as competitive optimization: at this threshold, the marginal cost
    of bid preparation would exceed any plausible expected return
    from participation under standard assumptions about win
    probabilities. The ROC analysis in Section~\ref{sec:detection_validation}
    confirms that the data-driven optimal threshold (Youden's $J$
    criterion, $J = 0.84$) corresponds to a multiplier of
    1.45$\times$IQR---nearly identical to our 1.5$\times$ rule,
    with an AUC of 0.94 against CADE ground truth.
    Robustness to the multiplier choice is reported in
    Section~\ref{sec:robustness}.}
    This yields approximately 2,735 FL firms.
\end{enumerate}

The treatment variable $\text{losers}_{igt}$ equals one if tender-item
$i$ in item group $g$ at time $t$ has at least one FL firm among its
participants, and zero otherwise.

Figures~\ref{fig:losses} and~\ref{fig:iqr} show the participation
distribution of always-losers, with the IQR threshold indicated. The
distribution is heavily right-skewed: most always-losers participate in
fewer than 10 tenders, while FL firms participate in 15 to over 400.

\subsection{Sample Construction}
\label{sec:sample}

We restrict the analysis sample as follows: (i) convite and preg\~ao
modalities only (phases 2 and 3); (ii) tender-items with a recorded
winner; (iii) item types that have at least one tender with FL
participation.%
\footnote{Restriction (iii) ensures that control tenders come from item
markets where FL firms could plausibly participate. We report results
without this restriction in Section~\ref{sec:robustness} and
Appendix~\ref{app:robustness}.}
The resulting sample contains 1,654,447 tender-items across 18,783
item types and 1,308 PBUs. FL firms participate in 4.8\% of
tender-items (79,456 tender-items).

\subsection{Structural Estimation Sample} % REVISION-V6: new subsection
\label{sec:structural_sample}

The structural model (Section~\ref{sec:structural}) requires bid-level
data with firm-level covariates. The estimation sample comprises
approximately 40 million individual bids from 41,000 unique firms across
all 2009--2019 tender-items. For each bid, we observe the bid amount,
the winning (negotiated) price, firm identity (CNPJ), firm
characteristics (size, CNAE sector), and the FL classification of the
bidder.

At the tender-item level, FL-present tenders exhibit a within-tender
coefficient of variation (CV) of bids of 0.56, compared to 0.37 for
non-FL tenders---a ratio of 1.52. This 52\% higher bid dispersion in
FL-present tenders provides the key moment for regime identification
(Prediction~\ref{pred:regime_v6}): the elevated dispersion is consistent
with Regime~1 (complementary cover bidding), where cover bids are drawn
from a wide distribution above the winning price, rather than Regime~2
(coordinated cover bidding), which would produce \emph{lower} dispersion.

\subsection{CADE Validation Sample} % REVISION-V6: new subsection
\label{sec:cade_sample}

To validate the FL screen against actual enforcement outcomes, we
construct a temporal cross-validation sample using CADE (Conselho
Administrativo de Defesa Econ\^omica) cartel conviction records:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Training period (FL definition):} 2009--2014. FL firms
    are classified using participation and win-rate data from this
    period only. This ensures that FL status is determined
    \emph{before} the validation outcomes are observed.
  \item \textbf{Validation period (CADE outcomes):} 2015--2019. We
    assess whether FL-flagged firms co-participate with CADE-convicted
    cartelists during this period.
\end{itemize}

The CADE validation data comprise 65 cartel cases involving procurement
(2009--2019), of which 47 convicted firms match to BEC identifiers. Among
2,735 FL firms, 193 (7.1\%) co-participate with at least one
CADE-convicted firm in at least one tender-item. The permutation test
(Section~\ref{sec:cade}) shows this rate is 3.5 times higher than
expected after controlling for participation intensity ($p < 0.001$).

The temporal split prevents information leakage: the FL screen's
performance is evaluated on \emph{future} enforcement outcomes not used
in defining FL status. This out-of-sample design is critical for the
ROC analysis in Section~\ref{sec:detection_validation}.
