\section{Data and Frequent-Losers Definition}
\label{sec:data}

\subsection{BEC Platform}
\label{sec:data_bec}

The data come from the Bolsa Eletr\^onica de Compras (BEC), São
Paulo's centralized electronic procurement platform, operated by
the State Treasury since $2002$. São Paulo state has $46.1$
million residents ($21.6\%$ of Brazil's population) and a
$2023$ GDP of R\$$3.45$ trillion ($31.5\%$ of Brazil's GDP; IBGE
\emph{Sistema de Contas Regionais}, $2025$). Treated as an
independent economy it would sit alongside Switzerland or
Argentina, and state-level procurement spending is large enough
in absolute terms to make the deployment value of an award-layer
screening stage a meaningful policy question. BEC serves $1{,}308$
purchasing units (PBUs)---state agencies, public universities,
hospitals, schools---and clears two procurement modalities during
our sample: \emph{convite} (sealed-bid invitation procedure with
a statutory three-bidder requirement, Lei~$\valLeiOriginal$) and
\emph{preg\~ao} (electronic reverse auction with no
minimum-bidder requirement, Lei~$\valLeiPregao$). The
$\valYearStart$--$\valYearEnd$ window comprises
$\valDataItemAvgVar{,}754$ contract-record items across $22$
calendar semesters, generated from $\valDataNbidsMean{,}357$
individual bids placed by $\valBECfirms$ distinct firms
(Table~\ref{tab:modality_year}). The contract-record layer
preserves participants, winners, negotiated prices, reference
prices, and procurement metadata; the bid-by-bid log is retained
in the operational system but exported only on administrative
request, the layer asymmetry characterized in
\S\ref{sec:institutional}. Preg\~ao expands from a minority share
toward near-universal coverage by $2019$, consistent with the
$2021$ consolidation of preg\~ao-style auctions as the
institutional default; convite remains a meaningful share in the
small-cap range and in early years, providing the within-platform
variation that the modal contrast in \S\ref{sec:results_ols} and
\S\ref{sec:mech_modal} reads.

\subsection{Frequent-Losers Definition}
\label{sec:fl_definition}

The framework's $\text{wins}=0$ filter is a separating choice
under standard expected-profit competition: with per-bid cost
$c$, mean order value $\bar V$, and gross margin $m$, a firm
that bids without ever winning crosses break-even on $n$ losses
at $n^{*}=\bar V m / c - 2$ (Online Appendix~A). At BEC parameters
($\bar V \approx$ R\$$\valDataItemAvgPay$, $m=0.10$, documented
bid-preparation cost $c \in [\text{R\$}200,\text{R\$}500]$),
$n^{*}\in[15,41]$. The empirical FL threshold of
$\valThreshold$ tender-items lies in this interval; the
$90$\textsuperscript{th}-percentile FL participates in $50+$
zero-win tenders and accumulates direct cost above
R\$$\valPermFirms$. Sustained zero-win participation past the
break-even is consistent with cover bidding, capacity signaling,
or eligibility maintenance; we organize the empirical work around
the cover-bidding reading without claiming it as the unique
interpretation (\S\ref{sec:lim_construct}).

We define frequent losers as the intersection of two conditions:
(i) \emph{always-losers}, firms with a zero win rate across all
$\valYearStart$--$\valYearEnd$ BEC tenders ($\valAlwaysLosers$
firms, $\valPctFortyone$ of all BEC participants), and
(ii) participation counts above
$\text{median}+1.5\!\times\!\text{IQR}$ of the always-loser
participation distribution.\footnote{We use median + $1.5\!\times$
IQR rather than the Tukey rule (Q3 + $1.5\!\times$ IQR) because
the participation distribution is heavily right-skewed; the
adopted rule yields a stricter threshold. The Tukey alternative
flags $\valFLQThreeIQR$ firms with AUC $\valAUCQThreeIQR$, against
$\valAUCFLfirm$ under the adopted rule. Robustness to the
multiplier choice is in \S\ref{sec:rob_threshold}.} The threshold
is fixed \emph{ex ante} on the participation distribution and
the framework's break-even, before any examination of CADE
labels, and yields $\valThreshold$ or more tenders with zero
wins ($\valFL$ frequent-loser firms). The treatment variable
$\text{losers}_{igt}$ equals one if tender-item $i$ in item
group $g$ at time $t$ has at least one frequent-loser
participant. The economic construct is broader than the binary
rule: Proposition~\ref{prop:exposure_stmt} identifies
$\log(1+\text{tenders\_count})$ as a sufficient ranking statistic
for cover-bidder type given award-record data, and the binary
rule is the information-coarsening of this statistic under the
framework's separating equilibrium; the horse race in
\S\ref{sec:mech_horse_race} confirms that the continuous
statistic strictly dominates the binary
(\citealt{delong1988comparing}, $p < \valDeLongP$). Out-of-sample
generalization of the threshold itself is reassuring: estimating
the cutoff on $2009$--$2016$ alone gives a value of
$\valThresholdTrain$ with firm-level AUC
$\valAUCStrictFirmFL$ ($\valAUCStrictFirmFLCI$); evaluating the
$2009$--$2016$-frozen score against $2017$--$2019$ items, the
binary indicator drops to $\valAUCStrictItemFL$ while the
continuous primitive retains $\valAUCStrictItemTC$
(Table~\ref{tab:strict_train_threshold}; full audit in
\S\ref{sec:rob_operational}).

\subsection{Sample and Descriptive Statistics}
\label{sec:data_sample}

The participation distribution among always-losers is heavily
right-skewed (Figures~\ref{fig:losses}--\ref{fig:iqr}). The
regression sample retains convite and preg\~ao tenders with a
recorded winner and item types with at least one frequent-loser
participation during the sample window; this conditioning ensures
that controls come from item markets where the screening
statistic can plausibly fire, and the unrestricted sample returns
a coefficient within $0.5$ percentage points of the baseline
(\S\ref{sec:robustness}). The regression sample contains
$\valSampleN$ tender-items across $\valFirmCount$ item types
defined at the finest product-code level; $\valDataFirmMean$
($82\%$) of those item types have both
frequent-loser-present and frequent-loser-absent tenders within
sample, contributing to within-item identification under
Equation~\eqref{eq:ols_main}. Frequent-loser firms participate
in $\valCartelMarkup$ of tender-items. The raw unconditional
price ratio is roughly elevenfold
(R\$$\valOCcount$ vs.\ R\$$\valDataItemMean$;
Table~\ref{tab:descstats}), reflecting the sorting of frequent
losers toward larger contracts; under within-item, within-year,
within-procuring-unit fixed effects, the $2.43$-log-point raw
gap compresses to roughly $0.05$ log points
(Table~\ref{tab:conditional_descstats}). The selection that the
fixed-effects structure absorbs is large; the residual
within-item association is small in log points but systematically
positive across estimators (\S\ref{sec:results_ols}). The CADE
adjudication portfolio that supplies the validation labels is
described in \S\ref{sec:cade_objects}.
