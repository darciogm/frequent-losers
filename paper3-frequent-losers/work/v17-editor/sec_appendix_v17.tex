\section{Cover-Bidding Framework and Proofs}\label{app:proofs}

This appendix formalizes the separating-equilibrium framework
the paper reads against. A cartel of $n$ firms in a procurement
market controls a designated winner with bid $b^*$ and deploys
$m \geq 0$ cover bidders, each submitting a bid $b_\ell > b^*$.
Per-bidder deployment cost is $c_1 > 0$, and the cartel faces a
detection probability $\theta_k \in (0,1)$ that depends on
oversight capacity in market $k$---smaller, less-supervised
buyers have lower $\theta_k$. Let $R(m, \theta_k)$ denote the
cartel's gross expected rents from deploying $m$ cover bidders
in market $k$. The cartel's expected net surplus is then
$\pi(m, \theta_k, c_1) = R(m, \theta_k) - c_1 m - \theta_k
\phi_0 m$, where the second and third terms are the per-bidder
deployment cost and the per-bidder marginal expected detection
penalty $\phi_0 > 0$. We impose two standard assumptions on
gross rents: A1 (rent positivity at the margin,
$\partial R / \partial m \big|_{m=0} > c_1 + \theta_k \phi_0$,
so that deploying at least one cover bidder is profitable);
and A2 (diminishing rents, $\partial^2 R / \partial m^2 < 0$,
which by linearity of the cost terms in $m$ yields
$\partial^2 \pi / \partial m^2 < 0$). Item-level deployment
within a market is treated as the cartel choosing, for each
item $i \in k$, whether to deploy a cover bidder; we use $m$
as the per-market deployment count to keep the choice problem
compact and recover item-level heterogeneity in
Proposition~\ref{prop:screening_value} below by indexing
item-specific rent shifters.\footnote{The reduced-form treatment of $R$
abstracts from the auction structure that generates rents; the
underlying micro-foundations are provided by
\citet{marshall2012economics} for the cartel's allocation rule
and the side-payment scheme that sustains $\kappa$ below.} The empirical
environment we read it against is described by award-record
data: each firm $i$ has a participation count
$\text{tenders\_count}_i$, a win indicator
$\text{wins}_i \in \{0, 1\}$, and a tenure $\tau_i$. The
framework delivers four results. Lemma~\ref{lem:wins_zero_stmt}
identifies the $\text{wins}=0$ filter as the equilibrium choice
of the cover-bidder type. Proposition~\ref{prop:exposure_stmt}
identifies $\log(1+\text{tenders\_count})$ as a sufficient
ranking statistic for cover-bidder type given award-record data.
Proposition~\ref{prop:optimal_m} characterizes the cartel's
optimal deployment rate $m^{*}$ in the principal cost of
detection $\theta_k$. And
Proposition~\ref{prop:screening_value} formalizes why the
broad-sample association $\beta$ and the overlap-restricted
association $\beta^{\text{ov}}$ are different empirical objects
under the framework's deployment problem.

\begin{lemma}[Wins-zero separating equilibrium]\label{lem:wins_zero_stmt}
Consider the bidding subgame in which a cartel allocates the
designated winner to one cartel member submitting bid $b^*$ and
the remaining $m$ cartel firms act as cover bidders, each
choosing a bid $b_\ell$. Assume (i) winning by a non-designated
cartel firm triggers either capacity revelation undermining the
cover identity in subsequent rounds or internal cartel sanctions
of magnitude $\kappa > 0$, and (ii) the cartel's within-cartel
allocation is incentive-compatible (assumption A3:
$\mathrm{rents} < \kappa$ for any non-designated firm; this is
the standard cartel-allocation IC condition derived from the
side-payment or trigger-strategy schemes in
\citet{marshall2012economics, asker2010leniency}). Then the
unique separating equilibrium of this subgame has $b_\ell > b^*$
for every cover bidder and therefore $\Pr(\text{win} \mid C) = 0$:
the bright-line filter $\text{wins} = 0$ is the equilibrium
choice of type $C$. Genuine entrants of type $G$ face no such
constraint and bid $b_G < b^*$ whenever expected payoff is
positive.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:wins_zero_stmt}]
Write the cover-bidder per-period payoff conditional on choosing
bid $b$ as
$u_C(b) = -c_1 - [\theta_k \phi_0 + \kappa] \cdot \Pr(\text{win}
\mid b)$, where the deployment cost $c_1$ is sunk on
participation and the bracketed term is the expected loss
conditional on winning. For $b > b^*$, the designated winner
captures the contract and $\Pr(\text{win} \mid b) = 0$, so
$u_C(b) = -c_1$. For any deviation $b' < b^*$ that becomes the
winning bid (i.e., $b'$ undercuts both $b^*$ and any genuine-entrant
bid), $\Pr(\text{win} \mid b') = 1$ and the cover bidder's
realized payoff becomes
$u_C(b') = -c_1 - \theta_k \phi_0 - \kappa + \mathrm{rents}$,
where $\mathrm{rents}$ is the rent the deviating firm captures
from winning. Under A3, $\mathrm{rents} < \kappa$, so
$u_C(b') < -c_1 = u_C(b)$ and the equilibrium strategy is
$b > b^*$ with $\Pr(\text{win} \mid C) = 0$. Type $G$ has no
$\kappa$ penalty and accepts $b_G < b^*$ when expected payoff is
positive; the empirical filter $\text{wins} = 0$ thus identifies
the type-$C$ subset, conditional on the cartel deploying its
allocation rule. Uniqueness of the separating equilibrium follows
from the strict inequality $u_C(b) > u_C(b')$ under A3, which
makes any pooling or mixed strategy strictly dominated for
type $C$. \qed
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Linearized cumulative exposure]\label{prop:exposure_stmt}
Suppose firm $i$ is a cover bidder of type $C$ deployed by a
cartel in market $k$ at per-period rate
$\lambda_C(\theta_k, c_1)$ and tenure $\tau_i$. Under A1--A2 and
a stationary deployment process with rate
$\lambda_C(\theta_k, c_1) = m^* / |k|$ (where $m^*$ is the
optimum of Proposition~\ref{prop:optimal_m} and $|k|$ is market
size), $T_i \sim \mathrm{Poisson}(\lambda_C \tau_i)$ and so
$\mathbb{E}[T_i \mid \text{type}=C, \tau_i] = \lambda_C\,\tau_i$.
For genuine competitive entrants of type $G$,
$\mathbb{E}[T_i \mid \text{type}=G, \tau_i] = \lambda_G\,\tau_i$
with $\lambda_G < \lambda_C$ on the conditioning subset
$\{\text{wins}_i = 0\}$ identified by
Lemma~\ref{lem:wins_zero_stmt}: type-$G$ firms that never win
are competitive losers in cartel-targeted markets and bid less
intensively than cover bidders, who are paid by the cartel to
participate.\footnote{The strict inequality $\lambda_G <
\lambda_C$ on $\{\text{wins} = 0\}$ is a model implication of
the cartel's incentive structure: cover bidders have positive
deployment intensity ($\lambda_C > 0$ from A1), while type-$G$
firms with permanent zero-win rate are by selection low-intensity
participants. The unconditional inequality $\lambda_G \leq
\lambda_C$ over all firms is not required.} The log-statistic
$\log(1+T_i)$ is a monotone, rank-equivalent transformation of
$T_i$, and---under monotone likelihood ratio in the Poisson
family with $\lambda_C > \lambda_G$---the posterior
$\Pr(\text{type}=C \mid T_i = t)$ is non-decreasing in $t$.
Combined with Lemma~\ref{lem:wins_zero_stmt}, the joint
statistic $(\log(1+T_i), \mathbf{1}[\text{wins}_i=0])$ is an
admissible Bayesian ranking statistic for the cover-bidder type
given award-record data; the binary frequent-loser rule
$\mathbf{1}[T_i > k]$ is one information-coarsening of this
posterior, with the empirical choice $k =
\mathrm{med}+1.5\!\times\!\mathrm{IQR}$ a convention motivated
by the empirical bid-distribution rather than by the framework.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:exposure_stmt}]
The expectation result is immediate from the law of large
numbers applied to the deployment counting process given constant
rate $\lambda$. The monotonicity claim follows from Bayes' rule:
$\Pr(C \mid t) = \Pr(t \mid C)\,\pi_C / [\Pr(t \mid C)\,\pi_C +
\Pr(t \mid G)\,(1-\pi_C)]$ is non-decreasing in $t$ if and only
if the likelihood ratio $\Pr(t \mid C)/\Pr(t \mid G)$ is
non-decreasing in $t$. For Poisson likelihoods with rates
$\lambda_C > \lambda_G$, this monotone-likelihood-ratio property
is standard. Hence $\log(1+\text{tenders\_count})$ is a
sufficient ranking statistic for the cover-bidder type and the
binary indicator $\mathbf{1}[\text{tenders\_count} > k]$ is its
information-coarsening form. \qed
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Optimal cover-bidder deployment]\label{prop:optimal_m}
Under A1--A2 and the net-surplus definition $\pi(m, \theta_k,
c_1) = R(m, \theta_k) - (c_1 + \theta_k \phi_0)\,m$, the
cartel's optimal cover-bidder deployment rate $m^*$ satisfies
the first-order condition
$\partial R / \partial m = c_1 + \theta_k \phi_0$. By A2 the
solution is unique and interior. Implicit differentiation of
the FOC yields the comparative statics
$\partial m^*/\partial \theta_k = \phi_0 / (\partial^2 R /
\partial m^2) < 0$ and $\partial m^*/\partial c_1 = 1 /
(\partial^2 R / \partial m^2) < 0$; both ratios are negative
because A2 ensures the denominator
$\partial^2 R / \partial m^2 = \partial^2 \pi / \partial m^2$
is negative. Cover-bidder deployment is more aggressive where
the principal cost of detection is lower
(\S\ref{sec:results_oversight}).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:optimal_m}]
Differentiating $\pi(m, \theta_k, c_1) = R(m, \theta_k) -
(c_1 + \theta_k \phi_0) m$ with respect to $m$ and setting
$\partial \pi / \partial m = 0$ yields $\partial R / \partial m
= c_1 + \theta_k \phi_0$. A1 ensures the optimum is interior
($m^* > 0$); A2 ensures the solution is unique. Define
$F(m, \theta_k, c_1) = \partial R / \partial m - c_1 - \theta_k
\phi_0$; the FOC is $F = 0$. By the implicit function theorem,
$\partial m^* / \partial \theta_k = -F_{\theta_k} / F_m =
\phi_0 / (\partial^2 R / \partial m^2)$ and
$\partial m^* / \partial c_1 = -F_{c_1} / F_m = 1 /
(\partial^2 R / \partial m^2)$. Both are negative because A2
gives $\partial^2 R / \partial m^2 < 0$. \qed
\end{proof}

Propositions~\ref{prop:exposure_stmt}--\ref{prop:optimal_m}
together imply that frequent-loser participation is not
distributed uniformly over items: under A1--A2, the cartel
deploys cover bidders preferentially in items where its expected
surplus is highest, conditional on the deployment cost $c_1$ and
the local detection probability $\theta_k$. Items the cartel
selects into therefore differ from items it does not on a vector
of unobserved characteristics $\mathbf{u}$ that governs the
cartel's expected surplus---chiefly, the rents available from
the designated winner's bid relative to the competitive
equilibrium price. The next proposition formalizes the
implication for the two empirical objects $\beta$ and
$\beta^{\text{ov}}$.

\begin{proposition}[Screening-value vs.\ treatment-effect objects]\label{prop:screening_value}
Let $D_i \in \{0,1\}$ indicate frequent-loser presence in item
$i$, $\mathbf{x}_i$ the vector of observables absorbed by fixed
effects and matching covariates, and $\mathbf{u}_i$ the vector
of unobservables governing the cartel's deployment optimum.
Suppose the cartel's optimal deployment satisfies
$\Pr(D_i=1 \mid \mathbf{x}_i, \mathbf{u}_i) = g(\mathbf{x}_i,
\mathbf{u}_i)$ with $g$ strictly increasing in the rent
component of $\mathbf{u}_i$. Then (1) the broad-sample
$\beta = \mathbb{E}[y_i \mid D_i=1, \mathbf{x}_i] -
\mathbb{E}[y_i \mid D_i=0, \mathbf{x}_i]$ integrates over
conditional distributions of $\mathbf{u}_i$ that differ
systematically in the rent component (Bayes' rule on the
deployment density $g(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{u}) f(\mathbf{u} \mid
\mathbf{x})$); (2) within the common-support cell $\Omega$,
defined by propensity scores
$\Pr(D_i=1 \mid \mathbf{x}_i)$ bounded away from $0$ and $1$,
the conditional means $\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{u}_i \mid
\mathbf{x}_i, \mathbf{x}_i \in \Omega]$ are bounded by the
maximum and minimum of $g(\mathbf{x}, \cdot)^{-1}$ on the bounded
propensity range, so the conditional distributions of
$\mathbf{u}_i$ across $D_i=1$ and $D_i=0$ within $\Omega$ are
more comparable in expectation than they are on the broad
sample (individual items in $\Omega$ may still have extreme
$\mathbf{u}_i$); and (3) under the auxiliary premise that the
rent component shifts the realized log price in the same
direction it shifts the deployment probability, the gap
$\beta - \beta^{\text{ov}}$ inherits the sign of the rent
co-movement, and an observed sign reversal $\beta>0$,
$\beta^{\text{ov}}<0$ is consistent with the deployment problem
rather than refuting it. The proposition does not derive the
sign reversal; it identifies the rent-component-co-movement
premise as a sufficient condition under which the observed sign
reversal is rationalizable within the framework. The
proposition is therefore better read as a structural remark
than as an identification result; we retain the proposition
form for cross-reference convenience.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:screening_value}]
Claim 1 follows from the law of iterated expectations: the
broad-sample $\beta$ averages
$\mathbb{E}[y_i \mid D_i, \mathbf{x}_i, \mathbf{u}_i]$ over the
conditional distributions of $\mathbf{u}_i$ given $D_i$ and
$\mathbf{x}_i$, and Proposition~\ref{prop:optimal_m} combined
with Bayes' rule applied to
$f(\mathbf{u} \mid D=1, \mathbf{x}) \propto g(\mathbf{x},
\mathbf{u}) f(\mathbf{u} \mid \mathbf{x})$ implies
$\mathbf{u}_i \mid D_i=1$ first-order stochastically dominates
$\mathbf{u}_i \mid D_i=0$ in the rent component at the same
$\mathbf{x}_i$. Claim 2 follows from the definition of common
support: cells in $\Omega$ have propensity score
$\Pr(D_i=1 \mid \mathbf{x}_i)$ bounded away from $0$ and $1$,
which by strict monotonicity of $g$ in the rent component
implies that the conditional mean of the rent component within
$\Omega$ is bounded by intermediate values. Individual items in
$\Omega$ may still have extreme rent realizations; the
proposition's comparability claim is about cell-level expectations,
not pointwise. Claim 3 is the comparative statement: under the
rent-component co-movement premise, the broad-sample average
inherits a positive contribution from the high-rent cells, while
$\beta^{\text{ov}}$ averages over a sub-population in which the
high-rent contribution is mechanically attenuated. The gap
$\beta - \beta^{\text{ov}}$ therefore inherits the sign of the
rent co-movement under the premise; an observed sign reversal is
consistent with this gap exceeding $|\beta^{\text{ov}}|$, but
the proposition does not derive sign-reversal as a unique
prediction. If the premise fails, the proposition is silent and
the observed sign reversal must be explained by some channel
other than the deployment problem. \qed
\end{proof}

The proposition is the formal counterpart to the screening-value
reading in \S\ref{sec:results_overlap}: under the rent-component
co-movement premise, the observed sign reversal is consistent
with the deployment problem rather than refuting it. The
proposition does not assert that the deployment problem
\emph{predicts} the sign reversal; it identifies a sufficient
condition (the rent-component co-movement) under which the sign
reversal is rationalizable within the framework. The model does
not by itself adjudicate whether the premise holds; the empirical
evidence of \S\ref{sec:cade}, \S\ref{sec:mech_horse_race}, and
the segment-level decomposition of \S\ref{sec:results_overlap}
together discipline the interpretation.

The framework is silent on the bid-level shape of $b_C$ beyond
the equilibrium constraint $b_C > b^*$. The early empirical
cover-bidding literature
\citep{porter1993detection,porter1999ohio,bajari2003deciding}
reads the constraint loosely: cover bidders submit bids
\emph{visibly} above the winning bid because the cartel's purpose
is to manufacture the appearance of competition, not to compete
credibly (the \emph{textbook} reading, R1). A more recent strand
reads it tighter: cover bids must remain plausible enough that
the procurement officer does not flag the bid distribution as
anomalous, and the cover bidder cycles through cover roles
across tenders generating cross-bid dispersion within firm
(the \emph{credible-cover-bidding} reading, R2;
\citealt{marshall2012economics,asker2010leniency}). The framework
above is consistent with either reading. The within-stratum
bridge of \S\ref{sec:cade_bridge} discriminates between them:
cobidders bid systematically closer to the eventual winner than
FL non-cobidders ($d=\valTBBLMedGapD$, $p<10^{-6}$) with
elevated within-firm dispersion ($d=\valTBBLSDGapD$); a
multivariate logit holding $\log(1+\text{tenders\_count})$
constant confirms both signs at $p<10^{-3}$. The directions are
opposite to R1 and consistent with R2; we adopt R2 as the
operational reading. The framework's predictions about firm-level
participation intensity (Proposition~\ref{prop:exposure_stmt}),
deployment proximity to direct CADE defendants, and portfolio
specialization are unaffected by the R1/R2 distinction; only the
bid-level reading of $b_C$ is.

\section{Adversarial-Adaptation Simulation}
\label{app:adversarial}

The screen's behavior under deliberate adversarial adaptation
(\S\ref{sec:lim_construct}) is bounded but not invariant. We
examine four strategic adaptations. Each holds the screening
statistic fixed and modifies the always-loser pool to mimic
plausible evasive behavior, and we recompute the firm-level
AUC of $\log(1+\text{tenders\_count})$ against the
$\valCobidders$ CADE-cobidder labels under each
(Table~\ref{tab:adversarial_adaptation}). Two are observable
adaptations the screen absorbs cleanly. Rotation ($20\%$ of
cover bidders retire annually and are replaced by fresh CNPJs)
and occasional wins ($5\%$ of threshold-crossing firms win one
tender) leave AUC essentially unchanged: the bright-line
$\text{wins}=0$ filter eliminates the small share of
newly-winning cover bidders---a feature of the framework's
separating equilibrium (Lemma~\ref{lem:wins_zero_stmt}), not a
heuristic robustness---and rotation does not compress the
$\log(1+\text{tenders\_count})$ ranking statistic across the
always-loser pool.

\input{./output/tables/tab_adversarial_adaptation}

Two are threshold-aware adaptations that degrade discrimination.
CNPJ splitting (each frequent loser splits into two CNPJs
carrying half the participation count) degrades AUC by
$\valAdaptCNPJAbs$ percentage points; threshold-aware capping
(firms cap participation at $13$ tender-items to stay below the
cutoff) degrades AUC by $\valAdaptCapAbs$ points; the combined
attack (capping plus $20\%$ rotation) drives AUC from
$\valAUCBase$ to $\valAUCAdaptCombined$, a
$\valAdaptCombinedAbs$-point compression. These adaptations
target the binary cutoff specifically; the continuous primitive
$\log(1+\text{tenders\_count})$ that
Proposition~\ref{prop:exposure_stmt} identifies as the
sufficient ranking statistic is not directly altered, but the
binary discretization is. Threshold capping is the most exposed
margin: a cartel that knows the cutoff can instruct cover
bidders to remain below it.

The implication for the screening-vs-forensic-stage architecture
of \S\ref{sec:forensic} is asymmetric. Under combined adaptation
AUC drops to $\valAUCAdaptCombined$---still above random
matching, still informative as a screening statistic, but
substantially reduced. The forensic stage is unaffected by
threshold-aware adaptation at the screening layer:
bid-distribution moments operate on a different data envelope
and are robust to the participation-pattern engineering that
defeats the binary rule. A combined two-stage architecture in
which a refreshable screening rule feeds a forensic stage on bid
microdata is therefore more adaptation-robust than either
component alone---the empirical evidence in \S\ref{sec:forensic}
shows the same conclusion by distinct means. The screening
statistic is not adaptation-proof; practical lifetime depends on
whether the threshold can be periodically refreshed against new
data and whether the screening stage is deployed alongside the
bid-layer forensic stage where microdata are available.

\section{Staggered Designs Attempted and Rejected}
\label{app:staggered}

Two design-based strategies that would extract a causal estimate
return null at the available bandwidths
(\S\ref{sec:lim_identification}). The sharp-RDD failure (gated
by the McCrary density test) is documented in
Table~\ref{tab:mccrary} of Online Appendix~B; this appendix
reports the parallel diagnostic for the
difference-in-differences strategy exploiting
Decreto~$\valDecreto$'s $2018$ raise of the convite cap. The
\citet{sun2021estimating} interaction-weighted estimator yields
imprecise coefficients with non-monotone dynamic patterns
incompatible with the smooth treatment-onset narrative the cap
raise would imply (Table~\ref{tab:did_revised}); a stacked
difference-in-differences design constructed around the
cap-raise event window returns coefficients that flip sign
across stacks and lack a clean pre-period
(Table~\ref{tab:stacked_did}). We treat both estimators as null
at the available bandwidths. The interpretive consequences are
limited to identification scope: the screening statistic does
not require the cap raise to be a clean treatment, and the
broad-sample $\beta$ of \S\ref{sec:results_ols} does not depend
on the staggered design surviving. What the null does constrain
is alternative framings of the empirical object that would
require a causal identification of $\beta$; the screening framing
trades that claim for the screening-value interpretation of
$\beta$ and reports the failures here for full disclosure.
