\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

A simple participation anomaly---never winning, yet bidding in far too
many tenders---turns out to be a reliable marker of suspicious
procurement environments. In 4.5 million tender-items from Brazil's
BEC platform, FL presence is associated with a 3.6--7.7\% conditional
price gap, and FL firms appear alongside convicted cartelists at
3.5$\times$ the baseline rate. Multiple mechanism diagnostics point
toward cover bidding, though none clinches the case on its own.

\paragraph{What the estimates do and do not show.}
The regressions capture price gaps within item--year--PBU cells, not
causal effects. Unobserved market characteristics remain a live
possibility, bounded but not eliminated by sensitivity analysis
($RV = 17.5\%$). The cross-fit (3.6\%) removes the mechanical link
between classification and outcome; even so, it rests on selection on
observables. The staggered DiD is underpowered, FL classification
inevitably involves misclassification, and cartels that rotate cover
bidders would slip through.

The network split complicates the picture: the entire price effect
comes from competitive markets (0.126, $p < 0.01$); concentrated
markets show nothing ($-0.018$, $p > 0.3$). Whether this means
concentrated-market cartels do not need cover bidders, or that the FL
label is noisier there, we cannot tell. For practical screening, FL
flags are most useful where competition is thickest---which is where
detection matters most.

\paragraph{Magnitudes.}
The 3.6--7.7\% range sits at the low end of the overcharge
distribution in \citet{connor2007price} (median 23\%). The FL
coefficient captures the price gap associated with FL presence, not
the full overcharge. Illustrative welfare calculations assuming full
causality---which the paper does not establish---are in
Appendix~\ref{app:extensions}.

\paragraph{Policy.}
As a diagnostic tool, FL screening runs on participation data, flags
firms for investigation, and slots into a two-stage workflow alongside
bid-level tools. A flag is a starting point, not a verdict. Leniency
and FL screening work different margins: leniency targets insiders
willing to defect; FL flags read the external footprint. In
jurisdictions with low leniency uptake, proactive screening may be
the only detection channel available.

\paragraph{Next steps.}
External replication beyond BEC is the most pressing need---other
states, federal procurement, other countries. Deeper network analysis
and pilot implementations by procurement agencies are natural
extensions. Whether frequent losers are unlucky competitors or cartel
operatives, their presence marks markets that deserve a closer look.
