\section{Conceptual Framework}
\label{sec:conceptual}

We develop a minimal model of cover bidding in procurement auctions that
generates five testable predictions. The framework deliberately avoids
full equilibrium analysis---which would require strong assumptions about
information structure and repeated-game dynamics---and instead focuses on
the observable implications of two distinct cover bidding regimes.

\subsection{Setup}

Consider a procurement auction with $n \geq 1$ genuine bidders and a
designated winner whose bid is $b^*$. A cartel controlling the winner
deploys $m \geq 0$ cover bidders (frequent losers) to inflate the
apparent number of participants. The procuring entity observes $n + m$
bids and a reference price $r$.

The cartel maximizes expected profit net of cover bidding costs:
\begin{equation}
  \max_{m} \; \pi(m, n) - C(m, \theta)
  \label{eq:cartel_foc}
\end{equation}
where $\pi(m, n)$ is the cartel's surplus (increasing in $m$ if cover
bidders help sustain supracompetitive prices), $C(\cdot)$ is the cost of
maintaining cover bidders (recruitment, coordination, risk of detection),
and $\theta \in [0,1]$ is the probability of detection. The first-order
condition implies that the optimal number of cover bidders $m^*$ is
increasing in $n$ (more genuine competitors require more cover to
maintain coordination) and decreasing in $\theta$ (higher detection
probability raises the marginal cost of cover bidding).%
\footnote{The comparative static $\partial m^* / \partial n > 0$ % REVISION-V5: note functional form dependence per referee minor 1
requires that the cross-partial $\partial^2 \pi / \partial m \, \partial n > 0$,
i.e., that the marginal return to cover bidding is increasing in the
number of genuine competitors. We maintain this as an assumption rather
than deriving it from a full equilibrium model.}

\subsection{Two Regimes of Cover Bidding}

We define two regimes that differ in how cover bids are calibrated:

\begin{definition}[Regime~1: Complementary cover bidding]
\label{def:regime1}
Cover bids are drawn independently from $U[\bar{b}, \bar{b} + \delta]$
where $\bar{b} > b^*$ and $\delta > 0$. Cover bidders submit bids
\emph{above} the winning bid without precise coordination on bid levels.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Regime~2: Coordinated cover bidding]
\label{def:regime2}
Cover bids are drawn from $N(\mu_c, \sigma_c^2)$ with $\mu_c$ close to
$b^*$ and $\sigma_c < \sigma_{\text{genuine}}$. Cover bidders calibrate
bids near the winning bid to make the auction appear competitive.
\end{definition}

Regime~1 is the simplest form of cover bidding: the cartel instructs
cover bidders to ``bid high'' without specifying exact amounts. This
requires minimal coordination and is consistent with weak oversight
environments where even crude cover bids are unlikely to be detected.
Regime~2 requires tighter coordination (cover bidders must know
approximately what the winner will bid) and produces bids that are
harder to distinguish from genuine competition---but also harder to
organize.

\subsection{Testable Predictions}

\begin{prediction}[Price effect]
\label{pred:price}
Tenders with FL presence have higher winning prices than comparable
tenders without FL, controlling for item type, year, and purchasing
unit characteristics.
\end{prediction}

\begin{prediction}[Strategic selection]
\label{pred:selection}
FL-present tenders have more total participants but at least as many
genuine (non-FL) competitors as FL-absent tenders. This reflects
strategic deployment: cartels add cover bidders on top of normal
competition rather than displacing genuine competitors.
\end{prediction}

\begin{prediction}[Regime identification]
\label{pred:regime}
Under Regime~1, FL bid dispersion exceeds genuine bid dispersion (cover
bids drawn from a wide uniform distribution). Under Regime~2, FL bid
dispersion is lower than genuine bid dispersion (tightly calibrated
cover bids).
\end{prediction}

\begin{prediction}[Exchangeability violation]
\label{pred:exchange}
FL bid residuals violate exchangeability with non-FL bid residuals. Under
both regimes, FL bids are generated by a different process than genuine
bids, producing distinguishable residual distributions. Under Regime~1,
FL residuals are shifted upward; under Regime~2, FL residuals have
lower variance.
\end{prediction}

\begin{prediction}[Conditional dependence]
\label{pred:independence}
FL bid residuals within the same tender exhibit positive correlation
(conditional dependence), with magnitude larger than for non-FL
residuals. Cover bids, whether complementary or coordinated, share a
common signal (the designated winning bid) that induces positive
covariance in their residuals.
\end{prediction}

\subsection{Comparative Statics}

Two institutional features of Brazilian procurement shape the expected
magnitude of cover bidding effects.

First, the \emph{convite} (sealed-bid) modality requires a minimum of
three bidders (Lei 8.666/93, Art.~22, \S7), creating a direct incentive
for cover bidding when fewer than three genuine competitors exist. The
\emph{preg\~ao} (electronic auction) modality has no such minimum but
allows real-time bid observation, which facilitates Regime~2 calibration.

Second, the detection probability $\theta$ varies across purchasing
units. Larger PBUs with dedicated procurement staff likely have higher
$\theta$, implying smaller $m^*$ and a weaker FL-price association.
Smaller PBUs with limited analytical capacity face lower $\theta$,
enabling more extensive cover bidding. We test this prediction through
oversight heterogeneity analysis (Section~\ref{sec:robustness}).
