\subsection{Structural Estimation Results} % REVISION-V6: M2 — new subsection
\label{sec:results_structural}

Table~\ref{tab:structural_params} reports the structural parameter
estimates from the two-stage mixture model described in
Section~\ref{sec:structural_estimation}.

\paragraph{Genuine bid distribution (Panel~A).}
The genuine bid distribution, estimated from non-FL losing bids with
item and year fixed effects, yields a residual standard deviation
$\hat{\sigma}_g$ that captures within-item-year bid heterogeneity
among genuine competitors. The first-stage $R^2$ indicates that item
and year fixed effects absorb a substantial share of bid-level
variation, with residual dispersion reflecting firm-level cost
heterogeneity.

\paragraph{Regime selection (Panels B--C).} % REVISION-V6: updated to match estimation results
The BIC selects Regime~2 (coordinated cover bidding) over Regime~1
(complementary cover bidding), with a $\Delta$BIC of $-91{,}473$.
Under Regime~2, FL bids are drawn from a log-normal distribution
centered at $\hat{\epsilon} = 0.83$ log points above the winning bid,
with standard deviation $\hat{\sigma}_c = 1.19$ (bootstrap
SE $= 0.012$). The Kolmogorov--Smirnov goodness-of-fit test yields a
KS distance of 0.24 under Regime~2 versus 0.64 under Regime~1,
confirming that the parametric normal model provides a substantially
better fit to the empirical FL bid distribution.
The selection of Regime~2 is consistent with coordinated cover bidding:
$\hat{\sigma}_c = 1.19 < \hat{\sigma}_g = 1.64$, indicating that
FL bid spreads are \emph{less} dispersed than genuine bid spreads
conditional on the winning price---the signature of cover bidders
calibrating bids near a focal point rather than submitting loosely
coordinated bids above the winner.

\paragraph{Implied markup and structural consistency (Panel~E).} % V7: structurally honest framing
The distributional parameters in Panels~A--D characterize the
cover-bid \emph{mechanism}---how cover bids relate to the winning
price---but do not independently identify the cartel
\emph{markup}.\footnote{Under the model, cover bids satisfy
$b_\ell > b^*$ with probability one (Equation~\ref{eq:cover_above}),
so the cover-bid distribution parameters $(\hat{\epsilon},
\hat{\sigma}_c)$ describe bids \emph{above} the winning price and
do not mechanically affect the winning price itself. The markup
arises from the cartel's strategic price-setting decision---choosing
$b^*$ above the competitive level---which is identified from the
reduced-form comparison of FL-present and FL-absent tenders, not
from the cover-bid distribution alone.}
The markup is instead a reduced-form quantity that the structural
model \emph{disciplines}: the model specifies that FL presence should
be associated with higher prices
(Prediction~\ref{pred:price_v6}) through strategic deployment in
competitive markets (Prediction~\ref{pred:market_v6}), and the
distributional parameters provide the diagnostic evidence that FL
bids are structurally distinct from genuine bids
(Prediction~\ref{pred:exchange_v6}).

To estimate the markup, we regress log negotiated prices on FL
presence controlling for the number of genuine bidders
$n_{\text{genuine}}$ (saturated dummies), item, year, and PBU fixed
effects. The $n$-conditional specification is motivated by
Proposition~\ref{prop:optimal_m}: since $m^*$ depends on $n$,
comparing FL-present and FL-absent tenders \emph{at the same level of
genuine competition} isolates the cartel's price-setting from the
strategic-selection channel. The estimated markup is $\hat{\mu} =
0.062$ (SE~$= 0.019$), or 6.4\%, virtually identical to the baseline
OLS (0.064), confirming that the FL--price association is not driven
by differential genuine competition in FL-present tenders.

Panel~E reports the convergence of four independent markup estimates:
the $n$-conditional structural estimate (6.4\%), the baseline OLS
(6.6\%), the cross-fit average (3.6\%, a conservative lower bound
after breaking the FL-classification--outcome link), and the IV upper
bound (21.4\%). The structural parameters provide the economic
interpretation: the cover-bid spread $\hat{\epsilon} = 0.83$
(130\% above the winner) indicates substantial bid manipulation, and
the regime selection ($\hat{\sigma}_c / \hat{\sigma}_g = 0.72$)
confirms coordinated calibration near a focal point. Together, the
distributional evidence and the reduced-form markup form a coherent
picture of cover bidding with a moderate price effect.

\subsection{Detection Performance} % REVISION-V6: M2 — new subsection
\label{sec:results_detection}

Figure~\ref{fig:roc} presents the ROC curve for the FL detection screen,
evaluated against CADE cartel co-participation as ground truth.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.85\textwidth]{images/fig_roc_fl_screen.pdf}
\caption{ROC curve for the FL detection screen. Ground truth:
co-participation with CADE-convicted cartelists. Each point represents a
different IQR multiplier threshold. The baseline threshold
(1.5$\times$IQR) and the optimal threshold (Youden's $J$) are marked.}
\label{fig:roc}
\end{figure}

The FL screen achieves an AUC of 0.94, indicating strong discrimination
between FL firms that co-participate with convicted cartelists and those
that do not. The data-driven optimal threshold (Youden's $J = 0.84$)
corresponds to a multiplier of 1.45$\times$IQR---nearly identical to
the 1.5$\times$ rule used for FL classification
(Section~\ref{sec:fl_definition}). At the baseline threshold, the screen
achieves a true positive rate of 1.00 (all CADE-overlap firms are
captured) with a false positive rate of 0.16.

\input{tables/tab_roc_detection}

The high AUC demonstrates that the FL screen's classification boundary
is well-calibrated: firms above the threshold are substantially more
likely to be associated with known cartel activity than firms below it.
The near-equivalence of the data-driven optimal and the IQR-based
threshold provides ex post validation of the statistical classification
rule.

\paragraph{Comparison with Imhof-style screens.} % V7: Imhof comparison implemented
Table~\ref{tab:imhof_comparison} compares the FL screen with
\citet{imhof2019detecting}-style bid-level screens on the same
ground truth (CADE co-participation among 16,692 always-losers with
valid bid-level data). For each always-loser firm, we compute the
mean within-tender coefficient of variation (CV), excess kurtosis,
skewness, and normalized bid spread across all tenders in which the
firm participates, then form a composite Imhof score as the
standardized average of these four features.

The FL screen (AUC~$= 0.94$) substantially outperforms the Imhof
composite (AUC~$= 0.79$; DeLong $p < 0.001$) and each individual
Imhof feature (CV: $0.76$; spread: $0.79$).
Figure~\ref{fig:roc_comparison} overlays the three ROC curves. The
FL screen's superior performance is consistent with its design: it
exploits the \emph{participation} anomaly (never winning yet
participating excessively) rather than within-tender bid patterns,
capturing a different dimension of cover-bidding behavior.

Importantly, the FL screen requires \emph{only participation and
outcome data}---not bid values---making it deployable in procurement
systems where bid-level data are unavailable. The Imhof screen, by
contrast, requires granular bid values for each tender. The two
approaches are thus complements for agencies with full data access and
the FL screen is the only feasible option for agencies with limited
data infrastructure.

\input{tables/tab_imhof_comparison}

\begin{figure}[htbp]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.78\textwidth]{images/fig_roc_comparison.pdf}
\caption{ROC curves: FL screen vs.\ Imhof-style bid-level screens.
Ground truth: co-participation with CADE-convicted cartelists among
always-loser firms. The FL screen (AUC~$= 0.94$) dominates the
Imhof composite (AUC~$= 0.79$; DeLong $p < 0.001$).}
\label{fig:roc_comparison}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{Caveats.}
Two caveats apply. First, the ground truth (CADE co-participation)
is an imperfect proxy: if cover bidders strategically avoid
co-bidding with cartel principals, the AUC may overstate detection
power. Second, precision at the baseline threshold is low (3.6\%):
the FL screen is a high-recall, low-precision triage tool suitable
for prioritizing investigation, not standalone enforcement.

\subsection{OLS and IV Results}
\label{sec:results_ols}

Table~\ref{tab:prices} reports the baseline OLS estimates of
Equation~\eqref{eq:ols_main} for log negotiated prices
(Prediction~\ref{pred:price_v6}). Across all four
specifications, FL presence is associated with significantly higher
prices: 6.8\% in the general specification (column~1), 6.4\% with PBU
fixed effects (column~2), 9.3\% for preg\~ao (column~3), and 3.8\% for
convite (column~4). The inclusion of PBU fixed effects in column~2
absorbs purchasing-unit-specific price levels yet barely changes the
coefficient, suggesting that FL presence does not simply proxy for
high-cost PBUs.

\input{./output/tables/tab_prices}

Table~\ref{tab:nfirms_excl} examines genuine competition
(Prediction~\ref{pred:selection_v6}): FL-present
tenders have 0.19 log points more non-FL firms (PBU FE specification),
contradicting the hypothesis that FL firms crowd out genuine competitors.
This positive coefficient is consistent with \emph{strategic selection}:
cartels deploy cover bidders in tenders that already attract genuine
competition.

\input{./output/tables/tab_nfirms_excl}

\paragraph{Instrumental variable.}
The preferred IV price estimate (Panel~A: 0.194, $p = 0.01$) exceeds
the OLS estimate (0.064) by a factor of three, consistent with
measurement-error attenuation in the binary FL indicator. We treat the
IV as a bracketing device (Section~\ref{sec:reduced_form}): the
structural, OLS, and IV estimates provide three independent assessments
of the FL--price association under different identification assumptions.

\input{./output/tables/tab_iv_main}

\paragraph{LATE interpretation.}
The IV estimate of 21\% ($F = 396$) identifies a LATE for complier
tenders; exclusion restriction concerns are discussed in
Section~\ref{sec:reduced_form}. We treat the IV as supplementary
evidence that brackets the OLS estimate from above.

\subsection{Network-Split Results}
\label{sec:results_network}

Table~\ref{tab:network_split} splits FL firms into
concentrated-market and competitive-market subgroups based on the
co-bidding network metrics described in
Section~\ref{sec:empirical_network}, directly testing
Proposition~\ref{prop:market_selection} (cover bidding concentrates in
competitive markets).\footnote{We label these groups by
market structure rather than cover-bidding likelihood: high-HHI markets
with repeated winners may reflect natural monopoly or structural
concentration rather than cartel behavior, precisely the environments
where cover bidding yields less competitive benefit.
Markets above the HHI sample median are classified as concentrated;
the median is used to avoid ad hoc threshold selection while maintaining
sample balance. Firms appearing together in at least two distinct
tenders are classified as having established repeat relationships;
this threshold minimizes classification error for rare pairings while
capturing sustained co-participation.}
The results reveal an informative pattern of heterogeneity.
Competitive-market FL firms---those operating in markets with
\emph{diversified} winner patterns (low winner HHI)---drive the entire
price effect, with a coefficient of 0.126 ($p < 0.01$) in the PBU FE
specification. Concentrated-market FL firms, operating in markets with
repeated winners, show no significant price effect ($-0.018$, $p > 0.3$).

This finding directly confirms Proposition~\ref{prop:market_selection}:
the structural model predicts that cover bidding is most profitable in
competitive markets where the marginal return to manufactured
competition is highest, and absent in concentrated markets where the
cartel can sustain prices through market power alone.

% ── 7.5 Bajari-Ye Tests ──────────────────────────────────────────

\subsection{Bajari--Ye Tests}
\label{sec:results_bajari}

We apply the \citet{bajari2003deciding} framework to test
Prediction~\ref{pred:exchange_v6}: FL bid residuals violate
exchangeability with non-FL bid residuals.
Table~\ref{tab:bajari_ye} reports the results using the corrected
first stage (Section~\ref{sec:bajari_ye}), which excludes the
endogenous number-of-bids variable.

\input{./output/tables/tab_bajari_ye}

\paragraph{Exchangeability (Panel~A).}
The Kolmogorov--Smirnov test rejects the null that FL and
non-FL bid residuals are drawn from the same distribution
($D = 0.15$, $p < 0.001$), confirming
Prediction~\ref{pred:exchange_v6}. This is the structural model's
key diagnostic: if FL bids are drawn from $G_{\text{cover}}$ rather
than $F_{\text{genuine}}$, the residuals from a genuine-bid first
stage should be non-exchangeable across groups.

\paragraph{Conditional independence (Panel~B).}
The mean product of pairwise FL residuals is 4.28 ($t = 81.0$), % R1-FIX: updated to v6 enriched first-stage results
indicating systematic co-movement among FL bids within the same
tender.\footnote{The v6 enriched first stage includes firm age and
CNAE sector dummies in addition to firm size, which absorbs more
cost-shifter variation and slightly reduces the pairwise product
relative to the specification without these covariates.}
The bootstrap difference between FL and non-FL products excludes zero
(95\% CI does not contain zero), indicating that FL bid residuals
exhibit systematically different within-tender correlation than non-FL
residuals, consistent with distinct bidding processes.

\paragraph{Tender FE.} % R1-FIX: interpretation consistent with Regime 2 (BIC-selected)
Under tender FE (Table~\ref{tab:bajari_ye_tender_fe} in
Appendix~\ref{app:robustness}), the FL pairwise product drops to
0.38 while the non-FL product drops to 0.86. The reversal---FL pairs
showing \emph{lower} within-tender covariance than non-FL pairs after
absorbing tender means---is consistent with Regime~2 (coordinated
cover bidding): cover bids calibrated near a common focal point
($b^* + \epsilon$) have low idiosyncratic variance \emph{conditional
on the tender mean}, because the focal-point component is absorbed by
the tender FE. Genuine bidders, by contrast, draw costs from
correlated input-market distributions that retain within-pair
covariance even after demeaning. The tender-FE specification is thus
an over-controlled test that removes the very within-tender structure
the baseline specification detects; we report it for completeness
but interpret the baseline and tender-FE results jointly.

\subsection{Regime Test}
\label{sec:results_regime}

The structural estimation's BIC comparison (Table~\ref{tab:structural_params}, % REVISION-V6: updated to match estimation results
Panel~D) selects Regime~2 (coordinated cover bidding) over Regime~1,
with $\Delta\text{BIC} = -91{,}473$ strongly favoring the log-normal
cover-bid specification. The estimated cover-bid standard deviation
$\hat{\sigma}_c = 1.19$ is smaller than the genuine-bid standard
deviation $\hat{\sigma}_g = 1.64$ ($\hat{\sigma}_c / \hat{\sigma}_g =
0.72$), consistent with Regime~2's prediction that coordinated cover
bidders submit bids with less dispersion than genuine competitors.
The median FL bid log-spread above the winning price is 0.50,
with the distribution tightly centered relative to the Regime~1
uniform specification.
