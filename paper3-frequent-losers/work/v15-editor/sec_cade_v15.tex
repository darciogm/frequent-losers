\section{Validation Against CADE Adjudications}
\label{sec:cade}

We validate the construct against CADE's procurement-cartel
adjudication portfolio (§\ref{sec:institutional},
§\ref{sec:data_cade}). Three independent pieces of evidence
support a market-level triage reading: a market-proximity test
on the full $\valFL$-firm flagged population
(§\ref{sec:cade_proximity}); a within-firm validation
identifying $3$ named firms flagged by the construct before
their CADE adjudications (§\ref{sec:cade_within_firm}); and a
bounds analysis distinguishing contemporaneous from prospective
discrimination (§\ref{sec:cade_bounds}). A final robustness
check confirms that the headline price gap does not depend on
the enforcement record (§\ref{sec:cade_robustness}).

% ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\subsection{Market-Proximity Test}
\label{sec:cade_proximity}
% ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Among $\valFL$ frequent-loser firms, $\valCobidders$
($\valCobidShareFL$) co-participate with at least one of the
$\valDirectCADE$ BEC-active CADE-defendant firms in the same
tender-item. A permutation test ($\valPermPilot$ iterations,
stratified by participation-count quartile to absorb mechanical
exposure through high participation volume) yields a permutation
mean of $\valCADEbase$ ($\text{SD} = 0.3\%$); the observed
$\valCobidShareFL$ is $\valMultThreeFive\!\!\times$ the permuted
mean and rejects the null of random co-participation at
$p < 0.001$. The participation-stratification is the
identifying move: it removes the alternative reading that the
overlap is mechanical exposure through high tender-count, and
isolates the residual concentration of co-participation among
flagged firms relative to non-flagged always-losers with
comparable participation profiles.

The test measures \emph{market proximity}, not cartel membership
firm by firm. A frequent-loser firm that co-participates with a
CADE defendant may be a cover bidder deployed by the cartel, an
unwitting competitor in a cartel-affected market, or a
bystander; the construct does not adjudicate among these
readings at the firm level (§\ref{sec:lim_construct}). What
the participation-stratified excess does establish is that the
construct's flag concentrates the cobidder population around
adjudicated cartels at a rate
$\valMultThreeFive\!\!\times$ the rate produced by random
matching at comparable participation volume.

% ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\subsection{Within-Firm Validation}
\label{sec:cade_within_firm}
% ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

A more demanding test asks how many of the $\valDirectCADE$
BEC-active CADE defendants the construct itself flags. Direct
defendants in adjudicated cases are typically frequent winners
(§\ref{sec:lim_construct}), and only $7$ of the $\valDirectCADE$
are always-losers in BEC. Of those $7$, $3$ ($\valPctFortythree$)
cross the frequent-loser threshold, against a population baseline
of $\valPctSixteenRef$ ($\valFL$/$\valAlwaysLosers$)---a
$2.6\!\!\times$ within-firm enrichment. The three flagged firms
are listed in Table~\ref{tab:cade_fl_firms}: \emph{Sol
Tecnologia} (CADE process $08012.001273/2010$-$24$, solar water
heaters; $84$ tenders, zero wins), \emph{Nova Esperan\c{c}a
Locadora} ($08700.005876/2019$-$85$, school transportation; $97$
tenders, zero wins), and \emph{Jofran Com\'ercio}
($08700.005789/2015$-$02$, trash bags, Operation Colludium;
$65$ tenders, zero wins). All three exhibit the defining
participation footprint---sustained participation across item
groups and years with no wins, identified by the construct on
$\valYearStart$--$\valYearEnd$ data alone.

Eight of the twelve cases were adjudicated after the
$\valYearEnd$ sample window closes
(§\ref{sec:institutional}), so the within-firm enrichment is
genuinely prospective: the construct flagged firms whose
collusive role enforcement confirmed years later, on
participation patterns observable years earlier.

% ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\subsection{Bounds: Contemporaneous vs Prospective Discrimination}
\label{sec:cade_bounds}
% ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

The full-portfolio results---$\valMultThreeFive\!\!\times$ market
proximity, $2.6\!\!\times$ within-firm enrichment, firm-level
AUC $\valAUCFLfirm$ ($\valAUCFLfirmCI$) against the cobidder
population---are the prospective reading: the ground truth
incorporates eight cases adjudicated after the construct was
estimated on $\valYearStart$--$\valYearEnd$ data. A stricter
reading restricts the ground truth to the four cases adjudicated
before $2020$ (coleta de lixo, aquecedores solares,
trens-metr\^os, and the $2019$-$10$ tecnologia-informa\c{c}\~ao
adjudication), covering $30$ distinct firm-defendant CNPJs, all
$30$ matching BEC participants. Always-losers co-bidding with at
least one of these $30$ firms in any tender-item number $210$,
of which $108$ are frequent losers. Under this construction, the
permutation-stratified co-bidding rate is $\valCADEenrich$ versus
$\valCADEbase$ at the participation-quartile-stratified baseline
($p < 0.001$, $\valPermPilot$ iterations), an excess ratio of
$\valMultThreeTwo\!\!\times$. The ROC AUC against the
$210$-positive contemporaneous ground truth is $\valAUCprePost$
($95\%$ DeLong CI $[0.713, 0.783]$).

The two readings bound the discriminating performance from
opposite sides. The contemporaneous reading (AUC
$\valAUCprePost$, $\valMultThreeTwo\!\!\times$ excess) is the
\emph{lower bound} under strict pre-registration of ground
truth: the construct uses no information from cases adjudicated
post-sample. The prospective reading (firm-level AUC
$\valAUCFLfirm$, $\valMultThreeFive\!\!\times$ excess) is the
\emph{upper bound} under the assumption that subsequent
enforcement decisions provide informative labels for cartel
involvement that was operationally active during the sample
window. Neither bound is the wrong number; they are answers to
different questions, and a referee preferring the contemporaneous
bound is right to do so. We adopt the pooled prospective reading
as the headline because it is the deployable projection an
oversight body would compute on award-record data alone, and the
contemporaneous AUC of $\valAUCprePost$ as the conservative
discriminating reference.

% ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\subsection{Robustness to the Enforcement Record}
\label{sec:cade_robustness}
% ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

A natural concern is that the headline price gap is driven
entirely by tender-items in which CADE eventually identified
cartel activity. If true, the construct would contribute no
information beyond the enforcement record itself. The data
reject this reading. Dropping all $\valCADEpermPerm$
CADE-involved tender-items and re-estimating the within-PBU
baseline (Equation~\eqref{eq:ols_main}) yields $\hat\beta = 0.062$
($N = \valCADEpermObs{,}954$, Table~\ref{tab:excl_cade}),
virtually unchanged from the full-sample $\valPctSixfour$. The
construct's price imprint operates independently of the
enforcement record. Either the residual sample contains
undetected cartels that the construct identifies before
enforcement does, or frequent-loser presence correlates with
high-price environments through channels orthogonal to
documented collusion. Both readings are consistent with the
diagnostic-screen claim of the paper, and the operational
pathway in §\ref{sec:conclusion} requires only the first.

The conjunction of the four pieces---the
$\valMultThreeFive\!\!\times$ market-proximity excess, the
$2.6\!\!\times$ within-firm enrichment with three named firms
flagged before their adjudications, the bounded $0.748$--$0.924$
discriminating range, and the price-gap insensitivity to the
enforcement record---supports a market-level triage
interpretation. The construct prioritizes procurement
environments connected to adjudicated collusion at rates well
above random matching at comparable participation volume; it
does not validate firm-level cartel guilt, and the
$\valDirectCADE$ direct-defendant AUC of $\valAUCdirectCADE$
(§\ref{sec:lim_construct}) places a hard upper bound on
broader detection claims outside the always-loser stratum.
