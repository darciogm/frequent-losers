\section{Validation Against CADE Adjudications}
\label{sec:cade}

The validation asks whether the construct discriminates
populations with a higher prior probability of cartel adjacency
than random selection. The labels are indirect by construction
and the exercise does not speak to firm-level membership.

\subsection{Conservative benchmark}
\label{sec:cade_contemporaneous}

The primary benchmark restricts the ground truth to four CADE
cases adjudicated before $2020$ (coleta de lixo, aquecedores
solares, trens-metr\^os, tecnologia-informa\c{c}\~ao
$2019$-$10$): $30$ firm-defendants, all active in BEC, with $210$
always-losers co-bidding with at least one of them, of which
$108$ are frequent losers. The participation-stratified co-bidding
rate is $\valCADEenrich$ against a baseline of $\valCADEbase$
($p<0.001$, $\valPermPilot$ permutations), an excess ratio of
$\valMultThreeTwo\!\!\times$. The ROC AUC against the
contemporaneous ground truth is $\valAUCprePost$ ($95\%$ DeLong
CI $[0.713, 0.783]$).

We treat this as the primary benchmark because the construct
uses no information from cases adjudicated post-sample and the
labels predate estimation: existence and membership were
established by enforcement before the construct was built.
Within the always-loser stratum, frequent losers co-occur with
adjudicated cartel members at rates above random matching at
comparable participation volume.

\subsection{Prospective benchmark and within-firm enrichment}
\label{sec:cade_prospective}

A more permissive benchmark uses CADE's full procurement-cartel
portfolio ($12$ cases, $65$ firm-defendants, of which
$\valDirectCADE$ are active in BEC). Among $\valFL$ frequent
losers, $\valCobidders$ ($\valCobidShareFL$) co-participate with
at least one of the $\valDirectCADE$; the participation-stratified
excess ratio is $\valMultThreeFive\!\!\times$ ($p<0.001$).
Firm-level AUC is $\valAUCFLfirm$ ($95\%$ CI $\valAUCFLfirmCI$)
in-sample and $\valAUCFLfirmTemp$ ($\valAUCFLfirmTempCI$) under
temporal holdout (train $2009$--$2016$, test $2017$--$2019$).
The benchmark treats post-sample adjudications as informative
labels for cartel involvement during the BEC window, and we
report it as complementary to the conservative anchor.

A within-firm exercise on the same portfolio: $3$ of $7$
always-loser direct defendants ($\valPctFortythree$) cross the
frequent-loser threshold against a population baseline of
$\valPctSixteenRef$. Table~\ref{tab:cade_fl_firms} lists them.

\subsection{Robustness to the enforcement record}
\label{sec:cade_robustness}

Dropping all $\valCADEpermPerm$ CADE-involved tender-items and
re-estimating the within-PBU baseline yields $\hat\beta = 0.062$
($N = \valCADEpermObs{,}954$), virtually unchanged from
$\valPctSixfour$. The price imprint operates independently of
the enforcement record. The residual sample either contains
undetected cartels or higher-price environments correlated with
frequent-loser presence through channels orthogonal to
documented collusion; both readings are consistent with the
triage interpretation.

\subsection{What the validation does and does not show}
\label{sec:cade_scope}

The validation rests on four pieces of evidence: a
$\valMultThreeTwo\!\!\times$ contemporaneous excess at AUC
$\valAUCprePost$, a $\valMultThreeFive\!\!\times$ prospective
excess at AUC $\valAUCFLfirm$ ($\valAUCFLfirmTemp$ under temporal
holdout), price-gap insensitivity to the enforcement record, and
AUC $\valAUCdirectStd$ against the $\valDirectCADE$ direct CADE
defendants in the broader BEC firm universe. The construct
prioritizes procurement environments connected to adjudicated
collusion at rates well above random matching at comparable
participation volume; it does not identify cartels, and the
direct-defendant AUC delimits its scope.
