\section{Validation Against CADE Adjudications}
\label{sec:cade}

The validation exercise speaks to one question: does loser-side
participation, defined on award records alone, discriminate
populations of firms with a higher prior probability of
cartel adjacency than random selection? It does not speak to
firm-level cartel membership, and the labels we use are indirect by
construction. We organize the section around object discipline:
which population is being labeled, what label is being used, and
which scope is being measured.

\subsection{Population nesting and validation objects}
\label{sec:cade_objects}

The validation operates on five nested or overlapping populations
documented in Table~\ref{tab:cade_populations}. The construct
targets the loser side; the validation labels are by-products of
how CADE adjudications match BEC participation.

The validation object is therefore cartel adjacency, not legal
proof of collusion. The screen is designed for triage, not for
adjudication. We report three benchmarks below, ordered from
conservative to prospective, and state plainly what each
benchmark does and does not establish.

\subsection{Conservative benchmark: contemporaneous validation}
\label{sec:cade_contemporaneous}

The most conservative benchmark restricts the ground truth to four
CADE cases adjudicated before $2020$ (coleta de lixo, aquecedores
solares, trens-metr\^os, and the $2019$-$10$ tecnologia-informa\c{c}\~ao
adjudication), covering $30$ distinct firm-defendant CNPJs, all
$30$ matching BEC participants. Always-losers co-bidding with at
least one of these $30$ firms in any tender-item number $210$, of
which $108$ are frequent losers. Under this construction, the
permutation-stratified co-bidding rate is $\valCADEenrich$ versus
$\valCADEbase$ at the participation-quartile-stratified baseline
($p<0.001$, $\valPermPilot$ iterations), an excess ratio of
$\valMultThreeTwo\!\!\times$. The ROC AUC against the
$210$-positive contemporaneous ground truth is $\valAUCprePost$
($95\%$ DeLong CI $[0.713, 0.783]$).

This is the validation we treat as primary. The construct uses no
information from cases adjudicated post-sample, and the cobidder
labels are constructed from cartels whose existence and membership
were established by enforcement before the construct was estimated.
The reading is: within the always-loser stratum, frequent losers
co-occur with adjudicated cartel members at rates above what random
matching at comparable participation volume would produce, and the
construct's ranking statistic discriminates these cobidders at
AUC $\valAUCprePost$. We do not claim that this number reflects
cartel-membership detection: cobidders include cover bidders,
unwitting competitors in cartel-affected markets, and bystanders.
We claim that environments containing frequent losers are
\emph{adjacent} to documented collusion at rates above random.

\subsection{Prospective benchmark and within-firm enrichment}
\label{sec:cade_prospective}

A more permissive benchmark uses CADE's full procurement-cartel
portfolio relevant to the sample, including eight cases adjudicated
after $\valYearEnd$. The portfolio comprises $12$ adjudicated cases
covering $65$ firm-defendants in total, of which $\valDirectCADE$
are active in BEC. Among $\valFL$ frequent-loser firms,
$\valCobidders$ ($\valCobidShareFL$) co-participate with at least
one of the $\valDirectCADE$ BEC-active CADE-defendant firms in the
same tender-item; the participation-stratified excess ratio is
$\valMultThreeFive\!\!\times$ ($p<0.001$). The firm-level AUC
against these $\valCobidders$ cobidders is $\valAUCFLfirm$
($95\%$ CI $\valAUCFLfirmCI$) in-sample and $0.864$ ($[0.859,0.870]$)
under temporal holdout (train $2009$--$2016$, test $2017$--$2019$).

The prospective benchmark is more assumption-dependent than the
contemporaneous one. It treats post-sample adjudications as
informative labels of cartel involvement that was operationally
active during the BEC window---a reasonable but not innocuous
assumption. We report it as a complementary reading, not as the
headline number.

A within-firm enrichment exercise on the same portfolio shows that
$3$ of $7$ always-loser direct defendants ($\valPctFortythree$)
cross the frequent-loser threshold, against a population baseline
of $\valPctSixteenRef$---a $2.6\!\!\times$ enrichment.
Table~\ref{tab:cade_fl_firms} lists the three firms.

These three firms are direct defendants who happen also to be
always-losers, so the construct flags them. They are the exception
within the direct-defendant population, where most firms are
frequent winners and lie outside the construct's target. We
report this exercise to make the point that the construct is not
indifferent to direct adjudications when the direct defendant
shares the always-loser footprint, but the population AUC against
direct defendants in the broader BEC firm universe remains
$\valAUCdirectStd$ by construction.

\subsection{Robustness to the enforcement record}
\label{sec:cade_robustness}

Dropping all $\valCADEpermPerm$ CADE-involved tender-items and
re-estimating the within-PBU baseline yields $\hat\beta = 0.062$
($N = \valCADEpermObs{,}954$), virtually unchanged from the
full-sample $\valPctSixfour$. The price imprint operates
independently of the enforcement record. Two readings are
consistent with this: the residual sample contains undetected
cartels that the construct picks up before enforcement does, or
frequent-loser presence correlates with higher-price environments
through channels orthogonal to documented collusion. Both readings
are consistent with the triage interpretation; only the first is
consistent with the screen anticipating future enforcement.

\subsection{What the validation does and does not show}
\label{sec:cade_scope}

The conjunction of the four pieces---the
$\valMultThreeTwo\!\!\times$ contemporaneous excess at AUC
$\valAUCprePost$, the $\valMultThreeFive\!\!\times$ prospective
excess at AUC $\valAUCFLfirm$ ($0.864$ holdout), the $2.6\!\!\times$
within-firm enrichment, and the price-gap insensitivity to the
enforcement record---supports a triage interpretation: the
construct prioritizes procurement environments connected to
adjudicated collusion at rates well above random matching at
comparable participation volume.

What the validation does not show is equally important. The
construct does not detect cartels: against the $\valDirectCADE$
direct CADE defendants in the broader BEC firm universe, AUC is
$\valAUCdirectStd$, which places a hard upper bound on broader
detection claims outside the always-loser stratum. The cobidder
labels we use are indirect: participation alongside an adjudicated
cartel member is consistent with several non-competitive readings
(cover bidding, capacity-transfer motives, market intelligence) and
with bystander participation. Within the always-loser stratum, the
construct discriminates cobidders well; outside that stratum, it
does not. The screen is designed for triage and is validated for
triage. It is not a substitute for adjudication.
