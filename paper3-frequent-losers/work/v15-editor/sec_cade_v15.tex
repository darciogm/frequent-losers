\section{Validation Against CADE Adjudications}
\label{sec:cade}

The validation asks whether the construct discriminates
populations of firms with a higher prior probability of cartel
adjacency than random selection. It does not speak to firm-level
membership. The labels we use are indirect by construction.

\subsection{Conservative benchmark}
\label{sec:cade_contemporaneous}

The conservative benchmark restricts the ground truth to four
CADE cases adjudicated before $2020$ (coleta de lixo, aquecedores
solares, trens-metr\^os, tecnologia-informa\c{c}\~ao $2019$-$10$),
covering $30$ firm-defendants, all matching BEC. Always-losers
co-bidding with at least one number $210$, of which $108$ are
frequent losers. The participation-stratified co-bidding rate is
$\valCADEenrich$ versus $\valCADEbase$ at the
participation-quartile-stratified baseline ($p<0.001$,
$\valPermPilot$ iterations), an excess ratio of
$\valMultThreeTwo\!\!\times$. The ROC AUC against the
$210$-positive contemporaneous ground truth is $\valAUCprePost$
($95\%$ DeLong CI $[0.713, 0.783]$).

This is the benchmark we treat as primary. The construct uses no
information from cases adjudicated post-sample, and the labels
come from cartels whose existence and membership were established
by enforcement before the construct was estimated. Within the
always-loser stratum, frequent losers co-occur with adjudicated
cartel members at rates above what random matching at comparable
participation volume would produce.

\subsection{Prospective benchmark and within-firm enrichment}
\label{sec:cade_prospective}

A more permissive benchmark uses CADE's full procurement-cartel
portfolio: $12$ adjudicated cases, $65$ firm-defendants, of which
$\valDirectCADE$ are active in BEC. Among $\valFL$ frequent
losers, $\valCobidders$ ($\valCobidShareFL$) co-participate with
at least one of the $\valDirectCADE$; the participation-stratified
excess ratio is $\valMultThreeFive\!\!\times$ ($p<0.001$). The
firm-level AUC is $\valAUCFLfirm$ ($95\%$ CI $\valAUCFLfirmCI$)
in-sample and $\valAUCFLfirmTemp$ ($\valAUCFLfirmTempCI$) under temporal holdout
(train $2009$--$2016$, test $2017$--$2019$). This benchmark
treats post-sample adjudications as informative labels for cartel
involvement during the BEC window---a reasonable but not
innocuous assumption. We report it as complementary, not as the
headline.

A within-firm exercise on the same portfolio: $3$ of $7$
always-loser direct defendants ($\valPctFortythree$) cross the
frequent-loser threshold against a population baseline of
$\valPctSixteenRef$. Table~\ref{tab:cade_fl_firms} lists them.

\subsection{Robustness to the enforcement record}
\label{sec:cade_robustness}

Dropping all $\valCADEpermPerm$ CADE-involved tender-items and
re-estimating the within-PBU baseline yields $\hat\beta = 0.062$
($N = \valCADEpermObs{,}954$), virtually unchanged from
$\valPctSixfour$. The price imprint operates independently of the
enforcement record. Either the residual sample contains
undetected cartels the construct picks up before enforcement
does, or frequent-loser presence correlates with higher-price
environments through channels orthogonal to documented
collusion. Both readings are consistent with the triage
interpretation.

\subsection{What the validation does and does not show}
\label{sec:cade_scope}

Three pieces of evidence anchor the triage interpretation: a
$\valMultThreeTwo\!\!\times$ contemporaneous excess at AUC
$\valAUCprePost$, a $\valMultThreeFive\!\!\times$ prospective
excess at AUC $\valAUCFLfirm$ ($\valAUCFLfirmTemp$ holdout), and the
price-gap insensitivity to the enforcement record. The construct
prioritizes procurement environments connected to adjudicated
collusion at rates well above random matching at comparable
participation volume. It does not detect cartels: against the
$\valDirectCADE$ direct defendants in the broader BEC universe
the AUC is $\valAUCdirectStd$, which places a hard upper bound on
broader detection claims outside the always-loser stratum.
