\section{Related Literature}
\label{sec:literature}

The paper sits at the conjunction of four literatures, organized
by the data layer at which each operates. Bid-coordination tests
read collusion off bid-function exchangeability conditional on a
researcher-supplied partition between colluders and a competitive
fringe. Bid-distribution screens read collusion off
within-tender moments of submitted bids. Cover-bidding studies
identify the loser side of collusive arrangements
retrospectively, after enforcement has produced the partition.
Work on enforcement design under incomplete information asks how
detection regimes should be structured when monitoring is
imperfect. The construct's positioning is determined by the
informational layer at which each literature operates: the first
two require bid-level observability that is often absent; the
third requires the post-adjudication record; the fourth motivates
the architectural separation between screening and forensic
stages that the empirical exercise instantiates.

\paragraph{Bid-coordination tests presuppose a partition}
\citet{bajari2003deciding} establish the canonical framework for
detecting collusion through exchangeability of bid functions and
conditional independence of residuals, conditional on a
researcher-supplied partition between suspected colluders and a
competitive fringe. The papers that build on Bajari--Ye inherit
the constraint: \citet{porter1993detection} and
\citet{porter1999ohio} detect bid rotation conditional on
assumed cartel membership; \citet{baldwin1997bidder} use
post-prosecution records to identify timber-auction colluders;
\citet{conley2016detecting} infer bidder groups from co-bidding
patterns---the closest precedent in data parsimony, recovering
group structure rather than firm-level flags;
\citet{clark2021collusion} and \citet{schurter2020identification}
study cartel dynamics with ex-post-known membership. The
partition is constructed \emph{ex post} or by assumption in
every case. The construct supplies a candidate \emph{ex ante}
firm-level flag computable from award-record data, which
bid-coordination tests can then take as input where microdata
exist.

\paragraph{Bid-distribution screens require the bid layer}
\citet{imhof2019detecting,imhof2018screening,huber2019machine}
and \citet{wallimann2023machine} train machine-learning
classifiers on tender-level bid statistics---within-tender
coefficient of variation, kurtosis, skewness, normalized
spread---reaching AUC above $0.85$ against ground-truth European
cartel cases. \citet{abrantesmetz2006a} pioneer the variance
screen; \citet{chassang2022robust} provide moment-based
theoretical foundations; \citet{harrington2008detecting} surveys
the field. Every implementation operates at the tender level and
requires per-bidder bid amounts. Real enforcement environments
often preserve only the award envelope; the construct runs on
that envelope and integrates with bid-distribution screens at
the forensic stage where microdata are recoverable
(§\ref{sec:rob_imhof}).

\paragraph{Cover-bidding identification has been retrospective}
\citet{porter1999ohio,pesendorfer2000study,asker2010leniency}
and \citet{marshall2012economics} document cover bidders in
prosecuted cartels through case records (Ohio milk, Italian
highway, NYC stamps, and the sequence reviewed by
Marshall and Marx); \citet{athey2011comparing} compare
cover-bidding incentives across auction formats theoretically.
In every empirical case the cover bidders are identified
\emph{ex post}---through leniency testimony, post-conviction
records, or judicial discovery---and after the cartel has been
adjudicated. We are not aware of a published prospective
identification of cover-bidder candidates from public
award-record data. The construct shifts the post-adjudication
observation into a pre-investigation screening stage.

\paragraph{Enforcement design under incomplete information}
A separate literature studies how detection regimes should be
structured when monitoring is imperfect.
\citet{becker1968crime} and \citet{stigler1970optimum} establish
the optimal-deterrence baseline under costly enforcement;
\citet{harrington2008detecting} surveys cartel-screening design
in this register; \citet{baker2002case} treats the optimal
sequencing of investigative stages under information asymmetry
between agency and target. Two implications travel to our
setting. First, when bid-level observability is absent, the
appropriate screening object is whatever statistic the
collusive arrangement generates that survives the
data-coarsening step---a question of identifiability under
monitoring constraints rather than of detection power against a
fixed informational benchmark. Second, screening stages and
forensic stages answer different questions: screening allocates
investigative attention; forensic analysis adjudicates a
specific allegation. The architectural separation between an
award-layer screening stage and a bid-layer forensic stage,
which the empirical exercise instantiates, is the natural
implication of this distinction.

\paragraph{Procurement institutions and the observability
environment} A growing empirical literature documents how
procurement institutions shape what enforcement can observe.
\citet{coviello2017tenure} show that longer political tenure
worsens procurement outcomes in Italy;
\citet{decarolis2020rules} show that discretionary procedures
and competition limits jointly predict corruption risk; and
\citet{decarolis2017corruption} documents the
procurement-officer channel. We treat these institutional
features as variation in the monitoring regime that the
construct's signal is read against. Heterogeneity in the
construct's informativeness across buyer-size quartiles
(§\ref{sec:results_oversight}) is interpreted in this register
rather than as identification of an oversight channel.

\paragraph{Synthesis} The four literatures define the
informational architecture in which the contribution sits.
Bid-coordination tests require a partition; bid-distribution
screens require the bid layer; cover-bidding identification
requires the post-adjudication record; enforcement-design work
asks what survives when observability is incomplete. The
construct addresses the conjunction: it identifies a
participation primitive that survives data coarsening, supplies
a candidate firm-level flag that bid-coordination tests can take
as input, recasts cover-bidder identification as prospective
rather than retrospective, and instantiates the screening--forensic
architectural separation under the data-layer constraint that
real enforcement faces. It does not dominate any individual
benchmark; it operates where each cannot and integrates with
each where it does.
