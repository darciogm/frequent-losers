\section{Mechanism Heterogeneity}
\label{sec:mechanisms}

The price gap and the procuring-unit-size gradient establish that
the construct has a measurable, institutionally patterned imprint.
This section reads the heterogeneity through the framework's
mechanism predictions. Three subsections take three interpretive
stakes: §\ref{sec:mech_horse_race} shows that the continuous
loss-intensity signal dominates the binary cutoff in
discrimination, consistent with the framework's identification of
$\log(1+\text{tenders\_count})$ as the sufficient ranking
statistic; §\ref{sec:mech_modal} reads the modal asymmetry between
preg\~ao and convite as scope information about where the
construct fires most cleanly; §\ref{sec:mech_failed} catalogs the
mechanism predictions that the data do not support and demotes
them to scope.

\subsection{Loss Intensity Dominates the Threshold Cutoff}
\label{sec:mech_horse_race}

The deployable binary rule that defines frequent losers
(more than $\valThresholdStat$ tender-items, zero wins) is an
operationalization, not a primitive. Proposition~\ref{prop:exposure_stmt}
identifies $\log(1+\text{tenders\_count})$ as a sufficient ranking
statistic for cover-bidder type given award-record data; the binary
filter is the information-coarsening of this latent posterior under
the separating equilibrium of Online Appendix~A. The horse
race in Table~\ref{tab:horse_race_v14} confirms the prediction
empirically.

\input{./output/tables/tab_horse_race_v14}

On a harmonized same-sample basis, the continuous
$\log(1+\text{tenders\_count})$ score discriminates the
$\valCobidders$ adjudicated CADE-cobidder firms inside the
always-loser stratum at AUC $\valAUClogtc$
($95\%$ CI $\valAUClogtcCI$), against AUC $\valAUCFLBinSameSample$
($\valAUCFLBinSameSampleCI$) for the binary FL14 indicator
(\citealt{delong1988comparing} test for the difference: $Z = \valDeLongZ$,
$p = \valDeLongP$). The continuous statistic is strictly more
informative.

Price coefficients in standalone specifications point in the same
direction (FL14 binary $+0.0653$, $p < 0.001$; continuous
$\log(1+\max\,\text{tc})$ $+0.0188$, $p < 0.001$;
Table~\ref{tab:horse_race_v14}, columns 1--2). When both are
included additively (column 3), the FL14 coefficient flips sign
($-0.0746$, $p < 0.05$) while the continuous coefficient nearly
doubles ($+0.0349$, $p < 0.001$). The flip is what the framework
predicts: once the continuous primitive is in the specification,
the binary indicator captures tail residuals against the continuous
dosage rather than independent information about cover-bidder type.
The construct's deployable rule and its discriminating
instrument are not in tension---they are two views of the same
latent posterior at different resolution.

The implication for deployment is clean. We adopt the binary FL14
rule as the headline operational implementation throughout the
paper because it is administratively interpretable (firms are
either flagged or not, and the rule maps onto an existing oversight
workflow). We adopt the continuous $\log(1+\text{tenders\_count})$
as the discriminating instrument when ranking firms within the
always-loser stratum, because it is the statistic the framework
identifies as sufficient. The headline range $\valHeadlineRange$
in §\ref{sec:results_ols} is computed on the binary; the AUC
headline $\valAUCFLfirm$ ($95\%$ CI $\valAUCFLfirmCI$) reported in
the introduction and in §\ref{sec:cade} is the binary
firm-level discrimination on the broader BEC-firm sample. Both are
conservative readings of a continuous primitive that delivers
strictly more information.

\subsection{Modal Heterogeneity: Preg\~ao Deeper than Convite}
\label{sec:mech_modal}

The construct fires with sharper signal-to-noise in
preg\~ao than in convite, on two independent dimensions of
evidence. On the price-imprint side, the within-modal estimates
reported in §\ref{sec:results_ols} place the binary frequent-loser
specification at $\valFalPregBin$ ($\valFalPregBinPSig$) inside the
preg\~ao subsample versus $\valFalConvBin$ ($\valFalConvBinPSig$)
inside convite, a ratio of $\valFalRatio\!\!\times$ in favor of
preg\~ao. On the discrimination side, the modal-by-modal AUC
against the $\valCobidders$ adjudicated CADE-cobidder firms inside
the always-loser stratum is $\valAUCpregPrim$ in preg\~ao primary
auctions versus $\valAUCconvPrim$ in convite primary auctions, with
a bootstrap difference of $\valAUCmodalDiff$ ($p \approx 0$). Both
dimensions point in the same direction; neither is reducible to
the other.

A naive reading would invoke convite's minimum-bidder rule
(Lei~$8.666/93$, Art.\ 22, \S\,3) as the institutional driver of
the construct---if statutorily mandated quorum-fillers are the
mechanism, the construct should fire \emph{more} cleanly in
convite, not less. The data flatly reverse this prediction: the
construct's price imprint and its discriminating power are both
sharper where the minimum-bidder rule is absent. We do not read
this reversal as positive evidence for any specific institutional
theory of where cover bidding originates; we read it as scope
information about where the construct's signal-to-noise is highest.
Two competing readings remain observationally compatible at the
firm level: preg\~ao's open and electronic structure may expose
loser-side participation footprints more visibly than convite's
sealed-bid invitation procedure, or convite's smaller and more
homogeneous bidder pools may compress the discriminating gap. We
do not adjudicate between them, because the design-based
strategies (RDD, DiD) that would do so return null at the available
bandwidths and the modal split is post-stratified rather than
randomized.

The convite-modal AUC carries a width caveat the preg\~ao-modal
estimate does not: only $\valConvNcobidders$ adjudicated
CADE-cobidder firms fall in the within-convite stratum, so
confidence intervals on the modal AUC difference are wider than the
point estimate would suggest. We report the point estimate because
it aligns in direction with the price-imprint evidence in
§\ref{sec:results_ols}, where the within-pregão coefficient
strictly exceeds the within-convite coefficient on a much larger
sample. For deployment, the operational implication is concrete:
oversight bodies on platforms dominated by preg\~ao-style auctions
can expect stronger signal-to-noise from the construct than
oversight bodies on convite-heavy registries. The cross-section of
Brazilian state procurement platforms is heavily preg\~ao, and the
new federal procurement law (Lei~14.133/2021) consolidates the
preg\~ao-style electronic-auction format as the institutional
default; the modal scope finding therefore strengthens, rather than
weakens, the deployable case for the construct.

\subsection{What the Data Do Not Support}
\label{sec:mech_failed}

Where §\ref{sec:mech_horse_race} and §\ref{sec:mech_modal}
establish the construct's discrimination signature and its modal
scope, this subsection refines its heterogeneity profile against
the framework's sharper mechanism predictions. Two
predictions---the cell-heterogeneity prediction across HHI and
repeated-pair density, and the first-time-deployment
prediction---do not adjudicate cleanly under disciplined
estimation. We catalog them as scope information about where the
construct's heterogeneity is most precisely identified.

The framework predicts that cover bidding should be most pronounced
in low-concentration (low HHI) and low-repeated-pair-density
environments, where rotation among cartelists is feasible without
detection. The data place the largest positive conditional price
gap exactly in the Low-HHI $\times$ Low-pairs cell, in the
predicted direction. The signal carries one calibrating
qualification: this cell is also the largest cell by item count,
so the configuration is consistent with both the structural
prediction and a generic compositional explanation, and our
descriptive scope does not separate the two readings. The audit of
the diagonally opposite High-HHI $\times$ High-pairs cell
(Table~\ref{tab:negative_cell_audit}) returns a negative price
coefficient ($-0.062$ to $-0.119$ depending on period and
modality), with the negative sign concentrated in the 2009--2013
sub-period and in convite---period and modal compositional drivers
rather than a clean refutation of the rotation prediction. Neither
pattern adjudicates the cartel-rotation prediction; both are
scope.

The framework also predicts a discrete first-time-FL effect: when a
firm crosses the threshold to frequent-loser status, its price
imprint should sharpen. The estimate sits in a $+\valFTpsCoef$ to
$+\valFTuncondCoef$ range across three specifications, in the
predicted direction throughout. The unconditional event-study
estimate is $+\valFTuncondCoef$ ($p = \valFTuncondP$,
$N = \valFTuncondN$); under coarsened exact matching the estimate
is $+\valFTcemCoef$ ($p = \valFTcemP$, $N = \valFTcemN$); under
propensity-score matching it is $+\valFTpsCoef$ ($p = \valFTpsP$,
$N = \valFTpsN$). Conditional matching compresses the magnitude
substantially without inverting the direction. We adopt the
propensity-score estimate as the conservative reference and report
the full specification grid in Online Appendix~C.

The construct's empirical content is the within-item conditional
price gap of §\ref{sec:results_ols} and the procuring-unit-size
gradient of §\ref{sec:results_oversight}; the mechanism
predictions of this subsection refine the heterogeneity profile
but are not the anchor. Where the predictions track the data,
they do so observationally; where the data do not adjudicate,
we report the gap as scope.
