\section{Heterogeneity of the Screening Signal}
\label{sec:mechanisms}

The screening object reads against the framework's deployment
problem. This section reports two pieces of within-evidence that
discipline the framing: first, that the continuous loss-intensity
statistic strictly dominates the binary rule in discrimination,
consistent with Proposition~\ref{prop:exposure_stmt}'s identification
of $\log(1+\text{tenders\_count})$ as the sufficient ranking
statistic (\S\ref{sec:mech_horse_race}); and second, that the
modal contrast between preg\~ao and convite reads as variation in
the observability environment within which the screening signal
is recovered (\S\ref{sec:mech_modal}). \S\ref{sec:mech_failed}
catalogs framework predictions that the data do not adjudicate
under disciplined estimation; we do not weight these against the
screening interpretation because the screening framing does not
require their identification.

\subsection{Loss Intensity Dominates the Threshold Cutoff}
\label{sec:mech_horse_race}

The deployable binary rule that defines frequent losers (more
than $\valThresholdStat$ tender-items, zero wins) is the
information-coarsening of the participation primitive
identified by the framework, not a primitive in itself.
Proposition~\ref{prop:exposure_stmt} identifies
$\log(1+\text{tenders\_count})$ as a sufficient ranking statistic
for cover-bidder type given award-record data; the binary rule is
the information-coarsening of the latent posterior under the
separating equilibrium of Online Appendix~A. The horse race in
Table~\ref{tab:horse_race_v14} confirms the prediction empirically.

\input{./output/tables/tab_horse_race_v14}

On a harmonized same-sample basis, the continuous
$\log(1+\text{tenders\_count})$ score discriminates the
$\valCobidders$ adjudicated CADE-cobidder firms inside the
always-loser stratum at AUC $\valAUClogtc$ ($95\%$ CI
$\valAUClogtcCI$), against AUC $\valAUCFLBinSameSample$
($\valAUCFLBinSameSampleCI$) for the binary FL14 indicator
(\citealt{delong1988comparing} test, $Z = \valDeLongZ$,
$p = \valDeLongP$). The continuous statistic is strictly more
informative.

Standalone price coefficients align with the discrimination
result: FL14 binary $+0.0653$ ($p<0.001$), continuous
$\log(1+\max\,\text{tc})$ $+0.0188$ ($p<0.001$);
Table~\ref{tab:horse_race_v14}, columns 1--2. When both are
included additively (column 3), the FL14 coefficient flips sign
($-0.0746$, $p<0.05$) while the continuous coefficient nearly
doubles ($+0.0349$, $p<0.001$). The flip is what the framework
predicts: once the continuous primitive is in the specification,
the binary indicator captures tail residuals against the
continuous dosage rather than independent information about
cover-bidder type. The dose-response pattern is consistent with
the screening interpretation and inconsistent with a
pure-confound reading of the broad-sample association
(\S\ref{sec:results_overlap}).

We adopt the binary FL14 rule as the operational implementation
throughout the paper because it is administratively
interpretable; we adopt the continuous statistic as the
discriminating instrument because it is what the framework
identifies as sufficient. Both are reported. The headline range
$\valHeadlineRange$ in \S\ref{sec:results_ols} is computed on
the binary; the AUC headline $\valAUCFLfirm$
($95\%$ CI $\valAUCFLfirmCI$) reported in
\S\ref{sec:cade_prospective} is binary firm-level
discrimination on the broader sample.

\subsection{Modal Contrast as Observability-Regime Variation}
\label{sec:mech_modal}

The screening signal is sharper in preg\~ao than in convite,
along two dimensions. On the price-imprint side, the within-modal
estimates of \S\ref{sec:results_ols} place the binary
specification at $\valFalPregBin$ ($\valFalPregBinPSig$) inside
preg\~ao versus $\valFalConvBin$ ($\valFalConvBinPSig$) inside
convite, a ratio of $\valFalRatio\!\!\times$. On the
discrimination side, the modal-by-modal AUC against the
$\valCobidders$ adjudicated CADE-cobidder firms is
$\valAUCpregPrim$ in preg\~ao primary auctions versus
$\valAUCconvPrim$ in convite primary auctions, with a bootstrap
difference of $\valAUCmodalDiff$ ($p\approx 0$). Both dimensions
point in the same direction.

The two BEC modalities expose different observability regimes
(\S\ref{sec:institutional}): preg\~ao records a real-time
electronic auction with public bidding interaction, convite a
sealed-bid invitation procedure with a statutory three-bidder
quorum requirement. A naive reading of the modal contrast would
invoke convite's minimum-bidder rule as the mechanism through
which the screening signal arises, in which case the screening
should fire more cleanly in convite, not less. The data
flatly reverse this prediction: the screening signal is sharper
where the minimum-bidder rule is absent. We do not interpret
the reversal as a positive test of any specific institutional
theory. We read it as variation in the screening signal across
observability regimes.

The reading is consistent with two compatible mechanisms that
the design cannot separate: preg\~ao's open electronic
interaction may expose loser-side participation footprints more
visibly than convite's sealed-bid envelope, or convite's smaller
and more homogeneous bidder pools may compress the discriminating
gap. The compatibility is itself informative for the JLEO frame:
the screening object's informativeness varies with the
observability regime in which it is computed, and the regime
that consolidates as the institutional default
(Lei~$14.133/2021$, the post-sample reform that
\S\ref{sec:institutional} catalogs) is the regime in which the
screening signal is sharpest. Whether this is read as
strengthening the construct's portability or as scope-restricting
it depends on which observability regime a candidate jurisdiction
operates under; both readings are consistent with the
screening-stage interpretation.

The convite-modal AUC carries a width caveat the preg\~ao-modal
estimate does not: only $\valConvNcobidders$ adjudicated
CADE-cobidder firms fall in the within-convite stratum, so
confidence intervals on the modal AUC difference are wider than
the point estimate suggests. We report the point estimate
because it aligns in direction with the price-imprint evidence
in \S\ref{sec:results_ols}, on a much larger sample.

\subsection{Predictions the Data Do Not Adjudicate}
\label{sec:mech_failed}

The framework generates two finer-grained predictions that the
data do not adjudicate under disciplined estimation. We report
them here for transparency. They are not load-bearing for the
screening interpretation, which rests on the participation
primitive (\S\ref{sec:mech_horse_race}) and the
observability-regime variation (\S\ref{sec:mech_modal}), not on
the cell-level structure of cartel rotation.

The first prediction concerns the
HHI--repeated-pair-density grid over which cover-bidder rotation
should be most pronounced (low HHI, low pairs). The data place
the largest positive conditional price gap exactly in the
Low-HHI $\times$ Low-pairs cell, in the predicted direction; but
this cell is also the largest cell by item count, so the
configuration is observationally consistent with both the
framework's prediction and a generic compositional explanation.
The audit of the diagonally opposite High-HHI $\times$
High-pairs cell (Table~\ref{tab:negative_cell_audit}) returns a
negative price coefficient ($-0.062$ to $-0.119$ depending on
period and modality), concentrated in the 2009--2013 sub-period
and in convite, which we read as period and modal compositional
drivers rather than as refutation of the rotation prediction.

The second prediction concerns a discrete first-time-FL
imprint as a firm crosses the threshold. The estimate sits in a
$+\valFTpsCoef$ to $+\valFTuncondCoef$ range across three
specifications: unconditional event-study $+\valFTuncondCoef$
($p=\valFTuncondP$, $N=\valFTuncondN$); coarsened exact matching
$+\valFTcemCoef$ ($p=\valFTcemP$, $N=\valFTcemN$);
propensity-score matching $+\valFTpsCoef$ ($p=\valFTpsP$,
$N=\valFTpsN$). Conditional matching compresses the magnitude
substantially without inverting the direction. We adopt the
propensity-score estimate as the conservative reference and
report the full specification grid in Online Appendix~C.

Neither prediction is load-bearing. Where the predictions track
the data, they do so observationally; where the data do not
adjudicate, we report the gap as scope rather than as
mechanism evidence.
