\section{Data and Frequent Losers Definition}
\label{sec:data}

\subsection{BEC Platform}
\label{sec:data_bec}

The data come from the Bolsa Eletr\^onica de Compras (BEC), São
Paulo's centralized electronic procurement platform, operated by
the State Treasury since $2002$. The empirical setting is
substantive in absolute terms rather than a peripheral national
case: São Paulo state has $46.1$ million residents
($2025$ IBGE estimate, $21.6\%$ of Brazil's population) and a
GDP of R\$$3.45$ trillion in $2023$, accounting for $31.5\%$
of Brazilian GDP (IBGE \emph{Sistema de Contas Regionais},
released November $2025$).\footnote{At the average $2023$
exchange rate of roughly R\$$5$/US\$, São Paulo's $2023$ GDP is
approximately US\$$690$ billion, which would place the state
around the world's top $20$ jurisdictions if treated as an
independent economy---comparable to Switzerland, Argentina, or
Sweden, and exceeding the GDP of Belgium, Norway, Austria,
Ireland, or Israel (World Bank, $2023$). State-level procurement
spending in São Paulo is correspondingly substantial in absolute
terms, which is the relevant scale for assessing the deployment
value of an award-layer screening stage.} BEC serves $1{,}308$
purchasing units (PBUs)---state agencies, public universities,
hospitals, schools---and clears two procurement modalities during
our sample:
\emph{convite} (sealed-bid invitation procedure with a
statutory three-bidder requirement, Lei~$\valLeiOriginal$) and
\emph{preg\~ao} (electronic reverse auction with no minimum-bidder
requirement, Lei~$\valLeiPregao$). Our $\valYearStart$--$\valYearEnd$
window comprises $\valDataItemAvgVar{,}754$ contract-record items
across $22$ calendar semesters, generated from
$\valDataNbidsMean{,}357$ individual bids placed by $\valBECfirms$
distinct firms (Table~\ref{tab:modality_year}). The contract-record
layer preserves participants, winners, negotiated prices, reference
prices, and procurement metadata; the bid-by-bid log is retained
in the operational system but exported to the analytical warehouse
only on administrative request, the data-layer asymmetry
characterized in §\ref{sec:institutional}.

The modal mix shifts over the sample window. Preg\~ao auctions
expand from a minority share at the start of the sample toward
near-universal coverage by $2019$, consistent with the $2021$
consolidation of preg\~ao-style electronic auctions as the
institutional default for Brazilian procurement
(§\ref{sec:institutional}). Convite remains a meaningful share
in the small-cap range and in early years, providing the
within-platform variation that the modal contrast in
§\ref{sec:results_ols} and §\ref{sec:mech_modal} reads.

\subsection{Frequent Losers Definition}
\label{sec:fl_definition}

\paragraph{Why a wins-zero filter}
The framework's $\text{wins}=0$ filter is a separating choice
under standard expected-profit competition: a firm that bids
without ever winning faces negative expected continuation profit
once cumulative bidding cost exceeds the gain from any single
plausible win. With per-bid cost $c$, mean order value
$\bar V$, and gross margin $m$, the break-even on $n$ losses is
$n^{*} = \bar V m / c - 2$ (Online Appendix~A). At BEC
parameters ($\bar V \approx$ R\$$\valDataItemAvgPay$, $m=0.10$,
and the documented bid-preparation cost $c \in [\text{R\$}200,
\text{R\$}500]$ for one to two staff-hours plus compliance),
$n^{*}$ falls in $[15, 41]$. The empirical FL threshold of
$\valThreshold$ tender-items lies in this interval; the
$90$\textsuperscript{th}-percentile FL participates in $50+$
zero-win tenders and accumulates direct cost above
R\$$\valPermFirms$. Sustained zero-win participation past the
break-even is consistent with cover bidding, capacity signaling,
or eligibility maintenance; we organize the empirical work
around the cover-bidding reading without claiming it as the
unique interpretation (§\ref{sec:lim_construct}). The break-even
calculation is a consistency check on the framework's
$\text{wins}=0$ filter, not an independent identification
device.

\paragraph{Two-step classification}
We define frequent losers as the intersection of two
conditions:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Always-losers}: firms with a zero win rate across
all $\valYearStart$--$\valYearEnd$ BEC tenders. The pool
comprises $\valAlwaysLosers$ firms ($\valPctFortyone$ of all BEC
participants); frequent losers are a small extreme subset of
this stratum.
\item \textbf{IQR threshold}: among always-losers, those with
participation counts above $\text{median}+1.5\!\times\!\text{IQR}$
of the always-loser participation
distribution.\footnote{We use median + $1.5\!\times$ IQR rather
than the Tukey rule (Q3 + $1.5\!\times$ IQR) because the
participation distribution is heavily right-skewed; the adopted
rule yields a stricter threshold. The Tukey alternative flags
$\valFLQThreeIQR$ firms with AUC $\valAUCQThreeIQR$
($\valAUCQThreeIQRCI$), against $\valAUCFLfirm$
($\valAUCFLfirmCI$) under the adopted rule. Robustness to the
multiplier choice between $1.0\!\!\times$ and $3.0\!\!\times$ is
reported in §\ref{sec:rob_threshold}.} The threshold yields
$\valThreshold$ or more tenders with zero wins, corresponding
to a statistical cutoff of $\valThresholdStat$. The threshold
was fixed \emph{ex ante} on the participation distribution and
the framework's break-even, before any examination of CADE
labels. The definition yields $\valFL$ frequent-loser firms.
\end{enumerate}

The treatment variable $\text{losers}_{igt}$ equals one if
tender-item $i$ in item group $g$ at time $t$ has at least one
frequent-loser firm among its participants, and zero otherwise.

\paragraph{The continuous primitive and its binary coarsening}
The economic construct is broader than the binary rule.
Proposition~\ref{prop:exposure_stmt} identifies
$\log(1+\text{tenders\_count})$ as a sufficient ranking statistic
for cover-bidder type given award-record data; the binary
$\valThreshold$-tender rule is the information-coarsening of
this statistic under the framework's separating equilibrium. The
horse race of §\ref{sec:mech_horse_race} confirms the prediction
empirically: the continuous statistic strictly dominates the
binary in discrimination
(\citealt{delong1988comparing} test, $p < \valDeLongP$). We use
the continuous statistic as the discriminating instrument and
the binary rule as the discrete cut-off the screening framing
flags.

\paragraph{Out-of-sample generalization of the threshold}
Pooling participation over the full $\valYearStart$--$\valYearEnd$
window maximizes within-stratum discrimination but does not
isolate the out-of-sample stability of the screening statistic.
Estimating the threshold on $2009$--$2016$ alone gives a cutoff
of $\valThresholdTrain$ and a firm-level AUC of
$\valAUCStrictFirmFL$ ($\valAUCStrictFirmFLCI$); evaluating the
$2009$--$2016$-frozen score against $2017$--$2019$ items, the
binary indicator drops to $\valAUCStrictItemFL$, while the
continuous primitive retains $\valAUCStrictItemTC$
(Table~\ref{tab:strict_train_threshold}). The full
generalization audit and the implied conservative AUC of
$\valAUCFLfirmTemp$ are reported in
§\ref{sec:rob_operational}.

\subsection{Sample Construction and Descriptive Statistics}
\label{sec:data_sample}

The participation distribution among always-losers is heavily
right-skewed (Figures~\ref{fig:losses}--\ref{fig:iqr}). The
regression sample retains convite and preg\~ao tenders with a
recorded winner and item types with at least one frequent-loser
participation during the sample window. The last restriction
conditions on the treatment in the sense that item types that
never attract frequent losers anywhere in
$\valYearStart$--$\valYearEnd$ are excluded; this ensures that
controls come from item markets where the screening statistic
can plausibly fire. The unrestricted sample returns a
coefficient within $0.5$ percentage points of the
restricted-sample baseline (§\ref{sec:robustness}), so the
conditioning is empirically innocuous.

The regression sample contains $\valSampleN$ tender-items across
$\valFirmCount$ item types, defined at the finest product-code
level available in BEC. Of the $\valFirmCount$ item types,
$\valDataFirmMean$ ($82\%$) have both frequent-loser-present
and frequent-loser-absent tenders within sample, contributing
to within-item identification under the fixed-effects
specification of Equation~\eqref{eq:ols_main}. Frequent-loser
firms participate in $\valCartelMarkup$ of tender-items.

Table~\ref{tab:descstats} reports unconditional descriptive
statistics by frequent-loser presence. The raw price ratio is
roughly elevenfold (R\$$\valOCcount$ vs R\$$\valDataItemMean$),
reflecting the sorting of frequent losers toward larger
contracts; frequent-loser-present tenders attract on average
$\valDataMedianWinPart$ bidders against $\valDataMeanWinPart$ in
frequent-loser-absent tenders. Under within-item, within-year,
within-procuring-unit fixed effects, the $2.43$-log-point raw
gap compresses to roughly $0.05$ log points
(Table~\ref{tab:conditional_descstats}). The selection that the
fixed-effects structure absorbs is large; the residual
within-item association is small in log points but
systematically positive across estimators
(§\ref{sec:results_ols}).
