\subsection{BEC Platform}
\label{sec:data_bec}
% ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

The data come from the Bolsa Eletr\^onica de Compras (BEC), São
Paulo's centralized electronic procurement platform, operated by
the State Treasury since $2002$. BEC serves $1{,}308$ purchasing
units (PBUs)---state agencies, public universities, hospitals,
schools---and clears two procurement modalities during our sample:
\emph{convite} (sealed-bid invitation procedure with a
statutory three-bidder requirement, Lei~$\valLeiOriginal$) and
\emph{preg\~ao} (electronic reverse auction with no minimum-bidder
requirement, Lei~$\valLeiPregao$). The two modalities provide a
within-platform institutional contrast that the empirical analysis
exploits in §\ref{sec:results_ols} and §\ref{sec:mech_modal};
the construct itself does not depend on the convite minimum-bidder
rule (§\ref{sec:institutional}). Our $\valYearStart$--$\valYearEnd$
window comprises $\valDataItemAvgVar{,}754$ contract-record items
across $22$ calendar semesters, generated from
$\valDataNbidsMean{,}357$ individual bids placed by $\valBECfirms$
distinct firms (Table~\ref{tab:modality_year}). The contract-record
layer preserves participants, winners, negotiated prices, reference
prices, and procurement metadata; the bid-by-bid log is retained in
the operational system but exported to the analytical warehouse
only on administrative request, an envelope asymmetry central to
the construct's portability claim (§\ref{sec:lim_external}).

The modal mix shifts over the sample window. Preg\~ao auctions
expand from a minority share at the start of the sample toward
near-universal coverage by $2019$, consistent with the $2021$
consolidation of preg\~ao-style electronic auctions as the
institutional default for Brazilian procurement
(§\ref{sec:institutional}). Convite remains a meaningful share
in the small-cap range and in early years, providing the modal
variation that powers the falsification check of
§\ref{sec:results_ols} and the discrimination comparison of
§\ref{sec:mech_modal}.

\subsection{Frequent Losers Definition}
\label{sec:fl_definition}
% ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

\paragraph{Behavioral anchor}
Sustained participation with a zero win rate is rationally
dominated under standard expected-profit competition. With
positive per-bid cost $c > 0$, the expected gain per bid for a
firm Bayesian-updating its win probability after $n$ consecutive
losses falls below $c$ once $n > \bar{V} m / c - 2$, where
$\bar{V}$ is the mean procurement-order value and $m$ is the
gross margin (Online Appendix~A). At BEC parameters
($\bar{V} =$ R\$$\valDataItemAvgPay$ from the sample mean,
$m = 0.10$ from documented procurement-supplier margins), this
threshold is approximately $n = 15$ for $c =$ R\$$500$ and
$n = 41$ for $c =$ R\$$200$. The plausible cost range is anchored
on documented BEC bid-preparation effort (one to two staff-hours
plus documentation compliance, $c \geq$ R\$$200$); at the FL
threshold of $\valThreshold$ zero-win participations expected
profit is already negative under that anchor, and at the
$90$\textsuperscript{th}-percentile FL participation count of
$50+$ zero-win tenders cumulative direct cost exceeds
R\$$\valPermFirms$. Sustained participation past this point
reveals one of three readings: a non-pecuniary objective
(cover-bidding, capacity-signaling, or eligibility maintenance);
systematically incorrect beliefs that fail to update on the loss
record; or zero-win participation in BEC offset by wins in
unobserved tenders elsewhere. The construct flags the
participation footprint these readings share; we adopt the
cover-bidding reading as the working interpretation
(§\ref{sec:lim_construct}).

\paragraph{Two-step classification}
We define frequent losers as the intersection of two conditions.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Always-losers}: firms with a zero win rate across
all $\valYearStart$--$\valYearEnd$ BEC tenders. The pool
comprises $\valAlwaysLosers$ firms ($\valPctFortyone$ of all BEC
participants), reflecting low electronic participation costs,
small firms bidding beyond their operational capacity, and the
broader compositional features of decentralized procurement
\citep{coviello2017tenure,decarolis2017corruption}. Frequent
losers are a small extreme subset of this population.

\item \textbf{IQR threshold}: among always-losers, those with
participation counts above $\text{median} + 1.5 \times
\text{IQR}$ of the always-loser participation
distribution.\footnote{We use median + $1.5 \times$ IQR rather
than the standard Tukey rule (Q3 + $1.5 \times$ IQR) because the
participation distribution is highly right-skewed; the rule we
use yields a stricter threshold and fewer false positives. The
referee's standard Tukey alternative flags
$\valFLQThreeIQR$ firms and yields AUC $\valAUCQThreeIQR$
($\valAUCQThreeIQRCI$), versus $\valAUCFLfirm$
($\valAUCFLfirmCI$) for the rule we adopt. Robustness to the
multiplier choice across $1.0\!\!\times$ to $3.0\!\!\times$ is
reported in §\ref{sec:rob_threshold}.} The threshold yields
$\valThreshold$ or more tenders with zero wins, corresponding
to a statistical cutoff of $\valThresholdStat$. The threshold
was fixed \emph{ex ante} on the participation distribution and
the behavioral anchor above, before any examination of CADE
labels. The definition yields $\valFL$ frequent-loser firms.
\end{enumerate}

The treatment variable $\text{losers}_{igt}$ equals one if
tender-item $i$ in item group $g$ at time $t$ has at least one
frequent-loser firm among its participants, and zero otherwise.

The economic construct is broader than the binary rule. The
continuous loss-intensity statistic
$\log(1 + \text{tenders\_count})$ is the primitive that
Proposition~\ref{prop:exposure_stmt} identifies as a sufficient
ranking statistic for cover-bidder type given award-record data;
the binary $\valThreshold$-tender rule is the
information-coarsening of this statistic under the framework's
separating equilibrium (Online Appendix~A). The horse
race in §\ref{sec:mech_horse_race} establishes that the
continuous statistic strictly dominates the binary in
discrimination (DeLong $p = \valDeLongP$). We treat the binary
rule as the deployable operational implementation and the
continuous statistic as the discriminating instrument.

\paragraph{Strict temporal validation}
The classification pools participation over the full
$\valYearStart$--$\valYearEnd$ window, which maximizes
within-stratum discrimination but does not isolate the deployable
projection. When we estimate the threshold only on the
$2009$--$2016$ training window, the cutoff falls to
$\valThresholdTrain$ and the firm-level AUC is
$\valAUCStrictFirmFL$ ($\valAUCStrictFirmFLCI$). Frozen against
$2017$--$2019$ items, the binary indicator drops to
$\valAUCStrictItemFL$, while the continuous loss-intensity
statistic retains $\valAUCStrictItemTC$
(Table~\ref{tab:strict_train_threshold}). The temporal-holdout
audit in §\ref{sec:rob_operational} reports the same gap on
operational metrics: in-sample precision overstates the
deployable projection by approximately $50\%$ at top-$500$, and
the temporal-holdout AUC of $0.864$ is the conservative
discriminating reference we adopt for operational claims. The
binary rule is a deployable triage trigger; the continuous
statistic is the more persistent identifier of the loser-side
participation footprint.

\subsection{Sample Construction}
\label{sec:data_sample}
% ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

The participation distribution among always-losers is heavily
right-skewed (Figures~\ref{fig:losses}--\ref{fig:iqr}). The
regression sample retains convite and preg\~ao tenders with a
recorded winner and item types with at least one frequent-loser
participation during the sample window. The last restriction
conditions on the treatment in the sense that item types that
never attract frequent losers anywhere in
$\valYearStart$--$\valYearEnd$ are excluded; the restriction
ensures controls come from item markets where the construct can
plausibly fire. The unrestricted sample yields a coefficient
within $0.5$ percentage points of the restricted-sample baseline
(§\ref{sec:robustness}), so the conditioning is empirically
innocuous.

The resulting regression sample contains $\valSampleN$
tender-items across $\valFirmCount$ item types, defined at the
finest product-code level available in BEC (not aggregated to
two-digit groups). Of the $\valFirmCount$ item types,
$\valDataFirmMean$ ($82\%$) have both frequent-loser-present and
frequent-loser-absent tenders within sample, contributing to
within-item identification under the fixed-effects specification
of Equation~\eqref{eq:ols_main}. Frequent-loser firms participate
in $\valCartelMarkup$ of tender-items.

Table~\ref{tab:descstats} reports unconditional descriptive
statistics by frequent-loser presence. The unconditional
negotiated-price ratio between frequent-loser-present and
frequent-loser-absent tenders---R\$$\valOCcount$ versus
R\$$\valDataItemMean$, a factor of approximately
eleven---reflects the sorting of frequent losers toward larger
contracts: frequent-loser-present tenders attract on average
$\valDataMedianWinPart$ bidders versus $\valDataMeanWinPart$ in
frequent-loser-absent tenders. Under within-item, within-year,
within-procuring-unit fixed effects, the raw $2.43$-log-point gap
compresses to roughly $0.05$ log points
(Table~\ref{tab:conditional_descstats}); the regressions compare
similar tenders within tight product-code categories rather than
across categories. Table~\ref{tab:descstats} establishes the
magnitude of the selection problem the fixed-effects structure is
designed to absorb, and the comparison to
Table~\ref{tab:conditional_descstats} shows the absorption is
substantial.

\subsection{CADE Validation Portfolio}
\label{sec:data_cade}
% ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

The construct's external validation rests on the
procurement-cartel adjudication portfolio of CADE, the federal
competition authority (§\ref{sec:institutional}). The portfolio
relevant to our sample comprises $12$ adjudicated cases covering
$65$ firm-defendants in total, with final decisions spanning
$2015$--$2025$. Of the $65$ firm-defendants, $\valDirectCADE$
match BEC participants by CNPJ root, and these $\valDirectCADE$
firms constitute the \emph{direct-defendant} ground-truth
population. Eight of the twelve cases were adjudicated after
$2019$, so the construct's discrimination against the
adjudicated population was computed on participation patterns
observable years before enforcement confirmed those
patterns---a prospective character we exploit in
§\ref{sec:cade}.

The validation operates against two distinct populations, and
the distinction is central to the construct's claim. The
\emph{direct defendants} are the $\valDirectCADE$ firms named in
CADE rulings as cartel members; in adjudicated cases these firms
are typically frequent winners, the operational core of the
collusive arrangement. The \emph{cobidders} are the
$\valCobidders$ firms that participated alongside the direct
defendants in the same BEC tender-items, inside the always-loser
stratum. Cobidders are cover-bidder \emph{candidates} by
structure: they share tenders with adjudicated cartel members
without ever winning. The construct's target is the cobidder
population, not the direct-defendant population. The AUC
differential---firm-level $\valAUCFLfirm$ ($95\%$ CI
$\valAUCFLfirmCI$) against cobidders versus $\valAUCdirectStd$
against direct defendants---is the empirical signature of this
design (§\ref{sec:cade}, §\ref{sec:lim_construct}).
