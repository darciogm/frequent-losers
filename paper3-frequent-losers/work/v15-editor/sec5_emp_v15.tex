\section{Empirical Strategy}
\label{sec:empirical}

\subsection{The Empirical Object: Screening Value Under Coarsened
Observability}
\label{sec:emp_estimand}

The empirical object is not a treatment effect of frequent-loser
presence on prices. It is the screening value of endogenous
loser-side participation given award-record data. The
distinction is conceptual rather than rhetorical and governs the
interpretation of every estimate in the paper.

A treatment-effect framing would ask: holding observable
features fixed, what is the average price difference between
items into which frequent losers enter and items into which they
do not? Under that framing the relevant estimator restricts
attention to environments in which treated and control items
genuinely overlap on observables, and any estimate computed off
that support is a confounded approximation of the within-support
average. The screening framing reverses this. Under the
separating equilibrium of Online Appendix~A, frequent-loser
participation is generated by the cartel's deployment problem;
the cartel chooses where and how often to deploy cover bidders
as a function of detection costs and item-specific incentives.
Endogenous sorting into the items where the cartel deploys is
constitutive of the screening object: the participation
footprint the construct flags is, by construction, the footprint
where the deployment optimum places it. The screening question
asks how informative this footprint is about the underlying
collusive environment when only award-layer outcomes are
observable. The relevant estimator integrates over the
distribution of items into which frequent losers actually enter,
not over a counterfactual support reweighted to remove the
sorting.

We therefore distinguish two empirical objects, both reported,
neither claimed to be the other.

\paragraph{Object 1: broad-sample association ($\beta$)}
The within-cell conditional mean difference in log negotiated
price between tender-items that include at least one
frequent-loser participant and otherwise comparable items in the
same product code, year, and procuring unit. We estimate this
object on $\valSampleN$ tender-items and bracket it with four
estimators (OLS, cross-fit, CEM, IPW). $\beta$ is informative
about the loser-side participation footprint where it is
deployed; it is the screening-value object the framework's
participation primitive is designed to recover. We do not claim
$\beta$ as a causal effect of frequent-loser presence and we do
not defend it against the standard treatment-effect-identification
critique. Under the screening framing, the question of whether
$\beta$ would survive a counterfactual that strips the deployment
sorting is not the relevant question.

\paragraph{Object 2: overlap-restricted association ($\beta^{\text{ov}}$)}
The conditional mean difference in log negotiated price computed
on the subsample of items where treated and control populations
genuinely overlap on observables. We estimate this object on the
overlap cell and on the propensity-score-trimmed support
(\S\ref{sec:results_ols}, Table~\ref{tab:item_level_scope_match}).
$\beta^{\text{ov}}$ is the within-support comparison a
treatment-effect framing would target; it is informative about
the price differential at the items where the two populations
look alike on observables, holding the participation footprint
fixed at that subsample. We report it as a different empirical
object from $\beta$, not as a sharper version of $\beta$.

The two objects answer different questions. The screening
framing privileges $\beta$ because the screening statistic is
read against the cartel's deployment distribution, not against a
counterfactual support. The treatment-effect framing privileges
$\beta^{\text{ov}}$ because identification is local to the
subsample on which the treated-and-control populations are
comparable. The empirical fact that
$\beta>0$ on the broad sample while $\beta^{\text{ov}}<0$ on the
overlap restriction (Section~\ref{sec:results_ols}) is the
empirical signature of this distinction: the screen's economic
content is generated by the deployment sorting that the overlap
restriction strips. We do not interpret the sign reversal as a
robustness failure of the broad-sample estimate, because the
broad-sample estimate is not a treatment-effect claim. We
interpret it as the diagnostic that locates the construct as a
screening-value object.

\paragraph{Two design-based strategies that return null}
A sharp regression discontinuity at the convite/preg\~ao
statutory value caps is gated by the absence of bunching:
McCrary-style density tests around the $\valCapOldR$ cap return
a left/right ratio of $\valMcCraryRatio$ and a log-density
discontinuity of $\valMcCraryDisc$
(Table~\ref{tab:mccrary}), with the convite share dropping
cleanly from $\valPctSixnine$ below to $\valPctFifnine$ above.
Local first-stage power is too weak to support a fuzzy RDD at
standard bandwidths. A difference-in-differences design using
Decreto~$\valDecreto$'s $2018$ raise of the convite cap fails
parallel trends at the available pre-treatment window. We
report both failures openly. They do not constrain the
screening interpretation, which does not require a causal
identification of $\beta$, but they do constrain alternative
framings of the empirical object that would.

\subsection{Reduced-Form Specification}
\label{sec:emp_reduced}

The reduced-form specification is
\begin{equation}
  y_{igt} = \beta \cdot \text{losers}_{igt}
  + \mathbf{x}_{igt}' \boldsymbol{\delta}
  + \alpha_g + \lambda_t + \gamma_k + \varepsilon_{igt}
  \label{eq:ols_main}
\end{equation}
where $y_{igt}$ is the outcome for tender-item $i$ in item group
$g$ at time $t$ and procuring unit $k$; $\text{losers}_{igt}$
indicates the presence of at least one frequent-loser participant;
$\mathbf{x}_{igt}$ includes a convite indicator and any
specification-specific controls; $\alpha_g$, $\lambda_t$, and
$\gamma_k$ are item, year, and procuring-unit fixed effects; and
$\varepsilon_{igt}$ is the disturbance, clustered at the item
level. The headline outcome is $\log p_{\text{negotiated}}$;
auxiliary outcomes (number of bidders, number of bids, number of
non-frequent-loser bidders) are reported in
§\ref{sec:results_ols} and the appendix.

We estimate Equation~\eqref{eq:ols_main} under four
specifications. The general within-item-and-year baseline drops
the procuring-unit fixed effect; the within-procuring-unit
specification adds it. Two modal subsamples, preg\~ao-only and
convite-only, decompose the headline along the observability-regime
contrast set up in §\ref{sec:institutional}. The estimation
sample comprises $\valSampleN$ tender-items
(§\ref{sec:data_sample}).

Three complementary estimators bracket
Equation~\eqref{eq:ols_main}. Cross-fitting defines the
frequent-loser list on odd years, estimates the equation on even
years, and reverses the assignment, removing within-sample
classification noise. Coarsened exact matching (CEM) and
inverse-probability weighting (IPW) adjust for selection on
observables (year, procedure type, item group, procuring-unit-size
quartile)---under the screening framing these estimators bracket
the broad-sample object $\beta$, not a treatment-effect target.
A leave-one-out instrumental-variable specification uses the
supply of frequent losers in other procuring units in the same
product market and year as instrument; we read
$\hat\beta_{\text{IV}}$ as a measurement-error diagnostic for
the binary indicator's coarsening of the underlying continuous
loss-intensity statistic, not as a causal estimate. The four
estimators (cross-fit, IPW, OLS, CEM) jointly bound the headline
range $\valHeadlineRange$ used throughout the paper
(§\ref{sec:results_ols}).

Standard errors are clustered at the item level as the baseline
choice. Robustness to procuring-unit clustering and to two-way
\citet{cameron2011robust} clustering is reported in
§\ref{sec:rob_threshold}.

\subsection{Identification Audits as Screening-Value Diagnostics}
\label{sec:emp_audits}

The screening framing reorganizes the role of the four
identification audits reported in §\ref{sec:rob_identification}.
Each audit answers a distinct question about the screening
object rather than a question about the credibility of a causal
estimate.

The robustness values of \citet{cinelli2020making}
($RV_{q=1}=\valRVqOne$) and \citet{oster2019unobservable}
($\hat\delta=261.64$) bound the broad-sample association against
selection on observables already absorbed by item, year, and
procuring-unit fixed effects. They calibrate how much
unobserved-confounder variance would have to load on
observables to explain $\beta$ away. Under the screening framing
this is the bound on selection-on-observables explanations of
the screen's signal.

Strict-overlap matching delivers the second empirical object
$\beta^{\text{ov}}$
(Table~\ref{tab:item_level_scope_match}). Its role is not to
sharpen $\beta$ but to instantiate the alternative empirical
object whose contrast with $\beta$ identifies the screening-value
interpretation.

The leakage audit (Table~\ref{tab:leakage_audit}) decomposes the
in-sample item-level discrimination AUC into a structural
component and a tautology component, by holding cobidder firms
out of score construction within cross-validation folds and by
evaluating under temporal holdout. It is a generalization
diagnostic for the screening statistic, not an identification
test for $\beta$.

The sub-threshold-always-loser placebo
(Table~\ref{tab:iv_placebo}) confirms that the participation
primitive operates through the threshold-crossing population the
construct identifies, rather than through generic always-loser
supply. It is a specification diagnostic for the participation
primitive, not an identification test for $\beta$.

The four audits are reported in §\ref{sec:rob_identification}
and the identification scope they delineate is consolidated in
§\ref{sec:lim_identification}.

\subsection{Heterogeneity Specifications}
\label{sec:emp_heterogeneity}

We characterize heterogeneity in $\beta$ along two dimensions.
Both are descriptive specifications of
Equation~\eqref{eq:ols_main} estimated within sub-samples; we do
not interpret either as institutional identification.

The first dimension is procuring-unit size, which the framework
of Online Appendix~A maps to the principal detection cost
$\theta_k$. We split the sample into quartiles of
procuring-unit size (annual contract volume in BEC) and
re-estimate Equation~\eqref{eq:ols_main} within each quartile.
Robustness to alternative size measures (cumulative item count,
headcount of distinct purchasers) is reported alongside the
headline. Under the screening framing, this specification reads
the construct's informativeness across detection regimes
(§\ref{sec:results_oversight}); the gradient is heterogeneity in
the screening object across the monitoring environment, not
identification of a causal channel.

The second dimension is the cell-heterogeneity grid in winner
concentration and repeated-pair density. For each frequent-loser
firm $j$, we compute the Herfindahl--Hirschman Index of winning
firms across all tenders in which $j$ participates,
$\text{HHI}_j = \sum_{w} s_{wj}^2$, where $s_{wj}$ is the share
of $j$'s tenders won by firm $w$. We classify $j$ as
high-HHI if $\text{HHI}_j$ exceeds the sample median and
low-HHI otherwise; orthogonally, $j$ is high-pairs if it shares
at least two tenders with at least one repeat partner among
winners, and low-pairs otherwise. The $2\!\times\!2$
cross-classification yields four cells, and we estimate
Equation~\eqref{eq:ols_main} with cell interactions to
characterize how the screening signal varies across cells. The
specification anchors the cell-heterogeneity discussion of
§\ref{sec:mech_failed}; we report the cell interpretation as
descriptive scope of the screening object, not as a positive
test of cartel-rotation predictions.
