\subsection{Estimand and Identifying Stance}
\label{sec:emp_estimand}
% ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

The empirical object is $\beta$, the conditional mean difference
in log negotiated price between tender-items that include at
least one frequent-loser participant and otherwise comparable
tender-items in the same item type, year, and procuring unit. We
read $\beta$ throughout as a \emph{conditional association},
not as a causal effect. The descriptive stance is by design: a
screen does not need to identify a causal parameter to allocate
oversight case-load, and the operational pathway in
§\ref{sec:conclusion} requires only that the construct flag
environments at higher prior risk than random selection would.

Two design-based strategies that would extract a causal estimate
from the data are reported and return null. A sharp regression
discontinuity at the convite/preg\~ao statutory value caps is
gated by the absence of bunching: McCrary-style density tests
around the $\valCapOldR$ cap return a left/right ratio of
$\valMcCraryRatio$ and a log-density discontinuity of
$\valMcCraryDisc$ (Table~\ref{tab:mccrary}); the convite share
drops cleanly across the cap from $\valPctSixnine$ below to
$\valPctFifnine$ above, consistent with rule-driven assignment
rather than strategic gaming. The local first-stage power is too
weak to support a fuzzy RDD at standard bandwidths
(Stock--Yogo $F$ below threshold). A
difference-in-differences design using Decreto~$\valDecreto$'s
$2018$ raise of the convite cap fails the parallel-trends
diagnostic at the available pre-treatment window. We report
both failures openly and treat the empirical content as
descriptive.

The descriptive claim is calibrated through four identification
audits that operate alongside the reduced-form specification.
The \citet{cinelli2020making} robustness value
$RV_{q=1} = \valRVqOne$ and the \citet{oster2019unobservable}
$\hat\delta = 261.64$ on the full sample bound the broad-sample
association against selection on observables of the kind
already absorbed by item, year, and procuring-unit fixed
effects. Strict-overlap matching restricts the comparison to
cells in which frequent-loser-present and -absent items
genuinely overlap on observables, returning a sign reversal that
we read as informative about selection within the always-loser
stratum. A leakage audit decomposes the in-sample item-level AUC
into a structural component (temporal-holdout AUC $0.864$) and
a pure-leakage component. A placebo using sub-threshold
always-losers as instrument confirms that the construct's price
imprint operates through the above-threshold population we
identify, not through generic always-loser supply. The four
audits are reported in §\ref{sec:rob_identification} and the
identification scope they delineate is consolidated in
§\ref{sec:lim_identification}.

\subsection{Reduced-Form Specification}
\label{sec:emp_reduced}
% ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

The reduced-form specification is
\begin{equation}
  y_{igt} = \beta \cdot \text{losers}_{igt}
  + \mathbf{x}_{igt}' \boldsymbol{\delta}
  + \alpha_g + \lambda_t + \gamma_k + \varepsilon_{igt}
  \label{eq:ols_main}
\end{equation}
where $y_{igt}$ is the outcome for tender-item $i$ in item group
$g$ at time $t$ and procuring unit $k$; $\text{losers}_{igt}$
indicates the presence of at least one frequent-loser participant;
$\mathbf{x}_{igt}$ includes a convite indicator and any
specification-specific controls; $\alpha_g$, $\lambda_t$, and
$\gamma_k$ are item, year, and procuring-unit fixed effects; and
$\varepsilon_{igt}$ is the disturbance, clustered at the item
level. The headline outcome is $\log p_{\text{negotiated}}$;
auxiliary outcomes (number of bidders, number of bids, number of
non-frequent-loser bidders) are reported in
§\ref{sec:results_ols} and the appendix.

We estimate Equation~\eqref{eq:ols_main} under four
specifications. The general within-item-and-year baseline drops
the procuring-unit fixed effect; the within-procuring-unit
specification adds it. Two modal subsamples, preg\~ao-only and
convite-only, decompose the headline along the institutional
contrast set up in §\ref{sec:institutional}. The estimation
sample comprises $\valSampleN$ tender-items
(§\ref{sec:data_sample}).

Three complementary estimators bracket Equation~\eqref{eq:ols_main}.
Cross-fit estimation defines the frequent-loser list on odd years,
estimates Equation~\eqref{eq:ols_main} on even years, and reverses
the assignment, recovering the structural component of $\beta$
free of within-sample classification noise. Matching estimators
adjust for selection on observables: coarsened exact matching
(CEM) on year, procedure type, item group, and procuring-unit-size
quartile, and inverse-probability weighting (IPW) on the propensity
of frequent-loser presence given the same observables. A
leave-one-out instrumental-variable specification uses the supply
of frequent losers active in other procuring units within the same
product market and year as instrument; we read $\hat\beta_{\text{IV}}$
as a measurement-error diagnostic rather than as a primary
estimator, since the binary frequent-loser indicator carries
classical attenuation toward zero when it proxies for the
underlying continuous loss-intensity statistic
(§\ref{sec:mech_horse_race}). The four estimators
(cross-fit, IPW, OLS, CEM) jointly bound the headline range
$\valHeadlineRange$ that we adopt as the reference throughout the
paper (§\ref{sec:results_ols}).

Standard errors are clustered at the item level as the baseline
choice. Robustness to procuring-unit clustering and to two-way
\citet{cameron2011robust} clustering is reported in
§\ref{sec:rob_threshold}; the point estimate is mechanical to
clustering choice, and significance is preserved under all three
regimes.

\subsection{Heterogeneity Specifications}
\label{sec:emp_heterogeneity}
% ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

We characterize heterogeneity in $\beta$ along two dimensions
that match distinct framework predictions. Both are descriptive
specifications of Equation~\eqref{eq:ols_main} estimated within
sub-samples; neither is institutional identification.

The first dimension is procuring-unit size, which the framework
of \ref{app:proofs} maps to the principal cost of
detection $\theta_k$ through the comparative static
$\partial m^*/\partial\theta_k < 0$. We split the sample into
quartiles of procuring-unit size, defined by annual contract
volume in BEC, and re-estimate Equation~\eqref{eq:ols_main}
within each quartile separately. Robustness to alternative
size measures (cumulative item count, headcount of distinct
purchasers) is reported alongside the headline result. The
specification anchors the procuring-unit-size oversight gradient
of §\ref{sec:results_oversight}.

The second dimension is the cell-heterogeneity grid in winner
concentration and repeated-pair density. For each frequent-loser
firm $j$, we compute the Herfindahl--Hirschman Index of winning
firms across all tenders in which $j$ participates,
$\text{HHI}_j = \sum_{w} s_{wj}^2$, where $s_{wj}$ is the share
of $j$'s tenders won by firm $w$. We classify $j$ as
high-HHI if $\text{HHI}_j$ exceeds the sample median and
low-HHI otherwise; orthogonally, we classify $j$ as
high-pairs if $j$ shares at least two tenders with at least one
repeat partner among the winners, and low-pairs otherwise. The
$2\!\times\!2$ cross-classification yields four cells, and we
estimate Equation~\eqref{eq:ols_main} with cell interactions to
characterize whether the conditional price gap varies across
cells. The specification anchors the cell-heterogeneity
discussion of §\ref{sec:mech_failed}; we adopt the cell
interpretation as descriptive scope, not as a positive test of
cartel-rotation predictions.
