\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This paper introduces \emph{frequent losers}---firms that participate
repeatedly in public procurement auctions without ever
winning---as a participation-only cartel screen for the cover-bidder
population. The construct delivers three contributions. It is
\emph{deployable}: built entirely on contract-award records, it is
computable in any jurisdiction where the procurement registry
preserves participant identity, winner identity, and item
identifier, at near-zero marginal data cost. It is
\emph{empirically grounded}: in São Paulo's Bolsa Eletrônica de
Compras ($\valYearStart$--$\valYearEnd$), within-item, within-year,
within-procuring-unit comparisons across $\valSampleN$ tender-items
associate the presence of frequent losers with a
$\valHeadlineRange$ higher log negotiated unit price across
estimators, and the gap falls monotonically from
$\valOversightQonePct$ in the smallest procuring-unit-size quartile
to $\valOversightQfourPct$ in the largest---a
$\valPBUgradientRatio\!\!\times$ gradient consistent with the
$\partial m^*/\partial \theta_k < 0$ comparative static of a
separating-equilibrium framework with cover bidders and genuine
entrants. It is \emph{theoretically grounded}: the construct is
derived from that framework (\ref{app:proofs}), under
which $\log(1+\text{tenders\_count})$ is a sufficient ranking
statistic for cover-bidder type given award-record data, and the
bright-line filter $\text{wins}=0$ is the equilibrium choice of
cover bidders themselves.

The administrative pathway built on the construct has three stages.
\textbf{Screen} computes $\log(1+\text{tenders\_count})$ on award
records and flags top-$k$ environments by tender-item count; the
deployable binary rule selects firms with more than
$\valThresholdStat$ tender-items and zero wins, which in BEC
identifies $\valFL$ firms out of $\valAlwaysLosers$ always-losers
and discriminates $\valCobidders$ adjudicated CADE-cobidder firms
inside the always-loser stratum at AUC $\valAUCFLfirm$
($95\%$ CI $\valAUCFLfirmCI$). \textbf{Triage} cross-references
flagged firms with adjudication records (CADE, CGU, TCE) and the
operational priors of the oversight body, narrowing the case-load
that proceeds to the forensic stage. \textbf{Forensic} deploys
bid-distribution methods where bid microdata are available
\citep{imhof2019detecting,wallimann2023machine}; the construct
narrows the case-load that reaches this stage and does not replace
the methods that operate there.

The scope of the construct is deliberately narrow. It targets the
loser side of the cartel population---the cover-bidder face of
bid-rigging arrangements---and by design does not flag the
$\valDirectCADE$ direct CADE defendants in the broader BEC firm
universe (AUC $\valAUCdirectStd$): direct defendants in
adjudicated cases are typically frequent winners, while the
construct concentrates on participation footprints with persistent
zero wins. Identification is descriptive throughout. Two
design-based strategies (a sharp regression discontinuity at the
convite/preg\~ao statutory caps; a difference-in-differences using
the 2018 cap raise) return null at the available bandwidths, and
the empirical content is reported as a descriptive association
rather than a causal estimate. Competing readings of the same
participation footprint (capacity-transfer motives, market
intelligence) are observationally compatible at the firm level; we
read the price-gap and gradient evidence as consistent with cover
bidding while not ruling out those alternatives.

Portability rests on three structural features---commodity-heavy
item composition, electronic platforms with publicly auditable
participant lists, and institutional asymmetry between the winner
and loser sides of the bidding pool---shared by Brazilian state
platforms and broadly present in jurisdictions where bid microdata
are not centrally collected
\citep{decarolis2017corruption,coviello2017tenure,oecd2019government}.
The construct is a candidate screening tool for those
jurisdictions; the three structural features are diagnostic
prerequisites, not optional ones.

Three directions for future work follow from the design. First,
inter-jurisdictional replication: federal and inter-state Brazilian
procurement registries (ComprasNet, Banco do Brasil) preserve the
same award-record envelope as BEC, and the construct can be tested
on those registries with no modification. Second, integration with
forensic methods: where bid microdata are available, the construct
can serve as a pre-screen that raises the prior of detectors in the
\citet{imhof2019detecting} and \citet{wallimann2023machine} family,
selecting the case-load on which those methods operate. Third,
non-commodity settings: the framework's comparative static does
not depend on commodity composition, but the empirical scope of
this paper does, and whether the gradient survives in services or
infrastructure procurement is an open empirical question.

The frequent-loser construct screens for cartels through the
participation footprint of the cover-bidder population, not
through the bid behavior of cartel ringleaders. It points
oversight bodies at the environments where that footprint is
sharpest, especially the smallest procuring units, where the
conditional price gap is $\valPBUgradientRatio\!\!\times$ what it
is among the largest buyers. The contribution is administrative.
The value proposition is prioritization at near-zero marginal
data cost.
