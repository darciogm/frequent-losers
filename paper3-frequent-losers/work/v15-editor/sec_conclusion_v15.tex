\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

The bid-rigging detection literature has been built on the
assumption that bid-level data are observable. Real enforcement
environments routinely violate that assumption: many electronic
procurement systems preserve only the contract-award envelope,
and the bid-distribution methods that work on the bid layer
become inoperative on the layer that survives. The paper takes
this informational mismatch as its starting point and asks what
collusion-relevant statistic can be recovered when only the
award layer is observable.

Endogenous loser-side participation is such a statistic. A
separating-equilibrium framework with cover bidders and genuine
entrants identifies $\log(1+\text{tenders\_count})$ together
with the $\text{wins}=0$ filter as a sufficient ranking statistic
for cover-bidder type given award-record data; the binary rule
we deploy is its information-coarsening. The empirical exercise
on São Paulo's BEC ($\valYearStart$--$\valYearEnd$) finds a
broad-sample association of $\valHeadlineRange$ in the
conditional log negotiated unit price across four estimators,
discriminating power against adjudicated CADE cobidders at
firm-level AUC $\valAUCFLfirm$ in-sample and
$\valAUCFLfirmTemp$ under temporal holdout, and a
$\valMultThreeTwo\!\!\times$ contemporaneous excess against the
conservative pre-2020 benchmark. The continuous primitive
strictly dominates the binary rule, consistent with the
framework's identification of the loss-intensity statistic. The
construct does not discriminate direct CADE defendants in the
broader BEC firm universe (AUC $\valAUCdirectStd$): the
asymmetry between cobidder and direct-defendant labels is the
design's empirical signature, not a failure.

The screening framing reorganizes how the empirical evidence is
read. The broad-sample estimate is informative about the
participation footprint where the cartel deploys it, integrated
over the deployment distribution; the overlap-restricted estimate
($\valMatchOverlapCoef$ to $\valMatchPSCoef$) is the
within-support comparison at items where treated and control
populations overlap on observables. The two are different
empirical objects; the sign reversal between them is the
diagnostic that locates the construct as a screening-value
object rather than a treatment-effect object. The buyer-size
gradient ($\valPBUgradientRatio\!\!\times$ across procuring-unit
quartiles) is read as variation in the screening signal across
detection regimes, not as identification of a causal channel
through which monitoring infrastructure operates on prices.
Three independent corroborations support the screening
reading: cartel-adjacency validation moves with the broad-sample
association; the continuous statistic delivers a model-predicted
dose-response; sensitivity bounds discipline selection on
observables of the kind the fixed effects already absorb.

Two implications for enforcement design follow. First, what
survives data coarsening is not a moment of submitted bids but
the equilibrium-generated participation footprint that the
collusive arrangement leaves on the award layer. The
information that the bid-layer literature reads off bid
distributions is partially recoverable from a different
statistic at the award layer when bid data are unavailable, and
the screening object the recovery delivers is a participation
statistic rather than a price moment. Second, the architecture
of enforcement under incomplete observability separates an
award-layer screening stage from a bid-layer forensic stage. The
screening stage produces candidate flags; the forensic stage
adjudicates suspected coordination on the bid distribution where
microdata exist. The two stages answer different questions
and require different statistics. The construct fills the first;
the bid-distribution literature fills the second; the
benchmark in \S\ref{sec:rob_imhof} confirms that the two
operate on the same underlying cases without dominating each
other.

The contribution stops at the screening stage by design. The
construct does not adjudicate cartel membership and cannot do so
under coarsened observability; the cobidder population is the
structurally appropriate validation object given the data layer.
Adversarial adaptation degrades the construct's discrimination
predictably (Online Appendix~G), and replication beyond São Paulo
remains an open empirical question that the construct's three
prerequisites---a winner-loser participation registry, an
electronic platform, and an institutional asymmetry between the
two sides of the bidding pool---will determine. The conceptual
content travels: wherever an enforcement environment exposes the
award layer without exposing the bid layer, the question is not
whether bid-distribution screens can be retrofitted but what
participation primitive the collusive arrangement generates that
survives the coarsening, and how the screening stage that reads
that primitive should be sequenced before the forensic stage
that requires bid-level evidence.
