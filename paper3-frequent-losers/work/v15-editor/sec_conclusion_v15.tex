\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

The participation-based screen we build extracts usable signal
for procurement triage from the data envelope every procurement
system already maintains. Frequent-loser presence on contract-award
records associates with a $\valHeadlineRange$ higher conditional
log negotiated unit price across estimators, and discriminates
cartel-adjacent populations at rates well above random matching:
AUC $\valAUCprePost$ on a conservative contemporaneous benchmark
restricted to four pre-2020 cases, and $\valAUCFLfirmTemp$ on temporal
holdout against a more permissive prospective benchmark. These
numbers describe the screen's operational footprint, not cartel
membership; the validation object is adjacency to documented
collusion, and the construct discriminates direct CADE defendants
in the broader BEC firm universe at AUC $\valAUCdirectStd$, by
design.

Two interpretive caveats anchor the reading. The conditional
price association reverses sign under strict-overlap matching
($\valMatchOverlapCoef$ to $\valMatchPSCoef$): the broad-sample
estimate combines a within-support comparison with the
systematic differences between items frequent losers select into
and items they do not, and the screen's signal turns on the
latter as much as on the former. The price
imprint shrinks $\valPBUgradientRatio\!\!\times$ across
buyer-size quartiles, consistent with thinner institutional
capacity moderating the imprint at smaller buyers, but buyer
size proxies for several correlated features and we do not
identify oversight as the operative channel.

The screen is designed for triage, computable on award records
alone, and integrates with bid-distribution methods where bid
microdata are available
\citep{imhof2019detecting,wallimann2023machine}. Adversarial
adaptation degrades performance predictably (Online Appendix~G),
replication beyond São Paulo remains an open empirical question,
and the contribution stops short of cartel detection by design.
