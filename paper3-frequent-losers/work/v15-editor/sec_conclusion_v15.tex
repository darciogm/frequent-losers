\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Award-record-based loser-side participation contains usable signal
for screening suspicious procurement environments when bid
microdata are unavailable. We document three findings, in
descending order of empirical strength.

First, frequent-loser presence associates with a $\valHeadlineRange$
higher conditional log negotiated unit price across estimators in
the broad sample of $\valSampleN$ tender-items in BEC
($\valYearStart$--$\valYearEnd$). The association reverses sign
under strict-overlap matching ($-9.7\%$ overlap-cell ATT, $-30.7\%$
propensity-score-trimmed), which we treat as a first-order
interpretive feature: the broad sample combines a within-cell
association with the systematic differences between items frequent
losers select into and items they do not. The two estimates are
descriptively valid at different scopes of selection. A triage
tool does not need to resolve this tension to be useful; it needs
to flag environments at higher prior risk than random selection.
The construct does so.

Second, the signal discriminates cartel-adjacent populations at
rates well above random matching. The conservative benchmark is
the contemporaneous validation: against four CADE cases adjudicated
before $2020$, the construct discriminates cobidder firms at
AUC $\valAUCprePost$ ($95\%$ DeLong CI $[0.713, 0.783]$) with a
$\valMultThreeTwo\!\!\times$ excess over the
participation-stratified baseline. The prospective benchmark,
using CADE's full portfolio including post-sample adjudications,
discriminates at AUC $\valAUCFLfirm$ in-sample and $0.864$ under
temporal holdout. The validation object is cartel adjacency, not
cartel membership: cobidders are firms that participate alongside
adjudicated cartel members, an indirect label consistent with
several non-competitive readings. Against the $\valDirectCADE$
direct CADE defendants in the broader BEC firm universe, the
construct discriminates at AUC $\valAUCdirectStd$, by design: the
construct targets the loser side, not the winner side.

Third, the price imprint is heterogeneous across procuring-unit
size, falling from $\valOversightQonePct$ in the smallest
procuring-unit-size quartile to $\valOversightQfourPct$ in the
largest---a $\valPBUgradientRatio\!\!\times$ ratio. We read the
gradient as consistent with thinner institutional capacity
moderating the imprint at smaller buyers, but buyer size proxies
for several correlated institutional features (procurement-officer
tenure, internal-audit infrastructure, item composition,
discretionary procedure use), and we do not separately identify
oversight as the operative channel. The policy implication
(oversight bodies operating on small-buyer environments face
thinner ex-ante information) is robust to the mechanism
interpretation; the mechanism interpretation is not.

The construct is built entirely from contract-award records. We
translate it into a three-stage administrative pathway---Screen,
Triage, Forensic. \textbf{Screen} computes
$\log(1+\text{tenders\_count})$ on award records and flags top-$k$
environments. \textbf{Triage} cross-references flagged firms with
adjudication records and the operational priors of the oversight
body. \textbf{Forensic} deploys bid-distribution methods where bid
microdata are available
\citep{imhof2019detecting,wallimann2023machine}; the construct
narrows the case-load that reaches this stage. Portability rests
on three structural features---commodity-heavy item composition,
electronic platforms with publicly auditable participant lists, and
institutional asymmetry between the winner and loser sides of the
bidding pool---shared by Brazilian state platforms and broadly
present in jurisdictions where bid microdata are not centrally
collected
\citep{decarolis2017corruption,coviello2017tenure,oecd2019government}.

The contribution is narrower than detection. The construct does
not identify cover bidders, does not adjudicate firm-level guilt,
and does not identify a stable causal effect of cover bidding on
prices. It is an award-record-based risk indicator that flags
procurement environments statistically associated with higher
prices and cartel-adjacent ground truth. Whether the signal
extends to other jurisdictions (federal Brazilian registries,
other state platforms, non-commodity settings) is an open
empirical question. Whether the signal is stable under deliberate
adversarial adaptation (firms splitting CNPJs, rotating cover
bidders) is a separate operational question. Within its narrow
scope, the construct provides oversight bodies with a triage tool
computable at near-zero marginal data cost on the data envelope
every procurement system already maintains.
