\section{Cover-Bidding Framework and Proofs}\label{app:proofs}

This appendix formalizes the separating-equilibrium framework
referenced throughout the main text. The framework supports four
results that the empirical exercise reads against. A lemma
establishes the $\text{wins}=0$ filter as the equilibrium choice
of the cover-bidder type (Lemma~\ref{lem:wins_zero_stmt}). A
proposition identifies $\log(1+\text{tenders\_count})$ as a
sufficient ranking statistic for the cover-bidder type given
award-record data (Proposition~\ref{prop:exposure_stmt}). A
proposition characterizes the cartel's optimal deployment rate
$m^{*}$ in the principal cost of detection $\theta_k$
(Proposition~\ref{prop:optimal_m}). And a final proposition
formalizes why the broad-sample association
$\beta=\mathbb{E}[y\mid\text{losers}=1,\mathbf{x}]-\mathbb{E}[y\mid\text{losers}=0,\mathbf{x}]$
and the overlap-restricted association
$\beta^{\text{ov}}=\mathbb{E}[y\mid\text{losers}=1,\mathbf{x},\text{overlap}]-\mathbb{E}[y\mid\text{losers}=0,\mathbf{x},\text{overlap}]$
are different empirical objects under the framework's deployment
problem, with the contrast between them diagnostic of the
deployment sorting that the screening statistic captures
(Proposition~\ref{prop:screening_value}). The framework is an
organizing device for the empirical patterns; it is not a source
of independent causal identification.

\paragraph{Setup}
A cartel of $n$ firms in a procurement market controls a
designated winner with bid $b^*$ and deploys $m \geq 0$ cover
bidders, each submitting a bid $b_\ell > b^*$. Per-bidder
deployment cost is $c_1 > 0$; the cartel faces a detection
probability $\theta_k \in (0,1)$ that depends on oversight
capacity in market $k$ (smaller, less-supervised buyers have
lower $\theta_k$). The cartel maximizes expected surplus
$\pi(m, \theta_k, c_1)$, the difference between cartel rents
and the sum of cover-bidder costs and expected detection
penalties. We impose two standard assumptions:

\begin{description}
\item[A1 (Profit positivity).] $\partial \pi / \partial m \big|_{m=0} > 0$:
the first cover bidder generates positive marginal cartel
surplus, otherwise no cartel deploys cover.
\item[A2 (Diminishing returns).] $\partial^2 \pi / \partial m^2 < 0$:
the surplus function is strictly concave in $m$, so an interior
optimum exists.
\end{description}

The empirical environment is described by award-record data:
each firm $i$ has a participation count $\text{tenders\_count}_i$,
a win indicator $\text{wins}_i \in \{0, 1\}$, and a tenure $\tau_i$.
The construct flags firms with $\text{wins}_i = 0$ and
$\text{tenders\_count}_i$ above the threshold
$\valThreshold$. Lemma~\ref{lem:wins_zero_stmt} below formalizes
the role of the wins-zero condition;
Proposition~\ref{prop:exposure_stmt} formalizes the role of the
participation-count ranking.

\begin{lemma}[Wins-zero separating equilibrium]\label{lem:wins_zero_stmt}
Suppose cover bidders draw their bid $b_C$ from a strategy that
satisfies (a) $b_C > b^*$ in equilibrium (cartel-allocation
constraint) and (b) winning triggers either capacity revelation
(undermining the cover identity in subsequent rounds) or internal
cartel sanctions on the deviating firm. Genuine entrants of type
$G$ face no such constraint and accept any $b_G < b^*$ that
yields positive expected payoff. Then in the separating
equilibrium, cover bidders satisfy
$\Pr(\text{win} \mid C) = 0$: the bright-line filter
$\text{wins}=0$ is the equilibrium choice of type $C$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:wins_zero_stmt}]
Write the cover-bidder per-period payoff as
$u_C(b) = -c_1 - \theta_k \phi_0 \cdot \mathbf{1}[\text{win}]
       - \kappa \cdot \mathbf{1}[\text{win}]$, where $\phi_0 > 0$
is the marginal expected detection penalty per cover bid and
$\kappa > 0$ captures expected cartel-internal sanction or
capacity-revelation cost. Choosing $b_C > b^*$ delivers
$\Pr(\text{win} \mid b_C, b^*) = 0$ in any equilibrium where the
designated winner submits $b^*$, so $u_C = -c_1$. Any deviation
to $b_C' < b^*$ delivers
$u_C' = -c_1 - \theta_k \phi_0 - \kappa + \mathrm{rents}$, where
$\mathrm{rents}$ is the deviation gain from winning. Under the
cartel's allocation rule, $\mathrm{rents} < \kappa$ for any cover
bidder (otherwise the allocation would not be
incentive-compatible). Hence $u_C > u_C'$, and the equilibrium
strategy is $b_C > b^*$ with $\Pr(\text{win} \mid C) = 0$. By
contrast, type $G$ has no $\kappa$ penalty and accepts $b_G < b^*$
when expected payoff is positive. Hence the empirical filter
$\text{wins} = 0$ identifies the type-$C$ subset, conditional on
the cartel deploying its allocation rule. \qed
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Linearized cumulative exposure]\label{prop:exposure_stmt}
Suppose firm $i$ is a cover bidder of type $C$ deployed by a
cartel in market $k$ at per-period rate
$\lambda_C(\theta_k, c_1)$ and tenure $\tau_i$. Under A1--A2 and a
stationary deployment process,
\[
  \mathbb{E}\!\left[\,\text{tenders\_count}_i \mid
                   \text{type}=C,\,\tau_i\right]
  \;=\; \lambda_C(\theta_k, c_1)\,\tau_i.
\]
For genuine competitive entrants of type $G$,
$\mathbb{E}[\text{tenders\_count}_i \mid \text{type}=G, \tau_i]
= \lambda_G\,\tau_i$ with $\lambda_G < \lambda_C$ in any
cartel-targeted market: by Proposition~\ref{prop:optimal_m}
below, $m^* > 0$ implies positive equilibrium deployment of
cover bidders, while genuine entry does not respond to cartel
demand for participation slots. The log-statistic
$\log(1+\text{tenders\_count}_i)$ is therefore monotone in
$\lambda$ given tenure, and---under monotone likelihood ratio in
the Poisson approximation
$T_i \sim \mathrm{Poisson}(\lambda \tau_i)$---the posterior
$\Pr(\text{type}=C \mid \text{tenders\_count}_i = t)$ is
non-decreasing in $t$. Combined with
Lemma~\ref{lem:wins_zero_stmt}, the joint statistic
$(\log(1+\text{tenders\_count}),\,\mathbf{1}[\text{wins}=0])$ is
a sufficient screen for the cover-bidder type given award-record
data; the binary frequent-loser rule is the information-coarsening
of this latent posterior under the separating equilibrium.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:exposure_stmt}]
The expectation result is immediate from the law of large
numbers applied to the deployment counting process given
constant rate $\lambda$. The monotonicity claim follows from
Bayes' rule:
\[
  \Pr(C \mid t) =
  \frac{\Pr(t \mid C)\,\pi_C}
       {\Pr(t \mid C)\,\pi_C + \Pr(t \mid G)\,(1-\pi_C)},
\]
which is non-decreasing in $t$ if and only if the likelihood
ratio $\Pr(t \mid C)/\Pr(t \mid G)$ is non-decreasing in $t$.
For Poisson likelihoods with rates $\lambda_C > \lambda_G$, this
monotone-likelihood-ratio property is standard. Hence
$\log(1+\text{tenders\_count})$ is a sufficient ranking statistic
for the cover-bidder type, and the binary indicator
$\mathbf{1}[\text{tenders\_count} > k]$ is its
information-coarsening form. \qed
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Optimal cover-bidder deployment]\label{prop:optimal_m}
Under A1--A2, the cartel's optimal cover-bidder deployment rate
$m^*$ satisfies the first-order condition
$\partial \pi / \partial m = c_1 + \theta_k \phi_0$, where
$\phi_0 > 0$ is the marginal expected detection penalty per
cover bidder evaluated at the optimum. The implicit comparative
statics are
\[
  \frac{\partial m^*}{\partial \theta_k}
  = -\,\frac{\phi_0}{\partial^2 \pi / \partial m^2} \;<\; 0,
  \qquad
  \frac{\partial m^*}{\partial c_1}
  = -\,\frac{1}{\partial^2 \pi / \partial m^2} \;<\; 0.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:optimal_m}]
The first-order condition follows directly from differentiating
$\pi(m, \theta_k, c_1)$ with respect to $m$ and equating the
marginal cartel surplus to the marginal cost of the cover bidder
($c_1$ deployment cost plus expected detection penalty
$\theta_k \phi_0$). By A2, $\partial^2 \pi / \partial m^2 < 0$,
so the implicit interior solution $\tilde m$ defined by the FOC
is unique. Implicit differentiation of the FOC yields the
comparative statics displayed, both negative because the
denominator is negative. The empirical implication is that
cover-bidder deployment is more aggressive where the procuring
unit's principal cost of detection is lower
(§\ref{sec:results_oversight}). \qed
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Why the screening object differs from a treatment-effect
target}
Propositions~\ref{prop:exposure_stmt} and~\ref{prop:optimal_m}
together imply that frequent-loser participation is not
distributed uniformly over items: under A1--A2, the cartel
deploys cover bidders preferentially in items where its expected
surplus is highest, conditional on the deployment cost
$c_1$ and the local detection probability $\theta_k$. Items
the cartel selects into therefore differ from items it does not
on a vector of unobserved characteristics $\mathbf{u}$ that
governs the cartel's expected surplus---chiefly, the rents
available from the designated winner's bid relative to the
competitive equilibrium price, the local detection environment,
and item-level characteristics that shape the cartel's
allocation rule. None of these unobservables is captured by the
fixed-effects controls or by the matching covariates: by
construction, item, year, and procuring-unit dummies absorb
sources of variation that are common to items within their
respective strata, and the matching covariates are observable
descriptors of the items themselves, while $\mathbf{u}$ is the
unobservable component of the cartel's deployment optimum.

The following proposition formalizes the implication for the
two empirical objects $\beta$ and $\beta^{\text{ov}}$.

\begin{proposition}[Screening-value vs.~treatment-effect
objects]\label{prop:screening_value}
Let $D_i \in \{0,1\}$ indicate frequent-loser presence in
item~$i$, $\mathbf{x}_i$ the vector of observables absorbed by
fixed effects and matching covariates, and $\mathbf{u}_i$ the
vector of unobservables governing the cartel's deployment
optimum (rents available, local detection environment,
item-specific allocation features). Suppose the cartel's
optimal deployment satisfies
$\Pr(D_i=1 \mid \mathbf{x}_i, \mathbf{u}_i)
= g(\mathbf{x}_i, \mathbf{u}_i)$ with $g$ strictly increasing in
the rent component of $\mathbf{u}_i$. Then:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Broad-sample $\beta$.} The conditional mean
difference
$\beta = \mathbb{E}[y_i \mid D_i=1, \mathbf{x}_i] -
        \mathbb{E}[y_i \mid D_i=0, \mathbf{x}_i]$
integrates over the distribution
$f(\mathbf{u}_i \mid D_i = 1, \mathbf{x}_i)$ versus
$f(\mathbf{u}_i \mid D_i = 0, \mathbf{x}_i)$. Under the cartel's
deployment optimum, these two conditional distributions of
$\mathbf{u}_i$ are systematically different in the rent
component: items the cartel selects into have
unobservable-shifted rents.
\item \emph{Overlap-restricted $\beta^{\text{ov}}$.} The
overlap-restricted comparison
$\beta^{\text{ov}} = \mathbb{E}[y_i \mid D_i=1, \mathbf{x}_i,
\Omega] - \mathbb{E}[y_i \mid D_i=0, \mathbf{x}_i, \Omega]$,
where $\Omega$ denotes the common-support cell, restricts
attention to items in which $D_i=1$ and $D_i=0$ have non-trivial
joint density given $\mathbf{x}_i$. Within $\Omega$, the
conditional distributions of $\mathbf{u}_i$ are by construction
more comparable: items where the cartel deploys at the
$\mathbf{x}_i$-margin must compete with items where the cartel
does not deploy despite similar observables, so the rent
component of $\mathbf{u}_i$ that drives the deployment optimum
must be smaller (otherwise the cartel would deploy with
near-certainty there as well).
\item \emph{Diagnostic.} If the rent component of $\mathbf{u}_i$
that drives the deployment optimum also moves prices in the same
direction (rent-rich items both attract cartel deployment and
realize higher prices conditional on observables), then
$\beta>0$ on the broad sample while $\beta^{\text{ov}}<0$ is
\emph{the prediction} of the deployment problem, not its
refutation: forcing overlap removes the cells where rents are
high, which removes precisely the items where the participation
footprint and the price imprint co-occur.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:screening_value}]
Claim 1 follows from the law of iterated expectations: the
broad-sample $\beta$ averages $\mathbb{E}[y_i \mid D_i,
\mathbf{x}_i, \mathbf{u}_i]$ over the conditional distributions
of $\mathbf{u}_i$ given $D_i$ and $\mathbf{x}_i$. The cartel's
deployment optimum implies that $\mathbf{u}_i \mid D_i=1$ has a
higher rent component than $\mathbf{u}_i \mid D_i=0$ at the same
$\mathbf{x}_i$, by Proposition~\ref{prop:optimal_m}. Claim 2
follows from the definition of common support: cells in $\Omega$
are those where the propensity score
$\Pr(D_i=1 \mid \mathbf{x}_i)$ is bounded away from $0$ and $1$,
which by the strict monotonicity of $g$ in the rent component
implies that the rent component takes intermediate values. The
$\mathbf{u}_i$-conditional distributions across $D_i=1$ and
$D_i=0$ within $\Omega$ are therefore more comparable than across
the broad sample. Claim 3 is the comparative statement: if the
rent component shifts both the deployment probability and the
realized price in the same direction, then averaging over
$\mathbf{u}_i$ on the broad sample produces a positive
$\beta$ while restricting to $\Omega$ trims the high-rent cells
out of the average, leaving a $\beta^{\text{ov}}$ that need not
be positive and may be negative for reasons unrelated to the
treatment itself. \qed
\end{proof}

The proposition formalizes the screening-value reading in
§\ref{sec:results_overlap}. The broad-sample $\beta$ is
informative because it integrates over the distribution of
items where the cartel deploys; under the screening framing of
§\ref{sec:emp_estimand} this integration is the empirical object
of interest. The overlap-restricted $\beta^{\text{ov}}$ trims
out exactly the cells where the deployment optimum places the
cartel, which is what produces the sign reversal under
Proposition~\ref{prop:screening_value}'s premise. The model does
not by itself adjudicate whether the rent-component-co-movement
premise holds; the empirical evidence of §\ref{sec:cade}
(cartel-adjacency validation moves with $\beta$),
§\ref{sec:mech_horse_race} (continuous primitive dose-response),
and §\ref{sec:results_ols} (sensitivity bounds) are the three
independent observations that, together, make the screening-value
reading more consistent with the data than a confound-only
reading of $\beta$.

\section{Adversarial-Adaptation Simulation}
\label{app:adversarial}

A sophisticated cartel could adapt its deployment to evade the
construct. We examine four strategic adaptations that hold the
construct fixed and modify the always-loser pool to mimic
plausible evasive behavior, then recompute the firm-level AUC of
$\log(1+\text{tenders\_count})$ against the $\valCobidders$
CADE-cobidder labels.

\input{./output/tables/tab_adversarial_adaptation}

The construct is resilient to rotation ($20\%$ of cover bidders
retire annually and are replaced by fresh CNPJs) and to
occasional wins ($5\%$ of threshold-crossing firms win one
tender): AUC is essentially unchanged because the bright-line
filter eliminates the small share of newly-winning cover bidders
and rotation does not compress the ranking statistic. CNPJ
splitting (each frequent loser splits into two CNPJs carrying
half the participation count) degrades AUC by
$\valAdaptCNPJAbs$ percentage points; threshold-aware capping
(firms cap participation at $13$ tender-items to stay below the
cutoff) degrades AUC by $\valAdaptCapAbs$ points; the combined
attack (capping plus $20\%$ rotation) drives AUC from
$\valAUCBase$ to $\valAUCAdaptCombined$, a
$\valAdaptCombinedAbs$-point compression.

The construct is therefore informative absent adaptation and
degrades predictably under specified attacks; the threshold rule
is the most exposed margin. Practical lifetime depends on
whether adaptation is observable and whether the threshold can
be periodically refreshed, and the screen should be combined
with bid-distribution methods where bid microdata are available
so that joint detection survives adaptation against either
component alone.
