\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

The empirical content of the paper has a deliberately narrow scope.
This section consolidates the limitations along three dimensions:
what identification can deliver in §\ref{sec:lim_identification},
what the construct flags and what it does not in
§\ref{sec:lim_construct}, and how the empirical claim travels
beyond São Paulo's electronic procurement platform in
§\ref{sec:lim_external}.

% ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\subsection{Identification scope}
\label{sec:lim_identification}
% ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Identification is descriptive throughout the paper. Two
design-based strategies that would extract a causal estimate from
the data return null at the available bandwidths: a sharp
regression discontinuity at the convite/preg\~ao statutory caps,
and a difference-in-differences exploiting the 2018 cap raise. The
first is gated by the absence of a McCrary-test-passing density
around the institutional thresholds in the BEC sample; the second
is gated by the absence of a clean pre-trend in the relevant
outcome series. We report the failures openly and treat the
empirical content as a descriptive association rather than as a
causal estimate.

The strict-overlap matching exercise developed in §\ref{sec:results_ols}
and audited in §\ref{sec:rob_identification} is the most
informative single observation about the limits of the descriptive
claim. The within-PBU coefficient of $\valPctSixfour$ on the broad
sample reverses to $-9.7\%$ under matching restricted to overlap
cells and to $-30.7\%$ under propensity-score trimming
(Table~\ref{tab:item_level_scope_match}). Neither the broad-sample
association nor the strict-overlap inversion carries causal weight
on its own. Both are conditional descriptions of the same
population from different angles: the broad-sample gap captures
the systematic differences between items frequent losers select
into and items they do not, and the strict-overlap inversion
removes those differences by construction. The sensitivity
bounds---\citet{cinelli2020making} robustness value
$\valRVqOne$ and the \citet{oster2019unobservable} $\hat\delta$ of
$261.64$ on the full sample---discipline the broad-sample
association against selection on observables of the kind already
captured by item, year, and procuring-unit fixed effects, but they
do not extend to the selection on unobservables that strict-overlap
matching surfaces.

What this implies for the paper's claims is concrete. The
$\valHeadlineRange$ headline range is a conditional descriptive
association across estimators, not an estimate of cover-bidding's
causal effect on prices. The $\valPBUgradientRatio\!\!\times$
procuring-unit-size oversight gradient is consistent with the
framework's comparative static $\partial m^*/\partial \theta_k < 0$,
but the consistency is observational; the gradient does not
identify the causal channel through which oversight infrastructure
moderates the participation footprint. For the construct's
intended use---prioritizing oversight case-load on environments
where the participation footprint is institutionally
structured---this descriptive scope is sufficient. A screen does
not need to identify causal effects to allocate scarce auditing
resources; it needs to flag environments at higher prior risk than
random selection would, and to do so on data the oversight body
already holds.

\subsection{Construct scope}
\label{sec:lim_construct}
% ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

The construct flags loser-side participation patterns, not cartel members. Inside
the always-loser stratum where the construct operates, it
discriminates the $\valCobidders$ adjudicated CADE-cobidder firms
at firm-level AUC $\valAUCFLfirm$ ($95\%$ CI $\valAUCFLfirmCI$) in
sample and at $0.864$ ($95\%$ CI $[0.859, 0.870]$) under temporal
holdout (§\ref{sec:rob_operational}). Against the
$\valDirectCADE$ direct CADE defendants in the broader BEC firm
universe, the construct discriminates at AUC $\valAUCdirectStd$
---random. The asymmetry is by design, not a failure: direct
defendants in adjudicated cases are typically frequent winners,
while the construct concentrates on participation footprints
characterized by persistent zero wins. A deploying oversight body
that wants to detect cartel ringleaders directly will need a
different tool; the construct flags the loser side of the cartel
population, and the empirical AUC differential against the two
populations is what that scope looks like in the data.

The participation footprint the construct flags is observationally
compatible with at least three non-competitive readings, and our
evidence does not adjudicate among them at the firm level. The
working reading we adopt throughout the paper is cover bidding
under cartel rotation, derived from the framework in
Online Appendix~A. Two competing readings remain: a
\emph{capacity-transfer} reading, in which firms maintain bidding
presence to signal capacity availability for subcontracting or for
affiliate consortia; and a \emph{market-intelligence} reading, in
which firms participate to observe prices, identify procurement
schedules, or build operational priors on competitor behavior.
These readings are not mutually exclusive, and the same firm can
have multiple non-competitive motives. The price imprint of
§\ref{sec:results_ols} and the oversight gradient of
§\ref{sec:results_oversight} are observationally consistent with
all three. We adopt the cover-bidding reading because the framework
derives from it and because the strongest heterogeneity
dimensions---the modal asymmetry between preg\~ao and convite
(§\ref{sec:mech_modal}) and the procuring-unit-size oversight
gradient (§\ref{sec:results_oversight})---match its comparative
statics most directly. The adoption is a working interpretation,
not an identification claim.

Two further mechanism predictions that the framework generates
remain undiscriminated by the data. The cell-heterogeneity
prediction across HHI and repeated-pair density places the largest
positive price gap in the Low-HHI $\times$ Low-pairs cell, in the
predicted direction (§\ref{sec:mech_failed}); but this cell is
also the largest cell by item count, leaving the configuration
observationally compatible with both the structural prediction and
a generic compositional explanation. The first-time-FL prediction
is in the predicted direction throughout
($+\valFTpsCoef$ to $+\valFTuncondCoef$ across specifications),
with conditional matching compressing the magnitude substantially
without inverting it (§\ref{sec:mech_failed}). Neither mechanism
prediction is refuted; neither is cleanly confirmed. The construct
flags a participation footprint with multiple non-competitive
readings, the supporting evidence places it in the framework's
predicted direction along every dimension we examine, and the
strength of the supporting evidence is highest where the design
provides the cleanest leverage.

\subsection{External validity and portability}
\label{sec:lim_external}
% ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

The empirical evidence in this paper comes from a single
jurisdiction and a single time window: São Paulo's Bolsa
Eletrônica de Compras, $\valYearStart$--$\valYearEnd$. The
construct's portability beyond this envelope rests on three
structural features of the procurement environment, all explicit
in BEC and identifiable in any candidate jurisdiction before
deployment: a commodity-heavy item composition that supports
within-product-code price comparisons; an electronic platform
with publicly auditable participant lists, so that participation
counts can be computed without privileged data access; and an
institutional asymmetry between the winner and loser sides of the
bidding pool that makes persistent zero wins informative about
strategic non-competitive participation rather than about
randomized matching outcomes. These are diagnostic conditions, not
optional ones.

Settings that this paper does not test directly are several. The
federal Brazilian procurement registry (ComprasNet) and other
state-level registries (Banco do Brasil, individual UF platforms)
preserve the same award-record envelope as BEC and are accessible
through the same legal regime; replication is technically
straightforward and substantively pending. Non-commodity
settings---services, infrastructure, public works---are within
the framework's logical scope but outside the empirical scope of
the present work, and the within-item identifying variation that
underwrites the headline price gap is not equally available in
those settings. Time periods outside the sample window are
likewise untested: the BEC sample begins after the platform's
operational stabilization and ends before the consolidation of
the new federal procurement law, and the construct's stability
across regulatory regimes is an open empirical question.

The new federal procurement law (Lei~$14.133/2021$) consolidates
the preg\~ao-style electronic-auction format as the institutional
default for Brazilian federal and inter-state procurement. The
modal-asymmetry result of §\ref{sec:mech_modal}---that the
construct's signal-to-noise is highest in preg\~ao---means that
the policy direction is favorable to the construct's portability,
not adversarial. The construct's three structural prerequisites
are increasingly the structural defaults of Brazilian public
procurement, and the deployment case for state and federal
oversight bodies operating on the new institutional baseline is
correspondingly stronger. Portability remains contingent, not
free; but the contingencies the construct requires are
recognizable, observable, and---in the institutional direction
the country is moving---increasingly satisfied.
