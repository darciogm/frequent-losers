\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Brazilian electronic procurement has a peculiar feature:
thousands of firms register, prepare bids, show up auction after
auction, and never win. In São Paulo's Bolsa Eletrônica de
Compras (BEC, $\valYearStart$--$\valYearEnd$),
$\valAlwaysLosers$ firms bid without ever winning a tender-item,
and $\valFL$ of them participated in more than
$\valThresholdStat$ tender-items. We call these firms
\emph{frequent losers}, and ask whether their footprint in
contract-award records carries usable signal for procurement
oversight.

Bid-rigging detection in the empirical literature relies on bid
microdata that few jurisdictions audit at scale
\citep{porter1993detection,bajari2003deciding,imhof2019detecting,wallimann2023machine}.
The much thinner envelope universally preserved by procurement
systems---contract-award records with winner identity,
participant identity, item identifier, and negotiated
price---has not been systematically exploited. This paper builds
a participation-based screen computable on that envelope alone.
The screen prioritizes procurement environments for oversight;
it does not adjudicate cartel membership, and the empirical
content is narrow by design.

\paragraph{Three findings}
First, frequent-loser presence associates with a
$\valHeadlineRange$ higher log negotiated unit price across five
estimators applied to $\valSampleN$ tender-items, comparing
within item, year, and procuring unit. Second, the construct
discriminates $\valCobidders$ adjudicated CADE-cobidder firms
inside the always-loser stratum at AUC $\valAUCFLfirm$ in-sample
and $\valAUCFLfirmTemp$ under temporal holdout, and at AUC
$\valAUCprePost$ on a stricter contemporaneous benchmark
restricted to four pre-2020 cases. Cobidders are firms that
participate alongside adjudicated cartel members; the label is
indirect by construction. Against the $\valDirectCADE$ direct
CADE defendants in the broader BEC firm universe the screen
discriminates at AUC $\valAUCdirectStd$, by design: direct
defendants are typically frequent winners and lie outside the
target population. Third, the price imprint shrinks from
$\valOversightQonePct$ in the smallest procuring-unit-size
quartile to $\valOversightQfourPct$ in the largest---a
$\valPBUgradientRatio\!\!\times$ gradient consistent with thinner
institutional capacity at smaller buyers, though buyer size
proxies for several correlated features and we do not identify
oversight as the operative channel.

\paragraph{The interpretive challenge}
The broad-sample price association reverses sign under
strict-overlap matching: overlap-cell ATT
$\valMatchOverlapCoef$, propensity-score-trimmed
$\valMatchPSCoef$. The two estimates answer different empirical
questions about the same population: the broad-sample range
captures deployment-relevant selection into the items frequent
losers participate in, while overlap-restricted estimates
isolate within-support comparisons. We report the broad-sample
range as the headline because triage operates on the population
frequent losers do select into, not on a counterfactual matched
on observables. The construct's task is not to recover a uniform
treatment effect; it is to flag environments at higher prior
risk than random selection, on both the price association and
the cartel-adjacency validation.

\paragraph{Theory}
A separating-equilibrium framework with cover bidders and
genuine entrants (Online Appendix~A) rationalizes the
$\text{wins}=0$ filter and the $\log(1+\text{tenders\_count})$
ranking we use to order loss intensity. We treat the framework
as an organizing device, not a source of identification.

\paragraph{Scope}
The participation-based screen flags procurement environments
statistically associated with higher prices and cartel-adjacent
labels. It does not detect cartels, adjudicate firm-level guilt,
or identify a stable causal effect of cover bidding on prices,
and it complements rather than replaces bid-distribution methods
where bid microdata are available (\S\ref{sec:rob_imhof}).

The remainder of the paper proceeds in four blocks.
Sections~\ref{sec:literature}--\ref{sec:empirical} situate the
contribution, set out the institutional and legal framework,
describe the BEC data, and state the descriptive empirical
strategy alongside the design-based strategies that return null.
Section~\ref{sec:cade} reports the CADE validation with explicit
object discipline.
Sections~\ref{sec:results}--\ref{sec:mechanisms} present the
within-item price association, the buyer-size heterogeneity, and
the mechanism evidence. Section~\ref{sec:robustness} collects
the robustness work, and
Sections~\ref{sec:limitations}--\ref{sec:conclusion} catalog
scope limitations and conclude.
