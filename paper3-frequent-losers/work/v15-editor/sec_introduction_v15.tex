\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Brazilian electronic procurement has a peculiar feature:
thousands of firms register, prepare bids, show up auction after
auction, and never win. In São Paulo's Bolsa Eletrônica de
Compras (BEC, $\valYearStart$--$\valYearEnd$),
$\valAlwaysLosers$ firms bid without ever winning a tender-item,
and $\valFL$ of them participated in more than
$\valThresholdStat$ tender-items. We call these firms
\emph{frequent losers}.

Bid-rigging detection in the empirical literature relies on bid
microdata that few jurisdictions audit at scale
\citep{porter1993detection,bajari2003deciding,imhof2019detecting,wallimann2023machine}.
Procurement systems instead universally preserve a much thinner
envelope: contract-award records, with winner identity,
participant identity, item identifier, and negotiated price. The
question we ask is whether \emph{this} envelope alone carries
exploitable signal for triage. The answer is yes, but narrowly.
Loser-side participation patterns associate with both higher
negotiated prices and cartel-adjacent ground truth at rates above
random matching, but the association is unstable across scopes
and the validation labels are indirect. The participation-based
screen we build prioritizes procurement environments for
oversight; it does not adjudicate cartel membership.

\paragraph{Three findings, in descending order of empirical strength.}
Within-item, within-year, within-procuring-unit comparisons
across $\valSampleN$ tender-items associate frequent-loser
presence with a $\valHeadlineRange$ higher log negotiated unit
price across five estimators. Against CADE's cartel records,
the screen discriminates $\valCobidders$ adjudicated cobidder
firms inside the always-loser stratum at AUC $\valAUCFLfirm$
in-sample, $\valAUCFLfirmTemp$ under temporal holdout, and $\valAUCprePost$
under a stricter contemporaneous benchmark restricted to four
pre-2020 cases. Cobidders are firms that participate alongside
adjudicated cartel members; the label is indirect by
construction. Against the $\valDirectCADE$ direct CADE
defendants in the broader BEC firm universe, the screen
discriminates at AUC $\valAUCdirectStd$, by design: direct
defendants are typically frequent winners and lie outside the
target population. The price imprint shrinks from
$\valOversightQonePct$ in the smallest procuring-unit-size
quartile to $\valOversightQfourPct$ in the largest---a
$\valPBUgradientRatio\!\!\times$ ratio. We read the gradient as
consistent with thinner institutional capacity at smaller
buyers, but buyer size proxies for several correlated
institutional features and we do not separately identify
oversight as the operative channel.

\paragraph{The interpretive challenge.}
The broad-sample price association reverses sign under
strict-overlap matching: $\valMatchOverlapCoef$ overlap-cell ATT, $\valMatchPSCoef$
under propensity-score trimming. The broad-sample estimate
combines a within-cell association with the systematic
differences between items frequent losers select into and items
they do not; strict-overlap matching isolates the former. Both
scopes are descriptively valid. We report the broad-sample range
as the headline because it maps to the deployment question
(which environments to flag) and the screen targets the
population frequent losers do select into, not a counterfactual
matched on observables. A triage tool need not identify a stable
causal effect; it needs to flag environments at higher prior
risk than random selection, and on both the broad-sample
association and the cartel-adjacency validation, the screen does
so.

\paragraph{Theory.}
We organize the construct through a separating-equilibrium
framework with cover bidders and genuine entrants
(Online Appendix~A). The framework rationalizes the
$\log(1+\text{tenders\_count})$ ranking and the $\text{wins}=0$
filter, and it predicts a direction for the buyer-size gradient.
It is an organizing device, not a source of identification.

\paragraph{What this paper does and does not do.}
The participation-based screen flags procurement environments
statistically associated with higher prices and cartel-adjacent
validation targets. It is not a cartel detector, does not
adjudicate firm-level guilt, and does not identify a stable
causal effect of cover bidding on prices. The screen is designed
for triage on award records and integrates with bid-distribution
methods where bid microdata are available
(\S\ref{sec:rob_imhof}).

The remainder of the paper proceeds in four blocks.
Sections~\ref{sec:literature}--\ref{sec:empirical} situate the
contribution, set out the institutional and legal framework,
describe the BEC data, and state the descriptive empirical
strategy alongside the design-based strategies that return null.
Section~\ref{sec:cade} reports the CADE validation with explicit
object discipline.
Sections~\ref{sec:results}--\ref{sec:mechanisms} present the
within-item price association, the buyer-size heterogeneity, and
the mechanism evidence. Section~\ref{sec:robustness} collects
the robustness work, and
Sections~\ref{sec:limitations}--\ref{sec:conclusion} catalog
scope limitations and conclude.
