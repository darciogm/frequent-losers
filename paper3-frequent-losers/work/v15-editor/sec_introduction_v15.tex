\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

The empirical literature on cartel detection reads collusion off
the distribution of submitted bids. Within-tender variance,
kurtosis, skewness, and normalized spread are informative about
coordinated bidding when the bid-level record is preserved
\citep{porter1993detection,bajari2003deciding,imhof2019detecting,wallimann2023machine}.
Real enforcement environments rarely match this benchmark. Many
electronic procurement systems archive only the contract-award
layer---winner identity, participant identity, item code,
negotiated price---and export bid-by-bid records to operational
logs that competition authorities and audit courts query slowly,
partially, or not at all. The methods designed for the bid layer
become inoperative on the layer that survives.

We ask what collusion-relevant information remains under that
coarsening. A separating-equilibrium framework with cover
bidders and genuine entrants (Online Appendix~A) makes the
question tractable. Two results anchor the empirical strategy.
In equilibrium cover bidders satisfy
$\Pr(\text{win}\mid C)=0$: the $\text{wins}=0$ filter is a
separating choice, not a descriptive coincidence. And given
award-record data, $\log(1+\text{tenders\_count})$ is a
sufficient ranking statistic for cover-bidder type under a
monotone-likelihood-ratio Poisson approximation. The framework
treats endogenous sorting into cartel-affected items as
constitutive of the screening object, not as a confound. The
binary $\text{median}+1.5\!\times\!\text{IQR}$ rule is its
information-coarsening; we call firms above the threshold
\emph{frequent losers}.

We test the framework on São Paulo's Bolsa Eletrônica de Compras
(BEC, $\valYearStart$--$\valYearEnd$), an electronic platform
that preserves the award envelope across $\valSampleN$
tender-items. Of $\valBECfirms$ active firms, $\valAlwaysLosers$
have a zero win rate; $\valFL$ cross the threshold. Their
presence associates with a $\valHeadlineRange$ higher
conditional log negotiated unit price across four estimators
(cross-fit, OLS, CEM, IPW). Restricting to items where treated
and control overlap on observables reverses the sign
(overlap-cell ATT $\valMatchOverlapCoef$,
propensity-score-trimmed $\valMatchPSCoef$). Under the
framework's screening interpretation the reversal is diagnostic
rather than refutation: the overlap restriction strips
precisely the equilibrium sorting that the screening statistic
is designed to capture. Three independent corroborations support
the reading. The construct discriminates $\valCobidders$
adjudicated CADE cobidders inside the always-loser stratum at
firm-level AUC $\valAUCFLfirm$ in-sample, $\valAUCFLfirmTemp$
under temporal holdout, and $\valAUCprePost$ on a strict
pre-$2020$ contemporaneous benchmark; against the $\valDirectCADE$
direct CADE defendants in the broader BEC firm universe AUC is
$\valAUCdirectStd$---the design's empirical signature. The
continuous primitive strictly dominates the binary cutoff
(\citealt{delong1988comparing} test, $p<10^{-3}$). And the price
imprint varies $\valPBUgradientRatio\!\!\times$ across
procuring-unit-size quartiles, evidence that the screening
signal scales with the detection regime.

The contribution sits at the conjunction of three literatures,
each constrained by the data layer at which it operates.
Bid-coordination tests
\citep{bajari2003deciding,porter1993detection,conley2016detecting}
require a partition between colluders and a competitive fringe;
the construct supplies a candidate \emph{ex ante} firm-level
flag computable from award records. Bid-distribution screens
\citep{imhof2019detecting,wallimann2023machine} require bid
microdata; the construct runs on the layer that survives the
coarsening, and integrates with bid-distribution methods at the
forensic stage where microdata exist (§\ref{sec:forensic}).
Empirical work on cover bidding has identified cover bidders
\emph{ex post}, through leniency testimony or judicial discovery
\citep{porter1999ohio,pesendorfer2000study,asker2010leniency}; we
are not aware of a published prospective identification of
cover-bidder candidates from public award-record data. The
cumulative claim is structural: under incomplete observability,
the appropriate enforcement architecture separates an award-layer
screening stage that produces candidate flags from a bid-layer
forensic stage that adjudicates suspected coordination, and the
two stages answer different questions
\citep{becker1968crime,stigler1970optimum,harrington2008detecting}.
The policy implication is direct: proactive cartel screening
does not require bid-microdata archival as a precondition.
Jurisdictions that lack the bid layer can deploy the award-layer
stage on data they already maintain, and reforms that mandate
bid archival expand the forensic stage that the screening stage
feeds rather than substitute for it.

Three things the paper does not claim. The broad-sample
association is not a causal estimate of cover bidding's effect
on prices; the screening framing does not require it to be.
The buyer-size gradient is scope information about where the
screening signal varies, not identification of an oversight
channel. And the construct does not adjudicate cartel
membership: cobidders are the validation object the data layer
supports, and the AUC asymmetry against direct defendants is
the design's signature.

The paper proceeds in five blocks.
Section~\ref{sec:literature} situates the contribution against
the bid-coordination, bid-distribution, cover-bidding, and
enforcement-design literatures.
Section~\ref{sec:institutional} describes the BEC data layers
and the CADE adjudication portfolio.
Sections~\ref{sec:data}--\ref{sec:empirical} state the
participation primitive, the binary rule, and the descriptive
identifying stance, alongside the two design-based strategies
(RDD, DiD) that return null.
Sections~\ref{sec:cade}--\ref{sec:results} report the CADE
validation and the within-item price association, treating the
sign reversal under overlap restrictions as the
screening-value-vs-treatment-effect distinction.
Sections~\ref{sec:mechanisms}--\ref{sec:robustness} develop the
modal informativeness contrast, the detection-regime gradient,
and the threshold/identification audits.
Section~\ref{sec:forensic} tests the screening-vs-forensic-stage
architecture against bid-distribution detectors trained on bid
microdata.
Sections~\ref{sec:limitations}--\ref{sec:conclusion} catalog
scope limitations and conclude.
