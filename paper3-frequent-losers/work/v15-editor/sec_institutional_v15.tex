\section{Institutional Setting and the Observability Architecture}
\label{sec:institutional}

The empirical exercise requires three institutional facts about
the data layer at which BEC enforcement operates and the source
of the validation labels we use. We catalog these here and refer
the reader to standard treatments of the underlying procurement
statutes for further detail.

\paragraph{Two procurement modalities, two informational regimes}
Brazilian public procurement during our $\valYearStart$--$\valYearEnd$
sample is governed by Lei~$\valLeiOriginal$ and Lei~$\valLeiPregao$.
Two modalities dominate BEC and differ in observability and
monitoring structure. \emph{Convite} (Lei~$8.666/93$, Art.~$22$)
is a sealed-bid invitation procedure with a statutory three-bidder
quorum requirement and a low value cap. \emph{Pregão}
(Lei~$\valLeiPregao$) is a reverse electronic auction in which
bidders submit progressively lower offers in real time, with no
minimum-bidder requirement and a public bidding interface. The
two modalities therefore expose different bidding behaviors to
public visibility: convite preserves only the final award record;
pregão preserves a richer real-time interaction whose footprint
is observable to other bidders during the auction itself. We
treat the modal contrast as variation in the observability
environment that the construct's signal is read against, not as
the mechanism the construct exploits. The empirical exercise in
§\ref{sec:mech_modal} reads modal heterogeneity as
informativeness across detection regimes; that the price imprint
and the cobidder discrimination are sharper in pregão is a
property of the regime, not a falsification of an alternative
mechanism. The $2021$ reform (Lei~$14.133/2021$) postdates our
sample and consolidates pregão-style auctions as the default,
shifting the modal mix toward the regime in which the construct
is most informative.

\paragraph{The data layer that survives}
The construct operates on the data layer that BEC's analytical
warehouse exports: winner identity, participant identity, item
code, and negotiated price. Bid-by-bid records exist in the
operational system but are not routinely exported to the layer
that competition authorities and audit courts query.\footnote{In
TCE-SP audits we examined, requesting bid-level microdata from
the BEC operational system is a multi-step process that delays
investigations by weeks; the award-record layer returns results
in minutes.} The asymmetry is not Brazil-specific: EU
member-state platforms preserve bid microdata heterogeneously,
and the U.S.~federal system (FPDS-NG) preserves awards but not
bid history at the contracting-officer layer. The data-layer
mismatch between the bid-distribution-screen literature and the
operational records that survive at most enforcement
agencies---which the construct's screening problem is built
around---is therefore a feature of the architecture, not a
parochial Brazilian artifact.

\paragraph{Adjudication portfolio for validation}
The Conselho Administrativo de Defesa Econ\^omica (CADE) is the
federal competition authority that adjudicates bid-rigging cases
under Lei~$12.529/2011$, with leniency provisions
(Art.~$86$) generating the disclosure trail the cartel-detection
literature uses as ground truth. CADE's procurement-cartel
portfolio relevant to our sample comprises $12$ adjudicated
cases covering $65$ firm-defendants ($\valDirectCADE$ active in
BEC during the sample window), with final decisions spanning
$2015$--$2025$. Eight of the twelve were decided after our
$2019$ sample cut. The temporal structure makes the validation
in §\ref{sec:cade} partly prospective: the construct's
discrimination against the adjudicated population is computed on
participation patterns observable years before enforcement
adjudicated those patterns. The interval between operational
cartel activity and final adjudication---$4$ to $16$ years from
process opening to ruling in our portfolio---is the relevant
benchmark for the screening-stage interpretation of the
contribution: an award-layer screen that flags candidate
environments at $t$ accelerates the path to forensic investigation
relative to the post-adjudication identification on which the
prior literature relies.

The administrative-audit institutions surrounding BEC (TCE-SP,
CGE) supervise procurement legality and maintain debarred-firm
registries; we do not require them as part of the empirical
strategy. We treat them as part of the broader monitoring
environment within which the construct's screening signal is
read.
