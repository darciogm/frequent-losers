\section{Legal and Institutional Framework}
\label{sec:institutional}

\paragraph{Procurement statutes.}
Brazilian public procurement during our $\valYearStart$--$\valYearEnd$
sample is governed by Lei~$\valLeiOriginal$ (the General
Procurement Statute, enacted to implement Article~$37$ of the
$1988$ Constitution) and Lei $\valLeiPregao$ (which introduced the
Pregão modality). Two modalities dominate BEC. \emph{Convite}
(Lei~$8.666/93$, Article~$22$) is a sealed-bid invitation
procedure: the buyer invites at least three suppliers from the
qualified-supplier registry, all proposals are submitted in writing
within a five-business-day window, and the contract is awarded to
the qualifying lowest bid. The statute requires three valid
proposals for the procedure to clear; if fewer appear, the buyer
must either re-issue the call or document the absence of bidders.
\emph{Pregão} (Lei~$\valLeiPregao$) is a reverse electronic
auction: bidders submit progressively lower offers in real time
until no firm improves the standing best offer. Pregão has no
minimum-bidder requirement. Statutory value caps (Lei~$8.666/93$,
Article~$23$) restrict convite to procurement below
$\valCapOldR$ for goods and services and $\valCapMidR$ for
engineering works during $2009$--$2017$; Decreto~$\valDecreto$
raised these caps to $\valCapNewR$ and $\valCapHighR$ respectively.
Above the caps, only pregão (or higher modalities not used in BEC)
is permitted.

The structural difference between the two modalities provides
useful institutional contrast for our analysis but is not the
mechanism through which the construct operates. The convite
minimum-bidder rule was introduced to ensure procedural
competition; the older competition-economics literature
\citep{marshall2012economics,asker2010leniency,porter1993detection}
has identified configurations of mandated bidder counts without
corresponding rules ensuring genuine competition as canonical
settings for regulatory cover bidding. The construct's empirical
signal does not match this institutional reading: the price
imprint and the discrimination of adjudicated CADE-cobidder firms
are sharper in preg\~ao (no minimum-bidder rule) than in convite
(§\ref{sec:results_ols} and §\ref{sec:mech_modal}). We
therefore present the convite statute as institutional context
rather than as the channel the construct exploits.

The 2021 reform (Lei~$14.133/2021$) replaces Lei~$8.666/93$ for
new contracts, restricts the convite modality, and tightens
habilitation requirements (mandatory federal qualified-supplier
registry, integrity programs above a value threshold). The reform
postdates our sample but shapes the policy relevance of the
construct: by consolidating preg\~ao-style electronic auctions as
the institutional default, the reform moves Brazilian procurement
toward the modal regime in which the construct's signal-to-noise
is highest (§\ref{sec:mech_modal}, §\ref{sec:lim_external}).

\paragraph{Enforcement architecture.}
Three institutions police BEC procurement. \textbf{Tribunal de
Contas do Estado de S\~ao Paulo} (TCE-SP) audits purchasing units
administratively, with binding power to require correction of
irregularities, fine officials, and report findings to
prosecutorial bodies; in $2023$, TCE-SP processed over
$\valPBUcount$ administrative procurement cases at first
instance. \textbf{Controladoria Geral do Estado} (CGE) oversees
executive-branch compliance with procurement law and integrity
programs, including the cadastros of debarred firms. The
\textbf{Conselho Administrativo de Defesa Econ\^omica} (CADE) is
the federal competition authority; it investigates and adjudicates
cartel and bid-rigging cases under Lei~$12.529/2011$, imposing
administrative fines of $\valAdminCost$--$\valPctTwenty$ of the
infringing firm's gross revenue in the year preceding the
investigation, and may refer cases to the Public Prosecutor's
Office for criminal prosecution under Lei~$8.137/90$ (Article~$3$,
prison sentences of two to five years for bid rigging). CADE's
leniency program (Lei~$12.529/2011$, Article~$86$) grants
immunity to the first cooperator and reduced fines to subsequent
ones, generating the disclosure trail on which the cartel-detection
literature relies for ground truth.

CADE's procurement-cartel portfolio relevant to our sample
comprises $12$ adjudicated cases covering $65$ firm-defendants in
total, of which $\valDirectCADE$ are active in BEC during the
sample window. Final decisions span $2015$--$2025$, with cases
ranging from \emph{Cartel dos trens-metr\^os} (adjudicated
$2019$-$07$-$08$) to \emph{Transporte escolar} (adjudicated
$2025$-$02$-$26$). Eight of the twelve cases were decided after
our $2019$ sample cut, so a portion of the consistency check in
§\ref{sec:cade} operates prospectively: the construct's
discrimination against the adjudicated cobidder population was
computed on participation patterns observable years before
enforcement confirmed those patterns. The interval between
operational cartel activity and final CADE adjudication---the
cases in our portfolio span $4$--$16$ years from process opening
to ruling---is the policy-relevant benchmark for the construct's
contribution: a screen that flags suspicious environments in real
time, even imperfectly, compresses the case-load that reaches the
forensic stage of the administrative pathway
(§\ref{sec:conclusion}).

\paragraph{The screening problem.}
Bid-level forensic tools---variance, kurtosis, skewness, and
spread analyses across submitted bids---detect coordinated
bidding when the required microdata are preserved
\citep{imhof2019detecting,huber2019machine,wallimann2023machine}.
The information-theoretic logic of these tools is unimpeachable;
their practical reach is bounded by data infrastructure. Many
electronic procurement systems, including BEC during much of our
sample, archive only winning prices and participant lists at the
contract-record layer: bid-by-bid records may exist in operational
logs but are not always exported into the analytical warehouses
oversight bodies query.\footnote{In TCE-SP audits we examined,
requesting bid-by-bid microdata from the BEC operational system
is a multi-step administrative process that typically delays
investigations by weeks or months; the contract-record layer
queried directly produces results in minutes.} The data asymmetry
is not specific to Brazil. EU member-state platforms preserve bid
microdata heterogeneously; the United States federal procurement
data system (FPDS-NG) preserves contract awards but not bid
history at the contracting-officer-accessible layer; subnational
systems are typically thinner still. A screen that requires only
participation records lowers the data threshold for proactive
detection to the level virtually every system maintains. That is
the construct's primary contribution to the law-and-economics of
procurement enforcement: not a more powerful test, but a test
deployable where the more powerful tests are not.
