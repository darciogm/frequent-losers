\section{Legal and Institutional Framework}
\label{sec:institutional}

\paragraph{Procurement statutes}
Brazilian public procurement during our $\valYearStart$--$\valYearEnd$
sample is governed by Lei~$\valLeiOriginal$ (the General
Procurement Statute) and Lei~$\valLeiPregao$ (which introduced the
Pregão modality). Two modalities dominate BEC. \emph{Convite}
(Lei~$8.666/93$, Art.~$22$) is a sealed-bid invitation procedure
in which the buyer invites at least three qualified suppliers and
the contract is awarded to the lowest qualifying bid; the statute
requires three valid proposals for the procedure to clear.
\emph{Pregão} (Lei~$\valLeiPregao$) is a reverse electronic
auction in which bidders submit progressively lower offers in
real time, with no minimum-bidder requirement. Statutory value
caps (Lei~$8.666/93$, Art.~$23$) restrict convite to procurement
below $\valCapOldR$ for goods and services and $\valCapMidR$ for
engineering works during $2009$--$2017$;
Decreto~$\valDecreto$ raised these to $\valCapNewR$ and
$\valCapHighR$. Above the caps, only pregão is permitted in BEC.

We use the modal contrast as institutional context rather than as
the mechanism the construct exploits: the price imprint and the
discrimination of adjudicated CADE-cobidder firms are sharper in
pregão (no minimum-bidder rule) than in convite
(§\ref{sec:results_ols}, §\ref{sec:mech_modal}). The 2021 reform
(Lei~$14.133/2021$) postdates our sample, restricts the convite
modality, and consolidates pregão-style electronic auctions as
the default, which is the modal regime in which the construct
performs best (§\ref{sec:mech_modal}, §\ref{sec:lim_external}).

\paragraph{Enforcement architecture}
Three institutions police BEC procurement.
\textbf{TCE-SP} (Tribunal de Contas do Estado de S\~ao Paulo)
audits purchasing units administratively;
\textbf{CGE} (Controladoria Geral do Estado) oversees
executive-branch compliance and maintains debarred-firm registries;
and \textbf{CADE} (Conselho Administrativo de Defesa Econ\^omica)
is the federal competition authority that adjudicates bid-rigging
cases under Lei~$12.529/2011$, with leniency provisions
(Art.~$86$) generating the disclosure trail that the
cartel-detection literature uses as ground truth.

CADE's procurement-cartel portfolio relevant to our sample
comprises $12$ adjudicated cases covering $65$ firm-defendants
($\valDirectCADE$ active in BEC during the sample window), with
final decisions spanning $2015$--$2025$. Eight of the twelve
were decided after our $2019$ sample cut, so the validation in
§\ref{sec:cade} operates partly prospectively: the construct's
discrimination against the adjudicated cobidder population was
computed on participation patterns observable years before
enforcement. The interval between operational cartel activity
and final adjudication---$4$ to $16$ years from process opening
to ruling in our portfolio---is the policy-relevant benchmark
for the construct's deployment value (§\ref{sec:conclusion}).

\paragraph{The screening problem}
Bid-level forensic tools---variance, kurtosis, skewness, and
spread analyses across submitted bids---detect coordinated
bidding when bid microdata are preserved
\citep{imhof2019detecting,huber2019machine,wallimann2023machine}.
Their reach is bounded by data infrastructure. Many electronic
procurement systems, including BEC during much of our sample,
archive only winners and participant lists at the contract-record
layer; bid-by-bid records may exist in operational logs but are
not exported to the analytical warehouses oversight bodies
query.\footnote{In TCE-SP audits we examined, requesting
bid-level microdata from the BEC operational system is a
multi-step process that delays investigations by weeks; the
contract-record layer returns results in minutes.} The asymmetry
is not Brazil-specific: EU member-state platforms preserve bid
microdata heterogeneously, and the U.S.~federal system (FPDS-NG)
preserves awards but not bid history at the contracting-officer
layer. A screen that requires only participation records lowers
the data threshold for proactive detection to what virtually
every system already maintains. That is the construct's primary
contribution: not a more powerful test, but one deployable where
the more powerful tests are not.
