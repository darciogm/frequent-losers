\section{Robustness}
\label{sec:robustness}

This section stress-tests the within-item conditional price gap of
§\ref{sec:results_ols} and the procuring-unit-size oversight
gradient of §\ref{sec:results_oversight} against four distinct
concerns. §\ref{sec:rob_threshold} addresses sensitivity to the
design choices embedded in the construct (the IQR threshold
multiplier, the clustering of standard errors, and the
binary-vs-continuous specification of loss intensity).
§\ref{sec:rob_identification} reports identification audits
(\citealt{cinelli2020making} robustness value, strict-overlap
matching, leakage diagnostics, and a placebo on random firm
assignment). §\ref{sec:rob_operational} compares in-sample
operational metrics to a temporal-holdout audit, the most
consequential disclosure for deployment claims.
§\ref{sec:rob_imhof} compares the construct's discrimination to
bid-distribution detectors trained on bid microdata, treating the
gap as informational complementarity rather than dominance.

% ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\subsection{Threshold and Specification Sensitivity}
\label{sec:rob_threshold}
% ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

The headline price gap and its oversight gradient hold across
reasonable variations in the design choices that define the
construct. We document three: the IQR threshold multiplier, the
clustering of standard errors, and the binary-versus-continuous
specification of loss intensity.

The IQR multiplier governs the bright-line cutoff that separates
frequent losers from the broader always-loser stratum. Our headline
multiplier is $1.5$, which yields a tender-count threshold of
$\valThresholdStat$ and a flagged population of $\valFL$ firms.
Table~\ref{tab:threshold} re-estimates the within-PBU
specification under five multipliers ranging from $1.0\times$ to
$3.0\times$ IQR. The price coefficient is positive and significant
in every row, declining monotonically from $+0.079$ at $1.0\times$
(threshold $10$, $3{,}442$ firms) to $+0.050$ at $3.0\times$
(threshold $24$, $1{,}456$ firms). The decline is a feature, not a
defect: as the cutoff tightens, the binary indicator filters fewer
firms with high loss-intensity, and §\ref{sec:mech_horse_race}
established that the underlying primitive is the continuous
$\log(1+\text{tenders\_count})$ statistic. The binary at $1.5\times$
is the operational sweet spot between coverage and signal density;
the construct neither evaporates at conservative thresholds nor
inflates at permissive ones.

The clustering of standard errors does not change the point
estimate---which is mechanical---but it does change inference.
Table~\ref{tab:clustering} reports three regimes:
item-level clustering (baseline, SE $0.0215$), procuring-unit-level
clustering (SE $0.0144$, tighter than baseline), and two-way
\citet{cameron2011robust} clustering on item and procuring unit
jointly (SE $0.0240$, looser than baseline). The coefficient is
significant at $p<0.01$ under all three regimes; the baseline
item-level cluster is the conservative middle choice.

The binary-versus-continuous specification of loss intensity is
discussed in detail in §\ref{sec:mech_horse_race}; we summarize
the implication for robustness here. The continuous
$\log(1+\text{tenders\_count})$ score discriminates the same
adjudicated CADE-cobidder population at AUC
$\valAUClogtc$ ($95\%$ CI $\valAUClogtcCI$) versus
$\valAUCFLBinSameSample$ ($\valAUCFLBinSameSampleCI$) for the binary
FL14 indicator (DeLong $Z = \valDeLongZ$, $p = \valDeLongP$).
Standalone price coefficients in
Table~\ref{tab:horse_race_v14}---FL14 binary $+0.0653$ ($p<0.001$)
and continuous $+0.0188$ ($p<0.001$)---point in the same direction.
The headline price gap is not an artifact of binary discretization.

\subsection{Identification Audits}
\label{sec:rob_identification}
% ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

The construct's identification status is descriptive throughout the
paper. This subsection collects four audits that calibrate the
strength of the descriptive claim under specific identification
concerns: omitted-variable sensitivity, common-support restriction,
leakage, and a placebo on a non-cover-bidding channel.

The \citet{cinelli2020making} robustness value reported in
§\ref{sec:results_ols} is $RV_{q=1} = \valRVqOne$: an unobserved
confounder would need to explain at least $\valRVqOne$ of the
residual variation in both frequent-loser presence and log price to
drive the coefficient to zero. The
\citet{oster2019unobservable} bounds in
Table~\ref{tab:oster_delta} agree directionally. The full-sample
$\hat{\delta} = 261.64$ implies that unobservables would need to be
more than two hundred times as important as the observables already
captured by item, year, and procuring-unit fixed effects to overturn
the result; the preg\~ao subsample yields an even larger
$\hat{\delta} = 634.00$. The convite subsample carries a smaller
margin ($\hat{\delta} = 43.62$, $\beta^*(\delta=1) = -0.1395$)---
consistent with §\ref{sec:mech_modal}'s finding that the
construct's signal-to-noise is highest in preg\~ao environments and
that the convite estimate is the noisier modal slice. Both
sensitivity tools agree: the broad-sample association is unlikely to
be explained away by selection on observables of the kind already
accounted for.

The strict-overlap matching exercise complements the sensitivity
bounds with a different identification angle. Where Cinelli and
Oster ask how strong unobserved selection would need to be to
overturn the result, strict-overlap matching restricts comparisons
to the cells in which frequent-loser items and non-frequent-loser
items genuinely overlap on observables.
Table~\ref{tab:item_level_scope_match} reports four specifications.
The baseline within-item-and-PBU coefficient is $+0.064$
($n = 1{,}653{,}658$, identical to the headline). Restricting to
overlap cells---requiring frequent-loser and non-frequent-loser
items to share the same observed pre-treatment cell---returns
$-0.097$ ($n = 1{,}517{,}066$). Trimming further to common-support
under propensity-score restriction returns $-0.307$
($n = 400{,}687$).

\input{./output/tables/tab_item_level_scope_match}
 The reversal under strict overlap restricts
comparisons to the items frequent losers \emph{would} have entered
with high probability, removing precisely the systematic selection
differences between selected and unselected items that the
broad-sample association captures (§\ref{sec:results_ols}). Both
signs describe the same population from different angles, neither
carrying causal weight on its own; we report the four
specifications jointly so the reader can locate the construct's
descriptive claim within the appropriate scope.

The leakage audit in Table~\ref{tab:leakage_audit} addresses a
distinct concern: that the in-sample item-level AUC of $0.995$
(reference row) is partially tautological, because cobidders are by
construction always-loser firms with elevated participation counts.
Audit~1 holds the score fixed and swaps the label to direct-CADE
defendants, returning $0.506$---random discrimination, confirming
the structural scope of the construct (cover bidders, not
ringleaders). Audit~2 breaks the construction by withholding
cobidder firms from the score-construction step within five
cross-validation folds and re-evaluating on items containing
held-out cobidders: AUC $0.891$ ($95\%$ CI $[0.887, 0.894]$).
Audit~3 breaks the construction temporally: train $2009$--$2016$,
test $2017$--$2019$, returning AUC $\valAUCFLfirmTemp$ ($95\%$ CI
$\valAUCFLfirmTempCI$). The screen retains discriminating power above
$0.85$ under both audits. The drop of $0.10$--$0.13$ is the
pure-leakage component, and we adopt the temporal-holdout AUC of
$\valAUCFLfirmTemp$ as the conservative discriminating reference for the
operational-metrics audit in §\ref{sec:rob_operational}.

A placebo specification using the supply of sub-threshold
always-losers (firms with persistent zero wins but below the IQR
cutoff) as an instrument completes the audit set. The first-stage
$F$-statistic is $33$, comfortably above the
\citet{stock2005testing} weak-instrument threshold, but the 2SLS
coefficient is $5.24$ (Table~\ref{tab:iv_placebo}, below)---an implausible
magnitude that signals the placebo instrument operates through
channels other than cover bidding. The construct's price imprint
draws on the above-threshold cover-bidder population we identify,
not on generic always-loser supply.

\subsection{Operational Metrics: In-Sample vs Temporal Holdout}
\label{sec:rob_operational}
% ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

The deployment claim rests on operational metrics computed under
the temporal-holdout regime, not under in-sample evaluation. The
construct's discriminating performance under temporal holdout is
firm-level AUC $\valAUCFLfirmTemp$ ($95\%$ CI $\valAUCFLfirmTempCI$) against the
$\valCobidders$ adjudicated CADE-cobidder labels, with the score
constructed using only $2009$--$2016$ participation counts and
evaluated on items in $2017$--$2019$. This is the operationally
honest projection for a screening tool that is built on past
participation data and deployed against a future case-load. We
report it as the operational reference throughout the paper.

Table~\ref{tab:operational_metrics} reports precision, recall, and
lift at five top-$k$ cuts under both in-sample and temporal-holdout
evaluation. In the deployment-relevant region, top-$100$ has precision
$0.070$ (lift $6.1\!\times$), top-$500$ has precision $0.070$
(lift $6.1\!\times$), and top-$1{,}000$ has precision $0.066$
(lift $5.8\!\times$). At top-$500$, the screen
flags $35$ adjudicated cobidder firms out of $193$ in the pool
(recall $18\%$); at top-$1{,}000$ the recall rises to $34\%$. The
construct's signal sustains through the deployable region, with
lifts in the $5.8\!\times$ to $6.6\!\times$ range across cuts. The
in-sample column over-states precision by approximately $50\%$ at
top-$500$ ($0.132$ in-sample versus $0.070$ holdout, retention
$53\%$). The inflation arises because roughly $47\%$ of the
top-$500$ in-sample ranking comes from $2017$--$2019$ participation,
after CADE adjudications were already underway for some of the
cartels, so the in-sample score is partly retrospective rather than
purely prospective. We disclose the gap explicitly and operate on
the temporal-holdout column.

Three operational implications follow. First, the construct is
deployable: discriminating AUC $\valAUCFLfirmTemp$ and lifts of $5.8\!\times$
to $6.6\!\times$ in the top-$100$ to top-$1{,}000$ region are
operationally meaningful for a screening tool that runs on
contract-award records alone, at near-zero marginal data cost.
Second, the temporal holdout is the honest reference: any
operational claim in this paper that requires a precision or lift
number is computed on the holdout column, and the in-sample column
is reported for comparison and transparency. Third, periodic
rescoring matters: an oversight body running the screen in
production should refresh the score window as new years of
contract-award data arrive, both to dampen drift and to keep the
prospective component dominant in the deployment ranking.

\subsection{Horse Race Against Bid-Distribution Detectors}
\label{sec:rob_imhof}
% ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

The construct's competitive position vis-à-vis bid-distribution
detectors that require bid microdata is the final calibration. The
relevant comparison is at fixed \emph{data cost}, not at fixed
sample, because the two pipelines operate on different data
envelopes: the construct uses award-record fields only (winner
identity, participant identity, item identifier), while the
bid-distribution pipeline requires per-bid offer values.

Table~\ref{tab:imhof_full} reports the comparison on the full
always-loser pool ($N = 16{,}779$ firms, $193$ adjudicated
cobidder labels). The construct discriminates at AUC $0.903$
($95\%$ CI $[0.884, 0.923]$) under the binary FL14 indicator and
$0.884$ ($95\%$ CI $[0.860, 0.908]$) under the continuous
$\log(1+\text{tenders\_count})$ score. The full Imhof--Wallimann
pipeline---five within-tender bid-distribution features (coefficient
of variation, skewness, kurtosis, spread, second-low ratio)
\citep{imhof2019detecting,wallimann2023machine}---reaches AUC
$0.888$ ($95\%$ CI $[0.865, 0.911]$) on the same target. The
two pipelines reach comparable discrimination on different data
envelopes. We do not claim that the construct outperforms the
bid-distribution pipeline; we claim that it reaches comparable
accuracy on a substantially thinner data envelope.

\input{./output/tables/tab_imhof_full}

The combined specifications in the bottom panel of
Table~\ref{tab:imhof_full} address the integration question. FL14
combined with the full Imhof pipeline reaches AUC $0.955$
($95\%$ CI $[0.943, 0.967]$); continuous loss intensity combined
with the full Imhof pipeline reaches AUC $0.962$ ($95\%$ CI
$[0.954, 0.969]$). Both combined classifiers gain $5$--$7$ AUC
points over either single construct, indicating non-redundant
signal in the participation-only and bid-distribution information
sources. Restricting attention to the same-sample audit in
Table~\ref{tab:imhof_incremental}, where all detectors evaluate
the identical subset of firms with bid microdata available, the
construct adds $+0.035$ AUC over the Imhof full pipeline alone
(DeLong $p = 0.014$), and the combined model adds
$+0.096$--$+0.098$ AUC over Imhof full alone (DeLong $p < 0.001$).

The construct's competitive position is therefore informational
complementarity, not dominance. It reaches comparable
discrimination at lower data cost when bid microdata are
unavailable, and it adds non-redundant signal when bid microdata
are available. The administrative pathway in
§\ref{sec:conclusion} integrates the construct with the
bid-distribution stage rather than substituting for it: the
\textbf{Screen} stage runs on award records, the \textbf{Forensic}
stage deploys bid-distribution methods, and the gain from running
both is identifiable in the data.
