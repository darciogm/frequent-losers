\section{Main Results}
\label{sec:results}

This section delivers the screening-value evidence in three
moves. First, the broad-sample association $\beta$ across four
estimators (§\ref{sec:results_ols}). Second, the
overlap-restricted association $\beta^{\text{ov}}$ and the sign
reversal as the diagnostic that locates the construct as a
screening-value object rather than a treatment-effect object
(§\ref{sec:results_overlap}). Third, the heterogeneity of the
screening signal across detection regimes
(§\ref{sec:results_oversight}).

\subsection{Broad-Sample Conditional Association ($\beta$)}
\label{sec:results_ols}

Table~\ref{tab:prices} reports OLS estimates of
Equation~\eqref{eq:ols_main}. Frequent-loser presence associates
with a higher log negotiated unit price in all four
specifications: $\valPctSixeight$ within item and year
($\valPctSixfour$ adding procuring-unit fixed effects), and
$\valPctNinethree$ in pregão against $\valPctThreeeight$ in
convite. The comparison set is the $\valSampleN$ tender-items in
the same product-code $\times$ year $\times$ procuring-unit cells
without frequent-loser participants.

\input{./output/tables/tab_prices}

The pregão--convite contrast is informative on whether the
construct's signal could be mechanically driven by the convite
minimum-bidder rule (Lei~$8.666/93$, Art.\ 22, \S\,3): a
quorum-filler reading would predict a sharper imprint in
convite, but the data show the opposite ($\valFalPregBin$ in
pregão vs.~$\valFalConvBin$ in convite;
Table~\ref{tab:falsification_modal}). The signal is therefore
not reducible to the quorum rule. We read the asymmetry as
informativeness across observability regimes
(§\ref{sec:mech_modal}), not as a positive test of any specific
institutional channel.

Cross-fit estimation yields $\valPctThreeSix$, CEM
$\valBidCoefMean$, and IPW $\valBidNonCV$. A leave-one-out IV
returns $\valBidPHigh$ ($p<0.05$, first-stage $F=396$), the
direction expected when the binary indicator coarsens the
underlying continuous loss-intensity statistic
(§\ref{sec:mech_horse_race}). The four broad-sample estimators
jointly bound the conditional range $\valHeadlineRange$.

Under the screening framing of §\ref{sec:emp_estimand}, $\beta$
is the empirical content the construct is designed to recover:
the conditional association at the items frequent losers do
participate in, integrated over the cartel's deployment
distribution. We do not present $\beta$ as a treatment-effect
estimate. The relevant question for what follows is not whether
$\beta$ would survive a counterfactual that reweights items to
remove the deployment sorting, but whether the deployment
sorting itself is part of the screening-value content.

\subsection{Overlap-Restricted Association ($\beta^{\text{ov}}$) and the
Screening-Value Diagnostic}
\label{sec:results_overlap}

Restricting the comparison to cells in which frequent-loser-present
and -absent items genuinely overlap on observables produces a
reversed conditional coefficient: overlap-cell ATT
$\valMatchOverlapCoef$, propensity-score-trimmed ATT
$\valMatchPSCoef$
(Table~\ref{tab:item_level_scope_match}).

\input{./output/tables/tab_item_level_scope_match}

This is a different empirical object. $\beta^{\text{ov}}$ is the
within-support comparison at the items where treated and control
populations look alike on observables, computed by holding the
participation footprint fixed at that subsample. It is the
estimand a treatment-effect framing would target.
$\beta$ is the broad-sample association at the items where
frequent losers actually deploy, integrated over the deployment
distribution. The two objects answer different questions; the
sign reversal is the empirical signature that they are
different.

\paragraph{Why we read the reversal as a screening-value
diagnostic} Under the separating equilibrium of
Online Appendix~A, frequent-loser participation is generated by
the cartel's deployment problem. The cartel's choice of where
to place cover bidders is informative: the deployment sorting is
not a confound to be matched away but the equilibrium response
to incentives we cannot observe directly. Three pieces of
empirical evidence are consistent with this reading rather than
with a treatment-effect-that-fails-on-overlap reading.

\textit{(i)} The broad-sample association moves with cartel
adjacency. The construct discriminates the $\valCobidders$
adjudicated CADE-cobidder firms inside the always-loser stratum
at AUC $\valAUCFLfirm$ in-sample, $\valAUCFLfirmTemp$ under
temporal holdout, and $\valAUCprePost$ on the contemporaneous
benchmark of §\ref{sec:cade}. Under a confound-only reading of
$\beta$, the construct's labels would not concentrate on
adjudicated cartel members; the empirical concentration is
inconsistent with that reading.

\textit{(ii)} The continuous loss-intensity statistic
strictly dominates the binary rule in discrimination of the same
labels (\citealt{delong1988comparing} test, $p<10^{-3}$;
§\ref{sec:mech_horse_race}). The participation primitive is
ranked, not categorical; the binary rule is the
information-coarsening of a deeper signal. A pure-confound
reading does not predict the dose-response.

\textit{(iii)} \citet{cinelli2020making}'s
$RV_{q=1}=\valRVqOne$ and \citet{oster2019unobservable}'s
$\hat{\delta}=261.64$ bound the broad-sample association
against selection on observables of the kind already absorbed
by the fixed effects. They do not bound the deployment
sorting that the overlap restriction surfaces, by construction;
under the screening framing, that sorting is the object, not the
threat.

We therefore report $\beta$ and $\beta^{\text{ov}}$ as paired
empirical objects. The construct's headline range is
$\valHeadlineRange$ on the broad sample. The
overlap-restricted coefficient
($\valMatchOverlapCoef$ to $\valMatchPSCoef$) is the
within-support comparison at the cells where treated and
control populations are observable comparables; it is reported
in parallel, not as a sharper version of the headline. Whether a
referee privileges $\beta$ or $\beta^{\text{ov}}$ depends on
which empirical object the reader treats as primary; we have
made our reasons explicit and report both.

\subsection{Heterogeneity Across Detection Regimes}
\label{sec:results_oversight}

Splitting the sample into quartiles of procuring-unit size and
re-estimating the within-item specification produces a
monotonic gradient: $\valOversightQonePct$ in the smallest
quartile, $\valOversightQtwoPct$, $\valOversightQthreePct$, and
$\valOversightQfourPct$ in the largest, all significant at
conventional levels with item-clustered standard errors. The
$\valPBUgradientRatio\!\!\times$ ratio is the largest source of
heterogeneity in the data and is preserved across alternative
measures of buyer size (annual contract volume, cumulative item
count, headcount of distinct purchasers;
Table~\ref{tab:regime_oversight}).

The framework in Online Appendix~A predicts a gradient in the
same direction through the detection-cost comparative static
$\partial m^*/\partial \theta_k < 0$. We read the gradient as
heterogeneity in the screening object across the monitoring
environment: the construct is more informative where the
detection regime exposes a thinner audit footprint. Buyer size
proxies for several correlated institutional features---procurement-officer
tenure \citep{coviello2017tenure}, internal-audit
infrastructure, item composition, and discretionary procedure
use \citep{decarolis2020rules,decarolis2017corruption}---none of
which can be separated with the variation we have. We do not
identify a causal channel. The implication for the contribution
is that the screening signal varies systematically with
features of the enforcement environment, in the direction the
framework's deployment problem predicts.
