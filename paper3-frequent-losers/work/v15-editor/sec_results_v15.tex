\section{Main Results}
\label{sec:results}

This section establishes two empirical findings and the
interpretive framework that organizes them. First, frequent-loser
presence associates with a positive within-item price gap in the
broad sample, but the conditional association reverses sign under
strict-overlap matching---a feature of the data we treat as
central, not as a footnote. Second, the price imprint is
heterogeneous in procuring-unit size: the gap shrinks monotonically
across quartiles to near zero among the largest buyers. We read
the gradient as consistent with thinner institutional capacity
moderating the imprint at smaller buyers, but we do not identify
oversight as the operative channel.

\subsection{Within-Item Conditional Price Gap and Selection Scope}
\label{sec:results_ols}
% ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Table~\ref{tab:prices} reports OLS estimates of
Equation~\eqref{eq:ols_main}. The presence of at least one frequent
loser is associated with a higher log negotiated unit price in all
four specifications. The general within-item-and-year estimate is
$\valPctSixeight$ (SE clustered by item-year); adding procuring-unit
fixed effects moves it to $\valPctSixfour$. The modal subsamples
bracket the within-PBU estimate at $\valPctNinethree$ in pregão and
$\valPctThreeeight$ in convite. The comparison set is the
$\valSampleN$ tender-items in the same product code × year ×
procuring unit that do not contain frequent losers.

The natural first question is whether the convite minimum-bidder
rule (Lei~$8.666/93$, Art.\ 22, \S\,3) generates the gap by
forcing quorum-fillers. The data say otherwise: the imprint is
sharper in pregão (no minimum-bidder rule) than in convite
($\valFalPregBin$ vs.~$\valFalConvBin$;
Table~\ref{tab:falsification_modal}). We report the modal contrast
as institutional context, not as evidence that the construct
exploits the convite quorum channel.

The headline range across estimators is informative about the
construct's signal but not about its causal interpretation.
Cross-fit estimation (defining the frequent-loser list on odd
years and estimating on even, then reversing) yields
$\valPctThreeSix$. Applying the odd-year list to the full panel
yields $\valBidConvCoefPct$. Coarsened exact matching
($\valBidCoefMean$, $N=\valCleanItems$) and inverse-probability
weighting ($\valBidNonCV$, $N=\valFilteredItems$) bracket the OLS
estimate on the common-support subsample. The leave-one-out IV
estimate is $\valBidPHigh$ ($p<0.05$, first-stage $F=396$), in the
direction expected under classical attenuation when the binary
indicator proxies for an underlying continuous participation count.
Across the four broad-sample estimators, the conditional range is
$\valHeadlineRange$.

\paragraph{The sign reversal under strict-overlap matching}
The four estimators above report the broad-sample association.
Restricted to cells in which frequent-loser-present and
frequent-loser-absent items genuinely overlap on observables, the
conditional coefficient \emph{reverses sign}: overlap-cell ATT
$-9.7\%$, propensity-score-trimmed ATT $-30.7\%$
(Table~\ref{tab:item_level_scope_match}). This is the most
important interpretive feature of the descriptive evidence and we
do not treat it as a robustness check.

The broad-sample estimate combines two components: (i) the
within-cell association where frequent-loser-present and -absent
items genuinely overlap on observables, and (ii) the systematic
differences between items that frequent losers \emph{select into}
and items they do not. Strict-overlap matching isolates (i) by
construction. The broad-sample estimate also includes (ii). Both
scopes are descriptively valid, but they answer different
questions: the broad sample asks ``where do frequent losers
participate, and how do prices compare?''; strict-overlap asks
``conditional on similar observable item-PBU-year cells, how do
prices differ when a frequent loser is present?''. The first
question gives a positive gap; the second gives a negative gap.
\citet{cinelli2020making}'s robustness value of
$RV_{q=1}=\valRVqOne$ and \citet{oster2019unobservable}'s
$\hat{\delta}=261.64$ on the full sample bound the broad-sample
association against selection on observables of the kind already
absorbed by item, year, and procuring-unit fixed effects, but
neither addresses the selection mechanism that strict-overlap
matching surfaces.

The implication for the screen's interpretation is concrete. The
construct's signal is partly about \emph{which environments
frequent losers select into}---environments that, on observables,
are systematically different from items where they do not
participate. Within the cells where the two populations overlap on
observables, the price comparison runs the opposite way; this
likely reflects the unobserved features that drive entry into the
selected cells in the first place. A triage tool does not need to
resolve this tension to be useful: it needs to flag environments
at higher prior risk than random selection. The construct does so
on both the broad-sample association and the cartel-adjacency
validation in §\ref{sec:cade}. We do not, however, claim the
broad-sample $\valHeadlineRange$ as a uniform descriptive effect
of frequent-loser presence on prices. We claim that frequent-loser
presence flags environments in which observable selection patterns
correlate with higher prices and with cartel-adjacent ground truth.

\subsection{Heterogeneity by Procuring-Unit Size}
\label{sec:results_oversight}
% ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Splitting the sample into quartiles of procuring-unit size and
re-estimating the within-item specification yields a steep,
monotonic gradient in the price coefficient: $\valOversightQonePct$
in the smallest quartile, $\valOversightQtwoPct$ in the second,
$\valOversightQthreePct$ in the third, and $\valOversightQfourPct$
in the largest, all significant at conventional levels with
standard errors clustered by item. The
$\valPBUgradientRatio\!\!\times$ ratio between the extreme
quartiles is the largest source of heterogeneity we find in the
data, larger than the pregão--convite gap. The pattern is preserved
across alternative measures of buyer size (annual contract volume,
cumulative item count, headcount of distinct purchasers).
Table~\ref{tab:regime_oversight} reports the coefficients.

The framework in Online Appendix~A delivers a
comparative-static prediction in the same direction:
$\partial m^*/\partial \theta_k < 0$, where $\theta_k$ indexes the
procuring unit's principal cost of detection. Read through this
mapping, the gradient is what the framework predicts. The mapping,
however, is not unique. Buyer size proxies for several correlated
institutional features, including procurement-officer tenure
\citep{coviello2017tenure}, internal-audit infrastructure, item
composition, supplier-pool depth, and discretionary procedure use
\citep{decarolis2020rules,decarolis2017corruption}. Any of these
can generate a size gradient absent any oversight channel. We
therefore present the gradient as \emph{consistent with} thinner
institutional capacity moderating the price imprint at smaller
buyers, not as evidence that oversight is the operative channel.

Three features distinguish the gradient from a mechanical artifact:
monotonicity across all four quartiles, a magnitude beyond what
fixed-effect noise typically produces, and a direction matching
both the framework's comparative static and the broader empirical
literature on procurement-officer infrastructure
\citep{coviello2017tenure,decarolis2017corruption,oecd2019government}.
We acknowledge that thinner item-PBU cells in small procuring
units, and systematically different bidder pools or item
compositions across buyer sizes, could in principle deliver a
gradient absent any institutional-capacity channel. Identifying the
operative mechanism would require variation in oversight that is
plausibly orthogonal to other correlated features---a research
design we do not have. We treat the gradient as a heterogeneity
result whose policy implication (oversight bodies operating on
small-buyer environments face thinner ex-ante information) is
robust to the mechanism interpretation, and whose mechanism
interpretation is not.
