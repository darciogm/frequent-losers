\section{Main Results}
\label{sec:results}

This section reports two findings. The first is a positive
within-item price association in the broad sample that reverses
sign under strict-overlap matching; we treat the reversal as
central rather than as a robustness check. The second is a steep
heterogeneity in procuring-unit size, consistent with thinner
institutional capacity at smaller buyers but not identifying
oversight as the operative channel.

% ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\subsection{Within-Item Conditional Price Gap and Selection Scope}
\label{sec:results_ols}
% ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Table~\ref{tab:prices} reports OLS estimates of
Equation~\eqref{eq:ols_main}. Frequent-loser presence associates
with a higher log negotiated unit price in all four
specifications: $\valPctSixeight$ within item and year
($\valPctSixfour$ adding procuring-unit fixed effects), and
$\valPctNinethree$ in pregão versus $\valPctThreeeight$ in
convite. The comparison set is the $\valSampleN$ tender-items in
the same product-code × year × procuring-unit cells that do not
contain frequent losers.

The convite minimum-bidder rule (Lei~$8.666/93$, Art.\ 22, \S\,3)
does not generate the gap by forcing quorum-fillers: the imprint
is sharper in pregão, where no minimum-bidder rule applies
($\valFalPregBin$ vs.~$\valFalConvBin$;
Table~\ref{tab:falsification_modal}). Cross-fit estimation
yields $\valPctThreeSix$, coarsened exact matching
$\valBidCoefMean$, and inverse-probability weighting
$\valBidNonCV$. A leave-one-out IV returns $\valBidPHigh$
($p<0.05$, first-stage $F=396$), in the direction expected under
classical attenuation when the binary indicator proxies for an
underlying continuous participation count. The conditional range
across these four broad-sample estimators is $\valHeadlineRange$.

\paragraph{The sign reversal under strict-overlap matching.}
Restricted to cells in which frequent-loser-present and -absent
items genuinely overlap on observables, the conditional
coefficient \emph{reverses sign}: overlap-cell ATT $\valMatchOverlapCoef$,
propensity-score-trimmed ATT $\valMatchPSCoef$
(Table~\ref{tab:item_level_scope_match}). The broad-sample
estimate combines two things: the within-cell association where
the two populations overlap, and the systematic differences
between items frequent losers select into and items they do
not. Strict-overlap matching isolates the first; the broad-sample
estimate carries both. Within overlap cells, the price comparison
runs the opposite way, which likely reflects the unobserved
features that drive entry into the selected cells.
\citet{cinelli2020making}'s $RV_{q=1}=\valRVqOne$ and
\citet{oster2019unobservable}'s $\hat{\delta}=261.64$ bound the
broad-sample association against selection on observables, but
neither addresses the selection mechanism the strict-overlap
exercise surfaces.

We report the broad-sample $\valHeadlineRange$ as the headline
because it maps to the deployment question---which environments
to flag---and a triage tool sees the population frequent losers
do select into, not a counterfactual matched on observables.
The within-cell coefficient answers a different question and is
reported in parallel, not in opposition. We do not claim the
broad-sample range as a uniform descriptive effect on prices; we
claim that frequent-loser presence flags environments in which
observable selection patterns correlate with higher prices and
with cartel-adjacent ground truth.

\subsection{Heterogeneity by Procuring-Unit Size}
\label{sec:results_oversight}
% ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Splitting the sample into quartiles of procuring-unit size and
re-estimating the within-item specification produces a steep,
monotonic gradient: $\valOversightQonePct$ in the smallest
quartile, $\valOversightQtwoPct$, $\valOversightQthreePct$, and
$\valOversightQfourPct$ in the largest, all significant at
conventional levels with item-clustered standard errors. The
$\valPBUgradientRatio\!\!\times$ ratio is the largest source of
heterogeneity in the data, larger than the pregão--convite gap,
and is preserved across alternative measures of buyer size
(annual contract volume, cumulative item count, headcount of
distinct purchasers; Table~\ref{tab:regime_oversight}).

The framework in Online Appendix~A predicts a gradient in the
same direction: $\partial m^*/\partial \theta_k < 0$, where
$\theta_k$ indexes the procuring unit's principal cost of
detection. The mapping is suggestive, not unique. Buyer size
proxies for several correlated institutional features, including
procurement-officer tenure \citep{coviello2017tenure},
internal-audit infrastructure, item composition, and
discretionary procedure use
\citep{decarolis2020rules,decarolis2017corruption}. We present
the gradient as consistent with thinner institutional capacity at
smaller buyers, not as evidence that oversight is the operative
channel. Identifying that channel would require variation in
oversight plausibly orthogonal to the other correlated
features---a research design we do not have. The policy
implication (oversight bodies operating on small-buyer
environments face thinner ex-ante information) is robust to the
mechanism interpretation; the mechanism interpretation is not.
