\section{Main Results}
\label{sec:results}

This section reports two findings: a positive within-item price
association in the broad sample that reverses sign under
strict-overlap matching, and a steep gradient across
procuring-unit-size quartiles.

\subsection{Within-Item Conditional Price Gap and Selection Scope}
\label{sec:results_ols}

Table~\ref{tab:prices} reports OLS estimates of
Equation~\eqref{eq:ols_main}. Frequent-loser presence associates
with a higher log negotiated unit price in all four
specifications: $\valPctSixeight$ within item and year
($\valPctSixfour$ adding procuring-unit fixed effects), and
$\valPctNinethree$ in pregão against $\valPctThreeeight$ in
convite. The comparison set is the $\valSampleN$ tender-items in
the same product-code $\times$ year $\times$ procuring-unit cells
without frequent-loser participants.

The pregão--convite contrast is informative on the role of the
convite minimum-bidder rule (Lei~$8.666/93$, Art.\ 22, \S\,3): a
quorum-filler interpretation would predict a sharper imprint in
convite, but the data show the opposite ($\valFalPregBin$ in
pregão vs.~$\valFalConvBin$ in convite;
Table~\ref{tab:falsification_modal}). The signal is therefore
not reducible to the quorum rule, though we do not interpret the
asymmetry as a positive test of any specific institutional
channel. Cross-fit estimation yields $\valPctThreeSix$, CEM
$\valBidCoefMean$, and IPW $\valBidNonCV$. A leave-one-out IV
returns $\valBidPHigh$ ($p<0.05$, first-stage $F=396$), the
direction expected under classical attenuation when the binary
indicator proxies for an underlying continuous loss-intensity
statistic. The conditional range across these four broad-sample
estimators is $\valHeadlineRange$.

\paragraph{The sign reversal under strict-overlap matching}
Once the comparison is restricted to cells in which
frequent-loser-present and -absent items overlap on observables,
the conditional coefficient reverses sign: overlap-cell ATT
$\valMatchOverlapCoef$, propensity-score-trimmed ATT
$\valMatchPSCoef$ (Table~\ref{tab:item_level_scope_match}). The
broad-sample estimate combines a within-support comparison and
the systematic differences between items frequent losers select
into and items they do not; overlap-restricted estimates isolate
the within-support component.
\citet{cinelli2020making}'s $RV_{q=1}=\valRVqOne$ and
\citet{oster2019unobservable}'s $\hat{\delta}=261.64$ bound the
broad-sample association against selection on observables of the
kind already absorbed by the fixed effects but do not address
the selection scope the matching exercise surfaces.

The two estimates answer different empirical questions about the
same population. We treat the broad-sample
$\valHeadlineRange$ as the headline because triage operates on
the population frequent losers select into, and report the
overlap-restricted coefficient alongside as the corresponding
within-support comparison. The construct flags environments in
which observable selection patterns correlate with higher prices
and with cartel-adjacent ground truth; it does not recover a
uniform treatment effect on prices.

\subsection{Heterogeneity by Procuring-Unit Size}
\label{sec:results_oversight}

Splitting the sample into quartiles of procuring-unit size and
re-estimating the within-item specification produces a
monotonic gradient: $\valOversightQonePct$ in the smallest
quartile, $\valOversightQtwoPct$, $\valOversightQthreePct$, and
$\valOversightQfourPct$ in the largest, all significant at
conventional levels with item-clustered standard errors. The
$\valPBUgradientRatio\!\!\times$ ratio is the largest source of
heterogeneity in the data and is preserved across alternative
measures of buyer size (annual contract volume, cumulative item
count, headcount of distinct purchasers;
Table~\ref{tab:regime_oversight}).

The framework in Online Appendix~A predicts a gradient in the
same direction. The reading is suggestive: buyer size proxies
for several correlated institutional features---procurement-officer
tenure \citep{coviello2017tenure}, internal-audit
infrastructure, item composition, and discretionary procedure
use \citep{decarolis2020rules,decarolis2017corruption}---and
none can be separated with the variation we have. We report the
gradient as consistent with thinner institutional capacity at
smaller buyers; the operational implication (oversight is more
informationally taxed in small-buyer environments) is robust to
the mechanism reading, the mechanism reading itself is not.
