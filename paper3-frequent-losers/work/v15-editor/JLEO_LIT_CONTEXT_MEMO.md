## 1. How the literature positioning changed

### A. Organizing principle: data layer, not data parsimony

| Before | After |
|---|---|
| Four literatures organized by *constraint* (need partition / need microdata / retrospective / resource gap). The construct positioned as the conjunction-filler. | Four literatures organized by the **data layer at which each operates** (bid-coordination tests on the bid layer; bid-distribution screens on the bid layer; cover-bidding identification on the post-adjudication record; enforcement-design work on the architectural layer). The construct positioned by the informational-layer constraint that real enforcement faces. |

The reorganization does the JLEO work: the literature review now reads as a map of the informational architecture in which screening problems live, not as a catalogue of methods that the construct outperforms.

### B. The fourth pillar replaced

| Before | After |
|---|---|
| Fourth paragraph: "Procurement enforcement is hardest where data are scarcest" — a developing-economy resource-gap paragraph anchored on \citet{sanchezgraells2019screening} and the OECD share-of-GDP statistic. Read as procurement-administration framing. | Fourth paragraph: **"Enforcement design under incomplete information"** — anchored on \citet{becker1968crime}, \citet{stigler1970optimum}, \citet{harrington2008detecting}, \citet{baker2002case}. Two implications drawn: (i) the screening object under incomplete observability is whatever the collusive arrangement generates that survives coarsening, and (ii) screening and forensic stages answer different questions and require different statistics. The architectural separation between an award-layer screening stage and a bid-layer forensic stage is the JLEO-style thematic pillar that the rest of the paper instantiates. |

This is the most consequential change in the literature review. The OECD/developing-economy framing is gone; the law-and-economics-of-enforcement framing replaces it.

### C. "Procurement institutions" paragraph reframed, not removed

| Before | After |
|---|---|
| "Procurement institutions shape what screens can observe": Coviello / De Carolis cited as motivation for the buyer-size oversight gradient; phrasing in administrative register ("oversight infrastructure thinning"). | "Procurement institutions and the observability environment": same citations, but reframed as **variation in the monitoring regime that the construct's signal is read against**. The buyer-size heterogeneity is now read in this register and not as identification of an oversight channel — consistent with the §6.3 reframing in the abstract and intro. |

### D. Synthesis paragraph rewritten

The closing synthesis no longer enumerates the four literatures as constraints with the construct as conjunction-filler. It enumerates them as **layers of the informational architecture** and positions the construct as identifying a participation primitive that survives data coarsening, supplying a candidate flag, recasting cover-bidder identification as prospective, and instantiating the screening--forensic separation. The JLEO architectural claim is now load-bearing in the synthesis.

## 4. Trade-offs and unresolved risks

### R8. Becker / Stigler / Baker citations are framing-level, not engagement-level
The new "enforcement design under incomplete information" paragraph cites \citet{becker1968crime}, \citet{stigler1970optimum}, \citet{baker2002case}, and \citet{harrington2008detecting} at framing level. A JLEO referee may read this as ornamental — the body of the paper does not engage with optimal-deterrence theory in any depth. Two mitigations: (i) the paragraph draws *two specific implications* that the empirical exercise instantiates, which is more than ornament; (ii) PART 4 may add one to two sentences in §11 (Limitations) acknowledging that the optimal-screening-design problem in our setting is not formally solved and is left for future work. We do not propose to solve the optimal-deterrence problem ourselves.

### R9. The institutional section now reads more abstractly
The earlier version had the texture of an institutional case study; the new version reads as an informational catalogue. A reader who wanted procurement-administration detail will not find it here. This is the intended effect of the reframe; we accept it.

### R10. The footnote on TCE-SP audit logistics is the only first-person-plural anchor in §3
Some house styles dislike first-person-plural in footnotes describing field observation. We retain it because it is the only operational anchor for the data-layer claim, and removing it would force us either to invoke an external citation (we do not have one of comparable specificity) or to move the operational anchor to §10 (where it would be late). Decision: keep, flag for copy-edit if a journal pushes back.

### R11. The "8-of-12 cases adjudicated after $2019$" fact now does double duty
The fact appears in §3 as institutional context for the prospective-character argument, and again in §6.3 as the headline framing of the prospective benchmark. The double duty is acceptable because §3 establishes the architectural fact and §6.3 deploys it in the validation; but a referee may read the §6.3 passage as redundant. Mitigation: §6.3 (in PART 4) should reference §3 for the institutional fact and focus its own prose on the $\valMultThreeFive\!\!\times$ excess and AUC numbers.

### R12. Procurement still appears as "the empirical application"
The §3 rewrite uses "BEC enforcement", "pregão-style auctions", "BEC's analytical warehouse", and similar concrete terms. The conceptual frame (data layer / observability environment / monitoring regime) is layered on top of these. A reader skimming §3 may still feel that the paper is principally about Brazilian procurement institutions; the JLEO frame depends on the conceptual layer being legible to a reader who skims. Mitigation: the section title ("Institutional Setting and the Observability Architecture") and the opening paragraph signal the JLEO frame explicitly. PART 4 should make sure §6 / §7 / §10 do not slip back into procurement-administration register.
