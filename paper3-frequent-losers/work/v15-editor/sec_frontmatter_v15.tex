\begin{abstract}
What signal can oversight bodies extract from contract-award
records when bid microdata are unavailable? We document, in
public-procurement records of the State of São Paulo, Brazil
($\valYearStart$--$\valYearEnd$), that \emph{frequent
losers}---firms that bid without ever winning---carry usable
participation-based signal for procurement screening. Of
$\valAlwaysLosers$ firms with persistent zero wins, $\valFL$
participated in $\valThresholdStat$ or more tender-items. Their
presence associates with a $\valHeadlineRange$ higher negotiated
unit price across estimators in the broad sample, but the
conditional association reverses sign under strict-overlap
matching ($-9.7\%$ to $-30.7\%$): the two estimates describe the
same population at different scopes of selection, and we treat
the screen's signal as partly about which environments frequent
losers select into rather than as a uniform descriptive effect.
The price imprint shrinks across procuring-unit size quartiles
(from $\valOversightQonePct$ to $\valOversightQfourPct$; ratio
$\valPBUgradientRatio\!\!\times$), consistent with thinner
institutional capacity moderating the imprint, though buyer size
proxies for several institutional features and we do not identify
oversight as the operative channel. Against CADE's adjudicated
cartel records, the screen reaches AUC $0.748$ on a strict
contemporaneous benchmark and $0.864$ on temporal holdout against
cobidder labels---an indirect benchmark of cartel adjacency, not
proof of cartel membership. The participation-based screen is
intended as a triage tool for procurement oversight where bid
microdata are absent.
\end{abstract}
