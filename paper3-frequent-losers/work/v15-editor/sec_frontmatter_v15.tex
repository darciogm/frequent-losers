\begin{abstract}
Can cartels be screened without bid-level data? Most
cartel-detection methods read collusion off the distribution of
submitted bids, but many enforcement environments preserve only
the contract-award layer. We study what survives that
coarsening. A separating-equilibrium framework identifies
$\log(1+\text{tenders\_count})$ together with the
$\text{wins}=0$ filter as a sufficient ranking statistic for
cover-bidder type given award-record data. In São Paulo's
electronic procurement platform ($\valYearStart$--$\valYearEnd$),
$\valFL$ of $\valAlwaysLosers$ persistent zero-win firms cross
the resulting threshold; we call them \emph{frequent losers}.
Their presence associates with a $\valHeadlineRange$ higher
conditional log negotiated unit price across estimators. Forcing
overlap on observables reverses the sign (overlap-cell ATT
$\valMatchOverlapCoef$; propensity-score-trimmed
$\valMatchPSCoef$): a signature of a screening-value object,
not a treatment effect. Against CADE's cartel records the
construct reaches AUC $\valAUCprePost$ on a contemporaneous
benchmark and $\valAUCFLfirmTemp$ under temporal holdout against
cobidder labels; AUC is $\valAUCdirectStd$ against direct
defendants, by design. The construct supplies an award-layer
screening stage that runs ahead of the bid-layer forensic stage
the prior literature requires.
\end{abstract}
