\begin{abstract}
What signal can oversight bodies extract from contract-award
records when bid microdata are unavailable? In São Paulo's
electronic procurement platform ($\valYearStart$--$\valYearEnd$),
$\valFL$ of $\valAlwaysLosers$ firms with persistent zero wins
participated in $\valThresholdStat$ or more tender-items; we call
these firms \emph{frequent losers}. Their presence associates with
a $\valHeadlineRange$ higher negotiated unit price across
estimators in the broad sample, but reverses sign under
strict-overlap matching ($-9.7\%$ to $-30.7\%$). The two estimates
describe the same population at different scopes of selection. We
report the broad-sample range as the headline because it maps to
the deployment question---which environments to flag---and we
treat the screen's signal as partly about selection rather than a
uniform descriptive effect. The price imprint shrinks
$\valPBUgradientRatio\!\!\times$ across procuring-unit-size
quartiles, consistent with thinner institutional capacity at
smaller buyers, though we do not identify oversight as the
operative channel. Against CADE's cartel records, the screen
reaches AUC $\valAUCprePost$ on a strict contemporaneous benchmark
and $0.864$ on temporal holdout against cobidder labels---an
indirect benchmark of cartel adjacency, not membership. The
screen is a triage tool for procurement oversight where bid
microdata are absent.
\end{abstract}
