\section{The Audit on a Second Platform}
\label{sec:comparative}

Section~\ref{sec:validation} draws its boundary on a single
platform. Under a reproducible, non-circular label, the award-layer
score concentrates adjudication-anchored loser-side exposure in the
raw data; yet almost nothing survives once procurement opportunity
is held fixed, and the prospective ranking collapses outside the
incumbent pool. What an agency can carry away from that exercise is
not the screen but the decomposition behind it---the sequence of
checks that tells it how far a thin award-layer statistic can be
trusted before bid-level reconstruction begins. A decomposition of
that kind earns its place only if it survives a move to a second
system. We therefore run the same protocol, unchanged, on the
federal ComprasNet platform and ask what it returns where the
institutions differ. The lesson is scoped, not a slogan: the audit
\emph{protocol}---and, with it, the first-stage opportunity
deflation---reproduces on an institutionally distinct platform read
against \emph{partially overlapping} legal anchors, while the
loser-side score again fails to survive the opportunity adjustment.
We are careful about what this does and does not establish. ComprasNet
is a stress test of the protocol's portability, not an independent
cartel universe; the two platforms share part of the same CADE record,
so the federal result is neither confirmation nor refutation of any
firm's legal status. And the federal within-stratum null is
power-bounded: it does not, by itself, rule out a residual in the
operationally relevant $0.55$ region (Section~\ref{sec:comparative_verdict}).
A genuinely independent cartel ground truth remains future work.

\subsection{A Different Procurement System}
\label{sec:comparative_setting}

ComprasNet differs from BEC--SP on exactly the dimensions that bear
on the protocol. It is a single national system: BEC is one
state-level platform inside the federal legal frame, whereas
ComprasNet aggregates procuring units (UASGs) across the entire
federal administration. Its modality mix is narrower as well. The
federal panel is effectively pure Preg\~ao---roughly $15\%$ regular
electronic auction and $85\%$ price-registration (SRP, \emph{sistema
de registro de pre\c{c}os})---and the sealed-bid Convite modality
that organizes the institutional contrast in the main text no longer
exists at the federal level. With no modality margin to stratify on,
the loser-side construct has to earn its keep on a panel stripped of
the variation that structured the BEC reading, which makes the test
harder rather than easier. The observable window is $2013$--$2019$,
where the two panels overlap.

The second contrast is what the data let an analyst see, and it is
on-thesis. The public federal release carries participation and the
winner flag but no bid microdata---there is no federal counterpart to
the BEC \texttt{LANCES} bid ladder. An award-layer screen is the only
screen one \emph{can} build here; the bid-level reconstruction that
the main text places downstream of the screen cannot be assembled
from the public federal release at all. That makes the federal panel
the natural place to stress-test a thin award-layer protocol, because
it is precisely the setting where bid-microdata screens are off the
table and the question of how far the award layer can be trusted on
its own stops being academic.

\subsection{Data, Label, and Linkage}
\label{sec:comparative_data}

The federal panel comprises $\valFedPanelRows$ participation rows
across $\valFedFirms$ distinct CNPJ-14 firms, built from the
Comptroller-General (CGU) open bulk dumps and restricted to the
Preg\~ao modalities (regular and SRP) under the canonical schema used
throughout the main text. Of these, $\valFedAlwaysLosers$ are
always-losers---firms with a win rate of zero over the observed
window. We apply the \emph{same} IQR rule used for
BEC---$\textit{median} + 1.5\cdot\textit{IQR}$ on the tenders-count
distribution of always-losers, not the Tukey upper-quartile
rule---which re-derives a federal frequent-loser threshold of
$\valFedFLthreshold$ (against $14$ on BEC) and a canonical federal
frequent-loser count of $\valFedFLcount$ firms above it. The
threshold is re-estimated from the federal data, never transported:
the construct is the rule, not the particular cutoff value, exactly
as the main text requires.

The legal anchors are the \emph{same} CADE cases that anchor the BEC
validation---$\valFedAudCases$ numbered adjudicated procurement-cartel
processes, with one unnumbered summary case excluded for lack of
firm-level identification. This fixes what the federal exercise is
and is not: a second, institutionally distinct platform read against
\emph{partially overlapping} legal anchors rather than a fresh cartel
population. The shared anchors are why the federal result speaks to
portability of the protocol and the loser-side construct, and why it
cannot stand in for a separately assembled ground truth---a point we
keep in view throughout the interpretation below. Direct defendants
are matched to federal participant records by the canonical
establishment-anchored CNPJ rule. Expanding the anchored tender-items
yields $\valFedAudCobiddersAll$ federal cobidders under the broad rule
and $\valFedAudCobiddersBroadAL$ under the broad always-loser
restriction that mirrors the main-text validation target. The
frequent-loser flag never enters the label, so the federal validation
is non-circular in the score by the same construction as on BEC.

\subsection{Interpretation}
\label{sec:comparative_interpretation}

On the federal panel the protocol returns the same verdict it
returns on BEC, and on the row that matters it returns it more
cleanly. Both platforms over-credit the screen at the raw level: the
award-layer ROC-AUC ($\valFedAudRawAUC$ federally) reads like
discrimination until one asks what an analyst would have predicted
from procurement opportunity alone. Federally the answer arrives in a
single line. The exposure-only ranking, which never sees the score,
reaches $\valFedAudExpOnlyAUC$---matching and if anything edging past
the raw score itself ($\valFedAudRawAUC$). Where on BEC the exposure
axis only approaches the raw number, federally it already equals it,
so the screen's apparent reach is opportunity arithmetic with no
conduct premium left to claim. This is not the same null encountered
twice. It is a second coordinate on the over-crediting surface
characterized in \ref{prop:inflation}: because the raw award score and
the contact-defined label both load on participation volume, the raw
AUC inflates by an amount that grows with volume dispersion and
\emph{shrinks} with the adjudicated base rate. The federal platform
carries a base rate roughly an order of magnitude lower than BEC's,
and that characterization predicts exactly what the federal numbers
show---that at the rarer target the inflation is so nearly complete
that the opportunity axis alone edges \emph{out} the raw score
($\valFedAudExpOnlyAUC$ against $\valFedAudRawAUC$) rather than merely
approaching it. The two platforms thus differ in base rate and volume
dispersion and land where the inflation characterization places them;
the federal replication is a second point on that curve, not a
restatement of the BEC verdict. The remaining checks confirm the
reading from below. Holding procurement opportunity fixed, the
within-stratum residual collapses to near chance
($\valFedAudWithinAUC$), the nested model adds nothing detectable over
the exposure-only benchmark ($\valFedAudNestedIncrement$ DeLong
increment, $p=\valFedAudNestedP$), and the matched-strata permutation
does not reject ($p=\valFedAudPermCp$)---the same collapse, the same
nulls, the same shape the BEC audit produces. We state the federal
power bound as a bound and ask it to carry no more. With a federal
positive set about a third the size of BEC's, the matched-permutation
null is informative against a within-stratum residual of $0.60$ but
underpowered at $0.55$ ($\valFedAudPowerSixty$ versus
$\valFedAudPowerFive$ detection probability), so the federal
deflation does not lean on the permutation alone. It rests on four
power-robust pillars that no sample-size argument dissolves: the
exposure-only ranking already matches the raw score, the
within-stratum point estimate sits \emph{below} $0.5$, the matched
permutation does not reject, and the negative controls return the
same generic geometry. The within-stratum positive control recovers
$\geq 0.99$ ($\valFedAudOiControlMedium$), so the design would have
caught a within-stratum signal had one been present. The deflation is
neither a federal artifact nor a BEC artifact; it is what a
disciplined decomposition returns whenever a thin award-layer
statistic is held to an opportunity-adjusted standard. That is the
finding, not a confession. What reproduces across the two
systems---read against partially overlapping legal anchors---is the
audit protocol and its first-stage opportunity deflation, not a
deployable loser-side ranking; and the federal within-stratum null
is a power bound, not a clean exclusion of a $0.55$ residual. An
agency on either platform that runs the protocol learns, before
opening any bid record, that loser-side concentration flags
opportunity and should not be carried as a stand-alone prioritization
device. On the federal platform that lesson binds harder still,
because the bid-level reconstruction that might follow the screen
cannot be built from public data at all. Establishing the same
deflation against a genuinely independent cartel ground truth, rather
than the shared CADE anchors, remains future work.

\subsection{Observability as an On-Thesis Finding}
\label{sec:comparative_observability}

What each platform lets an analyst see is not a nuisance to be
controlled away---it is part of the finding. The two systems differ
first in the scale of the target: the federal positive base rate is
roughly an order of magnitude lower than BEC's. Any cross-platform
PR-AUC comparison has to be read against that base rate, since a
rarer target mechanically depresses precision-recall area; the
federal PR-AUC sits below BEC's for this reason alone, before any
question of discrimination arises. The systems differ, too, in what
counts as a product. Federal item codes live in buyer-specific
catalogs rather than a shared classification, so the item-group
margin the BEC audit stratifies on (the COARSE cell) has no clean
federal counterpart, and the federal opportunity cells are built on
buyer$\times$year margins instead (\ref{app:federal_audit_submission}).
The bid tier is missing outright: with no federal bid ladder there is
no bid-dispersion benchmark, no bid-layer comparator, and no way to
run the downstream forensic recovery on public data. Both gaps point
the same direction. An award-layer protocol is exactly the protocol
that survives where the richer records are absent---the common
predicament of an enforcement agency facing a national platform that
offers participation records and little else. That the loser-side
construct can be re-derived, the label re-anchored, and the full
deflation battery re-run on a system with no bid microdata is itself
the portability result. The thin-record screen runs where bid-layer
screens cannot be built, and the decomposition tells the agency how
far to trust it before bid-level reconstruction---should the records
ever become available---is worth commissioning.
