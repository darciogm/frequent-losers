\section{Evidence-Allocation Framework and Legal Scope}
\label{app:framework_submission}

\subsection{The pre-proof ranking problem}
\label{app:framework_primitives}

The model is intentionally spare: it justifies an ordering rule for
scarce forensic attention, not the internal organization of a
cartel (formation, side payments, leniency, and damages are treated
in Asker, 2010, and Marshall and Marx, 2012). The narrower object
is the pre-proof enforcement problem---why a firm that repeatedly
loses while still appearing in procurement records can be a useful
target for bid-level follow-up. The device is a reduced-form
evidence-allocation rule.

% B-COMPRESS: merged primitives + zero-win lemma prose into one block.
In a cartel-targeted tender one firm is assigned the winning role
and submits the designated bid $b^*$; a losing-role participant
submits $b_\ell>b^*$ and is not meant to win. Winning by deviation
creates an internal sanction $\kappa>0$ (loss of future rents,
relational standing, or capacity-allocation rights); let $r$ be the
private gain from deviating into the winner role. Firms outside the
winning role are of two reduced-form \emph{model} types: type $C$,
cartel-deployed losing-role participants, and type $G$, ordinary
losing participants (inefficient, capacity constrained, or
unlucky). The labels are a device for stating the ranking
condition; $C$ and $G$ are not observed classes. The empirical
screen conditions on the observed event $W_i=0$ ($W_i$ is the
firm's number of wins) and ranks firms by participation count
$T_i$.

\begin{lemma}[Zero-win losing role]
\label{lem:submission_zero_win}
If the internal sanction from winning in a losing role exceeds the
private deviation gain, $\kappa>r$, a losing-role participant
avoids winning: it submits $b_\ell>b^*$, so
$\Pr(W_i>0\mid C)=0$ over tenders in which the firm is deployed in
the losing role.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Submitting $b_\ell>b^*$ yields the deployment payoff net of the
role cost. Deviating to a winning bid gains $r$ but pays $\kappa$,
which is unprofitable when $\kappa>r$. The inequality fixes the win
probability of the losing role, not the numerical bid.
\end{proof}

The inequality $\kappa>r$ supports conditioning on the zero-win
event but does \emph{not} imply that all zero-win firms occupy a
losing role: the conditioning set is intentionally broad, since
many ordinary firms also never win. The evidentiary content comes
from the second observable---participation intensity---and from the
dynamic margin governing who remains in the zero-win set.

\subsection{Dynamic participation, exit, and the ranking statistic}
\label{app:framework_exit}

The ranking statistic is informative only if persistent zero-win
participation is more characteristic of a deployed losing role than
of an ordinary losing firm. We obtain that property
\emph{endogenously} from a participation-and-exit margin rather
than assuming a type-specific rate gap. An ordinary zero-win firm
$G$ pays a participation cost $c_G>0$ and earns expected gross
surplus $m_G(H_{it})$, with $H_{it}$ its loss history at item $i$,
time $t$; under Bayesian updating, repeated losses weakly lower
$m_G$. It continues iff
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:exit_G}
  V_G(H_{it}) \;=\; m_G(H_{it}) - c_G + \beta\,
  \mathbb{E}\!\left[V_G(H_{i,t+1})\mid H_{it}\right] \;\ge\; 0,
\end{equation}
and \emph{exits} when $V_G(H_{it})<0$. A cartel-deployed losing-role
firm $C$ instead values continuation through a role value
$a_C(H_{it})$ (future rents, relational or subcontracting value,
internal compensation for sustained losing). It continues iff
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:exit_C}
  V_C(H_{it}) \;=\; a_C(H_{it}) - c_C + \beta\,
  \mathbb{E}\!\left[V_C(H_{i,t+1})\mid H_{it}\right] \;\ge\; 0 .
\end{equation}
Because $a_C$ derives from the role rather than the firm's own win
prospect, it does not erode with the loss count the way $m_G$ does,
so after sufficiently many zero-win periods
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:exit_wedge}
  a_C(H_{it}) - c_C \;>\; m_G(H_{it}) - c_G ,
\end{equation}
and $C$ keeps participating while $G$ is selectively removed. The
empirical consequence is a \emph{selection} result: among surviving
zero-win firms,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:lambda_rank}
  \lambda_C \;>\; \lambda_G \qquad
  \text{on the surviving conditioning set } W_i=0 ,
\end{equation}
\emph{derived} from differential exit under
\eqref{eq:exit_G}--\eqref{eq:exit_wedge}, not posited as an
exogenous type effect.

\begin{assumption}[Role-value persistence]
\label{assu:submission_persistence}
Among surviving zero-win firms, cartel-deployed losing-role
participants have weakly greater net continuation value from
repeated losing than ordinary zero-win firms after sufficiently
many losses: there is a loss count $\bar L$ such that for
histories with accumulated losses exceeding $\bar L$,
$a_C(H_{it})-c_C \ge m_G(H_{it})-c_G$.
\end{assumption}

% B-COMPRESS: monotone-ranking subsection folded in here.
Conditional on survival and zero wins, let participation counts
follow a Poisson approximation,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:poisson}
  T_i \mid \tau_i, q \sim \text{Poisson}(\lambda_q \tau_i),
  \qquad q\in\{C,G\},
\end{equation}
with $\tau_i$ the length of time firm $i$ is observed; \eqref{eq:lambda_rank}
supplies the ordering $\lambda_C>\lambda_G$ on this surviving set.

\begin{proposition}[Persistence ranking]
\label{prop:submission_monotone}
Suppose (1) losing-role firms avoid winning when $\kappa>r$
(Lemma~\ref{lem:submission_zero_win}); (2) ordinary zero-win firms
exit when continuation value falls below the outside option
(\eqref{eq:exit_G}); (3) role value keeps $C$ in the risk set
longer, so the selection wedge
\eqref{eq:exit_wedge}--\eqref{eq:lambda_rank} holds
(Assumption~\ref{assu:submission_persistence}); and (4) among
survivors effective participation intensity is higher for $C$ than
for $G$, $\lambda_C>\lambda_G$. Then the posterior
$\Pr(C\mid T_i=t,W_i=0,\tau_i)$ is non-decreasing in $t$, and
$T_i$ and $\log(1+T_i)$ are monotone ranking statistics for
forensic priority within the zero-win stratum.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
By Bayes' rule the posterior odds of $C$ against $G$ are
proportional to the Poisson likelihood ratio
$\exp[-(\lambda_C-\lambda_G)\tau_i](\lambda_C/\lambda_G)^t$, which
under $\lambda_C>\lambda_G$ is increasing in $t$ (monotone
likelihood ratio of the Poisson family; Karlin and Rubin, 1956).%
\footnote{Karlin, S. and H.~Rubin (1956). The theory of decision
procedures for distributions with monotone likelihood ratio.
\textit{Annals of Mathematical Statistics} 27(2), 272--299.}
The posterior is non-decreasing in the count, and $\log(1+T_i)$
preserves the ranking.
\end{proof}

Condition~(4)---the surviving participation gap---is the key
empirical content, validated against adjudication-anchored labels
in \S\S4--6. This is a ranking result under maintained behavioral
conditions, not an identification claim. The main-text binary rule
(flagging firms above the median plus $1.5$ times the interquartile
range of zero-win participation) is an operational coarsening of
this ranking; the threshold is not a structural parameter, and
agencies could instead use the continuous score, a top-$k$ list, or
a budget-calibrated cutoff.

\begin{corollary}[Legal scope]
\label{prop:submission_scope}
Under Lemma~\ref{lem:submission_zero_win},
Assumption~\ref{assu:submission_persistence}, and
Proposition~\ref{prop:submission_monotone}, the frequent-loser
statistic orders expected investigative value within the zero-win
stratum. It is not sufficient for liability, damages, or cartel
membership.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Triage requires an ordering of expected investigative value, which
Proposition~\ref{prop:submission_monotone} supplies. Liability and
damages require different predicates---proof of agreement, conduct
in particular tenders, and a measure of price injury---that the
statistic, carrying only participation and win information, omits.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Empirical survival audit}
\label{app:framework_survival}

The exit margin of \S\ref{app:framework_exit} predicts who remains
on the platform: if role value sustains cartel-deployed losing
participation while ordinary zero-win firms are selectively removed,
the most exposed firms should display longer continued
participation. We audit this descriptively on a firm-year panel of
the $16{,}843$ always-losers ($101{,}366$ firm-years, 2009--2019,
right-censored at 2019). Because the audit uses \emph{full-sample}
labels, it is retrospective and mechanism-supporting at most; it
does not test cartel membership or conduct. ``Survival'' means
continued BEC \emph{participation}, not legal-entity survival.

% B-COMPRESS: collapsed the two descriptive paragraphs into one; hazard table moved to supplement.
The pattern is in the predicted direction.
Table~\ref{tab:survival_summary_submission} shows that FL cobidders
persist markedly longer: mean active years $3.51$ versus $2.87$ for
FL non-cobidders and $1.61$ for non-FL always-losers; median
duration $3$ versus $2$ versus $1$ years; exit share before 2019 of
$0.63$ versus $0.73$ versus $0.85$. A discrete-time hazard model
(cloglog, duration and calendar-year fixed effects, clustered by
firm) confirms the ordering, with exit-hazard ratios of $0.231$ for
FL cobidders and $0.365$ for FL non-cobidders relative to non-FL
always-losers---both far below one---so exposure to the labels is
associated with substantially lower exit hazard (full hazard
estimates, the Cox specification, and the sensitivity battery are
reported in the Online Supplement). The cobidder coefficient is
sharply estimated ($z=-22.2$, $p<10^{-100}$), and the
Kaplan--Meier curves are clearly separated. The direction is stable
across robustness variants---alternative exit definitions,
excluding late cohorts, and excluding the largest CADE case (ratio
$0.281$)---and a timing-disciplined to-date label attenuates it to
$0.768$ but leaves it below one.

\subsection{Evidentiary scope}
\label{app:framework_scope}

% B-COMPRESS: merged survival caveat + evidentiary-scope into one scope paragraph.
The survival result is consistent with the exit-margin logic---role
value sustaining persistent losing participation---but is
mechanism-supporting at most; ordinary zero-win firms may exit for
many reasons. More generally, even if the model is correct, a high
value of $\log(1+T_i)$ raises the posterior probability of
loser-side exposure only relative to other zero-win firms: it does
not identify an agreement, assign cartel membership, prove any
specific bid was cover, or establish a damages base. Those claims
require the richer record (bid values, communications, leniency
material, winner-rotation patterns). This boundary is the legal
reason for the staged architecture in the main text: the award
layer ranks firms on a statistic that is cheap, observable, and
monotone under a transparent behavioral assumption, while the bid
layer and case record do the evidentiary work that liability
requires. The paper's identification continues to rest on the
opportunity-adjusted and timing-disciplined tests of \S\S4--6.
