\section{Economic Content and Ordinary-Loser Alternatives}
\label{sec:cobidder_type}

The validation results show that the award-layer ranking
concentrates adjudication-anchored loser-side exposure. This
section gives that exposure target economic content through a
demanding within-stratum comparison: among firms already inside
the frequent-loser stratum, do the firms our screen targets---%
those observed co-bidding with direct CADE defendants---differ
from other frequent losers in dimensions relevant for loser-side
forensic prioritization? Throughout, ``cobidder'' denotes an
adjudication-anchored exposure label, not legal membership.
Because the samples are large (tens of thousands of firms), we
report standardized mean differences (SMD, Cohen's $d$) rather
than $p$-values: significance is uninformative at this $N$, while
standardized differences speak to economic magnitude. The full
profile, opportunity-adjusted differences, monotonicity bins,
binary-versus-continuous comparison, and the ordinary-loser
adjudication table are reported in
Appendix~\ref{app:profile_submission}; we summarize the headline
findings here.

\subsection{Economic content and network specificity}
\label{sec:profile_5_1}

Cobidders are economically distinct from other frequent losers,
and not marginally so. The $\valProfCob$ cobidders are almost
entirely electronic-auction firms---a Preg\~ao share of
$\valProfPregaoCob$ against $\valProfPregaoNoncob$ for the
$\valProfNcob$ other frequent losers (SMD $\valProfPregaoSMD$)---%
they are deployed more broadly, reaching $\valProfBuyersCob$
distinct buyers versus $\valProfBuyersNoncob$ (SMD
$\valProfBuyersSMD$), they are more persistent across active years
(SMD $\valProfYearsSMD$), and they sit far closer to the legal
anchors used in validation (standardized difference in CADE cases
touched $\valProfNcasesSMD$). These are deployment and proximity
measures, not legal conclusions: cobidders occupy a wider,
longer-lived loser-side footprint within the already-selected
frequent-loser pool, anchored to the adjudicated network. The
ranking is also specific to that network. Negative controls that
replace the true CADE defendants with matched placebo anchors and
re-score yield a real-anchor ROC-AUC of $\valProfNegCtrlRealAUC$
that dominates the placebo-anchor distribution (mean
$\valProfNegCtrlPlaceboAUC$; empirical $p < 0.001$), and the
global zero-win definition of always-losers is not dominated by
any market-specific alternative (best alternative
$\valProfMarketZeroWinBest$, well below the global
$\valProfNegCtrlRealAUC$). The screen's ranking quality is tied to
the actual defendant network, not to generic high-volume
co-bidding---the central strength of the validation
(Appendix~\ref{app:profile_submission}). This reading is
consistent with evidence that auction collusion can operate
through bidder roles, repeated relationships, and cartel
organization
\citep{baldwin1997bidder,porter1999ohio,pesendorfer2000study,asker2010leniency,clark2021collusion}.

\subsection{An exposure ranking, not a collusion-intensity ranking}
\label{sec:opp_adjusted_5_2}

Much of the raw distinction is procurement-environment composition
and partly mechanical proximity that attenuates---and in one case
vanishes---once we adjust for opportunity, the expected contact and
exposure a firm would accumulate given its participation profile.
The item-group concentration difference, raw SMD
$\valProfHHIigRawSMD$, \emph{vanishes} under adjustment (adjusted
SMD $\valProfHHIigAdjSMD$): the apparent product specialization of
cobidders is procurement-environment composition, not an intrinsic
portfolio trait. Proximity to the legal anchors roughly
\emph{halves}---the CADE-cases SMD falls from $\valProfNcasesSMD$ to
$\valProfNcasesAdjSMD$---consistent with a large part of raw
proximity being the mechanical consequence of broader participation
in defendant-bearing cells. Persistence survives; product
specialization does not (Appendix~\ref{app:profile_submission}).

The most informative diagnostic concerns what the ranking actually
orders. Binning firms by participation $T_i$, cobidder prevalence
rises monotonically with participation---from zero in the lowest
bins to roughly $17\%$ in the highest (rank correlation
$\valProfMonoPrevRho$)---but opportunity-adjusted excess contact
$X_i = O_i - E_i$ is flat-to-negative across the same bins (rank
correlation $\valProfMonoExcessRho$). Higher-ranked firms are not
contacting defendants \emph{more than their exposure predicts};
they are simply more exposed. The award-layer rank is therefore an
\textbf{exposure ranking, not a collusion-intensity ranking}---%
exactly what the reach-and-limits reframe expects. This also
reframes the binary FL14 cutoff as an \emph{administrative}
implementation of the exposure ranking, not a structural break: the
continuous score $\log(1+T_i)$ ties or beats the FL14 binary in
every full-sample configuration (full-universe ROC-AUC $0.939$
continuous vs.\ $0.924$ binary), and there is no bunching at the
threshold---the density ratio at $T=14$ is $\valProfBunchRatio$,
essentially flat (Appendix~\ref{app:profile_submission}). FL14 buys
deployability and a simple reporting cut, not a behavioral
discontinuity.

\subsection{Ordinary-loser alternatives and what Section~5 establishes}
\label{sec:ordinary_5_4}

Could the same profile arise from ordinary, non-collusive reasons
that firms lose persistently? The honest summary is that
ordinary-loser alternatives are \emph{narrowed but not
eliminated}. Cobidders are wider and longer-lived rather than
narrow and short-lived, with a markedly lower Convite and
minimum-quorum share, so they are not low-capacity quorum-fillers.
But the proxies that would settle the remaining alternatives---%
distance-to-winner, geography and logistics, and later wins---are
\textsc{not\_observed} at the award layer, so benign specialization
and geography remain possible
(Appendix~\ref{app:profile_submission}). What distinguishes
cobidders from these ordinary stories is the one axis the
alternatives do not predict: contact with the adjudicated CADE
network, the axis the screen is built on.

Section~5 therefore gives the award-layer exposure target genuine
but bounded economic content. The strengths are real---cobidders
are economically distinct and the ranking is specific to the real
defendant network ($p < 0.001$ on ROC against matched placebo
nulls)---but the limits are equally clear: much of the raw
distinction is procurement-environment composition and partly
mechanical proximity that attenuates under opportunity adjustment;
the rank is monotone in exposure but flat in opportunity-adjusted
excess contact; FL14 is administrative, not structural; and
ordinary-loser alternatives are narrowed, not eliminated. The
construct is a disciplined exposure ranking with genuine economic
content. It does not establish membership, agreement, liability, or
cover bidding in any tender, and it is not diagnostic by itself.
Read together with the validation of
Section~\ref{sec:validation} and the operational sequencing of
Section~6, it supports triage, not liability: the award layer
orders attention toward adjudication-anchored loser-side exposure,
and richer records evaluate what the screen has prioritized. The
next section turns from content to implementation, asking whether
the award-layer rank can be placed before bid-distribution
forensics in a sequence that saves bid-microdata recovery while
preserving adjudication-anchored recall.
