\section{Economic Content and Ordinary-Loser Alternatives}
\label{sec:cobidder_type}

The validation establishes that the award-layer ranking concentrates
adjudication-anchored loser-side exposure. Its target is the broad
set of $\valMainCobidders$ always-loser cobidders---unique
always-loser firms that share at least one tender-item with a
BEC-active direct CADE defendant---of which $\valMainCobFL$
($52.4\%$) satisfy the frequent-loser cut and $\valMainCobNonFL$
($47.6\%$) do not. The label is thus not conditioned on
frequent-loser status. What that target lacks so far is economic
content, and this section supplies it through a deliberately
demanding within-stratum comparison: among always-loser firms, do
the ones the screen targets---those observed co-bidding with direct
CADE defendants---look different from other always-losers along
dimensions that would matter for loser-side forensic prioritization?
We run the profile contrast inside the frequent-loser stratum (the
$\valMainCobFL$ FL cobidders against the FL non-cobidders), holding
the cut fixed so that no gap can be read back into the cut itself.
Throughout, ``cobidder'' is an adjudication-anchored exposure label,
never a statement of legal membership. With thousands of firms in
each group, $p$-values carry no information, so we report
standardized mean differences (SMD, Cohen's $d$), which speak to
economic magnitude instead. The full profile, the
opportunity-adjusted differences, the monotonicity bins, the
binary-versus-continuous comparison, and the ordinary-loser
adjudication table all appear in \ref{app:profile_submission}; here
we keep to the headline findings.

\subsection{What opportunity adjustment removes, and what survives}
\label{sec:profile_5_1}

The headline here cuts against the screen. Once we adjust for
opportunity---the expected contact and exposure a firm would
accumulate given its participation profile---the breadth,
persistence, and specialization gaps between cobidders and other
frequent losers \emph{vanish}. The item-group concentration
difference falls from a raw SMD of $\valProfHHIigRawSMD$ to an
adjusted $\valProfHHIigAdjSMD$, so the apparent product
specialization of cobidders is a feature of the procurement
environments they happen to compete in, not an intrinsic portfolio
trait. The breadth gap (distinct buyers) and the persistence gap
(active years) go the same way. What survives is only the mechanical
defendant-contact variables: proximity to the legal anchors
attenuates modestly, with the CADE-cases SMD slipping from
$\valProfNcasesSMD$ to $\valProfNcasesAdjSMD$. That residual is no
corroboration of anything, because the label is itself built from
defendant contact---the mechanical share cannot be peeled apart from
a behavioral one, and the contact variables cannot stand as evidence
about conduct. Hold opportunity fixed, and almost none of the
non-mechanical distinction is left (\ref{app:profile_submission}).

The raw contrasts that follow are exactly the ones opportunity
adjustment erases; we report them as descriptive texture rather than
findings. The $\valProfCob$ FL cobidders are predominantly
electronic-auction firms---a Preg\~ao share of $\valProfPregaoCob$
against $\valProfPregaoNoncob$ for the $\valProfNcob$ other frequent
losers (SMD $\valProfPregaoSMD$). They reach more distinct buyers
($\valProfBuyersCob$ versus $\valProfBuyersNoncob$, SMD
$\valProfBuyersSMD$), they persist modestly longer across active
years (SMD $\valProfYearsSMD$), and they sit closer to the legal
anchors used in validation (CADE-cases SMD $\valProfNcasesSMD$).
These are measures of breadth and proximity, not legal conclusions,
and the breadth and persistence gaps among them have already failed
the opportunity benchmark above.

Network specificity points the same way. The negative controls show
that the raw concentration is generic co-participation arithmetic
rather than a signature of the adjudicated network: swap the true
CADE defendants for matched placebo anchors and the raw ranking
reappears (real-anchor ROC-AUC $\valProfNegCtrlRealAUC$ against a
placebo-anchor mean of $\valProfNegCtrlPlaceboAUC$; empirical
$p=0.46$), and anchors built from non-CADE high-volume winners
generate raw discrimination at least as high (null mean $0.78$;
$p=0.91$). Put plainly, non-CADE high-volume winners sit at least as
tightly inside the network as the real defendants do---the same
reading Section~\ref{sec:validation_exposure} reaches through the
opportunity benchmark. The global zero-win definition does retain
more raw discrimination than any market-specific alternative (best
alternative $\valProfMarketZeroWinBest$ against the global
$\valProfNegCtrlRealAUC$), so the definition itself is not where the
construct weakens (\ref{app:profile_submission}).

The most telling diagnostic concerns what the ranking actually
orders. Binning firms by participation $T_i$, cobidder prevalence
climbs monotonically with participation---from near zero in the
lowest bins to roughly $28\%$ in the highest (rank correlation
$\valProfMonoPrevRho$)---while opportunity-adjusted excess contact
$X_i = O_i - E_i$ stays flat-to-negative across the same bins (rank
correlation $\valProfMonoExcessRho$). Higher-ranked firms are not
contacting defendants \emph{more than their exposure predicts};
they are simply more exposed. The award-layer rank is therefore an
\textbf{exposure ranking, not a collusion-intensity ranking}---%
exactly what the reach-and-limits reframe expects. This also
reframes the binary FL14 cutoff as an \emph{administrative}
implementation of the exposure ranking, not a structural break: the
continuous score $\log(1+T_i)$ ties or beats the FL14 binary in
every full-sample configuration (full-universe ROC-AUC $0.761$
continuous vs.\ $0.688$ binary), and there is no bunching at the
threshold---the density ratio at $T=14$ is $\valProfBunchRatio$,
essentially flat (\ref{app:profile_submission}). FL14 buys
a simple reporting cut, not a behavioral discontinuity. This reading
is consistent with evidence that auction collusion can operate
through bidder roles, repeated relationships, and cartel
organization
\citep{baldwin1997bidder,porter1999ohio,pesendorfer2000study,asker2010leniency}.

\subsection{Ordinary-loser alternatives and what this section establishes}
\label{sec:ordinary_5_4}

Could the same profile arise from the ordinary, non-collusive
reasons firms lose persistently? The honest answer is that
ordinary-loser alternatives are \emph{narrowed but not eliminated}.
Cobidders are wider and longer-lived rather than narrow and
short-lived, and they carry a lower Convite and minimum-quorum share,
which weakens the low-capacity quorum-filler story without ruling it
out. The proxies that would settle what remains---distance-to-winner,
geography and logistics, later wins---are unobserved at the award
layer, so benign specialization and geography stay on the table
(\ref{app:profile_submission}). The one axis these ordinary stories
do not predict is the axis the screen is built on: contact with the
adjudicated CADE network.

Taken together, Section~\ref{sec:cobidder_type} gives the
award-layer exposure target modest, bounded economic content, and on
balance the limits dominate. Most of the raw distinction is
procurement-environment composition and partly mechanical proximity
that vanishes or attenuates sharply once opportunity is held fixed;
the breadth and persistence gaps do not survive that adjustment; the
raw ranking is reproduced by placebo and non-CADE anchors, so it is
not specific to the adjudicated network; the rank is monotone in
exposure yet flat in opportunity-adjusted excess contact; FL14 is
administrative rather than structural; and ordinary-loser
alternatives are narrowed, not eliminated. The construct is a
disciplined exposure ranking whose non-mechanical economic content is
modest. It establishes no membership, agreement, liability, or cover
bidding in any tender, and on its own it is not diagnostic. Read
alongside the validation of Section~\ref{sec:validation} and the
operational sequencing of Section~\ref{sec:screening_forensics}, it
supports triage rather than liability: the award layer orders
attention toward adjudication-anchored loser-side exposure, and
richer records then judge what the screen has prioritized.
Section~\ref{sec:screening_forensics} turns from content to
implementation, asking whether the award-layer rank can be placed
ahead of bid-distribution forensics in a sequence that spares
bid-level reconstruction while preserving adjudication-anchored
recall.
