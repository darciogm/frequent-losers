\begin{abstract}
Cartel enforcement must allocate costly proof-producing effort
before legal proof exists. Contract-award records are routine and
cheap; the bid-level evidence needed to prove coordination is
costly to recover, clean, and inspect. Using S\~ao Paulo's BEC
procurement platform ($\valYearStart$--$\valYearEnd$) and CADE
adjudications, we build a \emph{frequent-loser} score that ranks
priority among zero-win firms from award records alone. Its
target is adjudication-anchored exposure among always-loser
cobidders, not cartel membership. We validate the ranking with
opportunity-exposure discipline and timing audits, and show it
adds non-redundant information to bid-distribution forensics on
the same target. Used as a gatekeeper, it concentrates the
bid-microdata pool the forensic stage must open

\end{abstract}
