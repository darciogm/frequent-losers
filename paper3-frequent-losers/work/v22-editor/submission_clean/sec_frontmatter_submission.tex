\begin{abstract}
Agencies must allocate proof-producing effort before proof exists,
when evidence-grade records are costly to open. On S\~ao Paulo's BEC
platform with CADE adjudications, we audit a minimal award-layer
screen---persistent zero-win participation---against a non-circular,
adjudication-anchored label. The first lesson generalizes beyond
procurement: validating a volume-loaded screen against a
contact-defined label over-credits it, and that inflation is an
estimable size-bias governed by participation-volume dispersion and
summarized by a leading-order sufficient statistic computable before
any bid file opens. The second is organizational: the award-to-bid
decision is a budget-dependent cost--recall frontier, the image of a cost--benefit optimum with no fixed cutoff to defend. Here
the raw screen's reach is mostly exposure rather than conduct, and
strict prospective ranking fails outside incumbent pools. On a second
platform with partially overlapping anchors, the over-crediting
characterization replicates at full power; the within-stratum
deflation holds subject to a stated federal power bound. Liability
stays in the richer record.
\end{abstract}
