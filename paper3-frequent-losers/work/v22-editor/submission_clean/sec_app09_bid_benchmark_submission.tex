\section{Bid-Layer Benchmark and Cost-Recall Details}
\label{app:bid_benchmark_submission}

This appendix documents the construction and diagnostics behind the
bid-layer benchmark used in Section~6.1, which asks whether the cheap,
award-layer frequent-loser signal adds value over an expensive screen
built from full bid microdata. The bid-layer benchmark is a
\emph{forensic-stage} diagnostic: it computes seven Imhof--Wallimann
within-tender bid-distribution moments, aggregates them to the firm, and
feeds them to a random forest evaluated by cross-validation. It is not a
deployable first-stage triage tool, because the moments require all
within-tender bids to be observed and recovered. Throughout,
``cobidder'' denotes an \emph{adjudication-anchored exposure label} --- a
firm that co-bid with a firm named in a CADE proceeding --- and never a
finding of participation in any agreement; the benchmark \emph{ranks and
prioritizes} a limited cobidder target and never \emph{detects}
cartelists. Granular per-feature, per-fold, per-case, and calibration
diagnostics, the full feature dictionary, and the supporting tables moved
out of this appendix are deposited in Online Supplement~E.

%--------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{LANCES Bid-Layer Data, Units, and Support}
\label{app:bid_data_submission}

The bid layer is built from
\texttt{v3/data/processed/bid\_level\_with\_prices.parquet}
(approximately 245~MB), one row per priced bid with columns
\texttt{c\'odigofornecedor} (firm), \texttt{numerodaoc} (OC sequence),
\texttt{c\'odigoitem} (item), and \texttt{bid\_price}; a bid is retained
only if \texttt{bid\_price IS NOT NULL AND bid\_price > 0}. The benchmark
is produced by script~31 (\texttt{31\_imhof\_full\_pipeline.R},
\texttt{arrow}\,+\,\texttt{data.table}, seed \texttt{20260430}) and a
DuckDB twin, script~49 (\texttt{49\_imhof\_incremental\_value.R}, seed
\texttt{20260501}); a dedicated validation run
(\texttt{09\_bid\_benchmark\_validation.R}, seed \texttt{20260603})
generates the audit numbers. The bid layer links to the award layer
through the tender-item key $(\texttt{numerodaoc},\texttt{c\'odigoitem})$
and, for firm attachment, through
$(\texttt{numerodaoc},\texttt{c\'odigoitem},\texttt{c\'odigofornecedor})$;
the within-tender moments are \emph{not} recoverable from the award layer,
which records no per-bid prices.

The modeling unit is the \emph{firm}: per-tender moments are shared across
all firms bidding on a tender-item and then firm-mean aggregated. Two
validation pools arise from a legitimate two-sample design (not a
computation discrepancy): the \emph{max-coverage} pool requires complete
cv/skew/kurt ($N=16{,}779$, $193$ positives, $2{,}733$ FL14), while the
\emph{same-universe incremental} pool additionally requires
\texttt{imhof\_cv\_sd} ($N=11{,}676$, $193$ positives). The
\texttt{imhof\_cv\_sd} feature is undefined for single-priced-tender firms
(missing for $30.68\%$ of the $16{,}843$ always-losers), which is the
entire source of the gap; all other moments are missing for fewer than
$0.4\%$ of firms. \textbf{All $193$ positive (cobidder) labels are
non-missing on every feature in both pools}: support loss falls entirely
on the negatives, so there is no positive attrition. The $47$ direct CADE
defendants are excluded from the cobidder candidate pool by upstream label
curation; the defendant-excluded validation pool is $N=16{,}731$ with
$190$ positives. (The full units-of-observation and per-feature
missingness tables are in Online Supplement~E.)

\subsection{Bid-Feature Families}
\label{app:bid_dictionary_submission}

Seven Imhof--Wallimann moments are constructed within each tender-item
with $\geq 2$ priced bids, then averaged across each firm's priced tenders
to the firm level. They fall into four families: \emph{dispersion}
(coefficient of variation \texttt{imhof\_cv\_mean} and its within-firm SD
\texttt{imhof\_cv\_sd}, undefined for single-tender firms);
\emph{level/relative spread} (normalized spread and log min--max,
\texttt{imhof\_spread\_mean}, \texttt{imhof\_minmax\_mean});
\emph{shape} (skewness \texttt{imhof\_skew\_mean} and excess kurtosis
\texttt{imhof\_kurt\_mean}); and \emph{rank/order} (the second-low gap
$\log(b_2/b_1)$, \texttt{imhof\_second\_low\_mean}, the only moment with a
winning-margin component, of low--moderate leakage risk but still a
field-level moment shared across firms, not a firm-specific outcome).
Two auxiliary counts accompany them, \texttt{n\_bids} (gating the $\geq 2$
filter) and \texttt{n\_tenders\_priced} (a firm exposure count). No
co-bidding/network or timing, bid-revision, or late-bid features are
implemented: the parquet read selects only firm, OC, item, and price, and
no timestamp column is touched. The full feature dictionary with all
columns and formulas is in Online Supplement~E.

\subsection{Model Specification}
\label{app:bid_model_submission}

The learner is a random forest (\texttt{ranger}, $500$ trees,
\texttt{probability = TRUE}, $\texttt{num.threads}=12$) --- \textbf{not} a
logit, despite an earlier script header that mislabeled it. There is no
hyperparameter tuning (fixed $500$ trees, default \texttt{mtry} and
\texttt{min.node.size}, no grid search) and no standardization (the forest
is scale-invariant). Missingness is handled listwise through the pool
restriction. The outcome is binary \texttt{is\_cade} ($1$ for a firm in
the $193$-firm cobidder file, in the candidate pool, not a direct
defendant). Evaluation is by $5$-fold cross-validation, each firm
receiving one held-out predicted probability; AUC and DeLong tests are
computed on these out-of-fold firm-level predictions. The numbers
reproduce to four decimals for the script~49 pool (per-fold seeds) and
within cross-validation jitter at the third decimal for the script~31
pool. (The full spec-by-spec reproduction audit is in Online
Supplement~E.)

\subsection{Cross-Validation and Holdout Designs}
\label{app:bid_designs_submission}

Four validation designs are run, increasing in stringency:
\textbf{Design~A} (pooled-random $5$-fold) is a diagnostic
\emph{upper bound}; \textbf{Design~E} (case-grouped / leave-one-case-out)
holds out an entire CADE case so its clustered positives never co-train;
\textbf{Design~F} (exclude-label-defining tenders) recomputes the moments
after dropping defendant-co-appearance tenders to test feature--target
contamination; and \textbf{Design~G} (environment holdout) holds out an
item-group. Strict bid-timing validation is \textbf{blocked}: the
within-tender moments cannot be cleanly time-limited without re-deriving
them on a per-period bid panel. Because positives cluster by case, random
folds (Design~A) are optimistic --- same-case positives appear in both
train and test --- and the pooled bid ROC drops from $0.891$ (A) to
$0.810$ (E), with per-case LOCO AUCs as low as $0.569$ against an award
score stable at $0.92$--$0.99$ across cases. (Full fold-composition and
per-case LOCO tables are in Online Supplement~E.)

\subsection{Leakage, Contamination, and Performance}
\label{app:bid_perf_submission}

The award score is \emph{clean} (a CADE-label-independent rank on
\texttt{log1p(tenders\_count)}), as is its strict-timing variant. The bid
and combined forests under full-period features and random folds are
\textbf{diagnostic-only}: random folds are optimistic, and the firm-mean
moments include label-defining tenders. The key result is that
performance \emph{survives} the leakage controls --- excluding
label-defining tenders (Design~F) moves the bid ROC only from $0.891$ to
$0.874$ (a $-0.017$ contamination effect), and the case-grouped design (E)
remains informative. The headline ranking performance is in
Table~\ref{tab:bid_perf_submission}: reading down the bid-RF rows shows
the optimism of random folds (ROC $0.891$ at A $\to 0.810$ at E
$\to 0.874$ at F). The combined model is the upper bound (ROC $0.958$
pooled, $0.936$ case-grouped), and on the headline same-coverage
comparison the Imhof bid screen reaches $0.888$, the FL14 binary award
screen $0.921$, and the combination $0.962$. The award and bid scores are
only moderately correlated (Spearman $\approx 0.42$; top-$500$ overlap
$\approx 17\%$), so the two layers order firms differently and combine
constructively: the incremental PR-AUC of adding the award signal to the
bid forest is $+0.151$ under pooled-random folds, shrinking to $+0.064$
under case-grouped folds (DeLong $p<0.001$ in both). The bid-RF is well
calibrated ($0.108$ predicted vs.\ $0.103$ observed cobidder rate in the
top decile; Brier $0.0095$). (The full leakage-audit, complementarity, and
calibration tables are in Online Supplement~E.)

\subsection{Limitations}
\label{app:bid_limitations_submission}

The bid-layer benchmark is a full-observability diagnostic, not a first
stage: the Imhof moments require all within-tender bids, which is exactly
the information cost the award-layer screen avoids; the headline
pooled-random numbers carry random-CV optimism; strict bid-timing
validation is infeasible; the benchmark is deliberately modest (untuned,
unstandardized ``Imhof--Wallimann moments in a random forest''); and the
target is the $193$ always-loser cobidders, an adjudication-anchored
exposure measure, so false positives are unlabeled firms under a narrow
target and nothing here constitutes proof of conduct.
