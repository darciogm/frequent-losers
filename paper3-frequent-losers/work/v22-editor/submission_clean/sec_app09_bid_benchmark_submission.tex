\section{Bid-Layer Benchmark: Construction and Diagnostics}
\label{app:bid_benchmark_submission}

This appendix documents the construction and diagnostics behind the
bid-layer benchmark used in Section~6.1 to ask whether the cheap,
award-layer frequent-loser signal adds value over an expensive screen
built from full bid microdata. The bid-layer benchmark is a
\emph{forensic-stage} diagnostic: it computes seven
Imhof--Wallimann within-tender bid-distribution moments, aggregates
them to the firm, and feeds them to a random forest evaluated by
cross-validation. It is not a deployable first-stage triage tool,
because the moments require all within-tender bids to be observed and
recovered. Throughout, ``cobidder'' denotes an
\emph{adjudication-anchored exposure label} --- a firm that co-bid with
a firm named in a CADE proceeding --- and never a finding of
participation in any agreement. The aim here is reproducibility: every
feature formula, aggregation rule, sample restriction, learner setting,
fold scheme, seed, and missingness convention is stated exactly as
implemented.

%--------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{LANCES Bid-Layer Data and Linkage}
\label{app:bid_data_submission}

The bid layer is built from
\texttt{v3/data/processed/bid\_level\_with\_prices.parquet}
(approximately 245~MB), which carries one row per priced bid with
columns \texttt{c\'odigofornecedor} (firm), \texttt{numerodaoc} (OC
sequence), \texttt{c\'odigoitem} (item), and \texttt{bid\_price}. A bid
is retained only if \texttt{bid\_price IS NOT NULL AND bid\_price > 0}.
The benchmark is produced by two parallel scripts: script~31
(\texttt{31\_imhof\_full\_pipeline.R}, \texttt{arrow}\,+\,\texttt{data.table},
seed \texttt{20260430}) and a DuckDB twin, script~49
(\texttt{49\_imhof\_incremental\_value.R}, seed \texttt{20260501}); a
dedicated validation run (\texttt{09\_bid\_benchmark\_validation.R},
seed \texttt{20260603}) generates the audit numbers reported below.

The bid layer links to the award layer through the tender-item key
$(\texttt{numerodaoc},\texttt{c\'odigoitem})$, which is identical to the
key used by the award-layer screen
(\texttt{BEC\_collapse\_final}, \texttt{LOSERS\_rebuilt}). Bid rows are
attached to firms, and thence to the award-layer score, through
$(\texttt{numerodaoc},\texttt{c\'odigoitem},\texttt{c\'odigofornecedor})$.
The within-tender moments are \emph{not} recoverable from the award
layer, which records no per-bid prices.

\paragraph{Learner correction.} The benchmark learner is a
\textbf{random forest} (\texttt{ranger}, $500$ trees,
\texttt{probability = TRUE}), \textbf{not} a logit. An earlier script
header and docstring described the model as ``logit / 10-fold CV''; the
executed code uses \texttt{ranger} with $5$-fold cross-validation. The
correct description of the benchmark is therefore
``Imhof--Wallimann moments in a random forest, evaluated by $5$-fold
cross-validation.''

\subsection{Bid-Feature Dictionary}
\label{app:bid_dictionary_submission}

Seven Imhof--Wallimann moments are constructed within each tender-item
with $\geq 2$ priced bids, then averaged across each firm's priced
tenders to the firm level. Table~\ref{tab:bid_dictionary_submission}
gives the exact formulas. Two auxiliary counts (\texttt{n\_bids}, a
tender-item gating variable, and \texttt{n\_tenders\_priced}, a firm
exposure count) accompany them. \textbf{Timing, bid-revision, and
late-bid features are NOT implemented} in this benchmark: the parquet
read selects only firm, OC, item, and price, and no timestamp column is
touched. Those features are discussed conceptually in the main text but
are not part of this pipeline.

\begin{table}[htbp]\centering
\begin{adjustbox}{max width=\textwidth,center}
\begin{threeparttable}
\caption{Bid-feature dictionary: seven within-tender moments.}
\label{tab:bid_dictionary_submission}\scriptsize
\begin{tabular}{p{2.7cm}p{6.0cm}p{2.5cm}p{1.5cm}}
\toprule
Feature & Per-tender formula (firm-mean unless noted) & Family & Leakage risk \\
\midrule
\texttt{imhof\_cv\_mean} & $\mathrm{cv}=\mathrm{sd}(b)/\mathrm{mean}(b)$; firm: $\mathrm{mean}(\mathrm{cv})$ & dispersion (CV) & low \\
\texttt{imhof\_cv\_sd} & firm: $\mathrm{sd}(\mathrm{cv})$ across tenders; undefined for single-tender firms & CV variability & low \\
\texttt{imhof\_skew\_mean} & $\mathrm{skew}=\mathrm{mean}((b-m)^3)/s^3$; firm: $\mathrm{mean}(\mathrm{skew})$ & skewness & low \\
\texttt{imhof\_kurt\_mean} & $\mathrm{kurt}=\mathrm{mean}((b-m)^4)/s^4-3$; firm: $\mathrm{mean}(\mathrm{kurt})$ & kurtosis & low \\
\texttt{imhof\_spread\_mean} & $\mathrm{spread}=(\max b-\min b)/\mathrm{mean}(b)$; firm: $\mathrm{mean}$ & spread & low \\
\texttt{imhof\_minmax\_mean} & $\log(\max b/\min b)$; firm: $\mathrm{mean}$ & log min--max & low \\

\midrule

\end{tablenotes}

\subsection{Model Specifications}
\label{app:bid_model_submission}

The learner is a random forest (\texttt{ranger}, $500$ trees,
\texttt{probability = TRUE}, $\texttt{num.threads}=12$). There is
\textbf{no hyperparameter tuning} (fixed $500$ trees, default
\texttt{mtry} and \texttt{min.node.size}, no grid search) and
\textbf{no standardization} (the random forest is scale-invariant).
Missingness is handled \emph{listwise} through the pool restriction:
each pool is the set of firms with the required complete feature set.
The outcome is binary \texttt{is\_cade} ($1$ for a firm in the
$193$-firm cobidder file, in the candidate pool, not a direct
defendant). Evaluation is by $5$-fold cross-validation, with each firm
receiving one held-out predicted probability from the fold in which it
is the test set; AUC and DeLong tests are computed on these out-of-fold
firm-level predictions.

Table~\ref{tab:bid_repro_submission} records the number-reproduction
audit. The script~49 pool ($N=11{,}676$) reproduces to four decimals
exactly because it sets per-fold seeds. The script~31 pool
($N=16{,}779$) reproduces within cross-validation jitter at the third
decimal because script~31 sets no per-fold random-forest seed; we
recommend adding \texttt{seed = 20260430 + k} to the \texttt{ranger}
call to make those numbers bit-reproducible (no manuscript number
changes). The single-feature \texttt{imhof\_cv\_only} specification is
the most fold-sensitive: its canonical value is $0.585$, and a single
re-run produced $0.547$; this is a near-coin-flip single-feature forest
and the canonical CSV value stands.

\subsection{Cross-Validation and Holdout Designs}
\label{app:bid_designs_submission}

Four validation designs are run, increasing in stringency:
\textbf{Design~A} (pooled-random $5$-fold) is a diagnostic
\emph{upper bound}; \textbf{Design~E} (case-grouped / leave-one-case-out)
holds out an entire CADE case so its clustered positives never
co-train; \textbf{Design~F} (exclude-label-defining tenders) recomputes
the moments after dropping defendant-co-appearance tenders to test
feature--target contamination; and \textbf{Design~G} (environment
holdout) holds out an item-group to test environment generalization.
Strict bid-timing validation is \textbf{blocked}: the within-tender
moments cannot be cleanly time-limited without re-deriving them on a
per-period bid panel.

Table~\ref{tab:bid_folds_submission} shows the fold composition. Random
folds (Design~A) spread the positives roughly evenly across five folds;
case-grouped folds (Design~E) put the positives in six very unequal
case folds, so a held-out case's positives are absent from the training
set. Table~\ref{tab:bid_loco_submission} reports per-case
leave-one-case-out AUCs, which range widely because most cases have very
few positives (two of six are too sparse to evaluate, with one positive
each).

\subsection{Leakage and Feature--Target Contamination}
\label{app:bid_leakage_submission}

Table~\ref{tab:bid_leakage_submission} mirrors the main-text leakage
audit. The award score is \emph{clean} (a CADE-label-independent rank on
\texttt{log1p(tenders\_count)}), and its strict-timing variant
($2009$--$2016 \to 2017$--$2019$ holdout) is also clean. The bid and
combined random forests under full-period features and random folds are
\textbf{diagnostic-only}: the random folds are optimistic because the
positives cluster by case, and the firm-mean moments include
\emph{label-defining tenders} (firms' co-appearances with named
defendants). Excluding those tenders (Design~F) moves the bid ROC from
$0.891$ to $0.874$, a $-0.017$ contamination effect --- minor, but
disclosed. Strict bid-timing is \textbf{blocked}, and the leave-one-case-out
design (E) is the case-holdout robustness check rather than an
operational claim.

\subsection{Performance and Calibration}
\label{app:bid_perf_submission}

Table~\ref{tab:bid_perf_submission} reports the four models across the
four designs. Reading down the bid-RF rows shows the optimism of random
folds: ROC $0.891$ (Design~A) $\to 0.810$ (Design~E, case-grouped)
$\to 0.874$ (Design~F, contamination-controlled). The combined model is
the upper bound (ROC $0.958$ pooled, $0.936$ case-grouped). The award
score is, by construction, invariant across Designs~A and~E (it is a
fixed rank, not a learned model). Figure~\ref{fig:bid_calibration_submission}
and Table~\ref{tab:bid_calib_submission} report the bid-RF calibration:
predicted probabilities track observed cobidder rates well in the top
decile ($0.108$ predicted vs.\ $0.103$ observed), with an overall Brier
score of $0.0095$.

\subsection{Complementarity Diagnostics}
\label{app:bid_complementarity_submission}

The award and bid scores are only moderately correlated
(Spearman $=0.42$ overall; $0.42$ under the case-grouped scoring), and
their top-$500$ rankings overlap by roughly $17\%$, so the two layers
order firms differently. Combining them therefore raises discrimination:
the incremental PR-AUC of adding the award signal to the bid forest is
$+0.151$ under pooled-random folds, which shrinks to $+0.064$ under
case-grouped folds (DeLong $p<0.001$ in both). The combined model is an
\emph{upper bound} on what full bid observability plus the cheap signal
can achieve, not an operational target.
Figure~\ref{fig:bid_scatter_submission} shows the two scores against
each other with cobidders highlighted.

\subsection{Limitations}
\label{app:bid_limitations_submission}

We close with the limitations that bound every claim in this appendix.
First, the bid-layer benchmark is a \textbf{full-observability
diagnostic}, not a first stage: the Imhof moments require all
within-tender bids to be observed and recovered, which is exactly the
information cost the award-layer screen avoids. Second, the headline
pooled-random numbers carry \textbf{random-CV optimism}: because the
cobidder positives cluster by CADE case, random folds let a case's
positives co-train, and the bid ROC falls from $0.891$ to $0.810$ once
cases are held out (Design~E). Third, the firm-mean moments include
\textbf{label-defining tenders}; recomputing them without those tenders
(Design~F) lowers the bid ROC by $0.017$, a minor but real
contamination. Fourth, \textbf{strict bid-timing validation is
infeasible}: the within-tender moments cannot be cleanly restricted to a
time window without re-deriving them on a per-period panel, so no
temporal-holdout bid claim is made. Fifth, the benchmark is
deliberately modest in construction --- it is ``Imhof--Wallimann moments
in a random forest,'' with no tuning and no standardization; it is not a
canonical or specially engineered model, and the comparison should be
read as one against a reasonable, transparent forensic screen.
Finally, the target is the $193$ always-loser cobidders, an
\textbf{adjudication-anchored exposure} measure, not membership in any
agreement; the false positives are unlabeled firms under a narrow target,
not ``bad firms,'' and nothing here constitutes proof of conduct.
