\subsection{Sequential Gatekeeping and the Cost-Recall Frontier}
\label{app:forensic_sequence_submission}

The sequential architecture in the main text separates two tasks that are
often conflated. The award-layer stage ranks firms using fields available
before any bid microdata are recovered; the bid-layer stage then
constructs richer forensic features only for the firms that survive the
first stage. This appendix states the sequence, measures the recovery
footprint each stage opens, and traces the cost-recall frontier that
results, so the empirical comparison reads as an evidence-allocation
design rather than a claim that thin records substitute for full bid
files. The output is a forensic-priority queue, not a liability finding.
Throughout, ``cost'' denotes a \emph{data-processing footprint} --- the
firms, tender-items, bid rows, and buyer-item-year cells that must be
opened to compute the bid-layer features --- and never a monetary agency
cost, which we do not observe. Discriminating performance is
adjudication-anchored: positives are the $\valCostNpos$ always-loser
cobidder firms that link to a CADE-adjudicated case, against the
complete-Imhof pool of $16{,}772$ firms. Throughout, the operating
points should be read as retrospective cost-footprint designs
conditional on the validated incumbent ranking, not as fully
prospective deployment tests.

\subsubsection{Candidate Pools and Ranking Rules}
\label{app:stage1_submission}

The evaluation pool (Pool~A) is the complete-Imhof firm set: $16{,}772$
firms, of which $\valCostNpos$ are adjudication-anchored positives; under
full observability it opens $116{,}112$ distinct tender-items and
$3{,}393{,}915$ within-tender bid rows. Three prediction scores are
defined over this pool: an \textbf{award score}, the zero-win-stratum
continuous participation score $s_i = \log(1+T_i)$, computable from
routine administrative fields with no bid values; a \textbf{bid score}, a
random forest on within-tender bid-distribution moments requiring the full
bid record of each opened tender-item; and a \textbf{combined score} on
the union of the two feature sets. We compare seven ranking rules
distinguished by which layer they consume and whether they triage before
opening bid microdata: award-only (cheap, no bid recovery; the main-text
screen); bid-only and joint full-observability (both require opening every
tender-item up front; the joint rule is an upper-bound comparison, not an
operational baseline); sequential award\,$\to$\,bid and award\,$\to$\,combined
(rank by award, keep the top-$K_1$ survivor pool, recover bid microdata
only inside it, then rerank survivors); FL14\,$\to$\,bid (a binary-cutoff
first-stage triage followed by bid reranking); and a random $K_1$-sized
survivor pool, the no-information floor. An opportunity-adjusted award
score cannot serve as a gate --- the exposure adjustment requires the
same tender-level coverage the bid layer recovers --- so it is reported
only as an in-sample diagnostic in the main text. For the case-holdout
exercise firms are grouped by the CADE case they link to, so held-out
positives share no case with the calibration set.

\subsubsection{Cost Denominators}
\label{app:joint_scoring_submission}

Bid-layer recovery is costlier at scale because within-tender moments
require the \emph{full} set of bids in each opened tender-item, not only
the focal firm's bids: opening a survivor firm means opening every
tender-item that firm participated in, in full. We therefore measure the
recovery footprint along several denominators rather than a single
headline number. Table~\ref{tab:G_denoms} defines them.

The firm count (D1) and the bid-row footprint (D4) diverge sharply, and
the divergence is the central caution of this appendix. Survivors are
high-participation firms: at the $K_1=2000$ operating point the survivor
pool is about $12\%$ of the firms ($2{,}000/16{,}772$, an $88\%$ firm
reduction), yet it opens about $67\%$ of the bid rows
($2{,}283{,}924/3{,}393{,}915$, only a $33\%$ bid-row reduction). The
firm-level reduction therefore overstates the saving; the tender-item and
bid-row denominators are the honest processing-footprint measures.

\subsubsection{Baselines, Timing, and Case-Holdout}
\label{app:baselines_submission}

The sequential rule sits well above a same-size random survivor floor
across the grid, confirming that it is the award ranking, not the mere act
of opening $K_1$ firms, that carries the signal. The award-ranked survivor
pool captures most of the $\valCostNpos$ positives \emph{before} any bid
rerank, so the cheap first stage alone already gathers the positives into
the survivor pool, and the bid rerank then reorders that pool into a
forensic-priority queue rather than recovering positives the award gate
missed. This is the precise sense in which the bid layer reorders rather
than discovers: the marginal contribution of the costly bid features is
ordering, an evidence-allocation gain, not a detection gain.

A timing-disciplined gate must rank on information available before the
investigation window. Re-scoring the award gate on a strict pre-period
(2009--2016) and evaluating on later positives, the award gate remains
informative under this discipline: its strict-timing discrimination is AUC
$0.684$ on the training-AL pool, against $0.761$ for the full-window score.
The drop is the honest cost of respecting the timing order. The sequential
rule cannot be subjected to the same test --- the within-tender moments
cannot be re-derived on a pre-period basis without re-pipelining the raw
LANCES bid records --- so we report it as infeasible rather than estimate
it on contaminated features.

Because adjudicated positives cluster in a handful of CADE cases, apparent
recall can be carried by a single large case: the largest case supplies
$32.0\%$ of the positives and $45.4\%$ of the true positives in the
top-$500$ queue. Grouping firms by case and holding out the largest case
lowers recall on the remaining smaller cases materially, with several
individual small cases near zero. Performance is therefore case-fragile and
generalization to out-of-sample cartels is weaker than the pooled figure
suggests. The estimated ranking is not a portable cartel score; the
transferable object is the decomposition framework. (The full random and
award-only baseline table and the case-holdout grid are in Online
Supplement~E.)

\subsubsection{Operating-Point Logic}
\label{app:operating_submission}

No single $(K_1,k)$ pair is preferred for every agency; the right point
depends on capacity and on whether the priority is recall or precision ---
the frontier is a menu, not a cutoff. At the $k=500$ queue depth the
frontier spans a capacity-constrained low end ($K_1=500$, smallest
footprint $1{,}356{,}346$ bid rows, recall $0.166$, precision $0.216$)
through $K_1=1{,}000$ (recall $0.190$, precision $0.248$) and $K_1=2{,}000$
(recall $0.178$, precision $0.232$) to $K_1=3{,}000$ (recall $0.177$,
precision $0.230$). Recovered positives are non-monotone in $K_1$:
$K_1=1{,}000$ beats $K_1=2{,}000$ ($124$ versus $116$ true positives), one
more sign that no $K_1$ is optimal. The $K_1=2{,}000$ point used in the
main text for the firm-versus-bid-row divergence is one operating point on
this frontier, not a universally preferred choice. The full
operating-points table with use-case labels, and the cost-per-true-positive
and false-positive frontier figures, are in Online Supplement~E.

\subsubsection{Algorithm}
\label{app:algorithm_submission}

The full gatekeeping procedure can be stated compactly.

\begin{quote}
\textbf{Algorithm E.1: Award-Layer Gatekeeping with Cost Measurement}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Define the firm universe from award records (participant
identity, winner identity, item code, negotiated price, procuring
unit, year, modality).
\item For each firm $i$, compute the win count $W_i$ and the
participation count $T_i$ over the calibration window; restrict to the
zero-win stratum $\{i : W_i = 0\}$.
\item Compute the award score $s_i = \log(1+T_i)$.
\item Over a grid of first-stage cuts $K_1$, form the survivor pool
$\mathcal{S}_{K_1}$ of the top-$K_1$ firms (or apply the
median-plus-$1.5\times$IQR threshold, or a capacity budget).
\item Recover bid microdata only for the tender-items touched by
$\mathcal{S}_{K_1}$, and record the cost denominators D1--D8 at that
$K_1$.
\item Compute the bid-layer forensic features inside $\mathcal{S}_{K_1}$.
\item Rerank survivors by the bid score alone or by the
award-plus-bid combined score, over a grid of queue depths $k$.
\item Deliver the top-$k$ queue from that ranking as a
forensic-priority list for investigative review.
\item Trace the cost-recall frontier over $(K_1,k)$ and select the
operating point that fits the agency's capacity and recall/precision
priority; treat the output as forensic priority, not as a liability
finding.
\end{enumerate}
\end{quote}

Joint scoring requires bid microdata for the full pool before triage;
sequential gatekeeping requires bid microdata only after the
award-layer survivor pool is formed. The size of the survivor pool, the
choice of threshold or top-$K_1$, the queue depth $k$, the bid-versus-
combined rerank, and the recalibration cadence are all institutional
choices that the algorithm leaves to the agency.

\subsubsection{Limitations}
\label{app:limitations_submission}

Cost is a processing footprint, not money --- we measure firms,
tender-items, bid rows, and cells opened, not monetary recovery cost,
legal-request volume, or LANCES export units. Firm reduction overstates
the saving (an $88\%$ firm reduction at $K_1=2000$ is only a $33\%$
bid-row reduction); analyst-hours are illustrative only; labels are
limited and false positives are unlabeled, so precision is a lower bound
on the queue's triage value; performance is case-fragile, with the largest
case supplying $32.0\%$ of positives and leave-largest-case-out recall
falling materially; the operating point is capacity-dependent with no
dominating choice; and the sequential timing test is blocked, so only the
award-only gate has a timing-disciplined evaluation.
