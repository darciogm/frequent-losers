\section{Sequential Gatekeeping and the Cost-Recall Frontier}
\label{app:forensic_sequence_submission}

The sequential architecture in the main text separates two tasks
that are often conflated. The award-layer stage ranks firms using
fields that are available before any bid microdata are recovered.
The bid-layer stage then constructs richer forensic features only
for the firms that survive the first stage. This appendix states
the sequence explicitly, measures the recovery footprint that each
stage opens, and traces the cost-recall frontier that results, so
that the empirical comparison is read as an evidence-allocation
design rather than as a claim that thin records substitute for full
bid files. The output of the procedure is a forensic-priority queue,
not a liability finding.

Throughout, ``cost'' denotes a \emph{data-processing footprint} ---
the firms, tender-items, bid rows, and buyer-item-year cells that
must be opened to compute the bid-layer features --- and never a
monetary agency cost, which we do not observe. The discriminating
performance reported here is adjudication-anchored: positives are
the $190$ firms in the candidate pool that link to a CADE-adjudicated
case, against the complete-Imhof pool of $16{,}772$ firms.

\subsection{Candidate Pools and Prediction Scores}
\label{app:stage1_submission}

The evaluation pool (Pool~A) is the complete-Imhof firm set:
$16{,}772$ firms, of which $190$ are adjudication-anchored positives.
Under full observability the same pool opens $116{,}112$ distinct
tender-items and $3{,}393{,}915$ within-tender bid rows.

Three prediction scores are defined over this pool.
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Award score.} Computable from routine administrative
fields --- participant identity, winner identity, item code,
negotiated price, procuring unit, year, modality. Within the
zero-win stratum it is the continuous participation score
$s_i = \log(1+T_i)$. No bid values enter.
\item \textbf{Bid score.} A random-forest score on within-tender
bid-distribution moments (dispersion, skewness, kurtosis, spread,
distance from the winning bid, and related statistics). These
features describe how bids are placed inside tenders and require the
full bid record of each opened tender-item.
\item \textbf{Combined score.} A random-forest score on the union of
award-layer and bid-layer features.
\end{itemize}
For the case-holdout exercise (\ref{app:gatekeeping_algorithm_submission}
below) firms are grouped by the CADE case they link to, so that
held-out positives share no case with the calibration set.

\subsection{Ranking Rules}
\label{app:stage2_submission}

We compare seven ranking rules, distinguished by which data layer
they consume and whether they triage before opening bid microdata.
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Award-only} (full-window participation score): cheap,
no bid recovery; the screen evaluated in the main text.
\item \textbf{Bid-only, full observability}: bid score over the
whole pool; requires opening every tender-item up front.
\item \textbf{Joint, full observability}: combined score over the
whole pool; the upper-bound comparison, not an operational baseline,
because it requires bid microdata for the full pool before any
triage.
\item \textbf{Sequential award\,$\to$\,bid}: rank by the award score,
keep the top-$K_1$ survivor pool, recover bid microdata only inside
that pool, then rerank survivors by the bid score.
\item \textbf{Sequential award\,$\to$\,combined}: identical first
stage; survivors reranked by the combined score.
\item \textbf{FL14\,$\to$\,bid}: a binary-cutoff first stage
(the deployable FL14 rule) followed by bid reranking.
\item \textbf{Random}: a $K_1$-sized random survivor pool, the
no-information floor (mean over draws with a $95\%$ band).
\end{itemize}
An opportunity-adjusted award score that conditions on each firm's
exposure is not deployable as a gate --- the exposure adjustment
requires the same tender-level coverage the bid layer recovers --- so
it is skipped here and reported only as an in-sample diagnostic in the
main text.

\subsection{Cost Denominators}
\label{app:joint_scoring_submission}

Bid-layer recovery is costlier at scale because within-tender
moments require the \emph{full} set of bids in each opened
tender-item, not only the focal firm's bids: opening a survivor
firm means opening every tender-item that firm participated in, in
full. We therefore measure the recovery footprint along several
denominators rather than a single headline number. Table~\ref{tab:G_denoms}
defines them.

The firm count (D1) and the bid-row footprint (D4) diverge sharply,
and the divergence is the central caution of this appendix. Survivors
are high-participation firms: at the $K_1=2000$ operating point the
survivor pool is about $12\%$ of the firms ($2{,}000/16{,}772$, an
$88\%$ firm reduction), yet it opens about $67\%$ of the bid rows
($2{,}283{,}924/3{,}393{,}915$, only a $33\%$ bid-row reduction). The
firm-level reduction therefore overstates the saving; the tender-item
and bid-row denominators are the honest processing-footprint measures.

\subsection{Random and Award-Only Baselines}
\label{app:baselines_submission}

The sequential rule is bracketed by two reference points. Below it,
a random survivor pool of the same size; above the first stage alone,
the award score with no bid recovery at all. The sequential rule sits
$3$--$12\times$ above the random floor across the grid (random recall
rises from $0.029$ at $K_1=500$ to $0.140$ at $K_1=3000$). Award-survivor
recall --- the share of the $190$ positives captured inside the
award-ranked survivor pool \emph{before} any bid rerank --- reaches
$0.779$ at $K_1=2000$ and $1.000$ at $K_1=3000$: the cheap first stage
alone already gathers the positives into the survivor pool. The bid
rerank then reorders that pool into a forensic-priority queue; it does
not recover positives the award gate missed. The full random and
award-only baseline table (all $K_1$, with $95\%$ bands) is in the
online supplement, \S S-G.

This is the precise sense in which the bid layer reorders rather than
discovers. Because the award gate already places the positives inside
the survivor pool, the marginal contribution of the costly bid features
is ordering, not capture --- an evidence-allocation gain, not a
detection gain.

\subsection{Timing-Disciplined Gatekeeping}
\label{app:timing_submission}

A deployable gate must rank on information available before the
investigation window. We therefore re-score the award gate on a strict
pre-period (2009--2016) and evaluate on later positives. The award gate
remains informative under this discipline: its strict-timing
discrimination is AUC $0.730$, against $0.938$ for the full-window
score that uses the entire panel. The drop is the honest cost of
respecting the timing order, and the strict-period figure is the one we
treat as deployable.

The sequential rule cannot be subjected to the same strict-timing test.
The bid-layer features were constructed over the full window, and the
within-tender moments cannot be re-derived on a pre-period basis
without re-pipelining the raw LANCES bid records. We therefore report
this exercise as \textbf{blocked} for the sequential rule rather than
estimate it on contaminated features: the strict-timing bid gate is
infeasible with the current pipeline
(\texttt{timing\_sequential\_blocked.csv}). The award-only strict-timing
frontier is the timing-disciplined result we stand behind.

\subsection{Operating-Point Choice}
\label{app:operating_submission}

No single $(K_1,k)$ pair is optimal for every agency; the right point
depends on capacity and on whether the priority is recall or precision.
At the $k=500$ queue depth the Pareto-efficient frontier spans a
capacity-constrained low end --- $K_1=500$, smallest footprint
($1{,}356{,}346$ bid rows), recall $0.342$, precision $0.130$ --- through
a balanced middle ($K_1=1000$: recall $0.437$, precision $0.166$) to a
high-recall end at $K_1=3000$ (recall $0.521$, precision $0.198$). The
$K_1=2000$ point used in the main text for the firm-versus-bid-row
divergence is one point on this frontier, not a universally preferred
choice. The full Pareto-efficient operating-points table, with use-case
labels, is in the online supplement, \S S-G.

Figures~\ref{fig:G_cost_per_tp} and \ref{fig:G_fp} render the trade-off
two further ways: the processing footprint spent per
adjudication-anchored true positive, and the false-positive frontier as
the queue deepens.

\subsection{Algorithm}
\label{app:algorithm_submission}

The full gatekeeping procedure can be stated compactly.

\begin{quote}
\textbf{Algorithm G.1: Award-Layer Gatekeeping with Cost Measurement}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Define the firm universe from award records (participant
identity, winner identity, item code, negotiated price, procuring
unit, year, modality).
\item For each firm $i$, compute the win count $W_i$ and the
participation count $T_i$ over the calibration window; restrict to the
zero-win stratum $\{i : W_i = 0\}$.
\item Compute the award score $s_i = \log(1+T_i)$.
\item Over a grid of first-stage cuts $K_1$, form the survivor pool
$\mathcal{S}_{K_1}$ of the top-$K_1$ firms (or apply the
median-plus-$1.5\times$IQR threshold, or a capacity budget).
\item Recover bid microdata only for the tender-items touched by
$\mathcal{S}_{K_1}$, and record the cost denominators D1--D8 at that
$K_1$.
\item Compute the bid-layer forensic features inside $\mathcal{S}_{K_1}$.
\item Rerank survivors by the bid score alone or by the
award-plus-bid combined score, over a grid of queue depths $k$.
\item Deliver the top-$k$ queue from that ranking as a
forensic-priority list for investigative review.
\item Trace the cost-recall frontier over $(K_1,k)$ and select the
operating point that fits the agency's capacity and recall/precision
priority; treat the output as forensic priority, not as a liability
finding.
\end{enumerate}
\end{quote}

Joint scoring requires bid microdata for the full pool before triage;
sequential gatekeeping requires bid microdata only after the
award-layer survivor pool is formed. The size of the survivor pool, the
choice of threshold or top-$K_1$, the queue depth $k$, the bid-versus-
combined rerank, and the recalibration cadence are all institutional
choices that the algorithm leaves to the agency.

\subsection{Limitations}
\label{app:limitations_submission}

Several limitations bound the claims of this appendix.
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Cost is a processing footprint, not money.} We measure
the firms, tender-items, bid rows, and cells that must be opened; we do
not observe monetary recovery cost, legal-request volume, or LANCES
export units (the last is an assumption, one tender-item per export).
\item \textbf{Firm reduction overstates the saving.} Survivors are
high-participation firms, so an $88\%$ firm reduction at $K_1=2000$ is
only a $33\%$ bid-row reduction. The tender-item and bid-row
denominators are the honest footprint.
\item \textbf{Analyst-hours are illustrative only.} The
$\alpha\cdot\text{tender-items}+\beta\cdot\text{bid-rows}/1000$ proxy is
a processing-burden illustration with no measured agency-time data
behind it.
\item \textbf{Labels are limited and false positives are unlabeled.}
Positives are the $190$ adjudication-anchored firms; firms outside the
queue and most firms inside it are unlabeled, so precision is a lower
bound on operational usefulness and an upper bound on confirmed error.
\item \textbf{Performance is case-fragile.} Recall leans on the largest
CADE case; leave-largest-case-out recall falls to about $0.34$ and some
small cases drop to near zero.
\item \textbf{The optimal $K_1$ is capacity-dependent.} No single
operating point dominates; the choice is institutional.
\item \textbf{The sequential timing test is blocked.} The bid features
cannot be re-derived on a strict pre-period without re-pipelining
LANCES, so only the award-only gate has a timing-disciplined evaluation.
\end{itemize}
