\section{Data Construction and Validation Labels}
\label{app:data_labels_submission}

This appendix records the construction choices behind the
award-layer screen and the adjudication-based labels. The aim is
to make the empirical object reproducible and to keep each
source in its proper evidentiary role. The screen is built from
procurement records. The validation labels come from CADE
adjudications and their BEC co-bidding environments. The design
therefore tests whether a cheap award-layer statistic ranks
firms near legally anchored cartel cases, while leaving legal
membership to the case record.

\subsection{Award-Record Universe}
\label{app:award_universe_submission}

The BEC procurement universe covers $\valYearStart$--$\valYearEnd$
and contains $\valSampleNfull$ tender-item records before the
final price-regression restriction. The main price sample contains
$\valSampleN$ observations. For the screening exercise, the unit
is the firm. A firm enters the outer screening universe if it
appears as a participant in at least one BEC tender during the
sample window. This gives $\valBECfirms$ participating firms.

\subsection{Screen Construction}
\label{app:screen_construction_submission}

For each firm $i$, we construct two award-layer variables:
\[
  T_i=\text{number of BEC tender-items in which firm } i
  \text{ participates},
\]
and
\[
  W_i=\text{number of BEC tender-items won by firm } i.
\]
The always-loser stratum is the set $\{i:W_i=0\}$ and contains
$\valAlwaysLosers$ firms. The continuous screening score is
$s_i=\log(1+T_i)$ within that stratum. The discrete frequent-loser
flag is
\[
  FL_i=\mathbf{1}\{W_i=0,\ T_i\geq\valThreshold\}.
\]
The threshold comes from the always-loser participation
distribution: median plus $1.5$ times the interquartile range,
with statistical cutoff $\valThresholdStat$ and integer rule
$T_i\geq\valThreshold$. This flags $\valFL$ firms, or
$\valFLrateAL$ of the always-loser stratum.

The threshold is not estimated from CADE labels. It is fixed from
the participation distribution alone. That matters because the
validation exercise then asks a genuine question: whether a
pre-specified award-layer rule concentrates firms later observed
near adjudicated cartel defendants.

\subsection{Matching CADE Defendants to BEC Firms}
\label{app:cade_matching_submission}

CADE adjudications identify firms by legal name and tax
identifier (CNPJ). The starting CADE portfolio contains $65$
firm-defendants across $12$ adjudicated procurement-cartel
cases linked to BEC subject matter. We match CADE defendants
to BEC participant records on the full $14$-digit CNPJ string,
zero-padded to preserve leading zeros that would otherwise be
truncated by numeric parsing. The match is implemented as exact
string equality on the firm-identifier field
(\emph{c\'odigofornecedor}) common to the CADE and BEC extracts;
no fuzzy or name-based matching is used, and no manual
overrides enter the direct-defendant set. Of the $65$
firm-defendants, $\valDirectCADE$ have at least one BEC
tender-item record during the sample window and define the
direct-defendant set used in validation. The remaining
defendants either operate outside BEC or do not appear under
matchable identifiers in the BEC participation extract; they are
omitted from the direct-defendant label.

Three terms recur below. A \emph{direct CADE defendant} is a firm
named in an adjudicated procurement-cartel case. A
\emph{BEC-active defendant} is a direct defendant with at least
one BEC tender-item record during the sample window; there are
$\valDirectCADE$ of these, corresponding to $48$ distinct
crossmatch CNPJ of which $\valFunnelDirectActiveFTM$ are
firm\_tender\_map-active. A \emph{defendant tender-item} is a BEC
tender-item in which at least one BEC-active defendant
participates; there are $\valFunnelDefItems$ such items. These
items locate the cartel-affected environments used to build
cobidder labels.

\subsection{Constructing Cobidder Labels}
\label{app:cade_labels_submission}

CADE adjudications provide the legal anchors. Direct defendants
are not the main target of the frequent-loser screen; they locate
cartel-affected bidding environments.

The validation target is the always-loser cobidder. A firm is an
\emph{always-loser cobidder} if it is a unique zero-win firm that
shares at least one tender-item with a direct CADE defendant.
Three construction choices fix the object. First, direct
defendants are themselves \emph{excluded} from the cobidder set:
the label measures loser-side exposure around anchors, not the
anchors. Second, always-loser status is \emph{global} over the
$\valYearStart$--$\valYearEnd$ window, not window-specific, so a
firm that ever wins is never a cobidder. Third, a firm appearing
across multiple cases or under duplicate CNPJ records is counted
once: all counts are unique firms. Under a narrow cartel-tender
restriction this definition yields the $\valCobidders$ main
target; under the broad shared-tender-item definition it yields
$\valFunnelALcobBroad$ always-loser cobidders
($\valFunnelFLcobBroad$ frequent losers) over the full portfolio.
The label is adjudication-anchored exposure, not legal
membership. It says the firm appears on the loser side of tenders
that include legally adjudicated cartel anchors. It does not say
the firm was a defendant, signed an agreement, or submitted a
cover bid in a particular tender.

\subsection{Conservative and Timing Benchmarks}
\label{app:conservative_timing_submission}

For the conservative validation benchmark, we restrict CADE cases
to adjudications with judgment date on or before
$\valConservativeCutoff$-12-31. This leaves $\valConservativeCases$
cases, $\valConservativeFD$ BEC-active direct defendants, and
$\valConservativeCobidders$ always-loser cobidders (under the
broad shared-tender-item definition). Among those cobidders,
$\valConservativeFL$ are frequent losers. This benchmark
sacrifices sample size to avoid using later case resolution to
define the validation target.

The conservative cut uses judgment dates because conduct-onset
dates are unavailable in the case record: only judgment dates are
observed, and for $9$ of the $12$ cases. The remaining $3$ cases
are undated and are placed last in any timing ordering.

The direct-defendant label is deliberately kept separate from the
cobidder label. The former is a legal membership anchor. The
latter is an exposure label tailored to the loser-side ranking.
That separation is central to the paper's interpretation: direct
defendants identify legally anchored environments, while
cobidders provide the validation target for a statistic designed
to find persistent losing participation.

\paragraph{Temporal-holdout timing.}
\label{app:timing_submission}
The temporal-holdout exercise computes participation scores from
$2009$--$2016$ and evaluates discrimination against labels
realized in $2017$--$2019$. This split follows the enforcement
logic rather than a purely statistical convention. At the moment
an agency uses the award layer, it does not know the completed
case file. The relevant question is whether earlier participation
helps rank firms whose cartel adjacency is later revealed through
adjudicated anchors.

The timing split is conservative in one respect and imperfect in
another. It is conservative because the score is computed before
the target period. It is imperfect because CADE adjudications
often occur after the conduct and can cover conduct spanning
multiple years. For that reason, the temporal design is an
out-of-time discrimination exercise, not causal prediction or a
field deployment. It asks whether the screen ranks
later-adjudicated cartel-adjacent losers before the later labels
are used to construct the score.

\subsection{Sample Restrictions and Denominator Changes}
\label{app:sample_restrictions_submission}

Several counts in the paper come from sample restrictions rather
than the label funnel, and it is worth keeping them distinct. The
price sample is the tender-item price-regression restriction of
the award universe, not a firm-label object. The bid-feature
common pool of $16{,}779$ firms and the gatekeeping pool of
$11{,}676$ firms are evaluation samples from the Imhof
horse-race common support (scripts 31/49) and the same-sample
gatekeeping exercise (scripts 63/64), respectively. They are not
label-funnel counts and must not be read as cobidder or
defendant tallies; the label-funnel objects are exactly the rows
of Table~\ref{tab:label_funnel_submission}.

\subsection{Reconciliation of Validation Samples}
\label{app:reconciliation_submission}

The validation benchmarks use two cobidder definitions, and the
counts only line up once the definition is fixed. The
$\valCobidders$ main target is the row count of a static
narrow-definition file (\texttt{cade\_fl\_cobidders.csv},
frequent-loser only, narrow cartel-tender restriction); its
original builder is archived and is not reproduced by the current
scripts, which is the one replication caveat we disclose here. The
$\valConservativeCobidders$ conservative count, and the
$210$/$108$ figures reported in earlier drafts, come instead from
the broad shared-tender-item definition applied to the
always-loser stratum over the early cases. The earlier
conservative defendant figure of $30$ was an over-count; the
reproducible value is $\valConservativeFD$, with
$\valConservativeCobidders$ always-loser and $\valConservativeFL$
frequent-loser cobidders.

The apparent paradox---that the conservative benchmark, drawn from
fewer cases, returns more cobidders than the $\valCobidders$ main
target---resolves on two axes. The main target counts the
narrower frequent-loser subset under the narrow cartel-tender
definition, while the conservative count uses the wider
always-loser stratum under the broad shared-tender-item
definition. Holding the definition fixed restores the expected
subset relations: $\valConservativeCobidders<\valFunnelALcobBroad$
always-loser cobidders and
$\valConservativeFL<\valFunnelFLcobBroad$ frequent-loser
cobidders. Table~\ref{tab:label_funnel_submission} reports all
benchmarks side by side.

\subsection{What Is Excluded From the Screen}
\label{app:excluded_screen_submission}

The award-layer screen does not use bid amounts, bid ranks,
within-tender dispersion, reference prices, LANCES timestamps,
CADE outcomes, or price-regression residuals. Those objects enter
later as validation, forensic benchmarks, or descriptive economic
corroboration. Keeping them outside the screen is essential. It
ensures that the first-stage statistic can be computed in
award-only environments and that the bid layer remains available
as an independent forensic stage.
