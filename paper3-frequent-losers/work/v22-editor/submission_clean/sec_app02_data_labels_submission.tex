\section{Data Construction and Validation Labels}
\label{app:data_labels_submission}

This appendix records the construction choices behind the
award-layer screen and the adjudication-based labels, and keeps
each source in its proper evidentiary role. The screen is built
from procurement records; the validation labels come from CADE
adjudications and their BEC co-bidding environments. The design
therefore tests whether a cheap award-layer statistic ranks firms
near legally anchored cartel cases, while leaving legal membership
to the case record.

\subsection{Award-Record Universe}
\label{app:award_universe_submission}

The BEC procurement universe covers $\valYearStart$--$\valYearEnd$
and contains $\valSampleNfull$ tender-item records before the
final price-regression restriction; the main price sample contains
$\valSampleN$ observations. For the screening exercise the unit is
the firm: a firm enters the outer screening universe if it appears
as a participant in at least one BEC tender during the sample
window, giving $\valBECfirms$ participating firms.

\subsection{Screen Construction}
\label{app:screen_construction_submission}

For each firm $i$ we construct two award-layer variables,
$T_i$ (number of BEC tender-items in which firm $i$ participates)
and $W_i$ (number of BEC tender-items won by firm $i$). The
always-loser stratum is the set $\{i:W_i=0\}$ and contains
$\valAlwaysLosers$ firms. The continuous screening score is
$s_i=\log(1+T_i)$ within that stratum, and the discrete
frequent-loser flag is
\[
  FL_i=\mathbf{1}\{W_i=0,\ T_i\geq\valThreshold\}.
\]
The threshold comes from the always-loser participation
distribution---median plus $1.5$ times the interquartile range,
with statistical cutoff $\valThresholdStat$ and integer rule
$T_i\geq\valThreshold$---and is fixed from that distribution alone,
not estimated from CADE labels. It flags $\valFL$ firms, or
$\valFLrateAL$ of the always-loser stratum. Because the threshold
is pre-specified, the validation exercise asks a genuine question:
whether an award-layer rule concentrates firms later observed near
adjudicated cartel defendants.

\subsection{Matching CADE Defendants and Constructing Cobidder Labels}
\label{app:cade_matching_submission}

The starting CADE portfolio contains $65$ firm-defendants across
$12$ adjudicated procurement-cartel cases linked to BEC subject
matter. We match CADE defendants to BEC participant records by
exact string equality on the full $14$-digit CNPJ
(\emph{c\'odigofornecedor}), zero-padded to preserve leading zeros;
no fuzzy, name-based, or manual matching enters the
direct-defendant set. Of the $65$ firm-defendants, $\valDirectCADE$
have at least one BEC tender-item record during the sample window
and define the direct-defendant set; the remainder operate outside
BEC or lack matchable identifiers.

% construction, and scope statement into one method+result paragraph.
Direct defendants are not the screen's target; they locate
cartel-affected bidding environments ($\valFunnelDefItems$
defendant tender-items, of $48$ distinct crossmatch CNPJ of which
$\valFunnelDirectActiveFTM$ are firm\_tender\_map-active). The
validation target is the \emph{always-loser cobidder}: a unique
zero-win firm that shares at least one tender-item with a direct
CADE defendant. Three choices fix the object. Direct defendants are
themselves excluded from the cobidder set, so the label measures
loser-side exposure around anchors rather than the anchors;
always-loser status is global over the
$\valYearStart$--$\valYearEnd$ window, so a firm that ever wins is
never a cobidder; and firms appearing across cases or under
duplicate CNPJ are counted once. The narrow cartel-tender
restriction yields the $\valCobidders$ main target; the broad
shared-tender-item definition yields $\valFunnelALcobBroad$
always-loser cobidders ($\valFunnelFLcobBroad$ frequent losers)
over the full portfolio. The label is adjudication-anchored
exposure, not legal membership: it says the firm appears on the
loser side of tenders that include adjudicated cartel anchors, not
that it was a defendant or submitted a cover bid in any particular
tender.

\subsection{Conservative and Timing Benchmarks}
\label{app:conservative_timing_submission}

The conservative validation benchmark restricts CADE cases to
adjudications with judgment date on or before
$\valConservativeCutoff$-12-31, sacrificing sample size to avoid
using later case resolution to define the target. This leaves
$\valConservativeCases$ cases, $\valConservativeFD$ BEC-active
direct defendants, and $\valConservativeCobidders$ always-loser
cobidders (broad definition), of which $\valConservativeFL$ are
frequent losers. The cut uses judgment dates because conduct-onset
dates are unavailable: only judgment dates are observed, for $9$ of
the $12$ cases, and the $3$ undated cases are placed last in any
timing ordering. Per-case timing and benchmark membership are
reported in the Online Supplement.

The temporal-holdout exercise computes participation scores from
$2009$--$2016$ and evaluates discrimination against labels realized
in $2017$--$2019$. The split follows the enforcement logic rather
than a statistical convention: at the moment an agency uses the
award layer it does not know the completed case file, and the
relevant question is whether earlier participation helps rank firms
whose cartel adjacency is later revealed through adjudicated
anchors. The design is conservative in computing the score before
the target period but imperfect because adjudications often
post-date conduct that can span several years; it is therefore an
out-of-time discrimination exercise, not causal prediction or a
field deployment.

The direct-defendant label is kept separate from the cobidder
label: the former is a legal membership anchor, the latter an
exposure label tailored to the loser-side ranking. The benchmarks
use two cobidder definitions, and the counts only line up once the
definition is fixed. The $\valCobidders$ main target is the row
count of a static narrow-definition file
(\texttt{cade\_fl\_cobidders.csv}, frequent-loser only, narrow
cartel-tender restriction); its original builder is archived and is
not reproduced by the current scripts, which is the one replication
caveat we disclose. The $\valConservativeCobidders$ conservative
count, and the $210$/$108$ figures in earlier drafts, come from the
broad shared-tender-item definition over the early cases; the
earlier conservative defendant figure of $30$ was an over-count,
and the reproducible value is $\valConservativeFD$. The apparent
paradox---that the conservative benchmark, drawn from fewer cases,
returns more cobidders than the main target---resolves because the
main target counts the narrower frequent-loser subset under the
narrow definition while the conservative count uses the wider
always-loser stratum under the broad definition. Holding the
definition fixed restores the subset relations
($\valConservativeCobidders<\valFunnelALcobBroad$ always-loser and
$\valConservativeFL<\valFunnelFLcobBroad$ frequent-loser
cobidders), and Table~\ref{tab:label_funnel_submission} reports all
benchmarks side by side.

Two further distinctions matter. The bid-feature common pool of
$16{,}779$ firms and the gatekeeping pool of $11{,}676$ firms are
evaluation samples from the Imhof horse-race common support and the
same-sample gatekeeping exercise; they are not label-funnel counts
and must not be read as cobidder or defendant tallies, whose exact
rows are those of Table~\ref{tab:label_funnel_submission}. Finally,
the award-layer screen itself uses no bid amounts, bid ranks,
within-tender dispersion, reference prices, LANCES timestamps, CADE
outcomes, or price-regression residuals; those enter later as
validation or forensic benchmarks, keeping the first-stage
statistic computable in award-only environments and the bid layer
available as an independent forensic stage.
