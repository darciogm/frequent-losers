\section{Data Construction and Validation Labels}
\label{app:data_labels_submission}

The screen and the labels it is validated against come from different
sources kept deliberately apart. The screen is built from procurement
award records alone; the validation labels come from CADE adjudications
and the BEC co-bidding environments around the adjudicated defendants.
The exercise that follows asks whether a cheap award-layer statistic
concentrates firms near those defendants, while leaving the question of
legal membership entirely to the case record.

\subsection{Award-Record Universe}
\label{app:award_universe_submission}

The BEC procurement universe covers $\valYearStart$--$\valYearEnd$
and contains $\valSampleNfull$ tender-item records before the
final price-regression restriction; the main price sample contains
$\valSampleN$ observations. For the screening exercise the unit is
the firm: a firm enters the outer screening universe if it appears
as a participant in at least one BEC tender during the sample
window, giving $\valBECfirms$ participating firms.

\subsection{Screen Construction}
\label{app:screen_construction_submission}

For each firm $i$ we construct two award-layer variables,
$T_i$ (number of BEC tender-items in which firm $i$ participates)
and $W_i$ (number of BEC tender-items won by firm $i$). The
always-loser stratum is the set $\{i:W_i=0\}$ and contains
$\valAlwaysLosers$ firms. The continuous screening score is
$s_i=\log(1+T_i)$ within that stratum, and the discrete
frequent-loser flag is
\[
  FL_i=\mathbf{1}\{W_i=0,\ T_i\geq\valThreshold\}.
\]
The threshold comes from the always-loser participation
distribution---median plus $1.5$ times the interquartile range,
with statistical cutoff $\valThresholdStat$ and integer rule
$T_i\geq\valThreshold$---and is fixed from that distribution alone,
not estimated from CADE labels. It flags $\valFL$ firms, or
$\valFLrateAL$ of the always-loser stratum. Because the threshold
is pre-specified, the validation exercise asks a genuine question:
whether an award-layer rule concentrates firms later observed near
adjudicated cartel defendants.

\subsection{Matching CADE Defendants and Constructing Cobidder Labels}
\label{app:cade_matching_submission}

CADE supplies $65$ firm-defendants across $12$ adjudicated
procurement-cartel cases with BEC subject matter. We match defendants
to BEC records by exact equality on the full $14$-digit CNPJ
(\emph{c\'odigofornecedor}), zero-padded to preserve leading zeros and
with no fuzzy, name-based, or manual step. Of the $65$, $\valDirectCADE$
appear in at least one BEC tender-item during the window and form the
direct-defendant set; the rest operate outside BEC or lack matchable
identifiers. Matching the defendants to tender-items also locates the
adjudication-anchored exposure set, and the $\valDirectCADE\to48\to
\valFunnelDirectActiveFTM$ reconciliation across the crossmatch and
participation tables is traced in Online Supplement
\ref{supp:data_detail}.

The validation target is not the defendant but the \emph{always-loser
cobidder}, fixed by one rule: a unique always-loser firm (win rate $=0$
over $\valYearStart$--$\valYearEnd$) that shares at least one BEC
tender-item with a BEC-active direct defendant. Three exclusions
sharpen it---defendants are dropped from the cobidder set, so the label
measures loser-side exposure \emph{around} the anchors; the unidentified
bidder code \texttt{-1} is dropped; and firms appearing across cases or
under duplicate CNPJ are counted once. The rule yields
$\valMainCobidders$ cobidders over the full $12$-case portfolio.

The frequent-loser flag plays no part in building this label---it is the
award-layer score evaluated \emph{against} it. Of the
$\valMainCobidders$ positives, $\valMainCobFL$ happen to be frequent
losers and $\valMainCobNonFL$ are not, a description rather than a
restriction. The label is adjudication-anchored exposure, not legal
membership: it records that a firm bid on the losing side of tenders
involving adjudicated defendants, not that it was itself a defendant or
filed a cover bid in any given tender.

\subsection{Conservative and Timing Benchmarks}
\label{app:conservative_timing_submission}

The conservative benchmark restricts CADE to adjudications judged on
or before $\valConservativeCutoff$-12-31, trading sample size for
freedom from using later case resolution to define the target. Under
the \emph{same} cobidder rule this leaves $\valConservativeCases$
cases, $\valConservativeFD$ crossmatch direct defendants, and
$\valConservativeCobidders$ cobidders, of which $\valConservativeFL$
are frequent losers. The cut uses judgment dates because conduct-onset
dates are unavailable (only $9$ of $12$ cases are dated; the $3$
undated cases are ordered last).

The temporal holdout scores participation from $2009$--$2016$ and
evaluates discrimination against labels realized in $2017$--$2019$.
These are \emph{retrospective adjudication anchors}: judgments
post-date the bidding and we claim no real-time legal knowledge at the
scoring date. The split follows enforcement logic, not statistical
convention---when an agency uses the award layer it does not yet hold
the completed case file. Among the $\valMainCobidders$ positives,
$\valStrictRankablePos$ are rankable incumbents with pre-$2017$ history
and $\valMainEntrantPositives$ are entrants with no history to rank
($\valEntrantPosShare$ of the target). The design is conservative in
scoring before the target period but imperfect because adjudications
often post-date multi-year conduct; it is a retrospective out-of-time
discrimination exercise, not causal prediction or field deployment.
Per-case judgment dates and benchmark membership appear in Online
Supplement \ref{supp:data_detail}.

The direct-defendant and cobidder labels stay separate---the first a
legal anchor, the second an exposure label tailored to the loser-side
ranking. Every benchmark in the paper uses the \emph{same} cobidder
rule; counts differ only because the candidate pool, case window, or
common-support restriction differs. The $\valConservativeCobidders$
conservative count is the same rule applied to the
$\valConservativeCases$ pre-$\valConservativeCutoff$ cases, hence a
strict subset of the $\valMainCobidders$ main target
($\valConservativeFL < \valMainCobFL$ frequent-loser cobidders): a
narrower case window can only remove cobidders, never add them.
Table~\ref{tab:positive_count_concordance} traces how the positive
count moves as the candidate pool changes while the definition is held
fixed---the $\valMainCobidders$ positives are stable, and the figures
that differ ($\valStrictRankablePos$ rankable incumbents,
$\valMainEntrantPositives$ entrants, $\valMainConsCobidders$
conservative) are common-support, timing, or case-window subsets, not
rival definitions.

One reading caution and one design point close the appendix. The
bid-feature common-support pool of $16{,}731$ firms is an evaluation
sample where the Imhof-style bid moments are complete; it retains all
$\valMainCobidders$ positives and is not a label tally, whose exact
rows are those of Table~\ref{tab:label_funnel_submission}. And the
award-layer screen uses no bid amounts, ranks, within-tender
dispersion, reference prices, LANCES timestamps, CADE outcomes, or
price residuals---these enter later only as validation or forensic
benchmarks, which keeps the first-stage statistic computable in
award-only environments and leaves the bid layer as an independent
forensic stage.
