\section{Data Construction and Validation Labels}
\label{app:data_labels_submission}

The screen and the labels it is validated against come from different
sources kept deliberately apart. The screen is built from procurement
award records alone; the validation labels come from CADE adjudications
and the BEC co-bidding environments around the adjudicated defendants.
The exercise that follows asks whether a cheap award-layer statistic
concentrates firms near those defendants, while leaving the question of
legal membership entirely to the case record.

\subsection{Award-Record Universe}
\label{app:award_universe_submission}

The BEC procurement universe covers $\valYearStart$--$\valYearEnd$
and contains $\valSampleNfull$ tender-item records before the
final price-regression restriction; the main price sample contains
$\valSampleN$ observations. For the screening exercise the unit is
the firm: a firm enters the outer screening universe if it appears
as a participant in at least one BEC tender during the sample
window, giving $\valBECfirms$ participating firms.

\subsection{Screen Construction}
\label{app:screen_construction_submission}

For each firm $i$ we construct two award-layer variables,
$T_i$ (number of BEC tender-items in which firm $i$ participates)
and $W_i$ (number of BEC tender-items won by firm $i$). The
always-loser stratum is the set $\{i:W_i=0\}$ and contains
$\valAlwaysLosers$ firms. The continuous screening score is
$s_i=\log(1+T_i)$ within that stratum, and the discrete
frequent-loser flag is
\[
  FL_i=\mathbf{1}\{W_i=0,\ T_i\geq\valThreshold\}.
\]
The threshold comes from the always-loser participation
distribution---median plus $1.5$ times the interquartile range,
with statistical cutoff $\valThresholdStat$ and integer rule
$T_i\geq\valThreshold$---and is fixed from that distribution alone,
not estimated from CADE labels. It flags $\valFL$ firms, or
$\valFLrateAL$ of the always-loser stratum. Because the threshold
is pre-specified, the validation exercise asks a genuine question:
whether an award-layer rule concentrates firms later observed near
adjudicated cartel defendants.

\subsection{Matching CADE Defendants and Constructing Cobidder Labels}
\label{app:cade_matching_submission}

The starting CADE portfolio contains $65$ firm-defendants across
$12$ adjudicated procurement-cartel cases linked to BEC subject
matter. We match CADE defendants to BEC participant records by
exact string equality on the full $14$-digit CNPJ
(\emph{c\'odigofornecedor}), zero-padded to preserve leading zeros;
no fuzzy, name-based, or manual matching enters the
direct-defendant set. Of the $65$ firm-defendants, $\valDirectCADE$
have at least one BEC tender-item record during the sample window
and define the direct-defendant set; the remainder operate outside
BEC or lack matchable identifiers.

Direct defendants are not the screen's target; matching them to
BEC tender-items locates the adjudication-anchored exposure set
($\valFunnelDefItems$ defendant tender-items, of $48$ distinct
crossmatch CNPJ of which $\valFunnelDirectActiveFTM$ are
firm\_tender\_map-active). The main validation target is the
\emph{always-loser cobidder}, constructed from the current scripts
by a single rule: a unique always-loser firm (win rate $=0$ over
$\valYearStart$--$\valYearEnd$) that shares at least one BEC
tender-item with at least one BEC-active direct CADE defendant.
Three exclusions fix the object. Direct defendants are themselves
excluded from the cobidder set, so the label measures loser-side
exposure around the anchors rather than the anchors; the sentinel
firm code \texttt{-1} (unidentified bidder) is excluded; and firms
appearing across cases or under duplicate CNPJ are counted once.
The rule yields $\valMainCobidders$ always-loser cobidders over the
full $12$-case portfolio.

\textit{The frequent-loser flag is not used to construct the
label.} It is the award-layer score that is evaluated
\emph{against} the label. Of the $\valMainCobidders$ positives,
$\valMainCobFL$ are frequent losers and $\valMainCobNonFL$ are not;
the composition is descriptive, not a label restriction. The label
is adjudication-anchored exposure, not legal membership: it says
the firm appears on the loser side of tenders that include
adjudicated defendants, not that it was a defendant or submitted a
cover bid in any particular tender.

\subsection{Conservative and Timing Benchmarks}
\label{app:conservative_timing_submission}

The conservative validation benchmark restricts CADE cases to
adjudications with judgment date on or before
$\valConservativeCutoff$-12-31, sacrificing sample size to avoid
using later case resolution to define the target. Under the
\emph{same} cobidder definition, this leaves $\valConservativeCases$
cases, $\valConservativeFD$ crossmatch direct defendants, and
$\valConservativeCobidders$ always-loser cobidders, of which
$\valConservativeFL$ are frequent losers. The cut uses judgment
dates because conduct-onset dates are unavailable: only judgment
dates are observed, for $9$ of the $12$ cases, and the $3$ undated
cases are placed last in any timing ordering. Per-case timing and
benchmark membership are reported in the Online Supplement.

The temporal-holdout exercise computes participation scores from
$2009$--$2016$ and evaluates discrimination against labels realized
in $2017$--$2019$. The timing-benchmark labels are
\emph{retrospective adjudication anchors}: the judgments post-date
the bidding, and we claim no real-time legal knowledge at the
scoring date. The split follows the enforcement logic rather than a
statistical convention: at the moment an agency uses the award
layer it does not know the completed case file, and the relevant
question is whether earlier participation helps rank firms whose
adjudication-anchored exposure is later revealed. Among the
$\valMainCobidders$ positives, $\valStrictRankablePos$ are rankable
incumbents (some pre-$2017$ participation history) and
$\valMainEntrantPositives$ are entrant positives with no
pre-window history to rank ($\valEntrantPosShare$ of the target).
The design is conservative in computing the score before the
target period but imperfect because adjudications often post-date
conduct that can span several years; it is therefore a
retrospective out-of-time discrimination exercise, not causal
prediction or a field deployment.

The direct-defendant label is kept separate from the cobidder
label: the former is a legal anchor, the latter an exposure label
tailored to the loser-side ranking. The main validation target and
every benchmark in this paper use the \emph{same} cobidder
definition; the counts differ only because the candidate pool, the
case window, or a common-support restriction differs. The
$\valMainCobidders$ main target applies the rule across the full
$12$-case portfolio. The $\valConservativeCobidders$ conservative
count applies the identical rule to the $\valConservativeCases$
cases judged on or before $\valConservativeCutoff$-12-31, so it is
a subset of the main target
($\valConservativeCobidders < \valMainCobidders$ always-loser
cobidders; $\valConservativeFL < \valMainCobFL$ frequent-loser
cobidders). No paradox arises: a stricter case window can only
remove cobidders, never add them.
Table~\ref{tab:label_funnel_submission} reports all benchmarks side
by side, and Table~\ref{tab:positive_count_concordance} traces how
the positive count moves across exercises as the candidate pool
changes while the definition is held fixed.

Two further distinctions matter. The bid-feature common-support
pool of $16{,}731$ firms is an evaluation sample on which the
Imhof-style bid moments are complete; all $\valMainCobidders$
positives are retained in it, so it is not a label-funnel count and
must not be read as a cobidder or defendant tally, whose exact rows
are those of Table~\ref{tab:label_funnel_submission}. Finally, the
award-layer screen itself uses no bid amounts, bid ranks,
within-tender dispersion, reference prices, LANCES timestamps, CADE
outcomes, or price-regression residuals; those enter later as
validation or forensic benchmarks, keeping the first-stage
statistic computable in award-only environments and the bid layer
available as an independent forensic stage.

The positive-count concordance in
Table~\ref{tab:positive_count_concordance} shows that the
$\valMainCobidders$ positives are stable across exercises under one
fixed definition; the counts that differ
($\valStrictRankablePos$ rankable incumbents,
$\valMainEntrantPositives$ entrant positives,
$\valMainConsCobidders$ conservative) are common-support, timing,
or case-window subsets, not alternative label definitions.
