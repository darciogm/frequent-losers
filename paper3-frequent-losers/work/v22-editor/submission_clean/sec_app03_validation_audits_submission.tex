\section{Validation Audits}
\label{app:validation_audits_submission}

The validation design has to address distinct mechanical
channels. First, frequent participants have more chances to
appear near any defendant, so raw co-bidding rates are not
enough. Second, firms may operate in the same procurement
environments as CADE anchors, giving them more opportunities to
meet those anchors even under ordinary competition. Third,
item-level labels can mechanically reuse the same participation
history that constructs the score. The main text reports the
headline results; this appendix records the audit logic and the
interpretation each audit licenses.

Throughout this appendix, ``CADE-defendant contact'' denotes an
\emph{adjudication-anchored exposure} label: a firm shares a
tender-item with a directly adjudicated CADE defendant. It does
not assert that the firm is a cartel member, party to any
agreement, or liable for anything. The audits validate forensic
priority, not membership, damages, or proof.

\subsection{Participation-Volume Placebo}
\label{app:permutation_submission}

The conservative CADE benchmark fixes the direct-defendant list
using cases adjudicated before the end of
$\valConservativeCutoff$. Within the always-loser stratum, we
then compare the observed frequent-loser share among cobidders
with a permutation distribution that preserves participation
volume. Firms are grouped by participation-volume quartile
defined within the always-loser stratum, cobidder status is
randomly permuted within those quartiles, and the frequent-loser
share among placebo cobidders is recomputed $\valPermPilot$
times. The number of cobidders per quartile is preserved across
permutations by construction; the within-quartile shuffle
reassigns the cobidder marker over the same set of firms,
leaving the per-quartile marginal unchanged. The $p$-value
reports the share of permutations whose placebo frequent-loser
share is at least as extreme as the observed share.

The observed share is $\valCADEenrich$, compared with a
permutation mean of $\valCADEbase$. The ratio is
$\valMultThreeTwo\!\times$, with $p<0.001$, and the conservative
ROC AUC is $\valAUCprePost$ (95\% CI $\valAUCprePostCI$,
computed via the DeLong method). The placebo answers the
participation-volume concern: the frequent-loser concentration
near CADE defendants is larger than activity volume alone
predicts. It does not by itself address opportunity-set
exposure, which is the purpose of the opportunity-cell audits
below.

\subsection{Opportunity-Cell Construction}
\label{app:opp_cells_submission}
\label{app:market_opportunity_submission}

A firm can appear near a CADE anchor simply because it operates
in procurement environments where defendants are active. To
discipline this opportunity-set exposure, we partition the
award layer into procurement-opportunity cells $g$ and benchmark
each firm against others facing the same cells. We use three
nested cell definitions of increasing granularity:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{COARSE} $=$ item group $\times$ year, where item
group is the two-digit prefix
($\texttt{SUBSTR}(\textnormal{c\'odigoitem},1,2)$) of the item
code.
\item \textbf{MEDIUM} $=$ item group $\times$ year $\times$
buyer/procuring-unit, where the buyer is the procuring-unit code
embedded in the OC key
($\texttt{SUBSTR}(\textnormal{numerodaoc},1,11)$). MEDIUM is the
reference definition used for the headline within-stratum
results.
\item \textbf{STRICT} $=$ item code $\times$ year $\times$
buyer/procuring-unit.
\end{itemize}

Procurement modality (Convite vs.\ Preg\~ao) is intentionally
\emph{dropped} from the cell keys. The firm--tender participation
layer used to build the cells carries item, year, and
procuring-unit identifiers but not modality; modality is
recoverable only through a join into a layer not present in the
award records used here. Because conditioning on modality would
further fragment already-sparse cells, modality is dropped from
the baseline cells and revisited as a sensitivity
(Appendix~\ref{app:opp_sensitivity_submission}). Cell granularity
trades bias against support: finer cells hold opportunity more
nearly fixed but leave most firms in singleton cells. The mean
cell defendant-contact rate $\bar p_g$ is around $0.017$--$0.019$
across the three definitions, and finer cells gain resolution at
the cost of support (under STRICT, $99.5\%$ of cells hold fewer
than five tender-items and $75.7\%$ are singletons). The full
cell-construction grid by granularity is in the online supplement,
\S S-D.

\subsection{Expected Defendant Contact and Excess}
\label{app:expected_contact_submission}

For firm $i$, let $n_{ig}$ be the number of tender-items the firm
holds in opportunity cell $g$ and let $p_g$ be the cell
defendant-contact rate (the share of tender-items in $g$ that
involve an adjudicated CADE defendant). The firm's
\emph{expected} defendant contact under ordinary cell-level
opportunity is
\[
E_i \;=\; \sum_g n_{ig}\, p_g ,
\]
and the \emph{observed} contact $O_i$ is the firm's actual count
of defendant-sharing tender-items. To prevent a firm from being
benchmarked against itself, the cell rate is computed
leave-one-out,
\[
p_{g,-i} \;=\; \frac{(\text{cell defendant-contacts}) - (\text{firm }i\text{'s contacts in }g)}{(\text{cell tender-items}) - n_{ig}},
\]
so the leave-one-out expectation is $E_{i}^{\text{loo}}=\sum_g
n_{ig}\,p_{g,-i}$ (a firm that is the entire cell receives zero
external opportunity). The firm \emph{excess} contact is
$X_i = O_i - E_i$, and its standardized form is
\[
Z_i \;=\; \frac{O_i - E_i}{\sqrt{\sum_g n_{ig}\, p_g\,(1-p_g)}},
\]
treating contacts within a cell as a sum of independent
opportunity-weighted Bernoulli draws.

Grouping always-losers by participation tier, the frequent-loser
cut and especially the very-high-participation tier ($T\ge 50$)
carry positive mean excess (mean $X_i=+0.068$ at $T\ge 50$), while
lower-participation tiers sit at or slightly below their
opportunity benchmark (mean $X_i$ around $-0.006$ to $-0.000$). The
pattern is that excess contact concentrates where the score is
high, but most of the level of $O_i$ is explained by $E_i$ ---
exposure does the heavy lifting, and excess is the residual the
score must still rank. The full observed-vs-expected-by-tier table
and the excess-by-score-decile figure are in the online supplement,
\S S-D.

\label{app:control_function_submission}

model. We estimate a ladder of logits and rank-tests

Table~\ref{tab:control_function_submission}:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{S0} --- raw baselines: continuous score, the FL14

\item \textbf{S1} --- flexible participation, $\textnormal{ns}(T_i,4)$.
\item \textbf{S2} --- score (or FL14) plus $\log(1+E)$, run at

\item \textbf{S3} --- score $+\,E\,+$ breadth controls.
\item \textbf{S4} --- score $+$ opportunity-decile and

\item \textbf{S5} --- score vs.\ FL14 ranking the binary excess

as the bulk of the predictive power.

\subsection{Opportunity-Preserving Permutation}
\label{app:opp_permutation_submission}

The permutation audit asks whether the raw score out-predicts a
null that already exploits opportunity exposure. Two
opportunity-preserving nulls are used, each with $B=2{,}000$ and
seed $20260603$:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Approach B (firm-exposure binomial simulation).}
For each firm we simulate contacts
$O_i^{\text{sim}}=\sum_g \textnormal{Binomial}(n_{ig},p_g)$,
preserving each firm's cell-level opportunity exactly and
re-deriving the precision/recall and PR-AUC of the simulated
contact pattern. This null embodies the hypothesis ``contact is
pure opportunity.''
\item \textbf{Approach C (matched-stratum label permutation).}
Cobidder labels are permuted within
exposure-decile $\times$ participation-bin strata, preserving the
joint exposure/volume distribution while breaking the
label--score link.
\end{itemize}
Approach A (within-cell relabeling) is feasible but is
statistically equivalent to Approach B and is substituted for it
to save computation.

\subsection{Matched and Stratified Validation}
\label{app:matched_opp_submission}

The matched audit holds opportunity fixed by coarsened exact
matching (CEM) on opportunity deciles and then compares
frequent-losers to non-frequent-losers within each stratum. The
FL-vs-non difference in cobidder rate ($\Delta P_{\text{cob}}$) is
positive in every exposure stratum and grows with exposure (from
$+0.033$ in the lowest stratum to $+0.190$ in the highest); the
excess gap ($\Delta X$) turns positive only in the
highest-exposure stratum. Within strata the score discriminates
cobidder contact at AUC $0.725$, and the CEM-opportunity-matched
AUC is $0.863$ on $6{,}039$ matched firms. The full per-stratum
table is in the online supplement, \S S-D.

\subsection{Common-Support and Sparsity Diagnostics}
\label{app:common_support_submission}

How much of the always-loser population can actually be benchmarked
depends on the cell definition and on how strictly we require
common support. We apply three rules --- minimal (a firm needs
any cell), moderate (cells must clear a sparsity floor), and
strict (cells must additionally contain a comparable non-firm
mass) --- and report retention honestly. The headline numbers:
under COARSE cells essentially all firms are retained ($\ge
97.9\%$); under MEDIUM cells the moderate and strict rules retain
only about $46\%$ of firms; under STRICT cells retention collapses
to about $9\%$. Sparsity is the binding constraint: under MEDIUM
cells $65.4\%$ of participation sits in cells with fewer than five
tender-items, and under STRICT $94.6\%$. We do not cherry-pick the
retained subset --- the within-stratum and matched results above
use MEDIUM cells, where roughly half the population survives. The
full retention grid by cell definition and rule is in the online
supplement, \S S-D.

\subsection{Sensitivity to Cell Definitions}
\label{app:opp_sensitivity_submission}

The result moves predictably across the COARSE/MEDIUM/STRICT
ladder. As cells get finer, the combined exposure-plus-score
logits rise mechanically (S2 score$+\log(1{+}E)$ AUC $0.958 \to
0.982 \to 0.988$ across COARSE/MEDIUM/STRICT), but this is the
inflation channel of Appendix~\ref{app:control_function_submission}:
finer cells let $E_i$ encode the label more tightly. The
opportunity-orthogonal quantities are far more stable. On the
strict-support subsample the \emph{raw} score (S6) still
discriminates cobidder contact at AUC $0.866$, and the
within-stratum / matched AUCs ($0.725$ within strata, $0.863$
CEM-matched) are computed at MEDIUM granularity. Dropping
modality from the cells (Appendix~\ref{app:opp_cells_submission})
is a coarsening in the same direction as moving from STRICT
toward COARSE: it widens cells and improves support at the cost
of within-cell homogeneity. The substantive conclusion --- a
small but significant opportunity-orthogonal increment, swamped
in level terms by exposure --- is invariant to where on the
granularity ladder we read it.

\subsection{Limitations}
\label{app:opp_limitations_submission}

Several limitations bound the interpretation of this appendix.
First, the opportunity cells are \emph{award-layer proxies}: they
are built from the item, year, and procuring-unit identifiers
present in the participation records, not from a full description
of each firm's true competitive opportunity set. Second,
procurement \emph{modality is dropped} from the cells because it
is not present in the award layer used here; the modality-augmented
construction is left as a sensitivity rather than the baseline.
Third, the result is exposure-concentrated: the largest single
CADE case contributes about $39\%$ of all observed
defendant-contacts, and the top two cases together about $66\%$,
so a handful of cases carry the level of contact. A formal
leave-one-case-out exercise (and the related observation that
roughly $47\%$ of true positives at $k=500$ trace to the largest
case) is the subject of a separate appendix and is not resolved
here. Fourth, and most importantly, the CADE-defendant labels are
\emph{adjudication-anchored exposure} labels, not membership
findings: a firm tagged here shared a tender-item with an
adjudicated defendant; it is not thereby a cartel member, a party
to any agreement, or liable for anything. The residual,
opportunity-orthogonal signal that survives these audits is
\emph{triage value} --- a basis for prioritizing scrutiny --- and
is \textbf{not legal proof, not identification of cartel members,
and not an estimate of damages or causal effect}.

\subsection{Direct-Defendant Scope Check}
\label{app:direct_scope_submission}

The direct-defendant result is a falsification of overreach. If
the statistic were a generic cartel-membership detector, it
should classify direct CADE defendants as well as cobidders. It
does not. The direct-defendant AUC is $\valAUCdirectStd$, close
to random classification. The item-level direct-defendant rows
in Table~\ref{tab:leakage_audit_submission} use different
samples and labels, but they are likewise near random. That
pattern is informative: the
statistic behaves like a loser-side ranking, while direct
defendants are legal anchors that often appear on the winner side
of the arrangement.

\subsection{Year-by-Year Holdout}
\label{app:year_holdout_submission}

The rolling-origin exercise trains the score on years preceding
each test year and evaluates cobidder discrimination in the test
year. The test-year AUC rises from $0.819$ (2014) to $0.922$
(2019), remaining separated from random classification in every
test year; the full year-by-year decomposition behind the
temporal-holdout figure in the main text is in the online
supplement, \S S-D.

The year-by-year pattern is not a claim that the environment
becomes progressively easier to police. The samples and
adjudication links change across years. The relevant point is
that the score remains separated from random classification in
each rolling-origin test year.

\subsection{Timing and Case-Composition Audits: Overview}
\label{app:timing_overview_submission}

The audits above hold \emph{opportunity} fixed. This block holds
\emph{time} and \emph{case composition} fixed. The question is
whether a regulator who froze the loser-side score \emph{before}
the evaluation window could have ranked the firms later
adjudicated as cobidders, without peeking at future
participation, future wins, a future-tuned threshold, or future
CADE adjudications --- and whether any surviving signal is
carried by a handful of cases rather than the construct. The
results are sobering and are reported as such. The honest summary
is that, on the full candidate universe, strict-timing
operational performance collapses to chance
(precision@$500=\valStrictFullPrec$); a within-pool, case-pooled
ranking signal survives but is thin and case-concentrated; and a
single CADE case carries most of the operational precision. We
state every window, candidate set, threshold, metric definition,
exclusion rule, and random seed, and we mark each quantity as
prospective or retrospective.

\subsection{Timing Information Sets}
\label{app:timing_sets_submission}

The CADE files carry only judgment dates

\texttt{DAY\_LEVEL\_TIMING\_UNAVAILABLE}. Ground truth is $193$
adjudicated cobidders (keyed on \texttt{c\'odigofornecedor}); the

from the cobidders after excluding one miscoded firm appearing in
both files. Table~\ref{tab:timing_sets_submission} enumerates the

\subsection{Rolling-Origin Validation}
\label{app:rolling_origin_submission}

The rolling-origin exercise expands the training window
year-by-year for $t=2014,\dots,2019$, freezes the score on all
years strictly before $t$, and evaluates cobidder discrimination
in year $t$ using \emph{training-window-only} scores. On the full
candidate universe, every origin year posts PR-AUC $\approx
0.004$--$0.006$ and precision@$500=$recall@$500=0$, with the worst
ROC at $0.497$ (2015), i.e.\ chance. The within-pool (training
always-loser) numbers improve monotonically across origins (ROC
$0.717$--$0.881$), but this is \emph{retrospective accumulation},
not learning: later origins train on more years and evaluate
against a label set that increasingly overlaps the construction
window, so rising AUC reflects a thicker training history, not a
screen that detects in real time. The full per-origin-year grid
(both universes) and the ROC-trajectory figure are in the online
supplement, \S S-D.

\subsection{Limitations of the Timing and Case-Composition Audits}
\label{app:timing_limitations_submission}

Five limitations bound this block. First, \textbf{all timing is
year-level} (\texttt{DAY\_LEVEL\_TIMING\_UNAVAILABLE}): the CADE
files carry only judgment dates (2015--2025), no conduct dates, so
we cannot align the screen to the calendar moment a cartel
operated, and the adjudication-observable design (set F) is
infeasible rather than estimated. Second, there is an
\textbf{entrant coverage hole}: $45/193$ ($23\%$) of positives are
firms with no training participation and carry score zero, a
structural blind spot the prospective screen cannot reach. Third,
the signal is \textbf{concentrated} --- by case (dropping the
largest case cuts PR-AUC by $71\%$; case-balanced precision is
$0.029$ vs.\ pooled $0.130$), by item group (dropping the largest
halves PR-AUC), and by year --- so the pooled operational number
overstates breadth. Fourth, \textbf{high ROC is a red herring}: at
sub-$1\%$ prevalence ROC saturates near $0.95$ while the
operational metrics (PR-AUC, precision@$k$) are low; the
operational metrics are the honest ones, and on the full
strict-timing universe they collapse to chance. Fifth, even the
strict-timing within-pool result is \textbf{triage-queue
validation, not proof}: it says a score an agency could have
computed in $2016$ ranks later-adjudicated cobidders above chance
within the always-loser pool --- it does not identify cartel
members, establish liability, or estimate damages. Throughout,
the CADE-defendant labels are \emph{adjudication-anchored exposure}
labels: a tagged firm shared a tender-item with an adjudicated
defendant; it is not thereby a member of, or party to, any
agreement.
