\section{Economic Profile and Robustness: Construction and Diagnostics}
\label{app:profile_submission}

The profiles characterize \emph{losing behavior} on the award layer and
feed a triage ranking; ``cobidder'' is an adjudication-anchored exposure
label throughout---a firm that co-bid with a firm named in a CADE
proceeding---and carries no inference of participation in any agreement.
All groups are drawn from the always-loser universe (firms with zero
recorded wins over the calibration window, $N=16{,}843$). The validation
target is the broad set of $\valMainCobidders$ always-loser cobidders, of
which $\valMainCobFL$ ($52.4\%$) satisfy the frequent-loser cut and
$\valMainCobNonFL$ ($47.6\%$) do not, so the label is not
frequent-loser-conditioned. The $\valDirectCADE$ direct CADE defendants
are \emph{winners} and are excluded from every cobidder group; the $6$
that appear in the always-loser universe are excluded from the profile
contrasts. The Section~5 profile contrast is run \emph{within} the
frequent-loser stratum---group D (FL non-cobidders, $N=2{,}394$) versus
group E (FL cobidders, $N=341$)---so the estimated gaps cannot be an
artifact of the cut. The full group register is in Online
Supplement~\ref{supp:profile_groups}.

Profile variables are computed from award records over the calibration
window in five panels: (A) participation and survival; (B) breadth and
concentration; (C) defendant-proximity / opportunity ($O_i$, expected
contacts $E_i^{\mathrm{MED}}$, excess $X_i^{\mathrm{MED}}$); (D)
modality-quorum proxies; and (E) timing. Three variable classes that
would sharpen interpretation are unobserved in the award layer: tender
value (price is recorded only for the \emph{winner}), distance-to-winner
(requires bid microdata), and fine geography / specialization. Because of
the latter two, the ordinary-loser alternatives in
Section~\ref{sec:cobidder_type} are \emph{narrowed but not eliminated}.

\subsection{Standardized Differences}
\label{app:profile_smd_submission}

Section~5 compares groups~D and~E using standardized mean differences
(Cohen's $d$) rather than $p$-values, which reject essentially any
difference at $N$ in the thousands. For a continuous variable with group
means $\bar{x}_E,\bar{x}_D$ and standard deviations $s_E,s_D$,
\[
d=\frac{\bar{x}_E-\bar{x}_D}{s_p},
\qquad
s_p=\sqrt{\tfrac{(n_E-1)s_E^2+(n_D-1)s_D^2}{n_E+n_D-2}} ,
\]
with the binary analogue obtained by substituting $\hat{p}(1-\hat{p})$
for the variances. A positive $d$ means cobidders (E) are higher.
Table~\ref{tab:profile_smd_submission} reports the top-ranked
differences; the full variable table is in Online
Supplement~\ref{supp:profile_smd_full}.

The defendant-proximity gaps in panel~C are \emph{partly mechanical}:
the cobidder label is defined by adjudication-anchored contact, so firms
with more contact are mechanically more likely to be labeled. Holding
exposure fixed---by regressing each profile variable on the cobidder
indicator with controls for $\log E_i^{\mathrm{MED}}$, a binned score
term, breadth, and active years, and by coarsened exact matching on
$E_i^{\mathrm{MED}}$ deciles---the item-group HHI gap falls from
$d\approx 0.13$ to $\approx 0$ and the breadth and persistence gaps
collapse toward zero, so they reflect procurement-environment
composition rather than intrinsic firm traits. Only the
defendant-contact variables survive, and that survival is partly
mechanical because the label is itself defined by defendant contact; it
should not be read as independent corroboration of conduct.

\subsection{Robustness and Limitations}
\label{app:profile_robustness_submission}
\label{app:profile_limits_submission}

Four robustness families corroborate the exposure-ranking reading; the
threshold sweep, the bunching histogram, the market-specific zero-win
variants, and the negative controls are in Online
Supplement~\ref{supp:profile_robustness}. In summary: cobidder
prevalence rises monotonically across participation bins (Spearman
$\rho=\valProfMonoPrevRho$) while mean excess contact does not
($\rho=\valProfMonoExcessRho$); the FL14 cut equals
$\mathrm{median}+1.5\times\mathrm{IQR}$ ($=13.5$), is administrative and
never published, and shows no discontinuity or strategic bunching (local
density ratio $\valProfBunchRatio$); the global zero-win definition is
not dominated by any home-market alternative (global ROC-AUC
$\valProfNegCtrlRealAUC$ vs.\ best alternative
$\valProfMarketZeroWinBest$); and against matched placebo anchors the
real-anchor ROC-AUC ($\valProfNegCtrlRealAUC$) is indistinguishable from
the placebo distribution (mean $\valProfNegCtrlPlaceboAUC$, $p=0.46$),
with non-CADE high-volume-winner anchors at least as discriminating
($p=0.91$). The negative controls corroborate the opportunity
decomposition rather than rescue the screen.

The defendant-contact variables carry the largest standardized gaps but
are partly a construction artifact, and the non-mechanical breadth and
persistence gaps largely collapse once opportunity is held fixed; the
score ranks firms by exposure to defendant-adjacent opportunities, not
by conduct intensity. Ordinary specialization and geographic-niche
alternatives are narrowed but not eliminated because distance-to-winner,
geography, and tender value are unobserved. Award-layer profiles
describe patterns of \emph{losing}; they are not proof of any agreement,
and ``cobidder'' is throughout an adjudication-anchored exposure label,
never an assertion of membership.
