\section{Economic Profile and Robustness: Construction and Diagnostics}
\label{app:profile_submission}

This appendix documents the construction and diagnostics behind the
Section~5 economic-profile and robustness exercises. The aim is
reproducibility: every comparison group, profile variable,
standardized-difference statistic, opportunity adjustment,
monotonicity bin, threshold grid, market-specific zero-win stratum,
and negative-control procedure used in the main text is defined here
exactly as implemented. Throughout, ``cobidder'' denotes an
\emph{adjudication-anchored exposure label} --- a firm that co-bid
with a firm named in a CADE proceeding --- and never a finding of
participation in any agreement. The profiles below describe
\emph{losing behavior} on the award layer. They are descriptive
inputs to a triage ranking; they are not proof of conduct.

%--------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Comparison Groups}
\label{app:profile_groups_submission}

recorded wins over the calibration window, $N=16{,}843$). The narrow

\subsection{Profile Variable Definitions}
\label{app:profile_vars_submission}

Profile variables are organized into five panels. All are computed
from award records over the calibration window.

\paragraph{Panel A --- Participation and survival.}
$T_i$ (\# tender-items the firm participated in);
$\log(1+T_i)$; $n\_years$ (\# distinct active years);
$items\_per\_active\_year$ ($T_i$ divided by active years).

\paragraph{Panel B --- Breadth and concentration.}
$n\_buyers$ (\# distinct procuring units);
$n\_items\_total$ (\# distinct tender-items);
$n\_item\_groups$ (\# distinct item groups);
$pregao\_share$ and $convite\_share$ (modality mix);
$hhi\_ig$ and $top\_ig\_share$ (item-group concentration, HHI and
top-group share); $hhi\_buyer$ and $top\_buyer\_share$ (buyer
concentration).

\paragraph{Panel C --- Defendant-proximity (opportunity layer).}
$O_i$ (observed defendant contacts);
$E_i^{\mathrm{MED}}$ (opportunity-expected contacts under the MEDIUM
cell definition); $X_i^{\mathrm{MED}}=O_i-E_i^{\mathrm{MED}}$ (excess
contacts); $n\_cases$ (\# CADE cases touched);
$n\_def\_firms$ (\# distinct defendants contacted);
$contact\_intensity=O_i/T_i$;
$share\_in\_def\_cells^{\mathrm{MED}}$ (share of participation in
defendant-bearing opportunity cells).

\paragraph{Panel D --- Modality-quorum proxies.}
$convite\_minq\_share$ (share of participation in Convite tenders at
or near minimum quorum); $mean\_n\_firms$ (mean number of competitors
faced).

\paragraph{Panel E --- Timing.}
$first\_year\_share$ (share of participation in the firm's first
active year); $late\_half\_share$ (share in the later half of the
firm's active span).

\paragraph{Variables \emph{not} observed (NOT\_OBSERVED).}
Three classes of variable that would sharpen the economic
interpretation are unavailable in the award records and are
\emph{not} used anywhere in Section~5:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Tender value / contract size.} Negotiated price is
recorded for the \emph{winner}, not for losing participants; a
loser's intended bid value is not in the award layer. We therefore
cannot scale exposure by tender value.
\item \textbf{Distance-to-winner.} The gap between a losing firm's
bid and the winning bid requires bid microdata, which the award
layer does not carry. Cover-bidding proximity claims that depend on
bid distance are therefore unidentified here.
\item \textbf{Geography / specialization detail.} Fine firm location
and product-specialization fields beyond item group are not reliably
populated. We cannot distinguish a geographically specialized loser
from a strategically passive one.
\end{enumerate}
% CO-AUTHOR EDIT: redirect dangling \ref to the main-text Section 5 (ordinary-loser alternatives live in 5.4)
Because of (2) and (3), the ordinary-loser alternatives discussed in
Section~\ref{sec:cobidder_type} are \emph{narrowed but not
eliminated}: specialization and geography cannot be fully ruled out.

\subsection{Standardized Differences}
\label{app:profile_smd_submission}

Section~5 compares groups~D and~E using standardized mean
differences (Cohen's $d$) rather than $p$-values. With $N$ in the
thousands, $p$-values reject essentially any difference and convey
no information about magnitude; the SMD is scale-free and
comparable across heterogeneous variables. For a continuous variable
with group means $\bar{x}_E,\bar{x}_D$ and group standard deviations
$s_E,s_D$,
\[
d=\frac{\bar{x}_E-\bar{x}_D}{s_p},
\qquad
s_p=\sqrt{\tfrac{(n_E-1)s_E^2+(n_D-1)s_D^2}{n_E+n_D-2}} .
\]
For a binary variable with group proportions $\hat{p}_E,\hat{p}_D$
the same formula applies with
$s_p=\sqrt{\tfrac{(n_E-1)\hat{p}_E(1-\hat{p}_E)+(n_D-1)\hat{p}_D(1-\hat{p}_D)}{n_E+n_D-2}}$.
A positive $d$ means the cobidder group~E is higher on the variable.
Table~\ref{tab:profile_smd_submission} reports the differences.

\subsection{Opportunity-Adjusted Profile Method}
\label{app:profile_adjust_submission}

The defendant-proximity gap in panel~C is \emph{partly mechanical}:
the cobidder label is defined by adjudication-anchored contact, so
firms with more contact are mechanically more likely to be labeled.
To separate any residual profile signal from this construction, we
re-estimate the D-vs-E contrasts holding exposure fixed by two
methods.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Regression / strata adjustment.} Each profile variable
is regressed on the cobidder indicator with controls for
$\log E_i^{\mathrm{MED}}$, a binned score $s_i/T_i$ term, breadth
($n\_buyers$, $n\_item\_groups$), and $n\_years$. The adjusted
contrast is the cobidder coefficient. Under this adjustment the
item-group HHI gap falls from $d\approx 0.45$ to $\approx 0.02$.
\item \textbf{Coarsened exact matching (CEM).} D firms are matched
to E firms on $E_i^{\mathrm{MED}}$ deciles (exact within decile);
the within-stratum mean difference is then averaged with
stratum weights.
\end{enumerate}
We state explicitly that proximity-variable survival under these
adjustments is \emph{partly mechanical} and should not be read as
independent corroboration of conduct.

\subsection{Monotonicity Bins}
\label{app:profile_monotonic_submission}

Section~5 reports two monotonicity patterns over participation bins.
Firms are sorted by $T_i$ and partitioned into bins (deciles of
$T_i$ within the always-loser universe; the top bins are sparse and
merged so that each bin carries a stable positive count). For each
bin we compute (i) cobidder \emph{prevalence} (share of firms in the
bin carrying the adjudication-anchored label) and (ii) mean
\emph{excess} contact $X_i^{\mathrm{MED}}$. Across bins, prevalence
rises monotonically (Spearman $\rho=+0.92$) while excess falls
(Spearman $\rho=-0.93$). The reading we adopt --- and the one
imposed by the discipline in this paper --- is that the score ranks
firms by \emph{exposure}, not by collusion intensity: more
participation buys more opportunities to be adjacent to a named
firm, and excess contact does not concentrate where prevalence is
highest.

\subsection{Binary-vs-Continuous, Threshold Sensitivity, and Bunching}
\label{app:profile_threshold_submission}

\paragraph{Threshold administrative, not behavioral.}
The $\mathrm{FL14}$ cut is $T_i\geq 14$, which equals
$\mathrm{median}+1.5\times\mathrm{IQR}$ of the always-loser
participation distribution ($=13.5$, rounded up). It is an
\emph{administrative} construction, computed ex post and
\emph{never published} to firms.

\paragraph{Threshold sensitivity.}
Table~\ref{tab:profile_placebo_submission} reports a grid of placebo
thresholds (decile cuts, fixed $T\geq k$, IQR multiples, top-share
rules). Discrimination changes smoothly across the grid with no
discontinuity at $14$; recall is $1.0$ for all cuts up to $T\geq 14$
and degrades gracefully thereafter, exactly as a continuous-score
reading predicts.

\paragraph{Bunching at $T=14$.}
A behavioral threshold would induce strategic bunching just below or
at the cut. Figure~\ref{fig:profile_bunching_submission} plots the

\subsection{Negative Controls}
\label{app:profile_negcontrol_submission}

To test whether the global screen's discrimination could arise by
chance, we compare the real adjudication-anchored target against two
matched null targets, each generated $B=500$ times with seed
\texttt{20260603}. Pseudo-positives in each draw are matched to the
real positives on $T_i\times$ exposure decile.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Placebo CADE anchors.} The CADE anchor firms are
replaced by random firms drawn from the same $T_i\times$ exposure
strata; the cobidder label is recomputed against these placebo
anchors.
\item \textbf{Non-CADE high-volume winners.} Anchors are replaced by
high-volume \emph{winning} firms never named by CADE, again matched
on $T_i\times$ exposure decile.
\end{enumerate}
Table~\ref{tab:profile_negcontrol_submission} reports the results
and Figure~\ref{fig:profile_negcontrol_submission} the null ROC-AUC
distribution. The real ROC-AUC ($0.939$) lies far above both null
distributions (means $0.755$ and $0.780$; empirical $p<0.001$ in
both designs).

\paragraph{Honest PR-AUC caveat.}
The placebo design does \emph{not} separate on PR-AUC (empirical
$p=0.70$). The reason is base-rate inflation: random
$T_i$-matched anchors touch on the order of $830$ firms per draw
(and the non-CADE-winner anchors touch $\sim\!2{,}100$), versus the
$191$ real positives, so the pseudo-positive base rate is several
times higher and PR-AUC --- which is base-rate sensitive --- is
mechanically inflated under the null. ROC-AUC, which is invariant to
the positive base rate, is the decisive ranking metric here, and on
ROC-AUC the real target separates cleanly from both nulls.

\subsection{Limitations}
\label{app:profile_limits_submission}

We close with the boundaries that govern how the Section~5 profiles
may be read.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Proximity is partly mechanical.} The defendant-contact
variables ($n\_cases$, $n\_def\_firms$, $O_i$) carry the largest
standardized gaps, but the cobidder label is defined by
adjudication-anchored contact, so these gaps are partly a
construction artifact rather than independent evidence
(\S\ref{app:profile_adjust_submission}).
\item \textbf{Exposure ranking, not collusion intensity.} The
monotonicity bins show prevalence rising with participation while
excess contact falls
(\S\ref{app:profile_monotonic_submission}). The score ranks firms by
how much they are \emph{exposed} to defendant-adjacent
opportunities, not by any intensity of conduct.
\item \textbf{Ordinary alternatives narrowed, not eliminated.}
Relative to ordinary losers, cobidders are wider and longer-lived
(not low-capacity), carry a lower Convite/quorum share (not
quorum-padding), and show lower first-year concentration (not
explore-then-exit). But distance-to-winner, geography, and tender
value are NOT\_OBSERVED (\S\ref{app:profile_vars_submission}), so
specialization and geographic-niche explanations cannot be fully
ruled out.
\item \textbf{Description, not proof.} Award-layer profiles describe
patterns of \emph{losing}. They are not proof of any agreement.
\item \textbf{Labels are exposure, not membership.} Throughout,
``cobidder'' is an \emph{adjudication-anchored exposure label} --- a
firm adjacent to a CADE-named firm in the bidding record --- and
never an assertion that the firm participated in any agreement.
\end{enumerate}
