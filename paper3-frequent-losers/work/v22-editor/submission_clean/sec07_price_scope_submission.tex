\section{Scope, Limits, and Price Corroboration}
\label{sec:price_scope_conclusion}

Section~\ref{sec:screening_forensics} carries the paper's central
enforcement-design result. Price evidence enters here in a narrower,
corroborative role only. It is scope evidence: it speaks to whether the
award-layer screen points to economically non-neutral environments. It is
\emph{not} a damages estimate, an overcharge, a cartel markup, a causal
price effect of frequent-loser presence, or proof of a cover-bidding
mechanism. Overcharge measurement has its own evidentiary tradition
\citep{bryant1991price,connor2007price}, distinct from screening
\citep{marshall2012economics,harrington2008detecting}. We state this
boundary once and hold to it throughout. The full price regressions are
reported in Appendix~\ref{app:scope_adaptation_submission}.

\paragraph{The broad price imprint.}
In the broad item-level regressions, frequent-loser presence is positively
associated with log negotiated unit price: $\valTabPricesColOne$ with item
and year fixed effects and $\valTabPricesColTwo$ after adding procuring-unit
fixed effects, with an implied range of $\valHeadlineRange$. The association
is larger in pregão ($\valTabPricesColThree$) than in convite
($\valTabPricesColFour$). If frequent losers were only mechanical quorum
fillers under the three-bidder rule, the imprint should be stronger in
convite; the larger association in the electronic-auction environment weighs
against a pure procedural-artifact reading. These are descriptive
associations used to assess economic non-neutrality, not causal effects.

\paragraph{The sign reversal, decomposed.}
The positive broad-sample coefficient ($+\valBetaBroad$, $p=\valBetaBroadP$)
reverses under overlap-cell ATT weighting to $\valScopeOverlapCoef$
($p\valScopeOverlapPlt$); under propensity-score trimming it becomes
$\valScopePSCoef$. Only $\valDroppedItemsShare$ of treated items lack a
within-cell counterfactual, so the reversal is a weighting result, not a
dropped-observation artifact. The two coefficients measure different objects.
The broad sample mixes a positive \emph{selection} component---cartels
concentrate in cells with structurally higher underlying prices: among
non-treated items, mean log price rises from $\valSelTestQOne$ (Q1) to
$\valSelTestQFive$ (Q5) across cell FL-share quintiles, a
$\Delta=\valSelTestDelta$ log-point gap, with a partialled-out coefficient of
$\valSelTestCoefFullFE$ (SE $\valSelTestSEFullFE$, $N=\valSelTestN$)---with a
negative \emph{within-cell} component, the overlap-cell ATT, which isolates
the latter. The progression is monotone: broad-sample $+\valBetaBroad$;
overlap-cell unweighted $+\valBetaUnwOverlap$ ($p=\valBetaUnwOverlapP$);
overlap-cell ATT $\valScopeOverlapCoef$ ($p<10^{-9}$). The negative ATT
reading survives both modalities (Convite $\valSignBetaConvATT$, Pregão
$\valSignBetaPregATT$), every PBU-size quartile, and tender-value quartiles
Q1--Q3; the only systematic positive cell is the high-value tail
($\valBetaQfour$, $p=\valBetaQfourP$), where coordination costs are spread
over larger contracts. The direct-CADE overlap estimate is null
($\valBetaCADEDirect$, $p=\valBetaCADEDirectP$): price regressions do not
recover the legal object of the case and supply no damages base. Full
regressions and the bidder-count boundary appear in
Appendix~\ref{app:scope_adaptation_submission}.

\paragraph{An honest limit on the mechanism.}
The within-cell price compression loads on the count of genuine bidders, not
on the frequent-loser count: adding $\log(\text{n\_firms})$ drives the
within-cell coefficient from $\valMechWinnerVsRef$ to
$\valMechWinnerVsRefControlled$ (not significant). The within-cell price
movement therefore operates through the bidder-count channel, and we do not
claim to have identified a cover-bidding mechanism from the price record; the
mechanism is \emph{not} identified by these data. The decomposition
establishes only that the broad positive imprint is selection-into-high-price-cells
rather than a treatment effect, and that price evidence stops aligning with
the loser-side scope outside the high-value tail. The institutional
implication stands: the award-layer signal is a trigger for richer evidence
collection, not a stand-alone measure of price injury. Price evidence may be
consistent with economic relevance, but it does not transform an award-layer
statistic into proof of agreement or price injury. Award records rank where
costly bid-level and documentary evidence should be assembled; bid records,
case documents, and legal process determine whether the conduct supports an
antitrust case. The architecture is more portable than the estimates: another
agency should validate the ranking against its own enforcement anchors and
observability constraints rather than importing the BEC cutoff or performance
metrics mechanically.

\subsection{Adaptive Deployment}
\label{sec:adaptive_deployment_submission}

The screen should be deployed as a moving queue rather than a static legal
trigger. Any administrative rule can generate strategic response once firms
learn which observable patterns attract attention; firms could rotate loser
roles, engineer occasional wins, fragment identities, or avoid a known
participation threshold. That possibility does not undermine the sequencing
architecture, but it rules out naive use of the binary cutoff as a permanent
enforcement boundary---a standard concern for cartel enforcement and
screening \citep{green1984noncooperative,haltiwanger1991the,
harrington2008detecting,sanchezgraells2019screening}. Agencies should use
continuous ranks or capacity-constrained top-$k$ queues, refresh thresholds,
monitor bunching below operational cutoffs, and combine award-layer screening
with bid-layer forensics whenever bid files can be recovered. A firm that
moves to avoid one observable pattern may create another: discontinuous
participation, suspicious identity changes, or shifts in bid-level behavior.
This is also the right legal posture. The screen is not a rule of liability
and should not be used as one. It is a way to order scarce investigative
attention when proof is costly and the first observable record is legally
thin---a disciplined place to begin without pretending that the beginning is
the end of the evidentiary process.
