\section{Federal Audit Battery (ComprasNet)}
\label{app:federal_audit_submission}

The federal ComprasNet panel ($2013$--$2019$) is a \emph{stress test} of
how far the audit ports across platforms, re-running the BEC validation
battery of \ref{app:validation_audits_submission} stage for stage under the
same definitions; it supplies the federal column of
Table~\ref{tab:comparative_audit} in Section~\ref{sec:comparative}. As on
BEC, ``CADE-defendant contact'' is an adjudication-anchored \emph{exposure}
label---a firm shares a tender-item with a directly adjudicated CADE
defendant---and what the battery measures is how far forensic priority
\emph{ports}, not membership or liability. The legal anchors are the same
numbered CADE cases used on BEC, \emph{partially overlapping} with the BEC
anchors by construction, so this is not an independent universe. The
federal panel has no sealed-bid modality, so no modality stratification is
reported. The full construction---label funnel
(mirroring Table~\ref{tab:label_funnel_submission}), opportunity-cell
construction, and the complete control-function
(mirroring Table~\ref{tab:control_function_submission}), timing
(mirroring Table~\ref{tab:timing_case_holdout}), leave-one-case-out,
negative-control, and armor batteries---is in Online Supplement~\ref{supp:federal}.

\subsection{What the Battery Returns}
\label{app:federal_summary}

The federal reading reproduces the BEC pattern on both points of the
opportunity-inflation curve. \emph{First}, the raw award-layer
concentration looks informative (ROC-AUC $\valFedAudRawAUC$), but once
opportunity exposure is held fixed it is opportunity arithmetic: the
label-blind opportunity ranking retains $\valFedAudLabelBlindAUC$ on its
own, the combined exposure-plus-score logit is mechanically inflated
because $E_i$ is built from the same defendant-contact rates that define
the label, and the score's isolated contribution---the nested DeLong
increment over the exposure-only model, $\valFedAudNestedIncrement$
($p=\valFedAudNestedP$), and the within-opportunity-stratum AUC
$\valFedAudWithinAUC$---is small. \emph{Second}, the within-stratum null is
power-bounded, not merely underpowered: the within-stratum positive control
reaches $\valFedAudOiControlMedium$ (so the design recovers a residual when
one exists), and the matched-strata permutation rejects with probability
$\valFedAudPowerFive$ once the true within-stratum AUC reaches $0.55$
(probability $\valFedAudPowerTen$ at $0.60$), yet the observed PR-AUC
\valFedAudPermVerdict\ ($p=\valFedAudPermCp$). The prospective ranking
collapses as it does on BEC, and against placebo and high-volume-winner
anchors the surviving ordering is \valFedAudNegControlVerdict.

\paragraph{The structural boundary holds on both platforms.}
The one positive cross-platform statement the graduation supports is a
\emph{negative} about the screen's reach, and it is on-thesis. On the
federal panel the firm-level AUC of the loser-side score against
\emph{direct} CADE defendants is $\valFedAudBoundaryAUC$ on the full
candidate universe and $\valFedAudBoundaryAUCALpool$ within the
always-loser pool (point estimates; no CI band was produced for the
federal scope check), consistent with the BEC boundary result of
$\valAUCdirectCADE$. The reason is the same on both platforms: direct
defendants in these adjudications are predominantly winners
($\valFedAudDirectsWinshare$ median win-share across the
$\valFedAudDirectsN$ federal direct-defendant firms), and a loser-side
construct by design does not rank winners. The score concentrates
loser-side exposure and declines to rank the very firms a naive reader
might expect it to flag; that boundary is a feature of the construct, and
it ports.

\paragraph{Reading the graduation.}
The federal stress test does not return a degraded-but-positive deployable
ranking, and the appendix does not claim one. What ports is the
\emph{audit}: the raw award-layer concentration looks informative until
opportunity exposure is held fixed, after which the within-stratum residual
is read directly off Table~\ref{tab:federal_graduation}, and the
prospective ranking behaves as it does on BEC. The deliverable an agency
carries across platforms is therefore the decomposition that tells it how
far a cheap award-layer statistic can be trusted before costly bid-layer
work begins---and on the federal system that lesson binds harder, because
the bid-layer step cannot be constructed from public data at all
(Section~\ref{sec:comparative_observability}).
