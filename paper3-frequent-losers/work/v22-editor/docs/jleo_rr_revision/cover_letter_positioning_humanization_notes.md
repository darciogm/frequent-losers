## Objective

contribution rather than a defensive "we are not claiming a cartel detector"
posture, while keeping the disciplined, non-overclaiming language intact.

## Opening contribution statement (as written)

> Instead of proposing a standalone algorithmic cartel screen, the paper
> contributes an organizational framework for auditing the reach, boundaries,
> and apparent performance of cheap administrative screens within public
> procurement platforms—before agencies commit to opening costly bid-level
> records.

(Lightly adapted from the brief for flow: added the em-dash clause "before
agencies commit to opening costly bid-level records" rather than a hanging
prepositional phrase. Substance preserved.)

## Structural changes

1. **Lead with the positive contribution** — the framework statement now opens
   the body (was previously framed first as "the contribution is not a claim
   that an award-layer screen identifies cartel members").
2. **Reframed the problem as enforcement design** — proof-producing effort
   allocated under costly observability; suspicion → investigation → proof as
   stages to be sequenced.
3. **Screen described as deliberately minimal** — persistent zero-win
   participation / frequent-loser flag; explicitly "not a legal category."
4. **Validation label foregrounded as reproducible + non-circular** — 651
   always-loser cobidders; direct defendants excluded; flag never used to build
   the label.
5. **Audit separates raw concentration from opportunity / timing / case
   concentration** — kept the deflationary verdict ("marginal at best and not
   robust across designs") as the institutional finding.
6. **Cost-recall frontier = organizational choice** — explicitly "an operating
   point ... not an optimum we recommend."
7. **Liability stays in the richer evidentiary record** — preserved.
8. **ComprasNet as stress test** — phrased as "stress-test the same audit ...
   on a second procurement system"; portability of the protocol, not the score.
   Did NOT use "demonstrated across two platforms."
