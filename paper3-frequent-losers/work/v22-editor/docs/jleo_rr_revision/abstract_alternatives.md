## Version A — USE-IN-MANUSCRIPT default (131 words)

*Macros expanded to literal values for readability. Requires revalidation of
83% / 131 / 193 after the cost frontier.*

Cartel enforcement must allocate costly proof-producing effort before legal proof
exists. Contract-award records are routine and cheap; the bid-level evidence
needed to prove coordination is costly to recover, clean, and inspect. Using São
Paulo's BEC procurement platform (2009–2019) and CADE adjudications, we build a
*frequent-loser* score that ranks priority among zero-win firms from award
records alone. Its target is adjudication-anchored exposure among always-loser
cobidders, not cartel membership. We validate the ranking with opportunity-exposure
discipline and timing audits, and show it adds non-redundant information to
bid-distribution forensics on the same target. Used as a gatekeeper, it
concentrates the bid-microdata pool the forensic stage must open (by 83%,
recovering 131 of 193 adjudicated cobidders). Cheap administrative records can
discipline where proof-producing investigation should begin while liability
remains in the richer evidentiary record.

## Version C — organization-heavy / governance-under-constraints (138 words)

*No specific counts; leads on the agency's internal sequencing of evidence
production. ≤150 words, no invented numbers.*

An enforcement agency producing evidence against cartels faces a sequencing
problem under incomplete observability: it must decide where to spend scarce,
costly forensic capacity before the evidence that would prove coordination has
been recovered. We study this allocation problem as a question of administrative
triage. Contract-award records are routine and cheap; bid-level evidence is
costly to recover, clean, and inspect. Using São Paulo's BEC procurement platform
(2009–2019) and CADE adjudications, we build a *frequent-loser* score that orders
priority among zero-win firms from award records alone, targeting
adjudication-anchored exposure among always-loser cobidders rather than cartel
membership. Disciplined for opportunity exposure and score timing, the ranking
adds non-redundant information to bid-distribution forensics and, used as an
internal gate, concentrates the costly evidentiary stage. Cheap administrative
records can govern where proof-producing investigation should begin while
liability remains in the richer evidentiary record.

## Note on selection

The manuscript uses **Version A** unless a build-consistency reason forces
Version B. A was chosen because it stays faithful to the current verified macros
(`\valYearStart`/`\valYearEnd`, `\valGateFootprintPct`, `\valGateSeqKTwoKOneTPIn`,
`\valCobidders`), expands cleanly to literal values, and already carries the
gatekeeping numbers inline — so B adds nothing A lacks. Version C is held in
reserve as a more JLEO/organization-theory-forward framing for a reviewer who
wants the governance/observability angle led harder; it drops specific counts and
would only be swapped in on an editor's explicit framing request.
