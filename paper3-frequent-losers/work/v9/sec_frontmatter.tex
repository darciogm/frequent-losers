\title{Frequent Losers as Cover Bidders in Public Procurement\texorpdfstring{\footnote{This version: March 2026.}}{}}

\author[insper]{Darcio Genicolo-Martins\corref{cor1}}

\begin{abstract}
We propose \emph{frequent losers}---firms that never win a single
public tender yet participate abnormally often---as a screening marker
for bid-rigging cartels. Using 4.5 million tender-items and 40 million
bids from Brazilian public procurement (2009--2019), we identify 2,735
frequent-loser (FL) firms and show that FL presence is associated with
3.6--6.4\% higher prices across five independent estimation approaches
(OLS, temporal cross-fitting, coarsened exact matching, inverse
probability weighting, and structural $n$-conditional); a
measurement-error-corrected IV provides an upper bound of 21.4\%.
Validated against competition-authority convictions, the FL screen
achieves an AUC of 0.94---outperforming bid-level screens that require
granular price data (AUC~$= 0.79$). We develop a structural model of
cover-bidder deployment that predicts the optimal number, bid
distribution, and market selection of cover bidders. The structural
estimates confirm coordinated cover bidding (cover-bid dispersion
28\% below genuine-bid dispersion), with the price effect concentrated
in competitive markets where manufactured competition is most
profitable. Policy counterfactuals show that removing the minimum-bidder
requirement would save R\$74M (OLS) to R\$211M (IV upper bound), and the welfare-maximizing
screening threshold nearly coincides with the statistical rule used for
FL classification.
\end{abstract}
