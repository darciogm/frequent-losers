\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

The structural model serves three functions: it generates the testable
predictions mapped in Section~\ref{sec:predictions}, it identifies the
cover-bid mechanism (Regime~2 vs.\ Regime~1), and it enables the policy
counterfactuals in Section~\ref{sec:counterfactual}. The markup itself
is identified by the convergence of six reduced-form approaches. We
present the reduced-form evidence first (H1), then the mechanism and
structural evidence (H2).

% ── 7.1 Reduced-Form Evidence (H1 core) ─────────────────────────

\subsection{OLS and Matching Results}
\label{sec:results_ols}

Table~\ref{tab:prices} reports the baseline OLS estimates of
Equation~\eqref{eq:ols_main} for log negotiated prices
(Prediction~\ref{pred:price_v6}). Across all four
specifications, FL presence is associated with significantly higher
prices: 6.8\% in the general specification (column~1), 6.4\% with PBU
fixed effects (column~2), 9.3\% for preg\~ao (column~3), and 3.8\% for
convite (column~4). Adding PBU fixed effects barely changes the
coefficient, indicating that FL presence does not proxy for high-cost
purchasing units.

\input{./output/tables/tab_prices}

FL-present tenders have 0.19 log points more non-FL firms
(Prediction~\ref{pred:selection_v6}; PBU FE specification),
contradicting crowding-out and consistent with \emph{strategic
selection}: cartels deploy cover bidders in tenders that already attract
genuine competition
(Table~\ref{tab:nfirms_excl} in Appendix~\ref{app:competition}).

\paragraph{Cross-fit estimate.}
FL status is defined using the same 2009--2019 data on which the price
regression is estimated, potentially creating a mechanical link between
classification and outcomes. To address this, we define FL using only
odd years and estimate on even years, and vice versa. The cross-fit
average is $\hat{\beta}_{\text{CF}} = 0.036$ (3.6\%), attenuated by
44\% relative to full-sample OLS. Three factors drive attenuation:
(i)~fewer FL firms per subsample (1,885--2,153 vs.\ 2,735);
(ii)~classification noise near the threshold; and (iii)~the temporal
split breaks within-period mechanical correlation. We treat the
cross-fit average as the \emph{conservative preferred estimate}.

\paragraph{Matching estimators.}
Coarsened exact matching (CEM: 0.077, $N = 969{,}751$) and inverse
probability weighting (IPW: 0.055, $N = 830{,}194$) bracket the OLS
and cross-fit estimates, confirming that the FL--price association
survives non-parametric reweighting on observables.

\paragraph{Instrumental variable (supplementary).}
The IV price estimate (0.194, $p = 0.01$, $F = 396$) exceeds OLS
by a factor of three, consistent with measurement-error attenuation
in the binary FL indicator. Balance tests reveal systematic but
economically negligible imbalance ($< 0.03\sigma$;
Table~\ref{tab:iv_balance} in Appendix~\ref{app:robustness}). We
treat the IV as an upper bound, not as the primary identification
strategy (Table~\ref{tab:iv_main} in Appendix~\ref{app:robustness}).

\paragraph{Summary.}
Six independent approaches yield markup estimates in a narrow range:
cross-fit (3.6\%), IPW (5.7\%), structural $n$-conditional (6.4\%),
OLS (6.6\%), CEM (8.0\%), and IV upper bound (21.4\%). The five
non-IV estimates span 3.6--8.0\%, a factor of 2.2. This convergence
across distinct identification strategies---each with different
assumptions and potential biases---indicates a genuine FL--price
association rather than an artifact of any single method.

% ── 7.2 Detection Performance (H1 validation) ───────────────────

\subsection{Detection Performance}
\label{sec:results_detection}

Figure~\ref{fig:roc} (Appendix~\ref{app:robustness}) presents the
FL ROC curve, evaluated against CADE cartel co-participation as ground
truth. The FL screen achieves an AUC of 0.94
(Table~\ref{tab:roc_detection} in Appendix~\ref{app:robustness}).
The data-driven optimal threshold (Youden's $J = 0.84$) corresponds
to 1.45$\times$IQR---nearly identical to the 1.5$\times$ baseline
rule---achieving TPR~$= 1.00$ with FPR~$= 0.16$.

\paragraph{Comparison with Imhof-style screens.}
The FL screen (AUC~$= 0.94$) substantially outperforms
\citet{imhof2019detecting}-style bid-level screens on the same
ground truth (Imhof composite AUC~$= 0.79$; DeLong $p < 0.001$).
The FL screen requires only participation and outcome data, making
it feasible where bid-level records are unavailable
(Table~\ref{tab:imhof_comparison}, Figure~\ref{fig:roc_comparison}
in Appendix~\ref{app:robustness}). Precision is low (3.6\%) given
the 0.58\% base rate; the FL screen is a high-recall triage tool.

\subsection{Network-Split Results}
\label{sec:results_network}

Table~\ref{tab:network_split} splits FL firms into
concentrated-market and competitive-market subgroups based on
co-bidding network metrics (Section~\ref{sec:empirical_network}),
directly testing Proposition~\ref{prop:market_selection}.\footnote{Markets
above the winner-HHI sample median are classified as concentrated;
firms sharing at least two tenders are classified as repeat
co-participants.}
Competitive-market FL firms---operating in markets with diversified
winner patterns (low HHI)---drive the entire price effect: 0.126
($p < 0.01$, PBU FE). Concentrated-market FL firms show no significant
effect ($-0.018$, $p > 0.3$). This directly confirms the model's
prediction: cover bidding is most profitable where genuine competition
is highest.

\subsection{Bajari--Ye Tests}
\label{sec:results_bajari}

We apply the \citet{bajari2003deciding} framework to test
Prediction~\ref{pred:exchange_v6}
(Table~\ref{tab:bajari_ye_corrected} in Appendix~\ref{app:robustness}).

\paragraph{Exchangeability.}
The KS test rejects the null that FL and non-FL bid residuals share
the same distribution ($D = 0.15$, $p < 0.001$): FL bids are drawn
from a distinct process, as the structural model predicts.

\paragraph{Conditional independence.}
The mean pairwise product of FL residuals is 4.28 ($t = 81.0$,
$p < 0.001$),\footnote{The enriched first stage includes firm age
and CNAE sector dummies alongside firm size, absorbing additional
cost-shifter variation.} with the bootstrap FL--non-FL difference
excluding zero, indicating systematically different within-tender
correlation consistent with coordinated bidding.

\paragraph{Tender FE.}
Under tender FE (Table~\ref{tab:bajari_ye_tender_fe} in
Appendix~\ref{app:robustness}), both products drop substantially
(FL: $5.16 \to 0.38$; non-FL: $2.21 \to 0.86$), with the FL product
falling below non-FL---a reversal predicted by Regime~2. Cover bids
cluster near $b^* + \epsilon$ with low variance; removing the tender
mean eliminates the shared focal-point component. The tender-FE
specification absorbs the within-tender price level that cover bidding
inflates, making it mechanically conservative.

\subsection{Structural Estimation Results}
\label{sec:results_structural}

Table~\ref{tab:structural_params} reports the structural parameter
estimates (Section~\ref{sec:structural_estimation}).

\paragraph{Regime selection.}
BIC selects Regime~2 (coordinated cover bidding) over Regime~1
($\Delta$BIC~$= -91{,}473$). Under Regime~2, FL bids center at
$\hat{\epsilon} = 0.83$ log points above the winning bid with
$\hat{\sigma}_c = 1.19$ (SE~$= 0.012$). The KS distance is 0.24
(Regime~2) versus 0.64 (Regime~1). The ratio
$\hat{\sigma}_c / \hat{\sigma}_g = 1.19/1.64 = 0.72$ confirms that
cover bids are \emph{less} dispersed than genuine bids---the signature
of coordinated calibration near a focal point.

\paragraph{Structural markup.}
Controlling for genuine bidder count $n$ (saturated dummies), item,
year, and PBU FE, the $n$-conditional markup is 6.4\%
(SE~$= 0.019$)---virtually identical to baseline OLS. The structural
parameters characterize the cover-bid \emph{mechanism}; the markup is
identified by the reduced-form comparison, disciplined by the model's
predictions.
