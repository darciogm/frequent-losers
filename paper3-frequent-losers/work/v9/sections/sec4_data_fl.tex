\section{Data and Frequent Losers Definition}
\label{sec:data}

\subsection{BEC Platform}
\label{sec:bec}

Our data come from the Bolsa Eletr\^onica de Compras (BEC), S\~ao
Paulo's centralized electronic procurement platform serving 1,308
purchasing units (PBUs). BEC handles two modalities:
\emph{convite} (sealed-bid, minimum three bidders, Lei~8.666/93) and
\emph{preg\~ao} (electronic reverse auction, no minimum-bidder rule).
The convite minimum-bidder rule creates a direct incentive for cover
bidding; under preg\~ao, cover bidders inflate apparent competition.
Our sample spans 2009--2019 and contains 4.5 million tender-items,
40 million bids, 41,000 firms, and 1,308 PBUs
(Table~\ref{tab:modality_year}, Appendix~\ref{app:competition}).

\subsection{Frequent Losers Definition}
\label{sec:fl_definition}

We define frequent losers (FL) in two steps:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Always-losers}: firms with a zero win rate across all
    2009--2019 tenders. 16,843 firms ($\sim$41\% of all
    BEC participants) never win a single tender.
  \item \textbf{IQR threshold}: among always-losers, we identify those
    with abnormally high participation counts using a median + 1.5
    $\times$ IQR threshold on the distribution of tender participations.%
    \footnote{We use median + 1.5 $\times$ IQR rather than the standard
    Tukey rule (Q3 + 1.5 $\times$ IQR) because the participation
    distribution is highly right-skewed. The median-based threshold
    yields a cutoff of approximately 14 participations and identifies
    2,735 FL firms. Economically, this cutoff identifies firms with
    participation frequency so extreme as to be difficult to rationalize
    as competitive optimization: at this threshold, the marginal cost
    of bid preparation would exceed any plausible expected return
    from participation under standard assumptions about win
    probabilities. The ROC analysis in Section~\ref{sec:detection_validation}
    confirms that the data-driven optimal threshold (Youden's $J$
    criterion, $J = 0.84$) corresponds to a multiplier of
    1.45$\times$IQR---nearly identical to our 1.5$\times$ rule,
    with an AUC of 0.94 against CADE ground truth.
    Robustness to the multiplier choice is reported in
    Section~\ref{sec:robustness}.}
    This yields approximately 2,735 FL firms.
\end{enumerate}

The treatment variable $\text{losers}_{igt}$ equals one if tender-item
$i$ in item group $g$ at time $t$ has at least one FL firm among its
participants, and zero otherwise.

The participation distribution is heavily right-skewed (Figures~\ref{fig:losses}--\ref{fig:iqr}, Appendix~\ref{app:robustness}).

\subsection{Structural Estimation Sample}
\label{sec:structural_sample}

The structural model requires bid-level data: 40 million bids from 41,000 firms, with bid amount, winning price, firm identity, and FL classification. FL losing bids exhibit a mean log-spread of 0.83 (SD = 1.19) above the winning price, versus a genuine-bid SD of 1.64. The ratio $\hat{\sigma}_c / \hat{\sigma}_g = 0.72$ is consistent with Regime~2 (coordinated cover bidding).

\subsection{CADE Validation Sample}
\label{sec:cade_sample}

We validate the FL screen using CADE cartel conviction records with a temporal split: FL firms are classified on 2009--2014 data, then evaluated against 2015--2019 enforcement outcomes, preventing information leakage. The CADE data comprise 65 cartel cases, of which 47 convicted firms match BEC identifiers. Among 2,735 FL firms, 193 (7.1\%) co-participate with CADE-convicted cartelists---3.5 times the expected rate ($p < 0.001$; Section~\ref{sec:cade}).
