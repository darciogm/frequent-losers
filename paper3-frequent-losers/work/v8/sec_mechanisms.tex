\subsection{M1: Strategic Selection vs.\ Competitive Displacement}

Under competitive displacement, FL firms would crowd out genuine
competitors, reducing effective competition. Under cover bidding, they
are added on top. We regress the number of genuine (non-FL) competitors
on FL presence: the OLS coefficient is $+0.143$ ($p < 0.01$), and the
IV estimate is $+0.404$ ($p < 0.01$). FL-present tenders have
\emph{more} genuine competitors, not fewer. Cartels appear to select
into already-competitive tenders and add cover bidders on top---ruling
out crowding-out and consistent with cover-bidding theory.

\subsection{M2: Reference Price Calibration}

BEC reference prices are set administratively---typically as the median % V7-T3: institutional detail + bad-control connection
of three supplier quotations or the average of recent comparable tender
prices---and are publicly observable, making them natural coordination
anchors. Designated winners can bid just below $r$ while cover bidders
bid above but within a plausible range. We regress
$\log(p_{\text{negot}} / p_{\text{ref}})$ on FL presence and find a
coefficient of $-0.041$ ($p < 0.01$): winning bids in FL-present
tenders are 4\% closer to the reference price, consistent with
calibrated cover bidding \citep{marshall2012economics}. This finding
also informs the bad-control concern in Section~\ref{sec:robustness}:
conditioning on $\log r$ may remove part of the treatment effect and
induce collider bias if prior cartel bids have already inflated $r$.

\subsection{M3: Reverse Causality}

Could FL firms select into high-priced tenders rather than causing
higher prices? We regress FL entry on lagged log prices within market
(item $\times$ PBU) using 427,474 market-year observations. The
coefficient is $0.0021$ (SE~$=0.0008$, $p = 0.012$)---statistically
detectable but economically negligible. The implied elasticity is
$\approx 0.004$: a 10\% price increase raises the FL entry probability
by 0.02 percentage points against a baseline of 4.8\%. This is two
orders of magnitude smaller than the 6--9\% main estimate, ruling out
price-chasing selection as a driver of results.

\subsection{Alternative Explanations: Learning and Auction Format}

FL firms are approximately one year younger than non-FL firms at first % IJIO-R1: minor-3 — learning caveat
BEC participation. This is consistent with recently formed shell firms,
but could also reflect incomplete market-specific learning by younger
entrants. We note that disentangling these channels would require
firm-level data on market entry sequencing, which is outside the scope
of this paper; both interpretations are consistent with the main
findings.

The FL coefficient is larger for preg\~ao (9.3\%) than for convite
(3.8\%). Preg\~ao's real-time format allows cover bidders to observe
competing bids and ensure they remain above the designated winner's
offer---directly facilitating Regime~1 coordination. Convite's
sealed-bid format makes calibration harder while also reducing the
marginal need for cover bids when three genuine bidders already satisfy
the minimum-bidder rule. The differential thus reflects both reduced
scope for cover bidding under sealed-bid format and a compositional
effect of the minimum-bidder threshold.
