\subsection{Additional Identification Evidence} % V8: addresses Concern 1
\label{sec:additional_id}

We present three additional tests that address the concern that FL
presence may be endogenous to market characteristics rather than
causing price inflation.

\paragraph{1. FL entry event study.}
For each PBU, we identify the first year in which any FL firm
participates and estimate a within-PBU event study with PBU and year
fixed effects (window: $t \in [-4, +4]$ relative to first FL entry).
Figure~\ref{fig:fl_entry_es} shows the results. Pre-event coefficients
at $t = -2$ and $t = -3$ are positive and marginally significant,
indicating that PBUs receiving FL entry \emph{already had higher
prices} before FL firms appeared. This pattern is consistent with
strategic selection (Prediction~\ref{pred:selection_v6}): cartels
deploy cover bidders into PBUs where high prices are sustainable,
rather than FL entry \emph{causing} the price increase.

This pre-trend pattern does not invalidate the FL--price association
but it reframes its interpretation: FL presence is a \emph{marker} of
markets where cover bidding is profitable (H1), not necessarily the
proximate \emph{cause} of higher prices (H2). The FL screen remains
valuable for detection precisely because it identifies these markets.

\caption{Event study: log prices around first FL entry in PBU. PBU and

\paragraph{2. Minimum-bidder constraint variation.}
Under convite, the minimum-bidder rule ($n + m \geq 3$) creates
exogenous variation in the \emph{necessity} of cover bidding: when
$n_{\text{genuine}} < 3$, the cartel must deploy cover bidders to
satisfy the rule (corner solution in
Proposition~\ref{prop:optimal_m}); when $n_{\text{genuine}} \geq 3$,
cover bidding is voluntary. We interact FL presence with a
constraint-binding indicator ($n_{\text{genuine}} < 3$):
\[
  y_{igt} = \beta_1 \cdot \text{FL}_{igt} + \beta_2 \cdot
  \text{FL}_{igt} \times \mathbf{1}[n < 3]
  + \gamma \cdot \mathbf{1}[n < 3] + \alpha_g + \lambda_t +
  \varepsilon_{igt}
\]
Within the convite subsample, the FL coefficient when the constraint
does not bind ($n \geq 3$) is $\hat{\beta}_1 = 0.076$ (SE~$= 0.021$,
$p < 0.001$)---a 7.6\% price premium attributable to \emph{voluntary}
cover bidding, close to the baseline OLS (6.4\%). The interaction
$\hat{\beta}_2 = -0.160$ (SE~$= 0.045$, $p < 0.001$) indicates that
the FL--price effect is 16 percentage points \emph{lower} when the
constraint binds. The total FL effect under constraint
($\hat{\beta}_1 + \hat{\beta}_2 = -0.084$) is negative, reflecting
that tenders with few genuine bidders are intrinsically less
competitive and cheaper.

This decomposition supports the model's distinction between mandatory
and strategic cover bidding: the price effect concentrates in
\emph{voluntary} deployments (where cartels choose to operate), not
in constraint-driven deployments (where cover bidding is a mechanical
requirement).

\paragraph{3. CADE enforcement shock.}
As reported in
Appendix~\ref{app:did}, the Callaway--Sant'Anna staggered DiD
using CADE conviction timing as an enforcement shock shows that FL
presence \emph{declines significantly} in treated PBU markets after
conviction (ATT~$= -0.275$, SE~$= 0.059$, $p < 0.001$), consistent
with cover bidders exiting markets where cartel principals are
prosecuted. The price ATT is positive but imprecise (0.145,
SE~$= 0.110$), reflecting limited statistical power at the
PBU--year level. The significant FL exit response constitutes
quasi-experimental evidence that FL presence is connected to
cartel activity, not to time-invariant market characteristics.
