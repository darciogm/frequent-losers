\subsection{Ground Truth Robustness} % V8: Concern 4
\label{sec:ground_truth}

A concern with the ROC validation is that co-participation with
CADE-convicted firms may identify \emph{markets where cartels operate}
rather than \emph{individual cover bidders}. We present three tests
that distinguish market proximity from cover-bidder behavior.

\paragraph{Test~1: Bid behavior in CADE-present tenders.}
In tenders where CADE-convicted firms participate, FL losing bids
exhibit a mean log-spread of 3.08 above the winning price, compared
to 1.84 for non-FL losing bids ($t = 12.1$, $p < 0.001$;
KS $D = 0.12$, $p < 0.001$). FL bids are drawn from a
\emph{different distribution} than non-FL bids even within the same
cartel-present tenders. If FL firms were merely innocent bystanders
in cartel markets, their bid distribution would resemble other
(non-FL) losing bids. The distributional difference supports the
interpretation that FL firms play a distinct role---consistent with
cover bidding---rather than merely co-locating with cartelists.

\paragraph{Test~2: Co-participation intensity.}
After conditioning on total participation count (which mechanically
increases co-participation opportunity), FL firms' share of tenders
in convicted-firm markets is statistically indistinguishable from
non-FL always-losers ($\hat{\beta}_{\text{FL}} = 0.0002$,
$p = 0.93$). This confirms that the unconditional FL--CADE
association (7.1\% vs.\ 2.0\%) is driven by high participation
volume, not by direct cartel affiliation. The permutation test in
Section~\ref{sec:cade} controls for this by stratifying on
participation quartile and still finds the FL rate 3.5 times higher
than expected, indicating that the FL signal goes beyond mechanical
co-participation.

\paragraph{Test~3: Structural flag triangulation.}
We flag always-losers whose mean bid spread falls within the
Regime~2 cover-bid range ($0 < \bar{s} < \hat{\epsilon} +
2\hat{\sigma}_c$, with SD $< 1.5\hat{\sigma}_c$). Among FL firms
with the structural flag, 2.5\% overlap with CADE; among FL firms
\emph{without} the flag, 6.2\% overlap. Among non-FL always-losers,
\emph{zero} overlap with CADE regardless of structural flag.

This pattern has two implications. First, CADE co-participation is
concentrated \emph{entirely among FL firms}, not among firms with
cover-bid-like bid patterns alone---confirming that abnormal
participation (the FL signal) rather than bid-level features drives
the CADE association. Second, FL firms without the structural flag
(whose bids deviate from the Regime~2 pattern) have \emph{higher}
CADE overlap, suggesting that sophisticated cover bidders may vary
their bid patterns to evade distributional screens, while the
participation anomaly remains detectable.

Together, these tests indicate that the ROC validation captures a
genuine association between FL status and cartel-market involvement
that operates through \emph{behavioral} channels (distinct bid
distributions, participation patterns) rather than mere geographic
or market proximity.
