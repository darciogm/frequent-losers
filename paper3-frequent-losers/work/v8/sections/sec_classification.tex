\subsection{Classification Robustness: IQR Rule vs.\ Model-Based Alternatives} % V8
\label{sec:classification_robustness}

A concern with the IQR threshold rule is that it may be ad hoc and
could capture inefficient firms rather than cover bidders. We compare
the IQR rule with two model-based alternatives on the same CADE
ground truth.

\paragraph{Structural classifier.}
Using the estimated Regime~2 parameters ($\hat{\epsilon} = 0.83$,
$\hat{\sigma}_c = 1.19$), we compute a log-likelihood ratio score for
each always-loser firm: the likelihood of the firm's mean bid spread
under the cover-bid distribution relative to the genuine-loser spread
distribution. Firms with high scores are more likely cover bidders
according to the structural model. This score achieves
AUC~$= 0.56$---barely above random---because the bid-spread
distributions of genuine losers and cover bidders overlap
substantially. The structural model identifies cover bidding at the
\emph{distributional} level (Regime~2 vs.\ Regime~1) but its
parameters are insufficiently discriminative at the \emph{individual
firm} level.

\paragraph{Machine-learning classifier.}
We train a random forest (500 trees, 5-fold cross-validation to
prevent overfitting) on seven firm-level features: number of
participations, number of tenders, mean/SD/median bid spread,
fraction of bids above the winner, and bid-level CV. The
cross-validated AUC is $0.90$---strong, but \emph{below} the IQR
rule's $0.94$ (DeLong $p = 0.04$).

\paragraph{Unsupervised latent class analysis.}
To verify that the FL classification has structural content beyond an
ad hoc threshold, we estimate a two-component Gaussian mixture model
on 8,275 always-loser firms with $\geq 3$ tenders, using five
firm-level features: mean bid distance from winner, SD of bid distance,
fraction of bids above the winner, winner HHI (co-bidding
concentration), and participation count. The model discovers two
latent classes without using FL labels. The ``cover bidder'' class
(identified by higher mean participation: 45.2 vs.\ 9.0 tenders)
contains 71.2\% FL firms, compared with 20.0\% in the ``genuine''
class (Table~\ref{tab:lca_validation}). The mean posterior probability
$\Pr(\text{cover} \mid \text{FL}) = 0.56$ falls below the 0.75
benchmark, with precision 0.71 and recall 0.55 ($F_1 = 0.62$). The
partial overlap reflects the multivariate LCA distributing weight
across correlated features, whereas the IQR rule concentrates on the
single most discriminative dimension---participation intensity. The
key result is that the unsupervised model independently recovers a
class whose defining characteristic (abnormally high participation
with zero wins) aligns with the FL definition, providing structural
support for the IQR rule even when the overlap is imperfect.

\paragraph{Interpretation.}
Table~\ref{tab:classification_comparison} and
Figure~\ref{fig:classification_comparison} summarize the supervised
comparison. The IQR rule dominates both the structural classifier and
the random forest. Combined with the LCA's independent recovery of a
cover-bidder class, this result has two implications. First, the
threshold rule is not merely convenient; it captures the most
discriminative feature of cover bidding---\emph{abnormally high
participation despite zero wins}---which neither ML nor unsupervised
methods can improve upon. Second, the structural model's bid-level
parameters are complementary (they identify the \emph{mechanism}) but
not substitutes (they do not improve \emph{detection}) for the
participation-based screen.
