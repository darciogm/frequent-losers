\subsection{Classification Robustness: IQR Rule vs.\ Model-Based Alternatives} % V8
\label{sec:classification_robustness}

A concern with the IQR threshold rule is that it may be ad hoc and
could capture inefficient firms rather than cover bidders. We compare
the IQR rule with two model-based alternatives on the same CADE
ground truth.

\paragraph{Structural classifier.}
Using the estimated Regime~2 parameters ($\hat{\epsilon} = 0.83$,
$\hat{\sigma}_c = 1.19$), we compute a log-likelihood ratio score for
each always-loser firm: the likelihood of the firm's mean bid spread
under the cover-bid distribution relative to the genuine-loser spread
distribution. Firms with high scores are more likely cover bidders
according to the structural model. This score achieves
AUC~$= 0.56$---barely above random---because the bid-spread
distributions of genuine losers and cover bidders overlap
substantially. The structural model identifies cover bidding at the
\emph{distributional} level (Regime~2 vs.\ Regime~1) but its
parameters are insufficiently discriminative at the \emph{individual
firm} level.

\paragraph{Machine-learning classifier.}
We train a random forest (500 trees, 5-fold cross-validation to
prevent overfitting) on seven firm-level features: number of
participations, number of tenders, mean/SD/median bid spread,
fraction of bids above the winner, and bid-level CV. The
cross-validated AUC is $0.90$---strong, but \emph{below} the IQR
rule's $0.94$ (DeLong $p = 0.04$).

\paragraph{Interpretation.}
Table~\ref{tab:classification_comparison} and
Figure~\ref{fig:classification_comparison} summarize the comparison.
The IQR rule dominates both the structural classifier and the random
forest. This result has two implications. First, the threshold rule
is not merely convenient; it captures the most discriminative feature
of cover bidding---\emph{abnormally high participation despite zero
wins}---which ML cannot improve upon by adding bid-level features.
Second, the structural model's bid-level parameters are complementary
(they identify the \emph{mechanism}) but not substitutes (they do not
improve \emph{detection}) for the participation-based screen.
