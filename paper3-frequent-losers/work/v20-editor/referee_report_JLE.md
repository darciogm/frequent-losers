# Referee Report — JLE

**Recommendation: Reject** (resubmission not encouraged at JLE; the paper may be a reasonable fit elsewhere, e.g., JLEO, after substantial revision).

I want to be clear at the outset that this is not a hostile reject. The paper is honestly written, the language discipline is real (it consistently says "flags," "ranks," "concentrates risk," and front-pages its own failures), and the empirical scaffolding is more transparent than most submissions I see. My recommendation rests on a fit problem that is structural to JLE, compounded by two specific defects — one missing number and one orphaned appendix — that together mean the paper as submitted does not yet establish the fact it claims to establish.

## Summary of the paper

The authors propose a cartel-screening tool built entirely from procurement *award* records, without bid microdata. Among firms that never win a tender ("always-losers"), they rank firms by participation count; persistently-participating zero-win firms are labeled "frequent losers" (operationalized as a ≥14-tender cutoff, though the authors concede a continuous log-count score dominates the binary). The claim is a triage one: cheap award-layer records can tell an enforcement agency *where* to begin costly bid-microdata forensics. They validate against a CADE-adjudication-anchored ground truth — 193 always-loser firms that co-bid with cartel defendants — and report firm-level AUC ≈ 0.92, a near-random AUC ≈ 0.49 against the 47 *direct* defendants (offered as a falsification of overreach), and a sequential-gatekeeper exercise that cuts the bid-microdata pool ~83% while recovering most cobidders.

A price section (sec07) decomposes a +3.6% to +7.7% winner-price gap in FL-present cells into a positive selection component and a negative within-cell "mechanism" component, interpreting the latter (operating through bidder-count inflation) as cover-bidding "theater." An appendix framework (App. A) formalizes the ranking as a Poisson posterior under the assumption that cartel-deployed losers participate more than ordinary zero-win firms.

## Fit for JLE

JLE rewards papers that *establish a fact* or a clean, credibly-identified empirical relationship of legal consequence, with transparent data and a tight causal or descriptive claim. This paper's central object is explicitly *predictive and operational*, not causal: it is a ranking tool whose value is decision-architectural ("triage where forensics should begin"). That framing lives more naturally at JLEO. The paper's own internal process reached the same conclusion, and I can make the editorial reasons concrete:

1. **The fact JLE would want is the one the paper cannot currently produce.** The legally consequential fact here would be: "persistent zero-win participation concentrates cartel-adjacent firms *above what mere market exposure mechanically produces*." The authors agree — they write that opportunity-set exposure adjustment is "a required audit, not optional robustness" (sec04, lines 130–131). But that audit reports **no number** (see Major 1). Without it, the headline 3.2× / AUC 0.92 cannot be separated from a mechanical artifact, so the paper has not yet *established a fact* in the JLE sense; it has established a correlation between two quantities that both load on participation volume.

2. **The validation target is adjudication-derived but partly circular, and JLE referees scrutinize ground-truth credibility harder than JLEO.** The "cobidder" label (always-loser co-appearing with a defendant) and the score (participation among always-losers) share a primitive: a firm that participates more has, by coupon-collector geometry, a mechanically higher chance of intersecting at least one defendant-bearing tender, independent of any cartel role. This is a *predictive* circularity question, and JLE will press on whether the relationship is anything more than mechanical.

3. **The result does not change how we understand the law.** The strongest law-and-economics hook — that the convite minimum-bidder rule manufactures the loser-side signal — is *contradicted* by the data (pregão AUC 0.952 > convite 0.816, opposite to the institutional prior). The authors honestly reframe this as "scope information, not institutional identification," but that reframe removes the legal-mechanism content that would make this a JLE paper rather than a prediction paper.

The gap-closing path for JLE is narrow but nameable: produce the exposure-adjusted number (Major 1), test reverse causality with a timing exercise (Major 4), and either derive the institutional mechanism or recover a different legal-consequence fact. That is a different paper, not a revision.

## Major concerns (ordered by decisiveness for JLE)

**1. The single most important identification defense produces no falsifiable quantity. [Fatal as submitted]**
*sec04 §4.2 lines 127–141; sec_app03 §D.2 (`app:market_opportunity_submission`) lines 45–75; validation-audit table line 98 ("Reported in text; see Appendix D.2"); values.tex — no exposure/opportunity macro exists.*
The authors correctly name opportunity-set exposure as "the main empirical threat" and call exposure adjustment "a required audit." Yet Appendix D.2 reports it "as a diagnostic construction rather than as a single new headline statistic" — and contains no AUC, no excess ratio, no coefficient, no retained-N. I confirmed that every other audit row in the table (lines 88–118) is bound to a macro (`\valMultThreeTwo`, `\valLeakCVAUC`, `\valAUCFLfirmTemp`, `\valAUCdirectStd`) while the exposure row alone resolves to prose. In a manuscript whose advertised discipline is macro-binding every number, the *one* audit the authors flag as decisive is an unfalsifiable promissory note. The volume permutation does not substitute — the authors themselves write it "does not by itself make firms' opportunities to meet CADE anchors comparable." **Resolution:** report the within-opportunity-set AUC (firms matched on item/modality/year/buyer strata, conditional on having any chance to meet a CADE anchor) as a bound macro with retained N, *and* the AUC of raw participation count against the cobidder label within the always-loser stratum. State a pre-committed rejection bound: if exposure-adjusted AUC falls toward 0.5, the triage claim fails. The number must exist either way; right now the paper's core fact is undemonstrated.

**2. A flat numerical contradiction in the headline validation paragraph. [Submission-blocking]**
*sec04 §4.1 lines 52–60; macros `valConservativeCobidders`=210, `valConservativeFL`=108, `valCADEenrich`=3.95%, `valCADEbase`=1.24%.*
Lines 52–53 state "210 always-loser cobidders. Among those cobidders, 108 are frequent losers" — i.e., an FL-among-cobidders share of 108/210 = **51.4%**. Lines 57–59 then state "the observed frequent-loser share among conservative cobidders is **3.95%**." Both phrases label the identical quantity and give 51.4% vs 3.95%. The 3.95/1.24 ratio reproduces the 3.2× lift (I verified: 3.19), so the error is in the prose denominator: 3.95% is almost certainly the FL share within the broader always-loser stratum, not within the 210 cobidders. A referee cannot tell which denominator backs the 3.2× lift and AUC 0.748 that the validation section rides on. **Resolution:** state numerator and denominator for each quantity, relabel them as distinct objects, and confirm which feeds the 3.2× and AUC=0.748.

**3. Two "common pools" (16,779 vs 11,676) carry near-identical definitions and a constant 193 positives, with no stated filter. [Submission-blocking for reproducibility]**
*sec06 lines 67, 76, 119–121 (`valImhofPoolN`=16,779: "always-loser pool for which bid-distribution features [can be computed]") vs lines 210, 266 (`valArchPoolSize`=11,676: "always-loser firms with computable bid-distribution features").*
The favorable horse-race AUC levels (Imhof 0.888, combo 0.962) are reported on the *larger* 16,779 pool; the operational gatekeeper table and the same-sample DeLong increment use the *smaller* 11,676 pool — and both descriptions are verbatim "the pool with computable bid features." The same 193 positives sit in both. A reader cannot reproduce either pool, and using the larger pool for the flattering levels while using the smaller for the operational table reads as sample selection until explained. Relatedly, the combined increment "+0.096 to +0.098" (line 157) only reconciles against the same-sample Imhof base of 0.846 (→ implied combo ≈0.942), a number never reported; against the 16,779-pool Imhof (0.888) the combo gain is only +0.074. **Resolution:** define each pool's N and positive count explicitly, state the 16,779→11,676 filter once, report base rates, and report the same-sample combo AUC level so +0.096–0.098 is verifiable.

**4. Reverse causality is assumed away, never tested — and JLE will demand the test. [Major]**
*sec_app01 §A.2 lines 74–90 (λ_C > λ_G "maintained, not derived"); sec03 lines 39–50.*
The entire ranking rests on λ_C > λ_G: cartel-deployed losers participate more than ordinary zero-win firms. But a sincere, optimistic, low-cost firm that bids on everything and rarely wins generates high participation with no cartel role, and conditioning on win_rate=0 (survivorship: firms that win once exit the stratum) compounds this. The model never distinguishes "FL is a pre-determined cartel role" from "persistent losing is an equilibrium of optimistic low-cost bidding." JLEO would accept this as a disclosed scope limit; JLE will not. **Resolution:** report a within-firm timing test — does cobidder participation spike around the cartel's active/case window (deployment) versus a flat profile for FL non-cobidders (sincere persistence)? Absent that, the validation cannot adjudicate mechanism and the framework's inequality should be labeled observationally equivalent to sincere persistence.

**5. The "mechanism" channel is under-identified against a benign-entry null, yet labeled "theater." [Major — rule-of-engagement violation]**
*sec07 §3.2 lines 157–172; Table 4 (`log(n_firms)` control drives the within-cell effect from −0.048 to +0.008 n.s.).*
The authors show the within-cell price effect "operates entirely through the bidder-count channel" and interpret the +0.507 log-bidder inflation as cover-bidder "theater" and "the cover-bidding mechanism that motivates the screen." But "more bidders compress the winning bid toward the reference price" is *exactly* the reduced-form prediction of genuine pro-competitive entry. Nothing in the section separates manufactured cover bidders from real competitors. Calling this "theater" is a verb the data has not earned and violates the paper's own "never proves/detects" discipline. **Resolution:** decompose the +0.507 into zero-win/FL entrants vs eventually-winning entrants; show the compression survives controlling for *genuine* bidder count but vanishes only when FL bidders are added. If the data cannot do this, downgrade to "bidder-count inflation of ambiguous origin" and state the benign-entry equivalence.

**6. The continuous-dominates-binary result inverts under the paper's own strictest audit, undisclosed. [Major]**
*sec04 lines 149–153; `valAUCStrictFirmFL`=0.767 vs `valAUCStrictFirmTC`=0.750.*
A stated contribution is that continuous log(tenders_count) dominates the FL14 binary (DeLong Z=−4.38, p=1.2e-05). In the strict 2009–2016 train-period audit — the one place the ranking is formed under genuine field-like information, with threshold falling to 7 — the binary (0.767) *beats* the continuous (0.750). The text reads this only as a generic "decline relative to the relaxed holdout" and does not flag that the central ranking ordering reverses precisely where deployability matters. The 0.767 is also not comparable to the headline 0.924 because the threshold-7 cut flags a different, larger population. **Resolution:** acknowledge the reversal, report the DeLong test for FL vs continuous on the strict pool, and lead the strict audit with the cutoff-free continuous AUC, noting the flagged populations differ.

**7. Temporal honesty is undercut by a rising prospective/retrospective split, and the operational precision number is buried. [Major]**
*sec04 lines 159–174; sec_app03 §D.5 (2014: 0.819 → 2019: 0.922); sec06 lines 213, 308–317.*
The rolling-origin AUC climbs monotonically to 0.922 by 2019 — essentially recovering in-sample — which is consistent with the known fact that ~47% of top-ranked firms come from 2017–19 participation, *after* CADE investigations were underway. The headline 0.864 is an average masking a prospective floor (~0.82) and a retrospective ceiling (~0.92). Worse, the operationally relevant cheap-first-stage precision@500 collapses from 0.132 in-sample to ~0.070 under temporal holdout (53% retention), disclosed only as a "47% TP-drop" and never in precision terms, while the more robust *sequential* precision is foregrounded. **Resolution:** bring the award-only temporal-holdout precision@500/@1000 into the main gatekeeper table, report prospective-only AUC restricted to pre-investigation-onset test years, and frame the "83% footprint reduction" headline against the temporal-holdout column.

## Minor concerns

1. **Orphaned external-validity appendix.** sec_app07 (ComprasNet) is `\input` by neither compiled document; I confirmed its 78 `\valFed*` macro usages are defined nowhere in values.tex, and pdftotext of the 58-page combined PDF returns zero "comprasnet" hits. The submission therefore contains *no* external validation. Worse, the appendix's own "honest interpretation" reports federal AUC ~0.60 vs BEC ~0.92 and that the threshold rule does not transport. **Either wire it in (and lead with the divergence) or cut the file from the tree** — gesturing at external validity that isn't delivered is an editorial-integrity problem.
2. **Participation placebo mean of exactly 0.500 (SD 0.013)** reads like an *unconditional* label shuffle, not the participation-stratified one the text describes; if stratified, a residual participation signal should push the mean above 0.5. State whether the B=2,000 AUC permutation is quartile-stratified; if not, relabel it a random-label null.
3. **D2 convite arm rests on ~6 cobidders.** Report this N inline wherever the pregão-vs-convite (0.952 vs 0.816) split appears; the −0.136 difference is statistically fragile and should be framed suggestive-only.
4. **Headline price range +3.6%–+7.7% does not reconcile** with the four coefficients it summarizes (`valOLSGeneral`=0.0677, …, Pregão 0.0933): exp(β)−1 gives 7.0%/6.6%/9.8%/3.9%; neither raw nor exp scale produces [3.6%, 7.7%]. The macro and the table must be sourced from the same regressions.
5. **Selection coefficient +22.65 → +3.55** (85% attenuation under full FE) is reported as vindication without anchoring units; report the within-support Q1→Q5 difference (1.4% vs 41% FL-share endpoints) as a percent with a CI, and reconcile the Δ=5.58 log-point gap (a ~574% difference).
6. **Modal contrast used inconsistently:** pregão > convite is "supporting evidence" in sec07 §3.1 but "scope info to be explained away" in the D2 material; and it vanishes under overlap-ATT (both ≈ −0.098). Pick one reading.
7. **Sequential rule beats the "full-observability upper bound" joint model** under temporal holdout (87 vs 85 @500; 114 vs 111 @1000). Either drop "upper bound" language for the temporal exercise or explain the overfitting that inverts the in-sample edge; report CIs so 87-vs-85 can be judged noise vs signal.
8. **Cobidder "economic content" (sec05) rests on a self-conceded null:** quadrant heterogeneity dies under PS matching (+0.081 p=0.072 → +0.062 p=0.235), one dimension is wrong-signed (repeat-pair share d=−0.38), and the surviving "breadth" difference is mechanically tied to the label. Match the surviving bid moments on firm covariates or demote them to descriptive.
9. **Direct-defendant AUC printed as both 0.49 and 0.491**; bind to one macro.
10. **Observed price coefficient (+0.064) falls *below* the placebo mean (+0.144)**, not merely inside the null band — interpret this rather than dismiss in one line; reconcile units with the sec07 +3.6%–+7.7% gap.
11. **Resampling/CI methodology underspecified:** seed, fold-assignment unit (must be firm-level to match the leakage claim), DeLong-vs-bootstrap, clustering, and why permutation counts differ (1,000 vs 2,000).
12. **Framework is a one-assumption relabeling** (Poisson MLR ⇒ monotone posterior), and Proposition 2 is a definitional restatement dressed as a theorem. Either derive λ_C > λ_G from primitives / extract a second testable implication, or reframe explicitly as "a ranking rationale, not a structural model."

## What the authors do well

The language discipline is genuine and should be credited: the paper never says "detects cartelists" or "proves," and it front-pages its own falsification — the near-random AUC ≈ 0.49 against direct defendants is a real, theory-implied prediction that came out as the scope claim requires. That asymmetry (cobidder ~0.92 vs defendant ~0.49) is the strongest part of the design and is used honestly as a guardrail against overreach rather than hidden. The leakage decomposition (0.995 → 0.891 CV → 0.864 temporal) is arithmetically self-consistent and I was able to reproduce the 3.2× ratio and the gatekeeper cost-recall cells. The macro-binding discipline, where applied, makes the paper unusually auditable. And the price-scope decomposition is a real economic argument, not smuggled damages — the section is scrupulous about disclaiming markup/treatment readings. This is a careful team that knows where its claims end.

## Bottom line

This is not a JLE paper, and the reasons are concrete rather than stylistic. JLE asks for an established fact of legal consequence; the paper's central fact — that loser-side participation concentrates cartel-adjacent firms *beyond mechanical market exposure* — is precisely the one the authors flag as requiring an exposure-adjusted audit, and that audit reports no number anywhere in the compiled submission (Major 1). Until that quantity exists, the headline correlation is indistinguishable from a coupon-collector artifact, the validation label is partly circular, and reverse causality is assumed rather than tested — three things a Chicago empirical referee will not waive. The institutional-law hook that would make this a law-and-economics paper (the convite quorum mechanism) is contradicted by the data and honestly demoted to "scope," which removes the legal-mechanism content. The single barrier that matters most is the missing exposure-adjusted number: produce it as a bound statistic with a pre-committed rejection region, and the paper either has a fact or it doesn't. As a predictive/decision-architecture contribution with disciplined hedging, it is a plausible JLEO paper after the numerical contradictions (Major 2–3) and the orphaned appendix are fixed; as a JLE fact-establishing paper, it is not there.
