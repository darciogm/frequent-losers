# Referee Report — JLEO

**Recommendation: Major revision.**

The paper is targeted intelligently at JLEO and is, on its own terms, an unusually disciplined retreat from "cartel detection" to "evidence triage under costly proof." The conceptual framing fits the journal, the language discipline is real, and the strongest empirical move — the deliberate direct-defendant null (AUC ≈ 0.49) used as a falsification of overreach — is genuinely persuasive. But the paper is not yet acceptable. Its single most important identification defense is an unfalsifiable promissory note, two headline validation numbers flatly contradict each other, the central mechanism claim is asserted rather than identified, and an external-validity appendix the project narrative leans on is physically absent from the compiled submission. None of these is fatal in the sense of "the idea is dead"; all are fixable. But they must be fixed before the validation pipeline can be trusted, and one of them (the exposure audit) could in principle bring the core claim down. Hence major, not minor.

## Summary of the paper

The authors propose a screen built entirely from procurement *award* records — among firms that never win a tender (zero-win "always-losers"), they rank firms by participation count, on the theory that a cartel's designated losing roles ("cover bidders") participate more frequently than ordinary inefficient firms. The claimed contribution is not detection but *triage*: cheap award-layer records can tell an antitrust agency *where* to deploy expensive bid-microdata forensics. The screen is validated against an adjudication-anchored label — always-losers that co-bid in the same tender-items as CADE cartel defendants (193 "cobidders") — yielding firm-level AUC ≈ 0.92, while it is correctly *random* (≈ 0.49) against the 47 direct defendants themselves. This asymmetry is sold as the design boundary, not a bug.

The paper layers on a leakage/temporal audit (in-sample item AUC 0.98 → CV 0.89 → temporal holdout 0.86), an information-cost "horse race" against Imhof-style bid-layer screens (award layer adds non-redundant signal; combined AUC 0.962), a sequential-gatekeeper exercise (conditioning bid-layer forensics on the award screen cuts the microdata pool ~83% while recovering most cobidders), a descriptive cobidder profile, and a price-scope section interpreting a positive raw price gap as a selection-plus-mechanism decomposition rather than damages. A theoretical appendix formalizes the ranking via a Poisson monotone-likelihood-ratio argument.

## Assessment of the contribution

For JLEO the relevant bar is not a clean causal parameter; it is whether the paper establishes a *useful, honestly characterized decision architecture* and whether the institutional/informational framing does real conceptual work. The triage idea — allocating scarce proof-producing resources by sequencing a cheap signal layer ahead of a costly one — is squarely in the journal's wheelhouse, and the direct-defendant null demonstrates the authors understand the difference between a triage tool and a liability finding. That is the paper's claim to the journal.

The problem is that two pillars of that claim are currently unsupported. First, the conceptual framework (App. A) does light work: it is a one-assumption relabeling whose entire content is the maintained inequality λ_C > λ_G ("cartel losers participate more"), which is asserted, never derived, and is close to tautological with the screen itself (the screen ranks zero-win firms by participation; the assumption says the targets are high-participation zero-win firms). It generates no non-obvious comparative static the validation could have refuted. Second, and more seriously, the empirical object's meaningfulness rests on an exposure-adjustment audit that reports *no number anywhere*. Until those two pillars carry weight, the paper reads as a well-disciplined description of a participation-count ranking dressed in triage vocabulary, rather than a contribution that earns the framing. The path to acceptance is concrete and within reach, which is why I recommend revision rather than rejection.

## Major concerns

**1. The exposure-adjusted audit — which the authors themselves designate as the required defense — produces no falsifiable quantity. (sec04 §4.2 lines 127–141; sec_app03 §D.2, `app:market_opportunity_submission`, lines 46–75; validation-audit table line 98.)**
This is the decisive issue for JLEO because the entire paper is the triage claim. The cobidder label (an always-loser co-appearing with a defendant) and the score (participation among always-losers) both load on the same primitive: a firm that participates more often has mechanically more chances to intersect a defendant-bearing tender-item, by coupon-collector geometry, independent of any cartel role. The authors recognize this exactly — they write that exposure adjustment is "a required audit, not optional robustness" (sec04 line 130). But sec_app03 §D.2 then reports the audit "as a diagnostic construction rather than as a single new headline statistic" (line 66), and the audit-map table row (line 98) literally reads "Reported in text; see Appendix D.2." Appendix D.2 contains no AUC, no excess ratio, no coefficient, no retained N. I confirmed by grep that *zero* exposure/opportunity macros exist in `values.tex`. The volume permutation does not substitute for this — the authors concede it "does not by itself make firms' opportunities to meet CADE anchors comparable" (sec04 line 110–111). As it stands, the headline 3.2× concentration and AUC 0.924 cannot be distinguished from mechanical exposure. *Resolution:* report the exposure-adjusted AUC (or excess ratio) with retained N as a bound macro in the validation table — AUC within matched item-modality-year-buyer opportunity strata, conditional on a firm having had any chance to meet a CADE anchor. State a pre-committed rejection region (e.g., "if within-opportunity-set AUC < 0.70, the award layer adds no triage value beyond exposure") and report whether the data clear it. The number must exist either way.

**2. The single quantity that would bound the circularity — participation count vs. cobidders — is conspicuously absent from the scope matrix. (sec04 §4.3 scope matrix lines 52–69; rows back-loaded by `\valScopeRowOne`…`\valScopeRowEight`.)**
The eight-row scope matrix reports Binary-FL-vs-cobidders (row 1), Binary-FL-vs-direct-defendants (row 3, 0.491), and *Participation-count*-vs-*direct-defendants* (row 4, 0.383) — but there is no row for *Participation-count*-vs-*cobidders*. That omission is exactly the cell a skeptic needs: if raw participation count already achieves most of the 0.924 against the cobidder label, then the FL/log-tc construction adds little beyond the mechanical exposure that *defines* the label, and the "economic content" claim weakens to a restatement of the label. The authors have the data — row 4 proves they computed participation-vs-defendants. *Resolution:* add the missing row (participation count | always-losers | cobidders | AUC = ?) and interpret the gap between it and 0.924 as the screen's incremental signal over raw volume.

**3. The headline conservative benchmark contradicts itself on the FL-among-cobidder share. (sec04 §4.1 lines 52–60; macros `\valConservativeFL`=108, `\valConservativeCobidders`=210, `\valCADEenrich`=3.95%.)**
Line 52–53: "210 always-loser cobidders. Among those cobidders, 108 are frequent losers" — i.e. 108/210 = 51.4%. Lines 57–59: "Within the always-loser stratum, the observed frequent-loser share among conservative cobidders is 3.95%." Both phrases label the FL share among the 210 conservative cobidders, but give 51.4% vs 3.95%. The 3.2× ratio (3.95/1.24) and AUC 0.748 are mutually consistent, so the prose label is almost certainly wrong: 3.95% is plausibly the FL rate within the *full always-loser stratum near defendants*, not within the 210 cobidders. As written this is a flat numerical contradiction in a headline validation paragraph, and a referee cannot tell which denominator feeds the 3.2× lift and AUC 0.748. *Resolution:* state numerator and denominator for each quantity explicitly, relabel them as distinct objects, and confirm which one drives the 3.2× ratio.

**4. The continuous-dominates-binary result — a stated contribution — silently reverses under the paper's own strictest audit. (sec04 §4.2 lines 143–157; `\valAUCStrictFirmFL`=0.767 vs `\valAUCStrictFirmTC`=0.750, `\valThresholdTrain`=7.)**
The headline (and gate result) is that continuous log(tenders_count) dominates the FL14≥14 binary (DeLong Z = −4.38, p = 1.2e-05). In the strict 2009–2016 train-period audit, where the threshold becomes 7 and *all* screen elements are formed ex ante — the one place the ranking is built under genuinely field-like information — the binary flag (0.767) *beats* the continuous score (0.750). The text reads this only as a generic "decline relative to the relaxed holdout" (line 153) and never acknowledges that the ordering flips precisely where deployability matters most. This undercuts the "true signal is loss intensity; FL14 is merely deployable" framing. *Resolution:* acknowledge the reversal, report the DeLong test for FL vs continuous on the strict pool (if 0.767 vs 0.750 is not significant, say so), lead the strict audit with the cutoff-free continuous AUC, and note that 0.924 and 0.767 differ partly because the strict threshold of 7 flags a different (larger) population — they are not the same ROC operating point.

**5. The price section's central mechanism claim ("cover-bidding theater") is asserted, not identified, against a benign-entry null. (sec07 §3.2 lines 157–170; Table 4 mechanism rows.)**
The within-cell winner-to-reference depression (−0.048) vanishes to +0.008 (n.s.) once log(n_firms) is added; the authors state "the within-cell price effect operates entirely through the bidder-count channel" (lines 163–165) and interpret the +0.507 log-bidder inflation (≈66% more bidders) as cover-bidder "theater." But "more bidders mechanically compress the winning bid toward the reference price" is *also* the exact reduced-form prediction of genuine pro-competitive entry. The section provides no contrast separating manufactured cover bidders from real additional competitors. Calling this "the cover-bidding mechanism that motivates the screen" (sec07 line 12) is a verb the data has not earned and brushes against the paper's own rule against claiming mechanism. *Resolution:* decompose the +0.507 into wins-eligible vs zero-win/FL entrants and show the compression survives controlling for genuine (eventually-winning or non-FL) bidder count while vanishing only when FL bidders are added. If the data cannot do this, downgrade "cover-bidding theater" to "bidder-count inflation of ambiguous origin" and state the benign-entry observational equivalence explicitly.

**6. The ComprasNet external-validity appendix is orphaned — not compiled, macros undefined, absent from the submission. (sec_app07_comprasnet_submission.tex, all 402 lines; not `\input` by either compiled document; absent from the app00 referee map.)**
I confirmed: `online_appendix_submission_clean.tex` inputs only app00–app06; app07 is never included. All ~70 `\valFed*` macros (and the BEC comparison `\valBEC*` macros it cites) are defined nowhere — grep returns zero `valFed` definitions in `values.tex`. So the submitted paper contains *no external validation at all*, despite the project framing it as a cross-jurisdiction contribution. Moreover, the appendix's own "honest interpretation" concedes the federal in-sample AUC is ~0.60 (vs BEC ~0.92), the IQR threshold does not transport, and the within-cell mechanism does not replicate. Wiring it in unedited would *weaken* the operational pitch. *Resolution:* decide explicitly. Either (a) wire it in — add the `\input`, define the `\valFed*`/`\valBEC*` macros with provenance, add a referee-map row, recompile — and reframe it from the first sentence as "qualitative structure survives, calibration does not," tying it to the sec08 portability hedge; or (b) cut the file from the tree so it cannot be mistaken for delivered content. Do not gesture at external validity you have not delivered.

**7. Two validation pools (16,779 and 11,676) carry near-identical verbatim definitions, validate against the same 193 positives, and the filter between them is never stated. (sec06 lines 75–76 and 118–133 vs 209–211 and 265–266; `\valImhofPoolN`=16,779, `\valArchPoolSize`=11,676, `\valArchNPos`=193.)**
The Imhof horse race (favorable levels: FL 0.921, Imhof 0.888, combo 0.962) uses the 16,779-firm pool described as "the always-loser pool for which the bid-distribution benchmark can be computed"; the gatekeeper cost-recall table and the DeLong same-sample subset use 11,676 firms "with computable bid-distribution features." The descriptions are effectively identical, the pools differ by 5,103 firms, and the same 193 positives sit in both with no stated filtering chain. Using the larger pool for the favorable AUC levels and the smaller pool for the operational table reads as sample selection until explained. Relatedly, the combined increment "+0.096 to +0.098" (line 157–159) only reconciles against the same-sample Imhof base of 0.846 (→ implied combo ~0.942), a level never reported, while the text juxtaposes it with the 16,779-pool combo of 0.962 (whose increment over 0.888 is only +0.074). *Resolution:* define each pool in one sentence (and the 16,779→11,676 filter), report base rates (193/16,779 vs 193/11,676), confirm the positive set is identical, state the same-sample combo AUC level so +0.096–0.098 is verifiable, and justify why the horse-race levels use the larger pool.

## Minor concerns

1. **"Full-observability upper bound" is exceeded by the restricted rule out-of-time.** Under temporal holdout the sequential rule recovers 87 TP@500 vs 85 for joint, and 114@1000 vs 111 (sec06 lines 311–313), yet joint scoring is repeatedly called an "upper bound" (line 270). An upper bound a cheaper rule beats is mislabeled or signals in-sample joint overfitting. Either drop the term for the temporal exercise or add one sentence explaining why staging generalizes better out-of-time (low-variance award feature vs higher-variance bid moments), and report CIs so 87-vs-85 can be judged as noise.

2. **The cheap first stage's operational collapse is disclosed only as a TP-drop, never in precision terms.** The award-only screen — the stage an agency actually deploys — loses 47% of in-sample TPs under temporal holdout (`\valArchTHFLDrop`), implying precision@500 ≈ 0.070 vs 0.132 in-sample. The temporal gatekeeper table shows only sequential/joint rows. Report award-only temporal-holdout precision@500 and @1000, and the share of the in-sample top-500 attributable to 2017–19 (post-investigation) participation. Frame the "83% footprint reduction" against the temporal column, not the in-sample one.

3. **Pooled temporal AUC 0.864 sits below every recent rolling-origin year (2017: 0.862, 2018: 0.897, 2019: 0.922).** A pooled 17–19 number should land between its constituents (mean 0.894), not at the 2017 value. State whether 0.864 is the frozen-09–16 score pooled over 17–19 while the year table uses a rolling, growing-window construction; if they are different estimators, label them distinctly and report the pooled N and positive count.

4. **The monotone rise to 0.922 by 2019 invites the retrospective reading.** The rolling-origin climb to near-in-sample by 2019 is consistent with the screen turning retrospective as investigations mature. The honest forward-looking figure is the early-year floor (~0.82), not the 0.864 average. Report a prospective-only AUC restricted to test years before plausible investigation onset and state that 0.864 is an average.

5. **The participation-stratified placebo mean of exactly 0.500 (SD 0.013) reads like an unconditional label shuffle.** If the cobidder label is participation-correlated (the paper's premise), a genuinely within-quartile-stratified permutation should center *above* 0.5. State whether the B=2,000 AUC sham is stratified or unconditional; if unconditional, relabel it a random-label null and lean on the (numbered) exposure audit for the participation-exposure threat.

6. **Direct-defendant AUC printed as both 0.49 and 0.491.** `\valAUCdirectStd`=0.49 (prose) vs `\valScopeRowThree`=0.491 (matrix). Bind both to one macro.

7. **Price headline range +3.6%–+7.7% does not reconcile with the four coefficients it summarizes.** The table coefs (0.0677, 0.0636, 0.0933, 0.0382) map to 3.89%–9.78% (exp scale) or 3.82%–9.33% (log-points). Neither produces [3.6%, 7.7%]; the upper bound is below the pregão estimate. Either redefine `\valHeadlineRange` to the min/max of the four reported coefficients or state which specifications the band comes from.

8. **The price sham null is stranger than reported.** Observed price coefficient +0.064 is *below* the sham mean +0.144 (p=0.989). A coefficient below the placebo mean is not merely "fails to reject"; interpret why FL adjacency associates with *less* price elevation than random volume reshuffling, and reconcile +0.064 with the sec07 +3.6%–7.7% gap (units must be stated).

9. **The modal asymmetry is used as corroboration in sec07 and explained away on the validation side.** sec07 (lines 70–77) cites pregão > convite as weighing against a procedural-artifact reading; the D2 material reframes the same directional fact as "scope information, not institutional identification." Worse, under overlap-ATT the negative effect survives in *both* modalities (≈ −0.098), so the modality contrast vanishes in the disciplined spec. State the modal interpretation once, consistently, and note the contrast lives only in the broad spec.

10. **Selection coefficient (+22.65 raw → +3.55 full FE) reported without a units anchor.** The 85% attenuation when all five cell-dimension FE are added is itself evidence that most "selection" is cell composition; the surviving +3.55 is presented as vindication without noting the attenuation. Report the within-support Q1-to-Q5 predicted log-price gap (1.4% vs 41% FL endpoints, not 0-to-1) with a CI, and confirm Δ=5.58 is log-points (a ~574% gap).

11. **Reverse causality (losing → FL vs FL → role) is assumed away, never tested.** λ_C > λ_G is observationally equivalent to sincere persistence by low-cost optimistic bidders; conditioning on W=0 compounds this via survivorship. A within-firm timing test (does cobidder participation spike around the cartel's active window?) would adjudicate. Absent it, soften the framework to disclose the equivalence.

12. **Reproducibility plumbing underspecified.** State the seed, the CV fold-assignment unit (must be firm-level to back the leakage claim), whether AUC CIs are DeLong or clustered bootstrap, and why permutation counts differ (1,000 vs 2,000). The 0.891 CI width of 0.007 is suspiciously tight for ~193 positives — confirm it is across out-of-fold scores, not within-fold.

13. **Convite arm of the modal split rests on ~6 cobidders.** Wherever the modal AUC difference appears, report the convite cobidder N inline and frame as suggestive-only.

14. **App. A Proposition 2 ("scope separation") is a definitional restatement, not a result.** Either fold it into prose or give it content — e.g., the ranking is the optimal triage order under a stated investigative cost/benefit objective, which would be a genuine, JLEO-appropriate result.

## What the authors do well

1. **The direct-defendant null is the paper's strongest move and is rare.** Designing a validation where AUC ≈ 0.49 (and below-chance 0.383 for participation-vs-defendants) is the *required* outcome, and then reporting it front-and-center as the design boundary, is exactly the kind of falsification most screening papers avoid. This is genuinely credible and worth foregrounding even more.

2. **Language discipline is real and sustained.** Across all eight sections the forbidden verbs ("detects," "proves," "outperforms") are avoided; the tables are explicitly labeled "information-cost"/"cost-of-evidence" rather than horse races; the conclusion does not overclaim cross-jurisdiction generality. This is a paper that knows what it is not claiming.

3. **The leakage decomposition is arithmetically self-consistent and honestly decaying.** 0.995 in-sample → 0.891 CV-holdout → 0.864 temporal is reported without flinching, and the firm-persistence fact (~90% of test-window firms unseen in training) is a real defense against the same-firm-artifact concern.

4. **The triage framing fits JLEO and, once the two pillars are repaired, is a real contribution.** Sequencing a cheap signal layer ahead of costly proof-production, with an explicit cost-recall frontier, is a decision-architecture argument the journal values. The skeleton is sound.

## Bottom line

The one thing that determines accept vs. reject at JLEO is **Major Concern 1**: the exposure-adjusted audit must produce a real, macro-bound number with a stated rejection region. The authors have correctly identified opportunity-set exposure as the main threat and then declined to quantify it — leaving the paper's central empirical claim (that the award layer concentrates cartel-adjacent risk beyond what mechanical participation volume buys) formally untested against its own acknowledged confound. If the within-opportunity-set AUC stays clearly above chance, the triage contribution holds and, with the reconciliations in concerns 3–7 and the ComprasNet decision, this becomes a publishable JLEO paper. If it collapses toward 0.5, the concentration is an exposure artifact and the core claim fails. Either way, the number must exist. Everything else in this report is fixable bookkeeping or honest reframing; this one is the paper.
