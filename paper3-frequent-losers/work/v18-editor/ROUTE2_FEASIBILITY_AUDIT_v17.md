## II. Bid-level data audit (the central factual risk)

This is the single most consequential finding of the audit.

### II.1. What the manuscript says

The v16 manuscript repeatedly frames itself around the absence of
bid-level / per-bidder bid-amount data at BEC. The phrase appears in 15
places across 11 section files (50 hits if all near-synonyms are counted).
The most strident wordings:

| Section | Line | Phrase |
|---|---|---|
| `sec_frontmatter_v16` | 31 | "detectors that require per-bidder bid amounts, the screening …" |
| `sec_introduction_v16` | 7 | "are informative about coordinated bidding when the bid-level …" |
| `sec_introduction_v16` | 68 | "per-bidder bid amounts (\citealt{imhof2019detecting} pipeline, …" |
| `sec_introduction_v16` | 113 | "require bid microdata; the construct operates on the …" |
| `sec_introduction_v16` | 136 | "jurisdictions that lack bid microdata can deploy the …" |
| `sec_institutional_v16` | 47 | "Bid-by-bid records exist in the operational system but are not routinely exported …" |
| `sec_institutional_v16` | 50 | "requesting bid-level microdata from the BEC operational system is a multi-step administrative process that delays investigations by weeks" |
| `sec_institutional_v16` | 60–72 | extended argument that FPDS-NG/TED/UK preserve award layer but bid microdata is heterogeneous |
| `sec5_emp_v16` | 12 | "negotiated price, but not per-bidder bid amounts" |
| `sec_cade_v16` | 118 | "layer does not preserve per-firm bid amounts and these predictions" |
| `sec_conclusion_v16` | 5 | "assumption that bid-level data are observable" |

The careful version of the argument (most defensible) is in
`sec_institutional_v16` lines 43–78 and `sec_forensic_v16` lines 60–110:
the **analytical warehouse layer** that audit courts and competition
authorities query does not export per-bid amounts; the operational system
does, but only via slow administrative requests. The construct is designed
to run on the analytical-warehouse layer for rapid screening, then hand off
to a forensic stage for the cases that warrant the operational-system
request.

### II.4. Reconciling the manuscript with the data

There is a defensible reading and an indefensible one.

**Defensible (and partially present in v16):** the construct is
*designed* to operate on the analytical-warehouse layer that contracting
officers and audit-court analysts can query in minutes. The team
themselves had to go through the slower administrative request to obtain
the bid microdata; their evaluation pipeline uses that data, but the
*screening object* does not require it for deployment. This is what
`sec_institutional_v16` and `sec_forensic_v16` argue, and the prose is
careful enough at those sections.

**Indefensible (current factual risk):** the abstract,
`sec_introduction_v16`, and `sec_conclusion_v16` use language ("when bid
microdata are unavailable", "without per-bidder bid amounts") loose
enough that a Referee 2 will read it as "the data don't exist" and then
catch the team running the full Imhof pipeline (which by definition
requires bid microdata). The Imhof comparison and the architecture story
both rely on having those data; the team obtained them; the abstract and
intro elide that fact.

### II.5. Verdict on factual risk

**Material but survivable.** The manuscript is not lying — it is
imprecise. The research-design framing is internally consistent (the
construct operates on the cheap layer; the comparison runs on the
expensive layer the team obtained for evaluation purposes), but the
imprecision is concentrated exactly in the high-visibility prose
(abstract, intro opening, conclusion closing). A clean cleanup is needed
even in the absence of any other Route 2 work.

**Required cleanup, even in a Route-2-minimal scenario:**

1. Replace "bid microdata unavailable" with "bid microdata not retrievable
   at the analytical-warehouse layer that screening must run on" in the
   four hot spots: abstract / `sec_frontmatter_v16` L31,
   `sec_introduction_v16` L113 + L136, `sec_conclusion_v16` L5 + L29 +
   L124.
2. Add one sentence in `sec5_emp_v16` (next to L12) and one footnote in
   `sec_forensic_v16` (next to L74) disclosing that the team accessed
   the bid microdata via an administrative TCE-SP-style request for
   evaluation purposes, that the screening object's deployability claim
   refers to operational deployment by audit-court analysts who would
   not undertake that request for routine screening, and that the same
   microdata access is what enables the Imhof-comparison numbers
   reported in §`sec:forensic`.
3. Update `sec_institutional_v16` L43–55 with one extra clause stating
   explicitly "the per-bid records do exist; they are not exported to
   the analytical-warehouse layer; the screening construct is
   architecturally designed for the layer that audit courts and
   competition authorities query directly."

This is a **floor**: even if all of Route 2's substantive moves are
abandoned, the disclosure cleanup must be done before submission. The
JLEO referee pool is small and overlaps with the Imhof/Huber/Wallimann
network; misreading risk is concrete.

## III. Front-by-front feasibility classification

### III.1. Front 1 — Sign-reversal decomposition

**Status:** ✓ Already executed in v16 (Subprompt 2). Memo
`SIGN_REVERSAL_DECOMPOSITION_v16.md` (130 lines), script
`scripts/59_sign_reversal_decomp.R`, six output CSVs, table
`tab_sign_reversal_decomp.tex`.

**What v16 already established:**

- The broad-sample $\widehat\beta = +0.064$ ($p=0.003$, $N=1{,}654{,}401$).
- The overlap subsample, *unweighted*, $\widehat\beta = +0.044$
  ($p=0.035$, $N=1{,}517{,}868$).
- The overlap subsample, ATT-weighted, $\widehat\beta = -0.097$
  ($p<10^{-9}$).
- The sign reversal is overwhelmingly an ATT-reweighting phenomenon,
  not an overlap-restriction phenomenon. The overlap restriction itself
  drops only 1.06% of treated items.
- Within the overlap sample, $\widehat\beta^{ov}$ is null among
  direct-CADE items ($-0.061$, $p=0.45$) and positive in the highest
  tender-value quartile ($+0.041$, $p=0.045$).
- Of six discriminating dimensions between dropped and surviving cells,
  one supports the screening reading (cobidder share concentrated in
  dropped cells, +6.1pp), two go the wrong way (convite share, PBU
  size), one is null, two characterize a thin-market story rather than
  a screening-deployment one.

**What Route 2 can add on top:**

- **Marginal upside.** The decomposition is already produced; what
  remains is wiring it into the manuscript and demoting the
  screening-value-as-diagnostic interpretation in the abstract / §1 /
  §7.2. This is line-edit work, not new computation.
- A minor extension is feasible: rerun the decomposition by year cohort
  and by item-group quintile, to test whether the ATT-weighted reversal
  is concentrated in time-periods or product-groups consistent with the
  cartel-adjudication portfolio. This would harden the "reweighting
  phenomenon" narrative against the alternative reading "reweighting is
  picking up something real about treated cells". Cost: 1 day. Upside:
  modest (already-computed ATT-weight-vs-not is the central result).

**Feasibility:** ✓ FULLY FEASIBLE. **Substantive upside left:**
**MEDIUM**. The intellectual work is done; the manuscript work is to
internalize the demotion that the memo prescribes and to surface the
factual three-step decomposition (broad → overlap-unweighted →
overlap-ATT-weighted) in the body and abstract.

### III.2. Front 2 — Theory–validation bridge

**Status:** ✓ Partially executed in v16 (Subprompt 3). Memo
`THEORY_VALIDATION_BRIDGE_v16.md` (111 lines), script
`scripts/60_theory_validation_bridge.R`, four output CSVs, table
`tab_theory_bridge.tex`.

**What v16 already established:**

- Within the always-loser stratum, cobidders match the cover-bidder type
  on 5 of 7 measured dimensions:
  - **P1 (deployment intensity):** mean 136.5 vs 76.7 participations,
    Cohen's $d = +0.67$.
  - **P1' (winners faced):** 24.8 vs 13.5, $d = +1.00$.
  - **P2 (deployment proximity):** 1.5% vs 0.2% direct-CADE share,
    $d = +0.46$.
  - **P3 (repeat co-bidding) count:** 2.28 vs 1.68, $d = +0.19$.
  - **P3' (repeat co-bidding share):** 21.6% vs 33.4%, $d = -0.38$
    (wrong direction — the v16 memo flags this).
  - **P4 (item-group HHI):** 0.380 vs 0.288, $d = +0.39$.
  - **P4' (distinct item-groups):** 7.6 vs 9.5, $d = -0.32$.

**What v16 explicitly says it cannot do** (memo §V Reservation 2,
manuscript `sec_limitations`):

> "Two predictions of the cover-bidder type — bid aggressiveness and
> bid dispersion — cannot be tested at the firm level given the data
> layer."

This is **factually incorrect.** The bid microdata are present in
`bid_level_full_v14.parquet` (see §II.2 above). For each cobidder firm
and each FL-non-cobidder firm we can compute:

- Mean and within-tender CV of `Valor Unitário Proposta`.
- Mean ratio `Valor Unitário Proposta / Valor Unitário Referência`
  (bid aggressiveness against reference price).
- Distribution of `(bid - winning_bid) / winning_bid` for losing bids
  (cover-bidding signature: positive and dispersed).
- Bid-sequence position in pregão (timestamp-ordered: do cover bidders
  bid early/late?).
- Within-bidder bid-price variance across tenders (cover-bidder profile:
  high cross-tender dispersion vs market firm: low).

**What Route 2 adds.** Extending the within-stratum bridge with three
bid-level dimensions converts P5 (bid aggressiveness) and P6 (bid
dispersion) from "untestable" to "tested". If cobidders show
systematically higher bid:reference ratios, higher within-tender CV,
and a more cover-bid-shaped sign of (bid − winner)/winner than FL
non-cobidders, the bridge tightens substantially and the "cobidder ≈
cover-bidder" claim moves from 5-of-7 dimensions on profile metrics to
7-of-9 dimensions including the two that the theory's primitive
mechanism actually predicts. This is the strongest substantive upside in
the entire Route 2 push.

**Effort:** ~1 day to extend `scripts/60_theory_validation_bridge.R`,
add bid-level joins on `Código Fornecedor`, compute the three new
metrics, regenerate `tab_theory_bridge.tex` with two extra rows.

**Risk:** the bid-level metrics may not move in the predicted
direction, in which case the bridge gets weaker, not stronger. This is
a real falsification risk and must be respected. If the bid-level
dimensions go the wrong way for cobidders, the v16 5-of-7 result is
already on the table and we report 5-of-9 instead — slightly weaker
bridge but not catastrophic. (If the 5-of-7 result were already weak,
this risk would matter more; given the existing $d$ magnitudes, the
risk is bounded.)

**Feasibility:** ✓ FULLY FEASIBLE. **Substantive upside left:**
**HIGH** — this is the front where v16 actively understated what the
data permit, and the upside flips a stated structural limitation into
an additional empirical fact.

## IV. Recommendation

**PROCEED WITH FULL ROUTE 2.**

Justification:

1. **The disclosure cleanup is mandatory regardless.** Even in a
   "do not proceed; further revision would be cosmetic" world, the
   v16 manuscript currently has factual risk on its abstract/intro/
   conclusion that a competent JLEO referee will catch in 30 minutes.
   The half-day cleanup is non-negotiable.

2. **Front 2 has high upside that v16 explicitly walked away from.**
   The v16 theory–bridge memo states bid-level cover-bidder predictions
   are not testable. The data show they are. Reversing this
   self-imposed limitation converts the bridge from "5-of-7 firm-level
   dimensions" to "5-to-7 of 9 dimensions including the theory's
   primitive mechanism", which is a meaningfully tighter bridge — and
   it is the dimension a top-field referee will press hardest on.
   ("Your model predicts cover bidders bid high and dispersed; you say
   you can't test that; can you?" Yes. We can.)

3. **Front 3 is half-wired and finishing it costs little.** The
   numbers exist; `sec_forensic` is already written. What is missing
   is operational language and one extra script-rerun. The
   regulatory-cost-frontier piece directly addresses the "operational
   metrics" question that the JLEO editorial map flagged.

4. **Front 1 is the lowest-upside front but the cheapest to integrate.**
   The decomposition memo is already in place; a 1-day pass turns it
   into manuscript prose with the right interpretive demotion.

5. **The hedge is intact.** v16 is byte-frozen at `work/v16-editor/`;
   its PDF is on disk; it can be deployed at any time. v17 carries the
   risk; if Route 2 over-extends or back-fires under further critique,
   we revert to v16 with no loss. The route's ceiling is high, the
   floor (mandatory disclosure cleanup) is positive, and the hedge cost
   is zero.

   don't collide with existing `\valImhof*`, `\valTheoryBridge*`. Plan:
   prefix with `\valBidBridge*` (P5/P6 metrics) and `\valArchOp*`

   v17-specific block in `paper_v17editor.tex` (no propagation). For

   latter for any value v17 introduces that v15/v16 should not change.
   Default rule: new constants → `values.tex`; new % wrappers and
   v17-specific reframings → v17 master.
