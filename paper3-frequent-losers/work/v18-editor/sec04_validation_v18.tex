\section{Validation Against Adjudicated Cartel Adjacency}
\label{sec:validation}

\subsection{A Conservative Adjudication Benchmark}
\label{sec:validation_conservative}

The cleanest validation uses only cartel cases already
adjudicated before the end of 2020. This leaves
$\valConservativeCases$ CADE cases, $\valConservativeFD$
firm-defendants active in BEC, and
$\valConservativeCobidders$ always-loser firms that bid
alongside at least one of those defendants. Of those cobidders,
$\valConservativeFL$ are frequent losers. Because both the
cartel finding and the defendant list precede the construction
of the screen, this benchmark avoids relying on later
adjudications to define the ground truth.

The main concern is mechanical: firms that participate often are
more likely to appear near anyone, including CADE defendants.
The benchmark therefore uses a participation-stratified
permutation rather than a raw co-bidding rate. Within quartiles
of participation volume, we randomly permute cobidder status
$\valPermPilot$ times and recompute the frequent-loser share
among the relabeled cobidders. The observed co-bidding rate is
$\valCADEenrich$, against a permutation mean of $\valCADEbase$,
an excess ratio of $\valMultThreeTwo\!\!\times$ with
$p<0.001$. The ROC AUC against the same conservative ground
truth is $\valAUCprePost$ (95\% DeLong CI
$\valAUCprePostCI$). The result is not that the screen proves
membership. It is that frequent losers are concentrated near
adjudicated cartel defendants at rates that participation volume
alone does not explain.

\subsection{Prospective Discrimination}
\label{sec:validation_temporal}

The broader CADE portfolio is more informative but less clean:
it includes cases adjudicated after the BEC sample closes. We
use it as a prospective benchmark. The question is whether
participation observed before the test window ranks cobidders
whose cartel adjacency is revealed only later by the
adjudication record. Computing the score from $2009$--$2016$
participation and evaluating it on $2017$--$2019$ labels gives
firm-level AUC $\valAUCFLfirmTemp$ (95\% CI
$\valAUCFLfirmTempCI$). In-sample AUC is higher,
$\valAUCFLfirm$, as expected; the holdout estimate is the better
reference for an enforcement screen that would be run before the
case file is complete.

\subsection{Participation Volume and Leakage}
\label{sec:validation_leakage}

The validation would be uninteresting if it only recovered its
own construction. We therefore separate the structural signal
from two mechanical components. The first is ordinary exposure:
active firms appear in more tenders and have more chances to
co-bid with any defendant. The stratified permutation above
handles that margin. The second is leakage in item-level
classification. If the same cobidder firms help construct the
participation score and define the label, in-sample item-level
AUC becomes almost tautological; the reference value is
$\valLeakRefAUC$.

Two audits reduce that concern. Holding cobidder firms out of
score construction within cross-validation folds leaves AUC
$\valLeakCVAUC$ (95\% CI $\valLeakCVAUCCI$). Computing the score
only on $2009$--$2016$ participation and predicting
$2017$--$2019$ items leaves AUC $\valAUCFLfirmTemp$ at the firm
level. The drop from the in-sample reference is real and should
be disclosed; it is the mechanical component. What remains is
the part that matters for the screening interpretation: the
participation primitive continues to rank cartel-adjacent
loser-side firms when the labels are held out of score
construction or placed out of time.

\subsection{The Direct-Defendant Asymmetry}
\label{sec:validation_direct}

The final validation check runs in the opposite direction. If
the award-layer statistic were a general cartel-membership
detector, it should also classify direct CADE defendants. It
does not. Against direct defendants in the broader BEC firm
universe, AUC is $\valAUCdirectStd$, essentially random. This is
the expected result for a loser-side screen. Direct defendants
are the firms named in the legal file and are often on the
winner side of the arrangement; the statistic is built from zero
wins and repeated loser-side participation. A screen designed to
rank that side of the bidding pool should not recover the
membership object symmetrically.

is recovered. They do not adjudicate membership, and they are not
asked to.
