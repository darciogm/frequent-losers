\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Brazilian electronic procurement platforms preserve contract-award
records in detail---winner identity, participant list, item identifier,
negotiated price---but the bid microdata that organize the empirical
bid-rigging literature since \citet{porter1993detection} are not
routinely audited at scale. Detecting institutional irregularities in
this data envelope requires constructs that operate on the
participation pattern alone. We document a regularity in São Paulo's
BEC platform that meets this description: $\valFL$ firms participate
in $\valThresholdStat$ or more tender-items between $\valYearStart$ and
$\valYearEnd$ without ever winning, in a population of
$\valAlwaysLosers$ always-losers and $\valBECfirms$ unique BEC
participants. We call the empirical object \emph{loser-side
concentration}: the persistent zero-win participation that
distinguishes a subset of always-loser firms from the rest.

\paragraph{The construct, theoretically derived.}
Under positive bid-preparation costs and persistent zero wins,
continued participation past any reasonable Bayesian threshold is
incompatible with expected-profit maximization
(\S\ref{sec:rationality} formalizes this calculation). Firms that
nevertheless persist past such a threshold do so for reasons
orthogonal to expected-profit competition---capacity transfer,
market intelligence, instrumental participation in coordinated
arrangements, or systematically misspecified priors. We do not
distinguish among these non-competitive readings; the construct
captures their shared empirical footprint. Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}
formalizes a two-type framework with cover bidders ($C$) and genuine
entrants ($G$). Two derivations make the empirical object
theoretically grounded rather than data-mined:
Proposition~\ref{prop:exposure_stmt} shows that
$\log(1{+}\text{tenders\_count})$ is a sufficient ranking statistic
for cover-bidder type given award-record data, under a Poisson
approximation to the cartel's deployment process; the posterior
$\Pr(\text{type}=C \mid t)$ is non-decreasing in $t$ by monotone
likelihood ratio. Lemma~\ref{lem:wins_zero_stmt} shows that the
bright-line filter $\text{wins}=0$ is the equilibrium choice of cover
bidders: winning either reveals capacity, undermining the cover
identity in subsequent rounds, or violates the cartel's allocation
rule and triggers internal sanctions. The joint statistic
$(\log(1{+}\text{tenders\_count}),\ \mathbf{1}[\text{wins}=0])$ is
therefore a sufficient screen for the cover-bidder type given the
data envelope. The binary $\valFL$ rule is the
information-coarsening of the latent posterior under the framework's
separating equilibrium.

\paragraph{Six complementary lines of empirical evidence.}
We test the framework's empirical content through six lines of
evidence, each separately verifiable and (within statistical noise)
robust to elimination of any other.

\emph{(L1) Conditional price premium.} FL-flagged environments
associate with $\valOLSGeneralPBU$ higher log negotiated unit prices in
within-item, within-year, within-PBU comparisons across $\valSampleN$
items. Confidence intervals are tight under item-clustered standard
errors. The association is not causal: matching diagnostics confirm
that the price gap does not survive overlap-strict reweighting
(Section~\ref{sec:robustness}, Table~\ref{tab:item_level_scope_match}).
We treat the price gap as a \emph{conditional} association.

\emph{(L2) Oversight-capacity gradient.} The price coefficient varies
by a factor of $\valPBUgradientRatio$ across quartiles of procuring-unit
size, monotonically: $\valOversightQone$ in the smallest quartile, falling
to $\valOversightQfour$ in the largest (Section~\ref{sec:robustness}).
PBU size is associated with several institutional features including monitoring capacity (smaller buyers have less
oversight infrastructure), and the gradient runs in the direction the
framework predicts: $\partial m^*/\partial \theta_k < 0$
(Proposition~\ref{prop:optimal_m}). The $12.5\!\!\times$ gradient is the
single largest source of heterogeneity in the data---larger than the
preg\~ao--convite gap, larger than the binary--continuous distinction.

\emph{(L3) Structural regime selection across modalities.} We
estimate competing cover-bid distributions on the FL-bid sample:
Regime~1 (uniform high-dispersion, evade-detection cover bidding) and
Regime~2 (truncated-normal low-dispersion, anchor-following cover
bidding). BIC selects Regime~2 over Regime~1 by large margins in
\emph{both} modalities ($\Delta\mathrm{BIC}=\valStructConvDeltaBIC$ in
convite, $\valStructPregDeltaBIC$ in pregão), with pregão exhibiting
sharper coordination ($\hat{\sigma}_c/\hat{\sigma}_g = \valStructPregRatio$
vs.\ $\valStructConvRatio$ in convite). The framework's Regime~2
prediction holds without modal heterogeneity in regime type.

\emph{(L4) Bayesian latent-class agreement.} A two-component
Gaussian mixture model on bid distributions classifies
$\valLCAcoverN$ firms as the cover-class type. The mean posterior
probability of cover-class membership is $\valLCAprobCoverFL$ for FL
firms versus $\valLCAprobCoverNonFL$ for non-FL---a $\valLCAratio\!\!\times$
ratio. Latent-class classification uses different identifying
assumptions from our construct (bid-distribution shape vs.\
participation-count signal), so this agreement is independent
evidence that the FL marker tracks coordination-related types,
without relying on enforcement labels.

\emph{(L5) Repeated FL--winner pairs above null.} Permutation
of FL labels within the always-loser pool ($1{,}000$ iterations,
uniform shuffle, script~\texttt{45\_legacy\_m1m3\_perm\_welfare.R}) yields a null
mean of $\valDyadicPairsPerm$ FL--winner pairs sharing $\geq 5$
tender-items; observed value is $\valDyadicPairsObs$, a $\valDyadicRatio\!\!\times$
ratio ($p < 0.001$). The top-10 pairs share $\valDyadicTopTenObs$
tender-items on average versus $\valDyadicTopTenPerm$ under permutation
($\valDyadicTopTenRatio\!\!\times$). Persistent FL--winner adjacencies are
informative even within the construct's own population.

\emph{(L6) CADE adjudication consistency check.} As one consistency
check among the lines above, we report that the FL marker
discriminates $\valCobidders$ adjudicated CADE-cobidder firms within
the always-loser stratum at AUC $\valAUCFLfirm$ ($95\%$ CI $\valAUCFLfirmCI$).
We do \emph{not} interpret this as
detection of cartelists in general: the FL marker does not
discriminate the $\valDirectCADE$ direct CADE defendants in the
broader BEC universe (AUC $\valAUCdirectStd$). Direct defendants in
adjudicated cartels were typically frequent winners, not losers; the
construct targets the loser-side of the bidding pool by design. The
within-stratum AUC is therefore informative about the construct's
behavior at the cobidder boundary, not about cartel detection scope.

\paragraph{What the evidence does and does not say.}
None of the six lines, taken alone, establishes a causal mechanism.
Two design-based identification strategies---a sharp RDD at the
convite/pregão statutory caps and a difference-in-differences using
the 2018 cap raise---both return null first stages and null effects
on FL prevalence under the available bandwidths and sample sizes
(Section~\ref{sec:robustness}). We treat the empirical content
descriptively. Mechanism heterogeneity is also not uniform: the
largest cell (Low HHI $\times$ Low pairs) carries a $+\valMechQLLLCoef\%$
premium consistent with the framework, but the small High HHI $\times$
High pairs cell returns $\valMechQLHHCoef\%$, an empirical tension we
acknowledge openly (Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}, ``Empirical tension
with A3''). We treat the empirical patterns as risk heterogeneity, not
as identification of a single mechanism.

\paragraph{Policy implications.}
We do not estimate causal welfare effects. As an illustrative
bound---explicitly conditional on the price gap reflecting the
cover-bidding mechanism the framework specifies, which our matching
diagnostics do not establish---counterfactual specifications in
Appendix~\ref{app:extensions} yield welfare gains in the range
$\valWelfareCrossfit$--$\valWelfareIV$ (approximately $\valWelfareCFOnePctLow$--$\valWelfareCFOnePctHigh$
of $\valWelfareDenom$ in BEC spending over the sample window) under
removal of the minimum-bidder rule.
These figures are illustrative bounds, not policy estimates;
oversight bodies considering the construct as a triage tool should
treat the price gap as conditional, not as overcharge.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
We make three contributions, of unequal weight. \emph{First}, we
introduce loser-side concentration as a theoretically derived
participation-only construct with formal equilibrium grounding
(Propositions~\ref{prop:exposure_stmt} and \ref{prop:market_selection},
Lemma~\ref{lem:wins_zero_stmt}, framework Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}).
\emph{Second}, we document six complementary lines of empirical evidence
consistent with the framework, each of which can be evaluated and
falsified separately. \emph{Third}, we connect the construct to the
institutional design of minimum-bidder rules in Brazilian procurement
law and translate it into an administrative pathway---Screen $\to$
Triage $\to$ Forensic---deployable in jurisdictions where bid microdata
are not centrally collected.

\paragraph{What we do not claim.}
The construct does not detect cartelist firms in general; the AUC
$\valAUCdirectStd$ against direct CADE defendants in the BEC universe is
the appropriate calibration of detection scope. The price gap is not
an overcharge estimate; matching diagnostics show it does not survive
overlap-strict reweighting. The framework is an organizing device for
interpreting the empirical patterns; it is not the source of causal
identification.
