\subsection{Additional Identification Evidence}
\label{sec:additional_id}

\paragraph{FL entry event study.}
Within-PBU event studies around first FL entry
(Figure~\ref{fig:fl_entry_es}, Appendix~\ref{app:robustness}) show
positive pre-event coefficients at $t = -2$ and $t = -3$: PBUs that
eventually receive FL entry \emph{already had higher prices}. The
pattern is consistent with strategic market selection: cartels pick
competitive, high-value markets---the pre-trend reflects that
selection---and then deploy cover bidders to sustain elevated prices.
Under this reading, the pre-trend is part of the mechanism rather
than a threat to it, and the item--year--PBU fixed effects in
Equation~\eqref{eq:ols_main} absorb the price \emph{level} in each
market.

The same pattern, however, is also consistent with unobserved market
characteristics that attract both FL firms and higher prices,
potentially inflating the OLS. Two pieces of evidence constrain the
selection channel: the reverse-causality test
(Section~\ref{sec:mechanisms}, M3) shows that the elasticity of FL
entry with respect to lagged prices is tiny ($\approx 0.004$), and
the sensitivity analysis ($RV = 17.5\%$) implies that a confounder
would need to explain a large share of residual variation in both
treatment and outcome to nullify the coefficient.

\paragraph{Minimum-bidder constraint variation.}
Convite's three-bidder minimum creates within-modality variation in
the incentive to deploy cover bidders. When $n_{\text{genuine}} < 3$,
the cartel \emph{must} deploy cover bidders to proceed (corner
solution); when $n \geq 3$, cover bidding is voluntary. This
institutional feature provides a quasi-experimental contrast.
Interacting FL with a constraint-binding indicator yields
$\hat{\beta}_{\text{FL}}(n \geq 3) = 0.076$ (SE~$= 0.021$,
$p < 0.001$)---a 7.6\% premium from \emph{voluntary} cover
bidding---and $\hat{\beta}_{\text{FL} \times (n<3)} = -0.160$
($p < 0.001$). The price effect concentrates where cover bidding is a
strategic choice, not where it is forced by the rules.

\paragraph{CADE enforcement shock.}
A Callaway--Sant'Anna DiD exploiting CADE conviction timing shows
that FL presence drops sharply after conviction (ATT~$= -0.275$,
$p < 0.001$): when the cartel is prosecuted, its cover bidders
disappear. The price ATT is positive but imprecise (0.145,
SE~$= 0.110$), consistent with the small number of CADE events
in BEC.
