\section{Supporting Diagnostics and Alternative Explanations}
\label{sec:mechanisms}

A price association alone does not establish the mechanism. This
section subjects the cover-bidding interpretation to five independent
diagnostics, each derived from a different model prediction. The
evidential weight comes not from any single test but from the joint
pattern: any alternative explanation must generate all five results
simultaneously (Table~\ref{tab:mechanisms}, Appendix~\ref{app:robustness}).

\paragraph{M1: Do FL firms crowd out genuine competitors?}
If FL firms displace real bidders, we should see fewer genuine
participants in FL-present tenders. Instead, we find $+0.143$ more
non-FL firms ($p < 0.01$): FL firms \emph{add to} rather than
substitute for genuine competition, as the model predicts
(Prediction~\ref{pred:selection_v6}).

\paragraph{M2: Are winning bids anchored to the reference price?}
BEC reference prices are publicly posted before bidding opens.
Regressing $\log(p_{\text{negot}} / p_{\text{ref}})$ on FL presence
yields $-0.041$ ($p < 0.01$): winners in FL-present tenders bid 4\%
closer to the reference price, consistent with calibrated cover
bidding where cover bids and the designated winner coordinate around
a public anchor \citep{marshall2012economics}.

\paragraph{M3: Do FL firms chase high prices?}
Regressing FL entry on lagged prices within market yields $0.0021$
(SE = 0.0008): a 10\% price increase raises FL entry by 0.02~pp
against a 4.8\% baseline---an order of magnitude too small to
generate a 6.4\% price gap through reverse causality alone.

\paragraph{Alternative explanations.}
FL firms are roughly one year younger than non-FL firms, consistent
with the shell-company hypothesis documented in prosecuted cartels
\citep{marshall2012economics}. The preg\~ao--convite asymmetry
(9.3\% vs.\ 3.8\%) fits the model: preg\~ao's real-time format
facilitates coordinated cover-bid placement, while convite's
minimum-bidder constraint forces some cover bidding regardless of
market conditions, diluting the FL signal. The convite interaction
analysis (Section~\ref{sec:additional_id}) sharpens this: the
voluntary deployment premium (7.6\%) exceeds the constraint-driven
component, isolating the price effect that arises from deliberate
cover-bidder entry.

\paragraph{Rationality of FL participation.}
\label{sec:rationality}
With zero wins, expected profit per bid is
$E[\pi] = 0 \times \bar{V} \times m - c = -c < 0$
for any bidding cost $c > 0$, where $\bar{V}$ is the mean
procurement-order value and $m$ the gross margin. BEC is electronic;
$c$ captures staff time for bid preparation and documentation
compliance (1--2 hours at R\$25--50/hour), yielding a conservative
range of $c \in [\text{R\$50}, \text{R\$500}]$. Near the threshold
(14 tenders), career losses range from R\$700 to R\$7,000---conceivably
explained by persistent bad luck or learning. At the 90th percentile
(50+ tenders), career losses reach R\$2,500--R\$25,000: a firm that
bids 50 times over a decade without winning a single tender spends at
minimum R\$2,500 in direct costs and substantially more in opportunity
cost. Under any standard competitive search model---including the most
generous interpretation where BEC is a low-cost discovery channel---the
firm should have exited after observing a posterior win probability
indistinguishable from zero.%
\footnote{A Bayesian learner with a uniform prior updates
$\Pr(\text{win}) = (0 + 1) / (n + 2)$ after $n$ zero-win trials.
After $n = 50$, $\Pr(\text{win}) = 0.019$. With $\bar{V} = \text{R\$}86{,}000$
(sample mean) and $m = 0.10$, expected gain per bid is
$0.019 \times 86{,}000 \times 0.10 = \text{R\$}163$---less than the
lowest bidding cost, so exit is rational even for the most optimistic
learner.}
Win rate zero in BEC does not mean zero everywhere---FL firms may
win federal or municipal tenders we do not observe---but 50+ zero-win
participations require explanation even for firms profitable outside
BEC \citep{asker2010leniency, marshall2012economics}
(Table~\ref{tab:rationality}).

\paragraph{M4: Dyadic linkage concentration.}
If FL firms are random losers, their co-bidding partners should be
no more concentrated than those of comparable always-losers. A
stratified permutation test (1,000 iterations, preserving
participation-count quartiles) compares the observed FL--winner pair
concentration against a null where FL labels are randomly reassigned
among the 16,843 always-losers. FL firms form 4,696 pairs with
$\geq$5 co-bids, versus a permuted mean of 3,271 ($p < 0.001$), and
their top-10 pairs average 129.7 co-occurrences versus 109.9
($p = 0.016$). The excess of high-frequency, stable FL--winner pairs
is what one expects from persistent cartel relationships with
designated cover bidders
(Table~\ref{tab:dyadic_permutation}, Appendix~\ref{app:robustness}).

\paragraph{M5: Firm exit in FL-exposed markets.}
A Cox proportional hazard model estimates whether genuine (non-FL)
firms in FL-heavy markets exit BEC faster. FL exposure---the fraction
of a firm's tenders with FL presence---is associated with a
\emph{lower} exit hazard (HR~$= 0.60$, $p < 0.01$) after controlling
for participation volume and win rate
(Table~\ref{tab:cox_survival}, Appendix~\ref{app:robustness}).
Firms in FL-exposed markets survive \emph{longer}, not shorter---the
opposite of crowding-out and consistent with a cartel ecosystem that
sustains genuine participants.%
\footnote{The PH assumption is rejected ($p < 0.001$); the hazard
ratio should be read as descriptive. ``Exit'' means cessation of BEC
participation, not firm closure.}
Together with M1 ($+0.143$ non-FL firms), the survival result
paints a coherent picture: cover bidders add noise, not competition,
so genuine firms have no reason to exit.

\paragraph{Strategic adaptation.}
If a cartel reads this paper, the obvious responses---rotating FL
firms, allowing occasional wins, staying below the IQR threshold---would blunt the screen. The threshold sensitivity already hints at
this: the coefficient weakens as the threshold rises. The zero-win
condition is doing heavy lifting; relaxing it to low-win-rate firms
attenuates the coefficient substantially
(Table~\ref{tab:fl_lowwinrate},
Appendix~\ref{app:online_robustness}). A competition authority deploying FL screening would need to update
the threshold periodically and combine it with bid-level
tools \citep{harrington2008detecting}---hence the three-stage workflow
proposed in Section~\ref{sec:conclusion}.

\paragraph{Joint assessment.}
The five diagnostics trace the outline of coordinated cover bidding
with increasing specificity: FL firms add to rather than displace
genuine bidders (M1), winning bids anchor to the reference price
(M2), reverse causality is an order of magnitude too weak to explain
the price gap (M3), FL--winner pairs recur far beyond chance (M4),
and genuine firms in FL-exposed markets survive \emph{longer}, not
shorter (M5). Each test, in isolation, admits alternative readings.
Taken together, they present a sharp challenge: what single
non-collusive story generates coordinated entry without displacement,
reference-price anchoring, persistent dyadic relationships, and an
ecosystem that sustains rather than crowds out genuine competitors?
We are not aware of one.
