\section{Supporting Diagnostics and Alternative Explanations}
\label{sec:mechanisms}

A price association alone does not establish the mechanism. This
section reports five diagnostics that assess the plausibility of the
cover-bidding interpretation and constrain the set of alternative
explanations. No single diagnostic is dispositive; the evidential
weight comes from the joint pattern
(Table~\ref{tab:mechanisms}, Appendix~\ref{app:robustness}).

\paragraph{M1: Do FL firms crowd out genuine competitors?}
If FL firms displace real bidders, we should see fewer genuine
participants in FL-present tenders. Instead, we find $+0.143$ more
non-FL firms ($p < 0.01$): FL firms \emph{add to} rather than
substitute for genuine competition, consistent with the framework
(Diagnostic~\ref{pred:selection_v6}).

\paragraph{M2: Are winning bids anchored to the reference price?}
BEC reference prices are publicly posted before bidding opens.
Regressing $\log(p_{\text{negot}} / p_{\text{ref}})$ on FL presence
yields $-0.041$ ($p < 0.01$): winners in FL-present tenders bid 4\%
closer to the reference price, consistent with calibrated cover
bidding where cover bids and the designated winner coordinate around
a public anchor \citep{marshall2012economics}.

\paragraph{M3: Do FL firms chase high prices?}
Regressing FL entry on lagged prices within market yields $0.0021$
(SE = 0.0008): a 10\% price increase raises FL entry by 0.02~pp
against a 4.8\% baseline. While this test cannot fully distinguish
reverse causality from strategic selection, the elasticity is small
relative to the 6.4\% price gap.

\paragraph{Alternative explanations.}
FL firms are roughly one year younger than non-FL firms, consistent
with the shell-company hypothesis documented in prosecuted cartels
\citep{marshall2012economics}. The preg\~ao--convite asymmetry
(9.3\% vs.\ 3.8\%) is consistent with the framework: preg\~ao's real-time format
facilitates coordinated cover-bid placement, while convite's
minimum-bidder constraint forces some cover bidding regardless of
market conditions, diluting the FL signal. The convite interaction
analysis (Section~\ref{sec:additional_id}) sharpens this: the
voluntary deployment premium (7.6\%) exceeds the constraint-driven
component, consistent with a price association that arises from voluntary
rather than constraint-driven FL participation.

\paragraph{Rationality of FL participation.}
\label{sec:rationality}
With zero wins, expected profit per bid is $-c < 0$ for any bidding
cost $c > 0$. At the FL threshold (14 tenders), career losses are
R\$700--7,000---conceivably bad luck. At the 90th percentile (50+
tenders), losses reach R\$2,500--25,000: a Bayesian learner updates
$\Pr(\text{win})$ to 0.019 after 50 zero-win trials, making exit
rational even under the most generous assumptions.%
\footnote{With $\bar{V} = \text{R\$}86{,}000$ and margin $= 0.10$,
expected gain is R\$163---below the lowest bidding cost (R\$50).
Win rate zero in BEC does not mean zero elsewhere, but 50+ zero-win
BEC participations require explanation even for firms profitable
outside the platform \citep{asker2010leniency, marshall2012economics}.}
Full rationality calculations are in Table~\ref{tab:rationality}
(Appendix~\ref{app:robustness}).

\paragraph{M4: Dyadic linkage concentration.}
A stratified permutation test (1,000 iterations, preserving
participation quartiles) shows FL firms form 4,696 high-frequency
co-bidding pairs ($\geq$5 shared tenders) versus a permuted mean of
3,271 ($p < 0.001$). The excess of stable FL--winner pairs is consistent with
persistent co-bidding relationships of the sort the framework
emphasizes
(Table~\ref{tab:dyadic_permutation}, Appendix~\ref{app:robustness}).

\paragraph{M5: Firm exit in FL-exposed markets.}
A Cox model shows FL exposure is associated with \emph{lower} exit
hazard (HR~$= 0.60$, $p < 0.01$): firms in FL-exposed markets survive
longer, the opposite of crowding-out.%
\footnote{The PH assumption is rejected ($p < 0.001$); the hazard
ratio is descriptive. ``Exit'' means cessation of BEC participation.}
Together with M1, the result is consistent with FL firms adding
noise rather than competition
(Table~\ref{tab:cox_survival}, Appendix~\ref{app:robustness}).

\paragraph{Strategic adaptation.}
Cartels could rotate FL firms or allow occasional wins to evade the
screen; the threshold sensitivity (Section~\ref{sec:robustness})
already hints at this. A deployed screen would need periodic threshold
updates combined with bid-level tools
\citep{harrington2008detecting}---hence the three-stage workflow in
Section~\ref{sec:conclusion}.

\paragraph{Joint assessment.}
Each diagnostic admits alternative readings in isolation. The joint
pattern---coordinated entry without displacement, reference-price
anchoring, negligible reverse causality, excess dyadic linkage, and
lower exit hazard---is coherent with coordinated cover bidding. We
present these as supporting evidence for the screen's economic
rationale, not as mechanism identification.
