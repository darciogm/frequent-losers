\title{Screening for Bid Rigging with Frequent Losers in Public Procurement\texorpdfstring{\footnote{This version: April 2026.}}{}}

\begin{abstract}
The most effective bid-rigging cartels are the hardest to detect:
when cover bidders coordinate their bids tightly around the
designated winner, the within-tender dispersion screens that
dominate the detection literature lose power. We develop a model
that formalizes this vulnerability and build a
participation-based screen immune to it: \emph{frequent losers}
(FL)---firms that never win yet participate in abnormally many
tenders. The screen requires only win/loss records, no bid
microdata. Applied to 4.5 million tender-items from Brazilian
public procurement (2009--2019), it achieves AUC~$= 0.94$ against
competition-authority convictions---outperforming both
machine-learning and structural classifiers trained on the same
ground truth. FL-flagged tenders show 3.6--7.7\% higher prices in
within-item comparisons, and FL firms co-participate with convicted
cartelists at 3.5$\times$ the baseline rate. Five independent
diagnostics produce a joint pattern difficult to generate without
coordinated entry: the structural model selects coordinated cover
bidding, Bajari--Ye tests reject bid exchangeability, the price
effect concentrates in competitive markets, FL--winner pairs recur
far beyond chance, and the premium is largest where cover bidding
is voluntary. The screen complements existing bid-level tools and
is deployable wherever participation records exist.
\end{abstract}
