\title{Screening for Bid Rigging with Frequent Losers\\in Public Procurement\thanks{This version: April 2026.}}

\author{Darcio Genicolo-Martins\thanks{Corresponding author. INSPER, S\~ao Paulo, Brazil. Email: darciogm1@insper.edu.br.} \and Paulo Furquim de Azevedo\thanks{INSPER, S\~ao Paulo, Brazil.}}

\date{}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
\noindent
Dispersion-based screens for bid rigging lose power against
coordinated cartels whose cover bids cluster near the winning
price. We propose a participation-based screen---\emph{frequent
losers} (FL), firms that never win yet bid abnormally often---that
requires only win/loss records. Applied to 4.5 million
tender-items in Brazilian procurement (2009--2019), the screen
achieves AUC~$= 0.94$ against competition-authority convictions
and complements bid-level screens that target different collusion
signatures. FL-flagged tenders show 3.6--7.7\% higher prices; structural
estimation reveals a \emph{dispersion paradox} ($\sigma_c/\sigma_g
= 0.72$) explaining why variance screens fail. Five diagnostics
jointly match coordinated cover bidding: regime selection,
Bajari--Ye tests, competitive-market concentration, dyadic
linkage, and voluntary-entry premium. The screen complements
bid-level tools and is deployable wherever participation records
exist.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} bid rigging, cover bidding, public procurement, cartel screening, frequent losers
