\title{Screening for Bid Rigging with Frequent Losers\\in Public Procurement\thanks{This version: April 2026.}}

\author{Darcio Genicolo-Martins\thanks{Corresponding author. INSPER, S\~ao Paulo, Brazil. Email: darciogm1@insper.edu.br.} \and Paulo Furquim de Azevedo\thanks{INSPER, S\~ao Paulo, Brazil.}}

\date{}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
\noindent
Detecting bid rigging requires triage tools that work with minimal
data; most existing screens demand granular bid amounts unavailable
in many procurement systems. We propose a participation-based
screen---\emph{frequent losers} (FL), firms that never win yet bid
abnormally often---requiring only win/loss records available in
virtually any procurement database. Applied to 4.5 million
tender-items in Brazilian procurement (2009--2019), the screen
achieves AUC~$= 0.94$ against competition-authority convictions
and complements bid-level screens that target different collusion
signatures. FL-flagged tenders show 3.6--7.7\% higher conditional
prices across four estimation approaches. Five diagnostics are
jointly coherent with coordinated cover bidding, strengthening the
case for screen deployment. The screen captures information nearly
orthogonal to bid-level tools (correlation 0.06) and is deployable
wherever participation records exist.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} bid rigging, cover bidding, public procurement, cartel screening, frequent losers
