\section{Empirical Strategy}
\label{sec:empirical}

The empirical analysis exploits two sources of institutional
variation to test the predictions in
Table~\ref{tab:predictions}: (i)~the convite--preg\~ao modality
split, which separates rule-bound from unconstrained deployment,
and (ii)~purchasing-unit size as a proxy for enforcement capacity.
We describe the baseline specification, the two heterogeneity
tests, and the threats to identification.

\subsection{Baseline Specification}
\label{sec:baseline}

The baseline regresses tender-item outcomes on FL presence:
\begin{equation}
  y_{igt} = \beta \cdot \text{losers}_{igt}
  + \delta \cdot \text{convite}_{igt}
  + \alpha_g + \lambda_t + \gamma_k + \varepsilon_{igt}
  \label{eq:baseline}
\end{equation}
where $y_{igt}$ is log negotiated price for tender-item $i$ in item
group $g$ at time $t$ and purchasing unit $k$;
$\text{losers}_{igt} \in \{0,1\}$ indicates FL presence;
$\text{convite}_{igt}$ is a modality indicator; $\alpha_g$,
$\lambda_t$, and $\gamma_k$ are item, year, and PBU fixed effects;
and $\varepsilon_{igt}$ is clustered at the item level.

The coefficient $\hat{\beta}$ is a conditional association, not a
causal effect. Item fixed effects absorb permanent cross-market
heterogeneity (more expensive product categories mechanically
attract different bidder pools); year and PBU effects absorb
aggregate price trends and procuring-unit characteristics. The
residual threat is time-varying, within-item confounders that
jointly attract FL firms and raise prices---for example, demand
shocks or supplier entry that simultaneously affect prices and
bidder composition.

We assess sensitivity using the \citet{cinelli2020making} framework.
The robustness value is $RV_{q=1} = 17.5\%$: an unobserved
confounder would need to explain at least 17.5\% of the residual
variation in both FL presence and log prices to drive $\hat{\beta}$
to zero---a high bar given the fixed-effects structure.

As a non-parametric check, we estimate the price effect using
coarsened exact matching on year, modality, item group, and PBU
size quartile (CEM: $\hat{\beta} = 0.077$) and inverse probability
weighting (IPW: $\hat{\beta} = 0.055$). Together with the OLS
baseline (0.064) and temporal cross-fitting (0.036), these four
estimators bracket the FL--price association in a 3.6--7.7\% range.%
\footnote{Full matching and cross-fitting results are in
\citet{genicolomartins2026screening}. We report the headline range
here and focus on the institutional heterogeneity.}

\subsection{Modality Split: Convite vs.\ Preg\~ao}
\label{sec:modality_split}

The central test in this paper estimates
Equation~\eqref{eq:baseline} separately by modality:
\begin{equation}
  y_{igt} = \beta_s \cdot \text{losers}_{igt}
  + \alpha_g + \lambda_t + \gamma_k + \varepsilon_{igt},
  \qquad s \in \{\text{preg\~ao}, \text{convite}\}
  \label{eq:modality_split}
\end{equation}
Prediction~P2 states that $\hat{\beta}_{\text{convite}} <
\hat{\beta}_{\text{preg\~ao}}$: the minimum-bidder rule forces
deployment in convite even where cover bidding would otherwise be
unprofitable, mixing mandatory (corner-solution) and voluntary
(interior-solution) deployments. The mandatory component adds noise
to the FL indicator without adding to prices, compressing the
coefficient toward zero. In preg\~ao, all deployment is voluntary,
so the coefficient captures only tenders where the cartel expects
cover bidding to be profitable.

We supplement the split with an interaction specification on the
full sample:
\begin{equation}
  y_{igt} = \beta_1 \cdot \text{losers}_{igt}
  + \beta_2 \cdot \text{losers}_{igt} \times \text{convite}_{igt}
  + \delta \cdot \text{convite}_{igt}
  + \alpha_g + \lambda_t + \gamma_k + \varepsilon_{igt}
  \label{eq:interaction}
\end{equation}
Under P2, $\hat{\beta}_2 < 0$: the convite interaction is
negative, indicating that the rule dilutes the FL signal.

\paragraph{Voluntary deployment premium.}
Within the convite sample, we can separate tenders where the
minimum-bidder rule binds ($n_{\text{genuine}} < 3$, requiring FL
deployment) from tenders where it does not ($n_{\text{genuine}} \geq
3$, FL deployment is voluntary). If the rule forces some
unprofitable deployments, the FL coefficient should be larger in
the non-binding subsample---the ``voluntary deployment premium.''
This within-convite comparison partially addresses the concern that
the modality split is confounded by differences in contract value
or item composition between convite and preg\~ao.

\subsection{Enforcement-Capacity Gradient}
\label{sec:oversight}

Prediction~P4 states that the FL price effect decreases in
enforcement capacity. We proxy enforcement capacity with annual
procurement volume by PBU: larger PBUs have dedicated procurement
staff, internal audit, and greater Tribunal de Contas visibility.
We split the sample into PBU-size quartiles and estimate
Equation~\eqref{eq:baseline} within each:
\begin{equation}
  y_{igt} = \beta_q \cdot \text{losers}_{igt}
  + \alpha_g + \lambda_t + \varepsilon_{igt},
  \qquad q \in \{Q1, Q2, Q3, Q4\}
  \label{eq:pbu_split}
\end{equation}
PBU fixed effects are absorbed within each quartile. The prediction
is that $\hat{\beta}_{Q1} > \hat{\beta}_{Q4}$: the FL--price
association is strongest where oversight is weakest.

\subsection{Threats to Identification}
\label{sec:threats}

Three threats deserve explicit discussion.

\paragraph{Selection into modality.}
Convite and preg\~ao differ in contract value, item composition,
and the population of eligible firms. The modality split is not
random assignment; it reflects legislative rules (value thresholds)
and procurement-officer discretion. We address this in two ways.
\emph{First}, item and year fixed effects absorb much of the
cross-modality heterogeneity: within a given product code and year,
the price comparison exploits variation in FL presence, not in
modality choice. \emph{Second}, the within-convite
voluntary-deployment test (Section~\ref{sec:modality_split})
provides a comparison that holds the modality constant.

\paragraph{Endogeneity of PBU size.}
PBU procurement volume may correlate with unobserved procurement
quality or local market structure. We do not claim that PBU size
causes differences in the FL--price association; the gradient is
descriptive. The prediction is that the pattern is \emph{monotone}
(decreasing in PBU size), which is harder to generate from
confounders that would need to vary monotonically with volume
while being orthogonal to the fixed-effects structure.

\paragraph{FL classification as treatment.}
The FL indicator is an imperfect proxy for cover-bidder presence.
Misclassification attenuates the OLS coefficient: some FL-flagged
firms are genuine losers, and some cover bidders escape the
threshold. The companion paper shows that a leave-one-out IV
(exploiting FL supply at other PBUs in the same product market)
yields a coefficient of 0.194---three times the OLS---consistent
with measurement-error attenuation. However, the IV has
exclusion-restriction concerns and we do not use it as a primary
estimate. The OLS coefficient is conservative: it understates the
association between cover-bidder presence and prices.
