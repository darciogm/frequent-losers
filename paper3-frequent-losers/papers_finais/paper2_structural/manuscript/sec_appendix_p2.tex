\section{Proofs of Propositions}\label{app:proofs}

\begin{proof}[\textbf{Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:optimal_m}}]
Relaxing the integer constraint, the FOC for $m$ is
$\partial \pi / \partial m = c_1 + \theta_k \phi_0$.
By A2, $\partial^2 \pi / \partial m^2 < 0$, so the SOC is satisfied
and $\tilde{m}$ is unique. When $\underline{n}(\tau) - n > \tilde{m}$,
$m^* = \underline{n}(\tau) - n$ (corner). Otherwise,
$m^* = \lfloor \tilde{m} \rfloor$.
Comparative statics by implicit differentiation:
$\partial m^* / \partial \theta_k =
-\phi_0 / (\partial^2 \pi / \partial m^2) < 0$;
$\partial m^* / \partial c_1 < 0$ by the same argument.
Property~(iii) follows from $\underline{n}(\text{convite}) = 3 >
0 = \underline{n}(\text{preg\~ao})$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[\textbf{Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:regime1}}]
Under information structure I1, the cover bidder knows only that
$b_\ell \in [b^*, b^* + \delta]$ (constraint~\eqref{eq:cover_above}
plus the upper bound from suspicion avoidance). She has no basis
for concentrating probability at any particular point in this
interval. The maximum-entropy distribution on a bounded interval
$[a, a + \delta]$ is the uniform $U[a, a + \delta]$
\citep[see, e.g.,][Ch.~12]{jaynes2003probability}. Therefore
$G_1 = U[b^*, b^* + \delta]$, with variance $\delta^2/12$.
Since $\delta$ reflects the cover bidder's uncertainty about
$b^*$, large uncertainty produces high dispersion:
$\sigma_{\text{cover}} > \sigma_{\text{genuine}}$ whenever
$\delta > \sqrt{12}\,\sigma_g$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[\textbf{Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:regime2}}]
Under information structure I2, the cover bidder targets
$b^* + \epsilon$ with execution noise $\eta \sim N(0, \sigma_c^2)$,
yielding $b_\ell = b^* + \epsilon + \eta$. The constraint
$b_\ell > b^*$ truncates the distribution from below at $b^*$.
The resulting distribution is
$b_\ell \mid b_\ell > b^* \sim
\text{TruncNormal}(b^* + \epsilon, \sigma_c^2, b^*, \infty)$.
For the dispersion result, note that the variance of a left-truncated
normal is strictly below the untruncated variance:
$\text{Var}(b_\ell \mid b_\ell > b^*) = \sigma_c^2[1 - \lambda(\alpha)
(\lambda(\alpha) - \alpha)]$ where $\alpha = -\epsilon/\sigma_c$
and $\lambda(\alpha) = \phi(\alpha)/[1 - \Phi(\alpha)]$ is the
inverse Mills ratio. Since $\epsilon > 0$ implies $\alpha < 0$,
the correction term is positive and the effective standard deviation
satisfies $\text{sd}(b_\ell \mid b_\ell > b^*) < \sigma_c$.
A fortiori, if $\sigma_c \leq \sigma_g$ (the coordinator's
information advantage reduces noise below the genuine level),
then $\text{sd}(b_\ell \mid b_\ell > b^*) < \sigma_g$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[\textbf{Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:market_selection}}]
From the FOC: $\partial \pi / \partial m = c_1 + \theta_k \phi_0$.
Implicit differentiation:
$\partial m^* / \partial \text{HHI} =
-(\partial^2 \pi / \partial m \, \partial \text{HHI}) /
(\partial^2 \pi / \partial m^2)$.
By A2, the denominator is negative; by~\eqref{eq:cross_deriv}, the
numerator is negative. Therefore $\partial m^* / \partial \text{HHI} < 0$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{IV Balance Tests}\label{app:iv_balance}
The leave-one-out IV ($F = 396$) exhibits statistically significant
but economically small imbalance across observables: FL-present
PBU-group-years differ from FL-absent ones in item composition
and PBU size (all differences $< 0.001$ in standardized units).
The imbalance is consistent with the model's market-selection
prediction (Proposition~\ref{prop:market_selection}) rather than
instrument invalidity, but precludes a causal interpretation of
the IV estimate. We treat the IV as a measurement-error diagnostic
(Section~\ref{sec:reduced_form}).

\subsection{Staggered DiD}\label{app:did}
A \citet{callaway2021difference} DiD and a stacked regression
\citep{cengiz2019effect} both produce insignificant ATTs
(C\&S: 0.014, SE $= 0.039$; stacked: $-0.006$, SE $= 0.014$).
The stacked 95\% CI ($-0.034$, $0.022$) excludes the OLS baseline
(0.064), consistent with failure of parallel trends---the pre-trend
coefficients at $t = -2$ and $t = -3$ confirm that FL entry is
endogenous to market conditions (Section~\ref{sec:results_additional}).
The DiD designs are invalid for this setting, reinforcing the
diagnostic interpretation of the price association.
