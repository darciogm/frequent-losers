\begin{abstract}
\noindent We develop a structural model of cover-bidder deployment and
estimate it on 40~million bids from Brazilian public procurement
(2009--2019), using an externally constructed frequent-loser (FL)
partition to separate suspected cover bids from genuine bids. The
model distinguishes two cover-bidding regimes. Under complementary
cover bidding, bids are loosely coordinated and disperse widely;
under coordinated cover bidding, bids cluster near a focal point and
disperse narrowly. BIC strongly favors the coordinated regime in this
setting ($\Delta$BIC~$= -91{,}473$), yielding a \emph{dispersion
paradox}: FL-labeled bids are 28\% \emph{less} dispersed than genuine
bids ($\hat{\sigma}_c / \hat{\sigma}_g = 0.72$), not more. Five
out-of-sample specification tests---including a novel Bajari--Ye
tender-FE reversal---are consistent with the model's predictions.
The calibrated complementarity elasticity ($\hat{\gamma} = 0.694$)
indicates that, conditional on the FL partition, cover-bidder
deployment scales with genuine competition. These results help
rationalize why participation-based screens (AUC~$= 0.94$ against
CADE convictions) outperform variance-based alternatives
(AUC~$= 0.79$) in this environment, and suggest that
high-dispersion screening assumptions may not hold in settings with
stronger bid coordination.
\end{abstract}
