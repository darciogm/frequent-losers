\begin{abstract}
\noindent How do bid-rigging cartels calibrate cover bids, and what does this
imply for detection? We develop and estimate a structural model of
cover-bidder deployment using 40 million bids from Brazilian public
procurement (2009--2019). The model distinguishes two regimes:
complementary cover bidding (high dispersion) and coordinated cover
bidding (low dispersion). BIC model selection strongly favors the
coordinated regime: cover bids cluster near the winning price
($\hat{\sigma}_c / \hat{\sigma}_g = 0.72$;
$\Delta$BIC~$= -91{,}473$). This \emph{dispersion paradox}---that
the dominant form of cover bidding reduces bid dispersion---implies
that variance-based screens are structurally misaligned with the
cartels they most need to detect. Five out-of-sample specification
tests, including a novel Bajari--Ye tender-FE reversal, confirm the
model's predictions. The structural mechanism also explains why a
simple participation-based screen (AUC~$= 0.94$ against CADE
convictions) outperforms variance-based alternatives
(AUC~$= 0.79$), and why minimum-bidder rules generate
institutional demand for cover bidders.
\end{abstract}
