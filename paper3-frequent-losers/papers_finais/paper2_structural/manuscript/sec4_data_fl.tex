\section{Data and Frequent-Loser Classification}
\label{sec:data}

\subsection{BEC Platform}
\label{sec:bec}

Our data come from the Bolsa Eletr\^onica de Compras (BEC), S\~ao
Paulo's centralized electronic procurement platform serving 1,308
purchasing units (PBUs) across state agencies, universities, hospitals,
and municipal bodies. BEC handles two modalities:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Convite} (sealed-bid): used for purchases up to
    R\$80,000, requires a minimum of three bidders (Lei~8.666/93,
    Art.~22, \S7). Bids are submitted simultaneously.
  \item \textbf{Preg\~ao} (electronic reverse auction): the default
    modality for standard goods and services. Bidders submit descending
    bids in real time; no minimum-bidder requirement.
\end{itemize}

The minimum-bidder rule for convite is central to the structural
model: if a cartel controls one genuine bidder, it needs at least two
cover bidders to meet the threshold
(Proposition~\ref{prop:optimal_m}, corner solution). Under preg\~ao,
cover bidders serve primarily to inflate apparent competition.

The sample spans 2009--2019: approximately 4.5 million tender-items,
40 million bids, 41,000 unique firms.

\subsection{Frequent-Loser Classification}
\label{sec:fl_definition}

We define frequent losers (FL) in two steps. First, \emph{always-losers}:
16,843 firms ($\sim$41\% of BEC participants) with a zero win rate
across all 2009--2019 tenders. Second, an IQR-based outlier rule:
among always-losers, those with participation counts exceeding
median + 1.5 $\times$ IQR ($\approx 14$ tenders) are classified as FL.
This yields 2,735 firms.

The classification, its validation against CADE convictions
(AUC~$= 0.94$), and a battery of definition-robustness checks are
developed in the companion paper
\citep{genicolomartins2026screening}. Here we take the FL labels
as given and use them as the mixture-model partition: FL bids enter
the cover-bid likelihood $g_{\text{cover}}$, and non-FL bids enter
the genuine-bid likelihood $f_{\text{genuine}}$
(Section~\ref{sec:likelihood}).

The treatment variable $\text{losers}_{igt}$ equals one if
tender-item $i$ in item group $g$ at time $t$ has at least one FL
participant, and zero otherwise. FL firms participate in 4.8\% of
tender-items.

\subsection{Estimation Samples}
\label{sec:samples}

\paragraph{Reduced-form sample.}
We restrict to convite and preg\~ao modalities, tender-items with a
recorded winner, and item types with at least one FL-present tender.
The resulting sample contains 1,654,447 tender-items across 18,783
item types and 1,308 PBUs.

\paragraph{Structural estimation sample.}
The mixture model requires bid-level data with firm covariates. After
restricting to losing bids with valid prices and item--year
fixed-effect coverage, the sample comprises 23.2~million genuine
(non-FL) losing bids and 189,381 FL losing bids. For each bid we
observe the bid amount, the winning (negotiated) price, firm identity
(CNPJ), and firm characteristics (size, CNAE sector, age).

At the bid level, FL losing bids exhibit a mean log-spread above the
winning price of 0.83 (SD $= 1.19$), compared to a genuine-bid
residual standard deviation of 1.64---a preview of the Regime~2
finding ($\hat{\sigma}_c / \hat{\sigma}_g = 0.72$;
Section~\ref{sec:results}).

\paragraph{CADE validation sample.}
\label{sec:cade_sample}
To prevent information leakage, we classify FL using 2009--2014 data
and validate against 2015--2019 CADE enforcement outcomes. Among
the 2,735 FL firms, 193 (7.1\%) co-participate with at least one
CADE-convicted cartelist---3.5 times the baseline rate within
participation-intensity strata ($p < 0.001$, stratified permutation
test). Full details of the CADE validation appear in
\citet{genicolomartins2026screening}; we summarize the key results in
Section~\ref{sec:results} when discussing detection performance.
