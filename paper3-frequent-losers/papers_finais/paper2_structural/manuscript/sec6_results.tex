% ── 6.1 Structural Estimation ─────────────────────────────────────

\subsection{Structural Estimates: The Dispersion Paradox Confirmed}
\label{sec:results_structural}

Table~\ref{tab:structural_params} reports the two-stage mixture-model
estimates.

\paragraph{Genuine bid distribution (Panel~A).}
The genuine bid parameters, estimated from 23.2~million non-FL losing
bids with item and year fixed effects, yield $\hat{\sigma}_g = 1.64$
(bootstrap SE~$= 0.003$). The first-stage $R^2$ indicates that
product-year fixed effects absorb a large share of bid-level
variation; the residual dispersion reflects firm-level cost
heterogeneity.

\paragraph{Regime selection (Panels~B--C).}
BIC selects Regime~2 (coordinated cover bidding) with
$\Delta$BIC~$= -91{,}473$---overwhelming evidence against Regime~1.
Under Regime~2, FL bids are drawn from a log-normal distribution
centered at $\hat{\epsilon} = 0.83$ log points above the winning bid,
with $\hat{\sigma}_c = 1.19$ (SE~$= 0.012$). The
Kolmogorov--Smirnov distance is 0.24 under Regime~2 versus 0.64
under Regime~1.

The key result is the dispersion ratio:
$\hat{\sigma}_c / \hat{\sigma}_g = 1.19 / 1.64 = 0.72$ (bootstrap
95\% CI: [0.70, 0.74]; 1,000 replications resampling tenders). The
confidence interval lies entirely below one, confirming that the
dispersion paradox is not a sampling artifact. Cover bids
are 28\% \emph{less} dispersed than genuine bids---confirming
Prediction~\ref{pred:regime} under Regime~2. Coordinated cover
bidders cluster near a focal point ($b^* + \epsilon$) with imperfect
but substantial precision. The ratio is below one but not near zero,
indicating general instructions (``bid around 80\% above the
winner'') rather than exact bid levels.

\paragraph{Goodness of fit.}
Figure~\ref{fig:qqplot} presents a QQ-plot of FL bid log-spreads
against the fitted truncated normal. The fit is good in the central
mass (10th--90th percentiles) but deviates in the right tail: the
empirical distribution has heavier tails than the fitted normal,
consistent with a small fraction of FL bids being submitted with less
precise instructions (a Regime~1 component within the predominantly
Regime~2 population). The KS distance of 0.24 reflects this
tail behavior; a mixture of 90\% truncated normal and 10\% uniform
reduces the KS to 0.09. The dispersion ratio $\hat{\sigma}_c /
\hat{\sigma}_g$ is robust to this tail heterogeneity because it is
driven by the central mass, not the tails.

\includegraphics[width=0.78\textwidth]{images/fig_qqplot_regime2.pdf}

\paragraph{Misclassification bounds (Panel~D).}
The FL label is a statistical rule that necessarily involves
misclassification. The unsupervised latent class model
(Section~\ref{sec:robustness}) provides an independent estimate of
the contamination rate: among FL-labeled firms, 71.2\% fall in the
``cover-bidder'' class and 28.8\% in the ``genuine'' class, implying
$\hat{\alpha} \approx 0.29$. Under linear contamination,
$\hat{\epsilon}_{\text{corrected}} = \hat{\epsilon}_{\text{noisy}} /
(1 - \hat{\alpha}) = 0.83 / 0.71 = 1.17$ log points, and
$\hat{\sigma}_c / \hat{\sigma}_g$ corrects downward (to
$\approx 0.61$), \emph{strengthening} the dispersion paradox:
the true cover-bid distribution is even more compressed than the
observed one. These bounds are conservative: they assume the
``genuine'' class firms in the FL group are drawn entirely from
$F_{\text{genuine}}$, ignoring the possibility that some are
cover bidders misclassified by the LCA.

\paragraph{Implied price association (Panel~E).}
Conditioning on the number of genuine bidders $n$
(saturated dummies), the $n$-conditional price coefficient is
$\hat{\mu} = 0.062$ (SE~$= 0.019$, 6.4\%)---virtually identical to
unconditional OLS (0.064). The structural parameters characterize the
cover-bid \emph{mechanism}; the price association comes from the
reduced-form comparison, disciplined by the model. That the
$n$-conditional and unconditional estimates coincide indicates that
differential genuine competition does not confound the FL--price
association.

\subsection{Calibrated Cartel Primitives}
\label{sec:results_calibration}

\input{tables/tab_cartel_primitives}

Table~\ref{tab:cartel_primitives} reports the
minimum-distance estimates of the cartel's decision parameters
(Section~\ref{sec:calibration}). The competition elasticity
$\hat{\gamma} = 0.694$ (SE~$= 0.026$) is precisely estimated
and positive, confirming the strategic complementarity predicted
by Proposition~\ref{prop:market_selection}: each 1\% increase in
genuine bidders is associated with a 0.69\% increase in
cover-bidder deployment. Cartels scale synthetic competition nearly
proportionally to real competition. This relationship survives PBU
fixed effects (within-PBU estimate: $+0.29$), ruling out
cross-sectional confounding by market size.
The per-bidder cost $\hat{c}_1 = 0.238$ is well identified
(CI~$[0.177, 0.306]$). The detection-penalty parameter
$\hat{\phi}_0 = 0.019$ is economically negligible and
statistically indistinguishable from zero (CI includes zero):
we cannot reject that enforcement variation across PBUs has no
effect on cover-bidder deployment. The concentrated-NLS estimate
$\psi^* = 0.1$ reinforces this finding---enforcement costs are
effectively constant across PBU sizes.

\paragraph{Out-of-sample validation.}
We assess predictive power via repeated split-sample validation:
the model is estimated on a random two-thirds of PBUs and used to
predict $\log \bar{m}$ on the holdout third. Across 100 random
splits, the median out-of-sample $R^2$ is 0.561
(IQR~$[0.537, 0.588]$), with a 36\% RMSE improvement over the
naive mean-only predictor. The model's out-of-sample fit is driven
almost entirely by the complementarity elasticity $\hat{\gamma}$:
knowing a PBU's average genuine-bidder count explains over half the
cross-PBU variation in cover-bidder deployment, even out of
sample.\footnote{We initially explored an overidentification test
based on corner-solution convite tenders (where the 3-bidder
minimum binds). However, convite and preg\~ao differ in multiple
dimensions beyond the bidder floor---contract size, electronic
participation, item composition---making the comparison confounded.
Within convite, FL count is approximately 1 regardless of
$n_{\text{genuine}}$, providing insufficient variation for a clean
test.}

% ── 6.3 Specification Tests ───────────────────────────────────────

\subsection{Out-of-Sample Specification Tests}
\label{sec:spec_tests}

The model's empirical bite comes from predictions on moments not used
in estimation. All five go in the predicted direction.

\paragraph{Convite--preg\~ao asymmetry
(Proposition~\ref{prop:optimal_m}(iii)).}
FL deployment differs sharply across modalities: convite tenders
have $\bar{m} \approx 1$ regardless of $n_{\text{genuine}}$, while
preg\~ao tenders show $\bar{m}$ ranging from 1.3 (at $n = 1$) to
3.5 (at $n \geq 5$). This gap persists even when the 3-bidder floor
is irrelevant ($n \geq 3$), indicating that modality affects
cover-bidder deployment through channels beyond the minimum-bidder
rule---likely the lower participation costs and broader reach of
electronic auctions. Within convite, FL participation appears to be
a minimal-effort response (one cover bidder per tender), consistent
with the model's prediction that the marginal return to cover bidding
is lower in sealed-bid formats.

\paragraph{Within-tender dispersion (Prediction~\ref{pred:regime}).}
In 29,398 tenders with both FL and non-FL bidders, FL bid CV is 0.57
versus non-FL 1.65; FL bids are less dispersed in 82.6\% of tenders
(paired $t = -104$, $p < 0.001$). This tender-level validation
confirms the distributional finding ($\hat{\sigma}_c < \hat{\sigma}_g$)
at the individual-auction level.

\paragraph{$m$--$\theta$ comparative static
(Proposition~\ref{prop:optimal_m}).}
Mean FL per tender decreases monotonically from Q1 (0.41) to Q4
(0.31) by PBU size, consistent with $\partial m^* / \partial
\theta_k < 0$: larger PBUs with more oversight capacity attract
fewer cover bidders.

\paragraph{Bajari--Ye exchangeability and the tender-FE reversal
(Prediction~\ref{pred:exchange}).}
FL bid residuals violate exchangeability (KS~$D = 0.15$,
$p < 0.001$) and exhibit excess pairwise correlation
(product~$= 4.28$, $t = 81.0$)---standard Bajari--Ye evidence of
non-competitive bidding. The novel test is the \emph{tender-FE
variant}, which the structural model predicts should reverse the
sign of the FL correlation.

The logic is as follows. Under Regime~2, each cover bid contains a
shared focal-point component: $b_\ell = (b^* + \epsilon) + \eta_\ell$,
where $\epsilon$ is the cartel's target spread above the winning price
and $\eta_\ell$ is idiosyncratic execution noise. In the baseline
specification (without tender FE), the pairwise product of FL
residuals is positive because $b^* + \epsilon$ is common to all
cover bidders in a tender, generating mechanical correlation. Under
tender FE, the tender mean absorbs this common component, leaving
only $\eta_\ell$---the execution noise, which under Regime~2 has
lower variance than genuine-bid residuals. The FL pairwise product
should therefore \emph{drop below} the non-FL product.

This is exactly what we observe: the FL product drops from 5.16
(baseline) to 0.38 (tender FE)---\emph{below} the non-FL product
(0.86; Table~\ref{tab:bajari_ye_corrected},
Appendix~\ref{app:robustness}). A fake-groups placebo (randomly
assigning ``FL'' labels to non-FL firms) shows no such reversal
(product~$= 0.83$ under tender FE), confirming that the pattern is
specific to FL firms.

This test is the strongest individual piece of evidence for the
structural model: it is a novel, directional prediction that only
Regime~2 generates, tested on moments orthogonal to the estimation
sample.

\paragraph{Market-selection gradient (Prediction~\ref{pred:market}).}
The model predicts that cover bidding concentrates in competitive
markets (Proposition~\ref{prop:market_selection}). We test this by
splitting FL firms by the winner-HHI of their tender portfolio
(Section~\ref{sec:empirical_network}). The FL--price association
is 0.126 ($p < 0.01$) for competitive-market FL firms (below-median
HHI) and statistically zero ($-0.018$, $p > 0.3$) for
concentrated-market FL firms---a 7:1 ratio.

The gradient is monotonic across HHI quartiles: the FL coefficient
decreases from Q1 (most competitive) through Q4 (most concentrated),
consistent with the model's comparative static
$\partial m^* / \partial \text{HHI} < 0$. No alternative explanation
easily generates this pattern: if FL firms were simply
low-quality bidders gravitating toward large markets, the price
association would not concentrate in competitive environments where
pricing power is lowest.

% ── 6.3 Detection Implication ─────────────────────────────────────

\subsection{Detection Implication of the Dispersion Paradox}
\label{sec:results_detection}

The structural finding has a direct detection consequence: if
Regime~2 dominates, screens that condition on bid dispersion should
underperform screens that condition on participation. We test this
using CADE convictions as ground truth (temporal split:
Section~\ref{sec:cade_sample}).

\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{lccc}
\toprule
Classifier & AUC & DeLong $p$ vs.\ IQR & Regime detected \\
\midrule
IQR rule (participation) & 0.94 & --- & 1 and 2 \\
Imhof CV composite (dispersion) & 0.79 & $< 0.001$ & 1 only \\
Random forest (7 bid features) & 0.90 & 0.04 & mixed \\
Structural LR score & 0.56 & $< 0.001$ & neither \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The participation-based IQR rule dominates all alternatives
(Figure~\ref{fig:roc}). The model explains the ranking: under
Regime~2, cover bids mimic genuine dispersion
($\hat{\sigma}_c / \hat{\sigma}_g = 0.72$), rendering bid-level
statistics uninformative; but the participation footprint---never
winning yet bidding repeatedly---is invariant to the bid
distribution. The structural LR score's near-random performance
(0.56) confirms that the mixture parameters identify the
\emph{regime}, not individual firm status: detection and mechanism
identification are complementary tasks, not substitutes.
The Youden-optimal threshold ($1.45\times$IQR) nearly coincides
with the baseline rule ($1.5\times$), providing an independent
validation of the statistical cutoff. Full ROC curves, threshold
sensitivity, and the Imhof comparison appear in
Appendix~\ref{app:detection}.

% ── 6.4 Reduced-Form Evidence ─────────────────────────────────────

\subsection{Reduced-Form Price Associations}
\label{sec:results_ols}

We now test the model's price and selection predictions using
reduced-form regressions. These estimates are conditional
associations---not causal effects---that serve as specification tests:
the structural model predicts their sign, relative magnitude across
modalities, and response to controls.

Table~\ref{tab:prices} reports OLS estimates of
Equation~\eqref{eq:ols_main} (Prediction~\ref{pred:price}). FL
presence is associated with 6.8\% higher prices in the general
specification, 6.4\% with PBU FE, 9.3\% for preg\~ao, and 3.8\%
for convite.

\paragraph{Reconciling the preg\~ao--convite asymmetry.}
At first glance, the larger preg\~ao coefficient appears to
contradict Proposition~\ref{prop:optimal_m}(iii), which predicts
weakly more cover bidders under convite. But more cover bidders does
not imply a larger price association, for two reinforcing reasons.
\emph{First}, under convite, 39.2\% of FL-present tenders are corner
solutions where the minimum-bidder constraint forces deployment
regardless of profitability; in these tenders, cover bidding serves
to meet the regulatory threshold rather than to sustain prices,
diluting the average FL--price association.
\emph{Second}, preg\~ao's real-time format enables Regime~2
coordination (Section~\ref{sec:cover_distributions}): the
coordinator observes auction dynamics and calibrates cover bids
precisely, making each cover bidder more effective at sustaining
the winning price. Under convite (Regime~1), cover bids are
dispersed and less effective per bidder.
The structural model thus predicts a \emph{larger quantity} of
cover bidders under convite but a \emph{larger per-bidder
effectiveness} under preg\~ao. The net price association is
an empirical question; the data show that preg\~ao's coordination
advantage dominates convite's quantity advantage.

\input{./output/tables/tab_prices}

Cross-fitting yields $\hat{\beta}_{\text{CF}} = 0.036$ (3.6\%);
CEM yields 0.077; IPW yields 0.055. The IV (0.194, $F = 396$)
provides an upper bound consistent with measurement-error
attenuation, though balance-test violations preclude a causal
interpretation (Appendix~\ref{app:robustness}). Six estimators
converge in a 3.6--8.0\% range (IV excluded), with the cross-fit
as the conservative lower bound and CEM as the upper. The stability
across specifications is consistent with the model's prediction but
does not identify a causal price effect.

FL-present tenders have 0.19 log points \emph{more} non-FL firms
(Prediction~\ref{pred:selection}; PBU FE), contradicting
crowding-out and consistent with the model's strategic-selection
prediction: cartels deploy cover bidders on top of genuine
competition, not as substitutes.

% ── 6.5 Additional Identification Evidence ─────────────────────────

\subsection{Event Study and Minimum-Bidder Constraint}
\label{sec:results_additional}

\paragraph{Event study.}
Within-PBU event studies around first FL entry show positive
pre-event coefficients at $t = -2$ and $t = -3$
(Figure~\ref{fig:fl_entry_es}), indicating strategic selection: FL
firms enter markets that \emph{already} have higher prices. This
precludes a causal interpretation of the FL--price association but
is consistent with the model's market-selection prediction
(Proposition~\ref{prop:market_selection}): cartels select into
markets that already have favorable conditions.

\caption{Event study: log prices around first FL entry. Pre-event

\paragraph{Minimum-bidder constraint interaction.}
Within convite, interacting FL with a constraint-binding indicator
($n_{\text{genuine}} < 3$) yields a \emph{voluntary} deployment
premium of 7.6\% ($p < 0.001$) when $n \geq 3$, compared with a
negative constraint interaction ($-16.0\%$, $p < 0.001$). The price
effect concentrates in voluntary deployments, consistent with the
model's interior-solution prediction: the cartel deploys cover
bidders because they are \emph{profitable}, not because they are
forced.
