\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This paper proposes a structural interpretation of a low-dispersion
pattern in cover bidding. Conditional on an externally constructed
FL partition, a coordinated cover-bidding regime fits FL-labeled bid
data much better than the complementary alternative in Brazilian
electronic procurement ($\Delta$BIC~$= -91{,}473$;
$\hat{\sigma}_c / \hat{\sigma}_g = 0.72$). Five out-of-sample moments are directionally consistent with the
model, with varying degrees of discriminating power. The
Bajari--Ye tender-FE reversal---where the FL pairwise product
flips sign once the shared focal-point component is absorbed---is
the most discriminating of these tests.

This interpretation helps rationalize the observed performance gap
between participation-based screens (AUC~$= 0.94$) and
variance-based alternatives (AUC~$= 0.79$) in this setting: under
the coordinated regime, bid-level statistics are uninformative,
while the participation footprint---never winning yet bidding
repeatedly---is invariant to the bid distribution. The illustrative
welfare counterfactuals suggest that convite minimum-bidder rules may
have facilitated cover-bidder deployment; Brazil's Lei~14.133/2021
has since abolished convite (Art.~28), moving policy in a direction
consistent with this implication.

\paragraph{Scope and boundaries.}
The paper does not identify true cartel membership, causal price
effects, or welfare losses. The FL labels are a statistical partition,
not forensic ground truth; the structural model characterizes
bid-generating processes conditional on that partition. The price
association (3.6--8.0\% across five estimators) reflects both
treatment and selection. All results come from a single electronic
platform (BEC/S\~ao Paulo); the coordinated regime requires
real-time information exchange, and in sealed-bid settings the
complementary regime may prevail---in which case high-dispersion
screens may perform adequately. The detection-penalty
$\hat{\phi}_0$ is indistinguishable from zero, limiting enforcement
counterfactuals. The main results are robust to threshold variation,
alternative clustering, latent-class validation, and placebo tests
(Section~\ref{sec:robustness}). Replication on platforms with
different institutional designs is essential before broader claims
are warranted.

\paragraph{Implications for detection methodology.}
The dispersion pattern documented here suggests caution about
screening approaches that assume cover bidding increases bid
dispersion. In settings with strong bid coordination,
participation-based screens---which detect the operational
footprint of cover bidding regardless of bid calibration---may be
more robust. Whether this lesson generalizes beyond BEC/S\~ao Paulo
is an open question.

\paragraph{Future research.}
Three directions merit investigation: (i)~replication in other
procurement systems, especially those with sealed-bid formats, to
test whether the dispersion pattern holds more broadly;
(ii)~dynamic models of cartel--authority interaction, where cartels
adapt to screening by rotating cover bidders; and (iii)~field
experiments in which procurement agencies deploy FL screening and
measure deterrence effects.
