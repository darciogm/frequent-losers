\section{Data and Frequent Losers Definition}
\label{sec:data}

\subsection{BEC Platform}

Our data come from the Bolsa Eletr\^onica de Compras (BEC), S\~ao
Paulo's centralized electronic procurement platform serving 1,308
purchasing units (PBUs) across state agencies, universities, hospitals,
and municipal bodies. BEC handles two modalities:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Convite} (sealed-bid): used for purchases up to
    R\$80,000, requires a minimum of three bidders (Lei~8.666/93,
    Art.~22, \S7). Bids are submitted simultaneously and opened together.
  \item \textbf{Preg\~ao} (electronic reverse auction): the default
    modality for standard goods and services. Bidders submit descending
    bids in real time; there is no minimum-bidder requirement.
\end{itemize}

The minimum-bidder rule for convite creates a direct incentive for cover
bidding: if a cartel controls one genuine bidder, it needs at least two
cover bidders to meet the threshold. Under preg\~ao, cover bidders serve
primarily to inflate apparent competition and discourage genuine entry.

Our sample spans 2009--2019 and contains approximately 4.5 million
tender-items, 40 million bids, 41,000 unique firms, and 1,308 PBUs.
Table~\ref{tab:modality_year} shows the evolution of procurement volume
by modality over time.

\subsection{Frequent Losers Definition}

We define frequent losers (FL) in two steps:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Always-losers}: firms with a zero win rate across all
    2009--2019 tenders. 16,843 firms ($\sim$41\% of all
    BEC participants) never win a single tender.
  \item \textbf{IQR threshold}: among always-losers, we identify those
    with abnormally high participation counts using a median + 1.5
    $\times$ IQR threshold on the distribution of tender participations.%
    \footnote{We use median + 1.5 $\times$ IQR rather than the standard
    Tukey rule (Q3 + 1.5 $\times$ IQR) because the participation
    distribution is highly right-skewed. The median-based threshold
    yields a cutoff of approximately 14 participations and identifies
    2,735 FL firms. Robustness to the multiplier choice is reported in
    Section~\ref{sec:robustness}.}
    This yields approximately 2,735 FL firms.
\end{enumerate}

The treatment variable $\text{losers}_{igt}$ equals one if tender-item
$i$ in item group $g$ at time $t$ has at least one FL firm among its
participants, and zero otherwise.

Figures~\ref{fig:losses} and~\ref{fig:iqr} show the participation
distribution of always-losers, with the IQR threshold indicated. The
distribution is heavily right-skewed: most always-losers participate in
fewer than 10 tenders, while FL firms participate in 15 to over 400.

\subsection{Sample Construction}

We restrict the analysis sample as follows: (i) convite and preg\~ao
modalities only (phases 2 and 3); (ii) tender-items with a recorded
winner; (iii) item types that have at least one tender with FL
participation.%
\footnote{Restriction (iii) ensures that control tenders come from item
markets where FL firms could plausibly participate. We report results
without this restriction in Section~\ref{sec:robustness} and
Appendix~\ref{app:robustness}.}
The resulting sample contains 1,654,447 tender-items across 18,783
item types and 1,308 PBUs. FL firms participate in 4.8\% of
tender-items (79,456 tender-items).
