\section{Robustness and Additional Tests}
\label{sec:robustness}

This section presents a battery of robustness checks and extensions that strengthen the causal interpretation of our main findings. We address concerns about the sensitivity of the frequent loser definition, the identification strategy, and the treatment variable, and provide additional evidence from new dependent variables and matching estimators.

\subsection{Sensitivity to the IQR Threshold}

Our baseline definition of frequent losers uses the standard 1.5$\times$ IQR threshold. Table~\ref{tab:threshold_robustness} shows that the main price result is robust to alternative multipliers (1.0$\times$, 2.0$\times$, 3.0$\times$). Figure~\ref{fig:threshold_stability} plots the coefficient on \textit{losers} across thresholds, demonstrating stability.

\subsection{Winsorization}

To ensure that outlier prices do not drive the results, we winsorize the log negotiated price at the 1st and 99th percentiles and re-estimate the main specification. The coefficient on \textit{losers} remains virtually unchanged.

\subsection{Coefficient Sensitivity (Sensemakr)}

Following \citet{cinelli2020making}, we assess how strong selection on unobservables would need to be---relative to selection on observables---to explain away our main result. Figure~\ref{fig:sensitivity_contour} plots the sensitivity contour. The robustness value $RV_{q=1}$ indicates the minimum confounding strength needed to reduce the coefficient to zero.

\subsection{Placebo Test}

As a falsification exercise, we randomly permute the \textit{losers} indicator within item$\times$year cells and re-estimate the main specification. The placebo coefficient is economically small and statistically insignificant, confirming that the baseline result is not an artifact of the sample structure.

\subsection{Ground-Truth Validation: CADE Cartel Convictions}
\label{sec:cade_validation}

To externally validate the frequent loser screen, we cross-reference our results with ground-truth data on cartel convictions from CADE (Conselho Administrativo de Defesa Econ\^{o}mica), Brazil's competition authority. We compiled all cartel convictions involving public procurement (licita\c{c}\~{a}o) judged between 2009 and 2025, identifying 12 unique processes and 65 convicted or investigated entities across sectors including school meals (\textit{merenda escolar}), trains and metro systems, solar water heaters, school transportation, pharmaceuticals, trash bags, and waste management. CNPJs were obtained through Receita Federal lookups and cross-referencing with legal databases.

Of these, 49 firm registrations could be matched to the BEC supplier registry by CNPJ or company name, spanning 8 CADE processes. The matching reveals a dual pattern. The majority of CADE-convicted cartel firms in BEC are \textit{winners}: 42 of the 49 matched registrations have positive win rates. However, 3 convicted firms are classified as frequent losers under our IQR definition, and 4 additional firms are always-losers below the FL threshold.

The three frequent losers among CADE-convicted firms are: (i) Sol Tecnologia em Energias Renov\'{a}veis, formerly Soletrol, convicted in CADE process 08012.001273/2010-24 for bid rigging in solar water heater procurement (84 BEC participations, zero wins); (ii) Nova Esperan\c{c}a Locadora de Ve\'{i}culos, also known as New Hope, convicted in process 08700.005876/2019-85 for bid rigging in school transportation in Fernand\'{o}polis-SP (97 participations, zero wins); and (iii) Jofran Com\'{e}rcio de Produtos para Higieniza\c{c}\~{a}o, investigated in process 08700.005789/2015-02 for bid rigging in trash bag procurement under Opera\c{c}\~{a}o Colludium (65 participations, zero wins). All three firms participated in BEC tenders across multiple item groups and years without ever winning---precisely the behavior that defines frequent losers.

The predominance of winners among convicted cartelists is consistent with the theoretical framework of bid-rigging cartels. In a rotating-winner scheme, the convicted firms are typically the \textit{designated winners}---the entities that collect the contract and distribute rents. Frequent losers serve a complementary role as \textit{cover bidders}: firms that submit non-competitive bids to simulate participation and avoid detection. The fact that some convicted firms are themselves frequent losers suggests that cartel organizations can include both designated winners and designated losers among their members.

A co-bidder analysis further supports this interpretation. We identify all tenders in which CADE-convicted firms participated and examine whether frequent losers co-bid in those same tenders. FL firms appear in 5.6\% of CADE-firm tenders, compared to a baseline of 2.1\% across all BEC tenders---a ratio of 2.6$\times$. For specific convicted firms, the FL co-bidding rate is substantially higher: 69.3\% for Mayfran (school transportation cartel), 68.8\% for Bosch Termotecnologia (solar heater cartel), 55.2\% for Tejofran (trains/metro cartel), and 31.7\% for Convida (school meals cartel).

These findings validate the frequent loser screen through two channels. First, the screen directly identifies some convicted cartel members (3 of 49 matched firms). Second and more importantly, the screen flags tenders that involve convicted cartelists at nearly three times the baseline rate, detecting the operational infrastructure of bid-rigging schemes. Flagging tenders with frequent loser presence can alert authorities to investigate the winning firms and their relationships with the frequent losers.
