\section{Appendix: Reduced-Form Benchmark}\label{app:did}

The structural exercise of Sections~\ref{sec:results}--\ref{sec:welfare} is anchored to a magnitude in the data: the absolute log-price effect of the \policyCutoffMonth{} cutoff. This appendix reports that magnitude as a difference-in-differences against the \controlGroupCount{} never-treated product groups, on the same sample window used by the structural exercise. The DiD is included for design validation rather than as a competing estimate; the structural exercise interprets the within-auction and participation-margin channels that this number aggregates.

The estimating equation is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:did}
\log p^{\mathrm{final}}_{i,j,t} \;=\; \alpha_i + \mu_t + \beta \, (\mathrm{g65}_j \cdot \text{Pre}_t) + X'_{i,j,t} \gamma + \varepsilon_{i,j,t},
\end{equation}
where $i$ indexes items, $j$ indexes product groups, $t$ indexes calendar months, $\alpha_i$ is an item fixed effect, $\mu_t$ is a calendar-month fixed effect, $X_{i,j,t}$ collects auction-format and quantity controls, and $\beta$ identifies the difference between the open-regime price level (Pre, $\mathrm{g65}=1$) and the post-cutoff level relative to the never-treated control set, in the difference-in-differences-in-reverse sense of \citet{kim2019}: a negative coefficient on the pre-period dummy is the open-regime discount that the SME-only rule undoes after \policyCutoffMonth.

\input{../output/tables/tab_prices.tex}

Table~\ref{tab:prices} reports $\hat\beta$ across the 6-, 12-, and 18-month windows on each side of the cutoff. The \structuralWindowM-month specification (the structural baseline) gives $\hat\beta = \didPriceEighteen$ without and $\didPriceEighteenPbu$ with PBU controls, both significant at the 1 percent level on standard errors clustered at the item level. The interpretation is that absolute winning prices were about \didUpperPct~percent lower under the open regime than they were after the SME-only extension. The 12-month and 6-month windows give similar estimates ($\didPriceTwelve$ and $\didPriceSix$ respectively); the 6-month coefficient captures the immediate post-cutoff price response before the participation margin has equilibrated, while the 12-month and 18-month coefficients reflect progressive absorption of the within-auction shock by SME entry, consistent with the structural-window reading in Section~\ref{sec:robustness}. The \didLowerPct--\didUpperPct~percent magnitude that anchors the structural calibration is therefore the \structuralWindowM-month log-price coefficient.

\input{../output/tables/tab_efficiency.tex}

Table~\ref{tab:efficiency} reports the same estimating equation with the price-to-reference ratio $p^{\mathrm{final}} / p^{\mathrm{ref}}$ as the outcome on a level (not log) scale, so coefficients are interpretable as percentage-point shifts in the ratio. The 6-month window gives $\didEffSix$ (with PBU controls), consistent with the \structuralWindowM-month log-price effect of $\didLowerDec$ once the average ratio of about $\didPreRefRatio$ in the pre-period is taken into account. The 12-month and 18-month coefficients on this level outcome are smaller in magnitude and statistically insignificant, reflecting that the price-to-reference ratio is bounded above by the buyer's reservation level and therefore averages over a compressed support; the log-price specification of Table~\ref{tab:prices} is the headline. The price-to-reference outcome is reported as a robustness check on the headline log-price magnitude rather than as a structural input.

\subsection{Event-study and placebo diagnostics}

The DiD specification is reinforced by an event-study around the \policyCutoffMonth{} cutoff and three placebo treatment dates. Figure~\ref{fig:eventstudy_prices} plots $\hat\beta_s$ for six semesters around the cutoff (each semester is six calendar months) with item and semester fixed effects, on the same Group-65 vs.\ \controlGroupCount-control panel used by Table~\ref{tab:prices}; the semester immediately preceding the cutoff is the omitted reference. The semester binning aggregates the monthly noise that would otherwise dominate the visual reading. Pre-cutoff coefficients are small in magnitude relative to the post-cutoff coefficients; the post-cutoff coefficients are stably negative and consistent with the $\didLowerDec$ to $\didUpperDec$ DiD coefficient. The placebo evidence in Table~\ref{tab:placebo} adds discipline at fake cutoff dates and is read jointly with the event-study below.

Table~\ref{tab:placebo} disciplines the timing of the cutoff against three placebo treatment dates---September 2017, March 2017, and June 2017---with placebo coefficients of $\placeboSeventeenSep$ (insignificant), $\placeboSeventeenMar$ (significant at 5 percent), and $\placeboSeventeenJun$ (significant at 1 percent). The relevant test for a structural-calibration anchor is magnitude separation, not absence of pre-trend: low-frequency variation in the treated group is detectable at fake cutoffs near the real one, but the actual \policyCutoffMonth{} coefficient of $\placeboLowerNp$ to $\placeboHigherNp$ is three to eight times larger in absolute value than any placebo coefficient and significant at the 1 percent level in every specification. Modern guidance on event-study contamination \citep{borusyak2024, sun2021} emphasizes that staggered or anticipated treatments combine the discrete-treatment effect with anticipation dynamics; the magnitude-separation argument above bounds the anticipation contribution at the difference between the real-cutoff and placebo coefficients, which is at least \placeboMagnitudeRatio~percent of the real-cutoff magnitude. Event-study and placebo evidence read jointly: the DiD coefficient is a treatment effect with at most moderate anticipation contamination, well within the band a structural calibration anchor can absorb.

\subsection{Bridge to the structural counterfactual}

The structural exercise of the main text recovers the within-auction and participation-margin decomposition of the $\didLowerDec$ DiD coefficient. Section~\ref{sec:results} translates the structural counterfactual into normalized units: $p_{S_3} - p_{S_1} = +\bneEffectTotalNp$ in non-pharma and $+\bneEffectTotalPh$ in pharma, of which the within-auction component accounts for \bneShareIntensNp{} and \bneShareIntensPh{} percent respectively. Expressed as a ratio to the open-regime baseline $p_{S_1}$, these are $\bneEffectTotalNp/\bneMeanSoneNp \approx \structRatioNp$ percent in non-pharma and $\bneEffectTotalPh/\bneMeanSonePh \approx \structRatioPh$ percent in pharma; the DiD-implied price ratio is $\exp(\didLowerDec) - 1 \approx \didImpliedRatio$ percent on absolute prices. The 2--3$\times$ gap between the two reporting layers is mechanical and predictable. The DiD averages over the realized price distribution including item- and PBU-level shocks that the structural exercise normalizes away; the structural counterfactual averages over a counterfactual primitive distribution that the DiD never observes. Section~\ref{sec:bridge} attributes the gap to four mechanical sources (sample restriction, UH cleaning, functional-form transformation, conditioning set) summing tightly to the structural object. The DiD validates the design; the structural exercise identifies the channels and prices the welfare consequence.
