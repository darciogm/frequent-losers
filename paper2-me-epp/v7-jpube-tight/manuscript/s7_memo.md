# S7 Memo: Why E-refinado, not D or A

Option E-refinado is the clean compromise between credibility and punch. The Codex referee reports in this thread were converging on three points: keep the main structural specification anchored in the ALS-style equilibrium-selection reading; elevate the entry-insurance result from a side remark to a main finding; and stop overselling the policy takeaway as a universal Pareto indictment of set-asides. E does exactly that.

Option D went too far in the other direction. By pushing `fc_sme_pre` into the main counterfactual scripts, D effectively imposed strict primitive invariance as the baseline. That solves one referee concern, but it does so by discarding the central economic content of the post-policy entrant pool: under a protected regime, who enters is part of the equilibrium object. Once that is stripped out, the main text is no longer reading the data through the model the paper actually motivates. The result is a cleaner but less defensible headline.

Option A had the opposite problem. It preserved the strongest policy punch, but too much of that punch depended on presenting the V0 vs V3 comparison as the headline conclusion. The referee feedback was pushing against exactly that move. In thick markets the preference dominance is robust; in thin markets it is informative but model-sensitive. Treating the policy ranking as the first-order contribution invited the reader to interpret any sensitivity as fragility rather than as evidence on mechanism.

E-refinado keeps the stronger economics and the cleaner rhetoric. The main specification returns to `fc_sme_post`, so the paper stays consistent with the equilibrium-selection interpretation and with the empirical fact that post-policy SME participation is not just larger but compositionally different. The strict-invariance case is not discarded; it is demoted to a robustness benchmark where it belongs. That allows the paper to say something sharper: the intensive margin dominates under any reading, entry is the government's partial insurance, and the welfare ranking of bidder exclusion versus a price preference is goods-characteristic-dependent. That is a better top-5 narrative than either D's overcorrection or A's overclaim.
