\section{Discussion: Policy Design}\label{sec:discussion}

The policy lesson is not that set-asides are universally dominated. Bidder-exclusion rules should be evaluated against the thickness of the protected pool, the heterogeneity of the good, and the welfare weight the planner places on SME surplus. Lei \leiNova{} allows both full SME set-asides and price preferences; the estimates here imply that the choice between them should be contingent on market structure rather than imposed as a uniform default.

\subsection{When does a set-aside become defensible?}

A full set-aside becomes more defensible when three conditions line up: the SME pool is thin enough that protecting it is a first-order objective, the good is heterogeneous enough that the identity of induced entrants matters for equilibrium selection, and the planner places a high welfare weight on SME surplus. Non-pharma sits at the opposite corner---thicker baseline SME pool, more standardized goods, the \optPrefThreshold{} price preference Pareto-superior under both the main equilibrium-selection reading and the strict-invariance benchmark. Pharma is closer to the knife-edge: thinner pool, higher heterogeneity, and a policy ranking that depends on whether the post-policy entrant mix is treated as selected equilibrium participation or forced to equal the pre-policy primitive.

Prior structural work on procurement preferences has reported the dominance of preferences over exclusion as a general result. \citet{athey2013} estimate that subsidizing small-bidder bids in U.S.\ timber would recover essentially all of the efficiency loss from set-asides; \citet{krasnokutskayaseim2011} find that California's five-percent bid preference delivers modest cost effects relative to the counterfactual full set-aside; \citet{marion2007} prices the standing bid preference at roughly four percent of procurement value. Each of those settings is a thick market for a relatively standardized good, where this paper's non-pharmaceutical reading reproduces the qualitative finding. The contribution of the partition documented here is to identify, within a single legal trigger and a single platform, the market conditions under which the standard ranking does not transport: a pool thin enough that the equilibrium-selection treatment of protected entrants becomes first-order, combined with within-class goods heterogeneity that catalog-level classification does not absorb. The conditional ranking is a structural-thresholds claim, not a hedge against a robustness failure---of the eight (class $\times$ type $\times$ period) cells in the structural sample, the V0/V3 reversal obtains in exactly the two pharma cells under strict invariance and in zero cells under the main specification.

\subsection{The welfare weight implicit in the current regime}

Following the generalized social-marginal-welfare-weight framework of \citet{saezstantcheva2016}, a social planner aggregating producer and consumer surplus with weight $w^{\text{SME}}$ on SME producer surplus is indifferent between the full set-aside (V0) and the \optPrefThreshold{} price preference (V3) when
\begin{equation}\label{eq:welfare_weight}
w^{\text{SME}}_\star
\;=\; \frac{\mathrm{Loss}(V_0) - \mathrm{Loss}(V_3)}
           {\mathrm{SMESurplus}(V_0) - \mathrm{SMESurplus}(V_3)}.
\end{equation}
Substituting the loss differentials (V0 loss $\welfTotalLossLthirtyNp/\welfTotalLossLthirtyPh$, V3 near zero) and SME-surplus differentials (implicit transfers $\welfTransferNp/\welfTransferPh$ under V0; near zero under V3) yields $w^{\text{SME}}_\star \approx \welfWeightMainNp$ in non-pharma and $\welfWeightMainPh$ in pharma under the main specification; under strict invariance non-pharma is unchanged while the pharma indifference weight falls to \welfWeightSiPh{} (Table~\ref{tab:v3_strict_invariance}). A planner willing to pay the non-pharma fiscal cost of bidder exclusion would have to value one real of SME surplus at more than twice a real of general welfare. Figure~\ref{fig:v3_welfare_weight} plots the implied weights against the empirical Bolsa~Fam\'ilia MVPF benchmark of \citet{bergstrom2025} ($\mvpfBolsaLo$--$\mvpfBolsaHi$ depending on GE-multiplier inclusion; cf.\ \citet{mendes2023}).

The preference grid (Table~\ref{tab:v3_preference_grid}) reads the trade-off without the welfare-weight identity. A \optPrefThreshold{} preference raises the SME win-rate by \prefSmeWinGainTenNp/\prefSmeWinGainTenPh{} percentage points relative to the open regime---substantially below the eighty-plus percentage points by which V0 raises it---at a welfare cost within Monte Carlo noise of zero in both classes. The choice between V3 and V0 is therefore a discrete one between a low-cost low-redistribution instrument and a high-cost high-redistribution one. Welfare cost stays inside Monte Carlo noise up to a \prefSafeBandHiNp{}-percent preference in non-pharma and a \prefSafeBandHiPh{}-percent preference in pharma, then accelerates to \welfPrefThirtyNp/\welfPrefThirtyPh~percent of $p_{S_1}$ at a thirty-percent preference.

\subsection{V3, V4, and current Brazilian instruments}

V3 should be read as a counterfactual reform rather than as a current Brazilian SME procurement instrument. The set-aside V0 is in force: \LCSmeStatute{} art.~48 (preserved by Lei \leiNova{}) imposes SME-only tenders for items below the threshold value, and PGE-SP Parecer \pareceNum{} extends that rule item-by-item to large procurement notices. Brazilian law also operates two non-exclusionary mechanisms in the SME-favoring family, both distinct from V3 mechanically. The first is the \emph{empate ficto} of \LCSmeStatute{} arts.~44--45 (also preserved by Lei \leiNova{}), which gives qualifying SMEs a discrete right to match the leading bid when their own bid is within \empateBand~percent (Preg\~ao) or ten percent (other modalities) of it---a right-to-match, not a continuous score discount. The second is the \emph{margem de prefer\^encia} of Lei \leiNova{} art.~26 (up to ten percent for nationally produced goods, up to twenty percent with innovation content), which is directed at domestic versus foreign products rather than SMEs.

The V4 \emph{empate ficto} is the closest behavioral analogue to V3 in current Brazilian law, and I quantify it directly by extending the BNE Monte Carlo to its outcome rule (Table~\ref{tab:v4_empate}). The empate fires in \vFourFireRateNp/\vFourFireRatePh~percent of auctions---low because the trigger requires both an SME bidder and an SME-leader gap within the \empateBand-percent band. The SME win-rate rises from \vFourSmeWinSoneNp/\vFourSmeWinSonePh{} to \vFourSmeWinVfourNp/\vFourSmeWinVfourPh~percent, a gain of \vFourSmeWinGainNp/\vFourSmeWinGainPh{} pp---less than half the V3 gain in non-pharma and roughly seventy percent in pharma. The MCPF distortion is zero by construction (the government pays the leading non-SME bid); the allocative DWL is bounded by Pr(empate fires) $\times$ within-band cost gap and totals \vFourLossPctNp/\vFourLossPctPh~percent of $p_{S_1}$---three orders of magnitude below V0 and at par with V3 in welfare terms. The empate-ficto-rule operative in Brazilian law today preserves the price-discipline channel at essentially zero welfare cost, but the distributive bite is narrow because the trigger event is rare; a continuous scoring rule (V3) or a banded rule with a larger band would scale the distributive impact at progressively rising welfare cost.

The U.S.\ SBA price-evaluation preference for HUBZone and disadvantaged firms and the EU Directive 2014/24 transposition rules are the most-studied international comparators; cross-platform tests on federal Brazilian procurement (ComprasNet, under the same Lei \leiNova{} framework), municipal procurement, and non-Brazilian set-aside reforms (e.g., SBA 8(a) program changes) offer natural extensions. The bifurcation positions non-pharma and pharma on opposite sides of a \smesPerAucBandLo--\smesPerAucBandHi{} SMEs-per-auction band (non-pharma at \bneNsmePostNp, pharma at \bneNsmePostPh); the structural framework identifies that band as the relevant policy threshold within this platform.

\subsection{Directions for follow-on work}

Three margins outside the current scope sit naturally on the agenda. \emph{Quantity response} would adjust the static fiscal cost by $\varepsilon \cdot \didUpperDec$ under low-elasticity assumptions on essential public-health goods, shaving the allocative DWL by \ppShavedLo--\ppShavedHi{} pp of $p_{S_1}$. \emph{Dynamic capacity accumulation} by SME firms protected by the rule would price an option-value contribution the static model does not capture \citep{ferraz2016, szerman2023}. \emph{General-equilibrium SME surplus} extends the partial-equilibrium SMESurplus in~\eqref{eq:welfare_weight} to a GE framework with capacity persistence and spillovers. None of the three changes the organizing lesson that the welfare ranking of bidder-exclusion rules is conditional on market structure rather than uniform; each could enlarge the SME-side benefit ex post or refine the policy comparison.
