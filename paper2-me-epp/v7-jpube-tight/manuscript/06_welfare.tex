\section{Welfare}\label{sec:welfare}

The welfare cost of the set-aside is the structural object that disciplines the policy comparison. Under the main equilibrium-selection specification it is \welfLossPctLthirtyNp~percent of $p_{S_1}$ in non-pharmaceuticals and \welfLossPctLthirtyPh~percent in pharmaceuticals at $\lambda = \lambdaMain$. The price effect is one piece of that account. Two others matter and are routinely left out of reduced-form accountings. \emph{Allocative waste} arises because the set-aside routes the contract to an SME winner whose cost may exceed the lowest cost in the full pool; the extra production cost is destroyed, not transferred. \emph{Tax-distortion loss} arises because the additional government outlay is financed from distortionary taxation. The transfer from government to SMEs---$\Delta_{\text{gov}} - $ DWL$_{\text{alloc}}$---is zero-sum in a social-planner ledger.

Formally, per-auction welfare loss is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:welfare}
\mathrm{Loss} \;=\; \underbrace{c_{(1)}^{S_3} - c_{(1)}^{S_1}}_{\mathrm{DWL}_{\text{alloc}}}
\;+\; \underbrace{\lambda \cdot (p_{S_3} - p_{S_1})}_{\mathrm{MCPF\ distortion}},
\end{equation}
normalized as $\mathrm{Loss} / p_{S_1}$.

\subsection{Point estimates}

Table~\ref{tab:v3_welfare} gives the decomposition at $\lambda = \lambdaMain$, the \citet{ballard1985} benchmark; \citet{hendren2020} and \citet{finkelstein2020} discuss the MVPF connection to the MCPF framework used here. In non-pharmaceuticals, $\Delta_{\text{gov}} = \welfDeltaGovNp$ of the reference price, DWL$_{\text{alloc}} = \welfDwlAllocNp$, implicit transfer $\welfTransferNp$, MCPF distortion $\welfMcpfDistLthirtyNp$, total $\welfTotalLossLthirtyNp$ ($= \welfLossPctLthirtyNp$~percent of $p_{S_1}$). In pharmaceuticals the same accounting is larger on every margin: $\Delta_{\text{gov}} = \welfDeltaGovPh$, DWL$_{\text{alloc}} = \welfDwlAllocPh$, transfer $\welfTransferPh$, MCPF $\welfMcpfDistLthirtyPh$, total $\welfTotalLossLthirtyPh$ ($= \welfLossPctLthirtyPh$~percent). The allocative component dominates in both classes, especially in pharma where a thin SME pool and higher goods heterogeneity make bidder exclusion more likely to route the contract to a high-cost protected winner.

The fixed-pool-vs-realized attenuation documented in Section~\ref{sec:results} (\latentShockRatioNp/\latentShockRatioPh~percent) carries over to the welfare formula: both terms in~\eqref{eq:welfare} load on the simulated price margin, so a fixed-pool calculation overstates the realized burden by the same factor. The participation response is part of the welfare object, not separable from it.

\subsection{Confidence intervals and \texorpdfstring{$\lambda$}{lambda} sensitivity}

Cluster bootstrap with $\bootReps$ replicates (Online Appendix) gives 95 percent CIs on $\mathrm{Loss}/p_{S_1}$ at $\lambda = \lambdaMain$ of $[\welfLossPctLoNp\%, \welfLossPctHiNp\%]$ in non-pharma and $[\welfLossPctLoPh\%, \welfLossPctHiPh\%]$ in pharma; even the lower bound in pharma exceeds the non-pharma point estimate. The class heterogeneity is structural, not sampling noise. Figure~\ref{fig:v3_welfare_forest} plots the CIs across the $\lambda$ grid and overlays the strict-invariance benchmark.

For $\lambda \in \{0.20, \lambdaMain, 0.40\}$, the welfare loss runs \welfLossPctLtwentyNp/\welfLossPctLthirtyNp/\welfLossPctLfortyNp~percent in non-pharma and \welfLossPctLtwentyPh/\welfLossPctLthirtyPh/\welfLossPctLfortyPh~percent in pharma (Table~\ref{tab:v3_welfare}). Across $\lambda \in [\welfLambdaRangeLo, \welfLambdaRangeHi]$ (full grid in the Online Appendix), non-pharma preserves V3 $>$ V0 under both specifications; pharma reverses only under strict invariance. The conditional ranking is driven by the modeling treatment of the post-policy SME pool, not by the choice of $\lambda$. Strict invariance preserves the direction of the welfare result (\siWelfLossPctNp/\siWelfLossPctPh~percent; Table~\ref{tab:v3_strict_invariance}); the pharma decline relative to the main specification is the footprint of equilibrium selection in the protected pool.

\subsection{Annual aggregate}\label{sec:annual_aggregate}

Combining the per-auction welfare loss with the empirical Group-65 spend gives the annual welfare cost (Table~\ref{tab:welfare_annual}). The upper bound assumes that every Group-65 reference real falls under the SME-only set-aside, delivering R\$\,\welfAnnualBRLHi~million (US\$\,\welfAnnualUSDHi~million) per year. The realistic case scales by the \adherenceGsixtyfivePost~percent state-level adherence rate documented in Section~\ref{sec:institutional}, delivering R\$\,\welfAnnualBRLLo~million (US\$\,\welfAnnualUSDLo~million) per year. Pharma carries the larger share (R\$\,\welfPharmaAdhBRL{} vs R\$\,\welfNpAdhBRL{} million at adherence-adjusted), reflecting the higher per-auction cost share in thin standardized markets. Across the realistic adherence range $[\welfAdherenceLoPct\%, \welfAdherenceHiPct\%]$ the annual welfare cost spans R\$\,\welfRangeLoBRL--\welfRangeHiBRL~million. The Online Appendix scales these to the furosemida vignette of Section~\ref{sec:data}.
