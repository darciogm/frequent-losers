\begin{abstract}
Governments restrict public tenders to small firms, yet the fiscal cost is unknown. Exploiting a natural experiment in Sao Paulo, Brazil---where medical supplies were uniquely exempt from mandatory SME-only tenders until a 2018 legal reinterpretation ended this exemption---I estimate these costs using a difference-in-differences in reverse design. Open tenders yielded prices 12--13\% lower and attracted 19\% more firms. A Gelbach decomposition reveals that increased participation explains only 6\% of the price reduction; most savings come from fiercer bidding. The fiscal cost for one product group alone is R\$84.5 million over 18 months.
\end{abstract}
