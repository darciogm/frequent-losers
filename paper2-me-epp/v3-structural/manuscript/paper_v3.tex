\title{\textbf{Set-Asides at Work: Structural Welfare Cost of SME Procurement Preferences\tnoteref{tfoot}}}
\tnotetext[tfoot]{\emph{This version: April 2026. Earlier reduced-form results appeared in v1 (\emph{The Intensive Margin of Small-Firm Protection}). v3 replaces the reduced-form evidence with a full structural model and welfare analysis. I thank Paulo Furquim de Azevedo for early discussions. Replication code and data dictionary at the project repository.}}
\author[INSPER]{Darcio Genicolo-Martins}\ead{darciogm1@insper.edu.br}
\address[INSPER]{INSPER, S\~ao Paulo}

\begin{abstract}
Brazilian law reserves most low-value public procurement for small firms through set-asides backed by costly opt-out. I estimate an asymmetric independent-private-values model of S\~ao Paulo's BEC electronic procurement platform to study what bidder exclusion does through bidding, entry, and welfare. Cost distributions are identified from Preg\~ao drop-out bids under the English-reverse logic and corrected for auction-level unobserved heterogeneity via Krasnokutskaya-style shrinkage. Entry is treated in the \citet{atheyseira2011} spirit: the main specification allows the post-policy SME cost distribution to differ from the pre-policy one as an equilibrium-selection object, and the appendix reports the strict-invariance limiting case. Three findings organize the results. First, the structural decomposition is stable across product classes: 75--85 percent of the set-aside price effect operates through the intensive margin, not through entry. Second, entry is the government's partial insurance: holding the SME pool fixed at its pre-policy size would raise the set-aside's price and welfare cost by roughly 60--70 percent. Third, the welfare ranking of set-asides versus a 10 percent price preference depends on market thickness and product heterogeneity. In thicker, more standardized non-pharma markets, the preference rule is Pareto-superior under both the main and strict-invariance readings. In thinner, more heterogeneous pharma markets, the preference rule is Pareto-superior under the main specification but the ranking reverses under strict invariance. I interpret that bifurcation as a property of bidder-exclusion rules in heterogeneous markets, not as a pharma-specific anomaly. Under the main specification, prices rise about 24\% of the reference price in non-pharma and 34\% in pharma, and total welfare loss is 29\% and 45\% of procurement value, respectively.
\end{abstract}
\end{frontmatter}
