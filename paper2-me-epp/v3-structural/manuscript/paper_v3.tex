\title{\textbf{Set-Asides at Work: Structural Welfare Cost of SME Procurement Preferences\tnoteref{tfoot}}}
\tnotetext[tfoot]{\emph{This version: April 2026. Earlier reduced-form results appeared in v1 (\emph{The Intensive Margin of Small-Firm Protection}). v3 replaces the reduced-form evidence with a full structural model and welfare analysis. I thank Paulo Furquim de Azevedo for early discussions. Replication code and data dictionary at the project repository.}}
\author[INSPER]{Darcio Genicolo-Martins}\ead{darciogm1@insper.edu.br}
\address[INSPER]{INSPER, S\~ao Paulo}

\begin{abstract}
Brazilian law reserves most low-value public procurement for small firms through set-asides backed by costly opt-out. The welfare cost of that preference has been studied as a reduced-form markup on winning prices; the structure behind the markup has not. I estimate an asymmetric independent-private-values model of S\~ao Paulo's BEC electronic procurement platform and decompose the price effect of an exogenous 2018 extension of the SME-only rule to medical supplies into an intensive margin (how each bidder bids) and an extensive margin (who enters). Cost distributions are identified from Preg\~ao drop-out bids under the English-reverse logic, corrected for auction-level unobserved heterogeneity via Krasnokutskaya-style shrinkage. Endogenous entry is modeled Athey-Levin-Seira-style; winners are treated as left-censored in a Turnbull NPMLE for robustness. Prices rise 24\% of the reference in non-pharma and 31\% in pharma classes; roughly three quarters of the effect is intensive. Including Brazil's marginal cost of public funds ($\lambda \approx 0.30$), total welfare loss is 28\% of procurement value in non-pharma (95\% CI 21--35\%) and 46\% in pharma (95\% CI 35--56\%)---two to four times earlier reduced-form estimates. A counterfactual 10\% SME price preference achieves comparable SME win-rate gains at essentially zero price cost, suggesting that full set-asides are Pareto-inferior to preference rules absent implausibly large distributional weights.
\end{abstract}
\end{frontmatter}
