\section{Data and Sample Construction}\label{sec:data}

The data come from BEC, the state of S\~ao Paulo's centralized electronic procurement platform. BEC handles all standardized-goods purchasing for state bureaus and affiliated units, and is the administrative source for item-level bids, transacted prices, firm identifiers, quantities, and auction identifiers. The raw extract covers January 2016 to December 2019---3.7~million item-level observations across 82{,}569 distinct items, 832{,}984 purchase orders, and 1{,}344 public buyer units (PBUs). Group~65 (medical, dental, and hospital supplies) accounts for roughly 27\% of total BEC transactions in the window and is the treated group; the remaining 77 product groups form the control set in earlier reduced-form work.

The structural analysis narrows to a sharper sample. The identifying variation is the 18-month window around the March~2018 policy cutoff, in Stata monthly-date units $[680, 715]$, which corresponds to September~2016 through August~2019. I restrict to Preg\~ao auctions within Group~65, because Preg\~ao is the descending-clock format that admits point identification from drop-out bids, and Group~65 is where the policy bound. Within the window, bids are organized at the firm-auction level: each row records the final bid submitted by a specific firm in a specific item-auction, along with an indicator for whether the firm won or lost the auction.

I further apply three cuts. First, bids must be strictly positive and at most three times the buyer's reference price ($c_\epsilon \in (0, 3]$); the upper bound removes implausible outliers (less than 0.4\% of bids) that are almost always data-entry errors at the hundred-times-reference level. Second, auctions must have at least two participating firms---below that, neither point identification of $F_c$ nor the BNE counterfactual is well-defined. Third, each bid is tagged by its firm type (SME or non-SME) using the BEC administrative SME flag, and by its pharma status using the CADMAT class code: classes 6531, 6532, 6536, and 6581---CMED-regulated pharmaceuticals and biologicals---are pharma-narrow; the remainder of Group~65 is non-pharma.

The resulting structural sample is 297{,}967 firm-auction observations across 97{,}993 distinct auctions, balanced across Pre (March~2017 to February~2018, $n = 48{,}740$ auctions) and Post (March~2018 to August~2019, $n = 49{,}253$). Auction-level unobserved heterogeneity is material---auctions within the same CADMAT class differ in scale, in contract complexity, and in ANVISA registration load---and is not fully absorbed by normalization against the item-level reference price. I handle it with a method-of-moments variance decomposition followed by a best-linear-predictor shrinkage of the auction-level component out of each log-bid; the cleaned bids enter all downstream estimation. Details are in Section~\ref{sec:identification}. The shrinkage recovers intraclass correlations of 0.47--0.62, in the range reported by \citet{krasnokutskaya2011} for California highway procurement.

Entry counts are computed separately. For each auction, I count the number of distinct firms of each type that submitted at least one bid, then average within (period $\times$ pharma) cells. Non-pharma shifts from 0.86 SMEs and 2.46 non-SMEs per auction pre-policy to 1.74 and 1.40 post-policy. Pharma shifts from 0.44 SMEs and 2.56 non-SMEs to 1.09 and 1.62. The pre-to-post doubling of SME participation is the object of the entry-response channel; the corresponding decline in non-SME participation is the composition shift that accompanies the set-aside. Entry counts feed the BNE counterfactual in Section~\ref{sec:results}.

Four ancillary datasets anchor the analysis. The reference price by item comes from the BEC catalog (\texttt{preco\_ref}) and is used both for normalization and for pricing out fiscal cost in reais. The CADMAT class codes come from the BEC product hierarchy. The historical SME flag---whether a firm had previously bid as SME at any point in the 2014--2019 period---comes from firm registries linked to the bid-level data; this prevents misclassifying firms that temporarily exited SME status during the window. The pharma indicator is constructed mechanically from the CADMAT code list above.
