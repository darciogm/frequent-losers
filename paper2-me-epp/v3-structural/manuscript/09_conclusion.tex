\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

A decade of work on auction preferences has shown that restricting a procurement auction to small firms raises winning prices. This paper estimates, within an asymmetric independent-private-values model of the Brazilian electronic reverse-auction platform, how much of that markup operates through each remaining bidder bidding less aggressively, and how much through a shift in the set of firms that show up. The exogenous March 2018 extension of S\~ao Paulo's SME-only regime to medical supplies supplies the identifying variation; Preg\~ao drop-out bids deliver point identification of the cost distributions by type; auction-level heterogeneity is controlled via the Krasnokutskaya shrinkage; the Turnbull NPMLE corroborates within five percent.

Three findings anchor the contribution. A counterfactual 10 percent price preference delivers SME win-rate gains of the same order as the full set-aside at essentially zero price cost; the welfare weight on SME surplus required to rationalize the current rule over the preference is 2.4--3.0, well above the 1.2--1.5 range implicit in Brazil's social-transfer programs. The welfare cost of the full set-aside is 28 percent of procurement value in non-pharma (95\% CI 21--35) and 46 percent in pharma (95\% CI 35--56)---two to four times the reduced-form predecessor's figure, with the gap traced to unobserved heterogeneity in the cost primitives and to explicit pricing of tax distortion. Within that total, the intensive margin dominates: roughly three quarters of the price effect runs through bidding behavior inside the restricted pool, and the extensive margin of SME entry offsets about a quarter.

The implication for procurement policy under Brazil's new Law 14{,}133/2021 is clear enough. For non-specialized items the set-aside should yield to the price preference; for pharma, where welfare losses are largest and SME participation gains smallest per unit of welfare cost, the case for relaxation is strongest. These are policy moves that the existing legal framework permits and that the numbers in this paper support.

Three extensions remain open. A first-price sealed-bid equilibrium with explicit preference rules would sharpen the V3 counterfactual; a dynamic model of SME capacity accumulation would price the longer-run benefits that the static framework leaves implicit; a general-equilibrium treatment of quantity adjustment would complete the welfare account. Each is a paper of its own. What this paper does is establish the static structural benchmark from which those extensions should depart.
