\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

A decade of work on auction preferences has shown that restricting a procurement auction to small firms raises winning prices. This paper estimates, within an asymmetric independent-private-values model of the Brazilian electronic reverse-auction platform, which mechanisms produce that markup and when they change the welfare ranking of the policy. The exogenous March 2018 extension of S\~ao Paulo's SME-only regime to medical supplies provides the identifying variation; Preg\~ao drop-out bids deliver the type-specific cost distributions; auction-level heterogeneity is controlled through Krasnokutskaya-style shrinkage; and the appendix reports both Turnbull point-identification and a strict-invariance benchmark.

Three findings anchor the contribution. First, the structural decomposition of the set-aside is stable across classes: 75--85 percent of the total effect operates through the intensive margin, not through entry. Second, entry is the government's partial insurance: the induced SME participation response offsets part of the intensive shock, and without endogenous entry the welfare cost would be roughly 60--70 percent larger. Third, the welfare ranking of set-asides versus preferences is goods-characteristic-dependent. In thicker, more standardized non-pharma markets, the set-aside is welfare-dominated by a 10 percent price preference under both the main and strict-invariance readings. In thinner, more heterogeneous pharma markets, the preference dominates under the main equilibrium-selection specification but the ranking depends on how entry selection is modeled.

These findings shift the policy interpretation. The relevant question is not whether bidder exclusion is always bad or always justified. The relevant question is when the protected pool is thin enough, the good heterogeneous enough, and the planner's welfare weight on SME surplus high enough to make full exclusion worth its allocative and fiscal cost. The model says those conditions are demanding.

In thick, standardized markets the set-aside is welfare-dominated by a 10 percent preference; in thin, heterogeneous markets the ranking depends on the modeling of entry selection; under any reading, the structural decomposition shows that the intensive margin dominates and entry is the partial insurance.
