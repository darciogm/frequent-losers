\section{Results}\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Entry response}

Pre-period average counts of SMEs per Preg\~ao auction are 0.86 in non-pharma and 0.44 in pharma. Post-period counts are 1.74 and 1.09---roughly a doubling in both classes. Non-SME counts move the other way: 2.46 to 1.40 in non-pharma, 2.56 to 1.62 in pharma. The entry response is therefore material and asymmetric: SMEs roughly double, non-SMEs fall by between one-third (pharma) and one-half (non-pharma). The full panel, including Convite modality as a consistency cross-check, is in Table~\ref{tab:v3_entry_rates}.

\subsection{BNE price decomposition}

Table~\ref{tab:v3_bne_decomp} reports the central decomposition. In non-pharma, $p_{S_1} = 0.76$ of the reference price under the observed open regime; $p_{S_2} = 1.11$ under SME-only at the Pre-period SME pool (intensive effect isolated, no entry response); $p_{S_3} = 1.01$ under SME-only at the Post-period pool. The total effect $p_{S_3} - p_{S_1} = +0.25$ splits into an intensive component $p_{S_2} - p_{S_1} = +0.35$ (seventy-seven percent of the effect before accounting for entry) and an entry component $p_{S_3} - p_{S_2} = -0.11$ (a negative twenty-three percent---entry partially offsets the intensive-channel price rise). In pharma the corresponding numbers are $p_{S_1} = 0.66$, $p_{S_2} = 1.17$, $p_{S_3} = 1.00$; total $+0.34$ with intensive 151\% of effect and entry $-51\%$---here entry overcorrects the intensive rise in the point estimate, though the bootstrap CIs overlap substantially and the qualitative split is similar.

The headline reading is that the intensive margin dominates. Across classes and methodological specifications, the intensive share of the price effect is in the 70--80\% range; entry is the smaller compensatory channel. This partially rehabilitates the reduced-form intuition that set-asides act ``inside the auction'' as much as ``at its door'' \citep{krasnokutskaya2011}, while quantitatively pinning down how much of each.

\subsection{Decomposition robustness: raw vs.\ UH-clean, fixed-pool vs.\ endogenous entry}

Table~\ref{tab:v3_decomp_grid} reports the $2 \times 2$ grid of methodological choices. The v3 specification (UH-clean cost distributions, endogenous entry) is the bottom-right cell of the grid. Moving to the top-left (raw costs, fixed-pool) delivers the proxy for a v2-style specification: in non-pharma the v2 proxy gives $\Delta_{\text{total}} = +0.40$, roughly 72\% larger than the v3 number of $+0.23$; in pharma the gap is $+0.48$ vs.\ $+0.29$, a 68\% overstatement. Both methodological choices matter. UH correction accounts for the larger share of the gap: the BLP shrinkage drops the intensive-margin estimate in pharma from 87\% to 67\% of effect. Endogenous entry accounts for the remainder.

The v2 headline of an 11.8\% DWL\footnote{``\(\Delta \text{total}\)'' in the decomposition is the per-auction price increase; the 11.8\% DWL in v2 is an aggregate accounting of allocative + transfer, computed under fixed-pool and raw-costs assumptions. The two objects are related but not identical.} was thus partly an artifact of two methodological omissions: losers-only cost identification (which overstates the tail) and fixed-pool counterfactual (which miss-attributes what the entry-response absorbs). The v3 numbers are lower in the raw price decomposition but higher in the welfare decomposition, for reasons developed in Section~\ref{sec:welfare}: MCPF on the higher gov payments is made explicit in v3.

\subsection{Bootstrap CI and Turnbull robustness}

A cluster bootstrap at the auction level (B=500, Table~\ref{tab:v3_bootstrap_ci}) puts the 95\% CI for $\Delta_{\text{total}}$ at $[0.19, 0.29]$ in non-pharma and $[0.25, 0.36]$ in pharma. The share-intensive 95\% CI is $[63\%, 88\%]$ (non-pharma) and $[62\%, 83\%]$ (pharma); no CI crosses zero. The \citet{turnbull1976} NPMLE robustness (Table~\ref{tab:v3_sensitivity_fc}) agrees with the all-bidders baseline within 5\% on the point estimate and well within the bootstrap CI at the margin: Turnbull gives $\Delta_{\text{total}} = 0.24$ (non-pharma, $+4\%$ over baseline) and $0.34$ (pharma, $+2\%$). The Turnbull intensive share in pharma is 81\%, six percentage points higher than the all-bidders estimate---if anything, point-identification \emph{strengthens} the intensive-margin interpretation.

\subsection{Entry cost calibration}

A zero-profit condition at the entry margin (Table~\ref{tab:v3_entry_cost}) delivers implied entry costs of R\$0.55 for SMEs and R\$2.46 for non-SMEs in non-pharma (4\% and 18\% of the median reference price, respectively), and R\$0.11 / R\$0.51 in pharma. The magnitudes are plausible in the BEC context, where responding to a Preg\~ao tender requires CNPJ-level documentation and a bid-specific quote but no site visits. The order-of-magnitude ratio of 5:1 between non-SME and SME entry costs---consistent across classes---supports the intuition that non-SMEs have heavier overhead associated with state-level procurement compliance, at least per-bid.
