\section{Conclusion}

SME set-asides are the most widespread procurement-preference instrument worldwide, but the literature has struggled to say \emph{how} they raise prices---whether through thinner bidder pools or through less aggressive bidding within surviving auctions. This paper provides the first direct bid-distribution evidence on that mechanism. Exploiting the March~2018 reinterpretation of Brazilian procurement law that extended Sao Paulo's SME-only regime to medical supplies---the only previously exempt product group---I show that the winning bid immediately moved ten percentage points closer to the buyer's reference price, and the standard deviation of phase-2 submitted bids shrank monotonically in the number of participating firms. These effects persist after conditioning on exactly two, three, or five-plus participating firms, directly isolating the intensive-margin channel. A~\citet{gelbach2016} decomposition attributes only six percent of the 12--13\% price increase to entry and winner composition; ninety-four percent reflects changes in bidding behavior within auctions, exactly as the theory of~\citet{bulow1996} and~\citet{krasnokutskaya2011} predicts.

Identification proceeds from a single-cohort staggered difference-in-differences with 76 never-treated controls, and the headline estimate is robust to the~\citet{callaway2021},~\citet{sun2021}, and~\citet{goodmanbacon2021} estimators. The Goodman-Bacon decomposition confirms that 100\% of the coefficient comes from the clean treated-vs-never-treated 2$\times$2 comparison; no ``forbidden'' implicit comparisons enter. A formal spillover test restricted to the 76 control groups finds that the implied bias from non-SME firms redirecting into control markets is less than one percent of the headline coefficient, sustaining the lower-bound interpretation of the estimates. Statistical inference is inherently limited by the single-unit treatment structure, so the evidence for causality rests on the consistency of effects across four outcomes and the convergence of multiple estimators rather than on a classical permutation test.

Two findings translate the mechanism result into concrete policy design.

First, 92\% of Group-65 procurement value is concentrated in the top value quartile, so a simple reference-value exemption---applying the SME-only rule only to items below approximately R\$7{,}600---recovers 90\% of the fiscal cost while preserving SME access to 75\% of items by count. Because SMEs lose access to the top-quartile contracts under this reform, it is not strictly Pareto-improving, but it substantially relaxes the efficiency-equity trade-off built into the uniform rule. The reform is a concrete lever for the ongoing implementation of Law~14{,}133/2021, Brazil's new public procurement framework, and illustrates how administrative procurement data can be used to calibrate second-best reforms that preserve the equity goals of SME policies while curbing their fiscal footprint.

Second, pharmaceuticals regulated by CMED's list-price ceilings (class~6531) bear 62\% larger price distortion than non-pharma medical supplies. The CMED cap---designed to contain public-health expenditures---does not shield procurement from the ME/EPP preference, consistent with actual contract prices lying below the cap and the restriction compressing competition within the residual slack. Two Brazilian regulatory regimes---SME procurement preferences and drug price controls---interact perversely: tightening either one without accounting for the other leaves procurement cost higher than either instrument alone would deliver. This interaction is new to the literature on procurement preferences and is particularly important for countries such as Brazil, India, and Mexico that combine SME set-asides with pharmaceutical price regulation.

Taken together, these results deliver an aggregate fiscal cost for the SME-only rule in Group~65 alone of R\$50--85 million over the 18-month window---7--12\% of the product group's procurement value, or approximately 0.04\% of Brazil's public-health procurement budget at the state level. Extrapolating proportionally to all product groups subject to SME-only tendering and to all Brazilian states would yield a cost several times larger, though the heterogeneity by item value, CNAE sector, and firm age documented here counsels against naive extrapolation. The one dimension along which the policy delivers on a stated objective is geographic proximity: winning firms are located 5--11~km further from buyers under open tenders, confirming that the SME preference encourages local sourcing, and the value-threshold reform proposed here would largely preserve that benefit.

The design has the standard limitations of a single-cohort event study and of a policy instrument that is strictly local in application. External validity to other product groups, states, and procurement regimes requires the untestable assumption of comparable treatment effects; the heterogeneity documented in the empirical section counsels against naive extrapolation of the \emph{magnitudes}, even as the underlying mechanism---the contraction of competitive bidding pressure under set-asides---should generalize. Several avenues for future research remain open. Extending the RAIS window to 2018--2022 would permit evaluation of the supply-side benefits of SME preferences (employment, wages, survival) that the present paper uses only for identification robustness. Structural estimation of an auction model with endogenous SME participation could quantify welfare consequences of alternative preference designs, including variants of the value-threshold reform the data here support.
