\section{Conclusion}

Restricting public tenders to small and medium-sized enterprises imposes a measurable fiscal cost on the state of Sao Paulo of R\$50--85 million over 18 months for medical supplies alone (R\$50 million real, R\$85 million nominal), or 7--12\% of the product group's procurement value. The estimate comes from a single-cohort staggered difference-in-differences induced by the March~2018 policy change that extended Sao Paulo's SME-only procurement regime to Group~65, the only previously exempt product group. Sign and significance are preserved across the~\citet{callaway2021},~\citet{sun2021}, and~\citet{goodmanbacon2021} estimators; the item-level Sun-Abraham estimate is numerically equivalent to the headline DiD, and the Goodman-Bacon decomposition confirms that 100\% of the coefficient comes from the clean treated-vs-never-treated 2$\times$2 comparison. A formal spillover test, placebo tests, Lee bounds, winsorization, and a wild cluster bootstrap all corroborate the finding within their respective power.

Most of the price reduction operates through the intensive margin. Phase-2 bid moments from the BEC electronic-auction data show the winning bid reaches approximately 10 percentage points deeper below the buyer's reference price under open tenders, and bid dispersion shrinks monotonically with the number of participating firms. These patterns persist when the sample is restricted to 2, 3, or 5-plus participating firms, holding the extensive margin constant. A~\citet{gelbach2016} decomposition assigns only 6\% of the price reduction to entry and winner composition; 94\% reflects changes in bid aggressiveness within auctions of a given size, exactly as the auction theory of~\citet{bulow1996} and~\citet{krasnokutskaya2011} predicts.

Two findings sharpen the policy implications. First, 92\% of Group-65 procurement value is concentrated in the top value quartile, so a simple reference-value exemption---applying the SME-only rule only to items below approximately R\$7,623---recovers 90\% of the fiscal cost while preserving SME access to 75\% of items by count. Because SMEs lose access to the top-quartile contracts under this reform, it is not strictly Pareto-improving, but it substantially relaxes the efficiency-equity trade-off built into the uniform rule and provides a concrete lever for the implementation of Law~14{,}133/2021, Brazil's new public procurement framework. Second, pharmaceuticals regulated by CMED's list-price ceilings (class~6531) bear 62\% larger price distortion than non-pharma medical supplies: the price cap does not shield procurement from the ME/EPP preference, consistent with actual contract prices lying below the cap. Two Brazilian regulatory regimes---SME procurement preferences and drug price controls---therefore interact perversely, and any reform of either instrument should account for the other.

The design has the limitations of a single-cohort event study. Only one product group switches status in March~2018, so the permutation $p$-value for an arbitrary group-specific shock is 0.317; the evidence for causality rests on the consistency of effects across four outcomes, the convergence of multiple estimators, and the legalistic nature of the 2018 reinterpretation rather than on randomization inference. External validity to other product groups, states, and procurement regimes requires the (untestable) assumption of comparable treatment effects; the heterogeneity by item value, CNAE sector, and firm age documented in the empirical section suggests caution in extrapolating the aggregate magnitudes, even as the underlying mechanism---the contraction of competitive bidding pressure under set-asides---should generalize.

The one dimension along which the policy delivers on a stated objective is geographic proximity: winning firms were located 5--11~km further from PBUs under open tenders, confirming that the SME preference encourages local sourcing. Whether this benefit justifies the fiscal cost is a value judgment policymakers must make; the value-threshold counterfactual developed here shows that the benefit can be largely preserved while most of the cost is eliminated.

Several avenues for future research remain open. Longer post-treatment data would test whether PBUs learn to mitigate procurement costs as they gain experience with the SME-only regime. Linking BEC winners to RAIS enables firm-level outcome analyses---employment, wages, survival---which the present paper uses only for identification robustness; extending the RAIS window to 2018--2022 would permit evaluation of the policy's supply-side benefits. Finally, structural estimation of an auction model with endogenous SME participation could quantify the welfare consequences of alternative preference designs, including the value-threshold reform the data here support.
