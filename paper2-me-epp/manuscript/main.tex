\title{\textbf{The Intensive Margin of Small-Firm Protection: Bidding, Rents, and the Fiscal Cost of Public Procurement Set-Asides\tnoteref{tfoot}}}
\tnotetext[tfoot]{\emph{This version: April 2026 (First version: January 2021).\\ \textit{An additional bidder is worth more to the auctioneer than any sophisticated bidding mechanism.} ---~\citet{bulow1996}, paraphrased.}}
\author[INSPER]{Darcio Genicolo-Martins} \ead{darciogm1@insper.edu.br} 
\address[INSPER]{INSPER}

\begin{abstract}
Small-firm procurement preferences are usually priced as an entry problem: fewer bidders, higher prices. This paper shows the larger channel operates inside the auction, not at its door. Exploiting a discrete March~2018 legal reinterpretation that extended S\~ao Paulo's SME-only regime to medical supplies---the last exempt product group---I estimate a staggered difference-in-differences against 76 never-treated controls. Winning prices rose 10--11\% (7--10\% real), narrowing the gap to the buyer's reference ceiling by six percentage points. A \citet{gelbach2016} decomposition leaves the effect essentially intact: entry and winner-composition channels offset one another, and the within-auction coefficient is if anything larger after conditioning on bidder number and SME identity. Phase-2 bid-moment evidence corroborates---the winning-bid discount widens 5.8~pp, a shift robust to holding firm count fixed. Because 92\% of procurement value sits in the top quartile, a value-threshold exemption recovers 90\% of the R\$50--74 million (US\$14--21 million) cost while preserving the preference on 75\% of items. The distortion doubles in price-regulated pharmaceuticals---two regulatory regimes interacting perversely. Set-asides do not empty the auction room; they soften every bid inside it.
\end{abstract}

\end{frontmatter}
