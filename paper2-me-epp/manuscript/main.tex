\begin{frontmatter}
\title{\textbf{The Price of Protecting Small Firms: Evidence from Public Procurement\tnoteref{tfoot}}}
\tnotetext[tfoot]{\emph{This version: April 2026 (First version: January 2021)}}
\author[INSPER]{Darcio Genicolo-Martins} \ead{darciogm1@insper.edu.br} 
\address[INSPER]{INSPER}

\begin{abstract}
Governments restrict public tenders to small firms, yet the fiscal cost is unknown. Exploiting a natural experiment in Sao Paulo, Brazil---where medical supplies were uniquely exempt from mandatory SME-only tenders until a 2018 legal reinterpretation ended this exemption---I estimate these costs using a difference-in-differences in reverse design. Open tenders yielded prices 7--13\% lower (12--13\% nominal, 7--10\% in real terms) and attracted 19\% more firms. A Gelbach decomposition reveals that increased participation explains only 6\% of the price reduction; most savings come from fiercer bidding. The fiscal cost for one product group alone is R\$50--85 million over 18 months, depending on the inflation adjustment.
\end{abstract}

\end{frontmatter}
\textbf{Keywords:} public procurement, SMEs, policy costs, restricted public tenders.\\
