\noindent This Online Appendix provides supplementary results for ``The Intensive Margin of Small-Firm Protection.'' All table and figure numbers are prefixed with OA to distinguish them from the main text. The appendix is organized in five parts: Part~I extends the identification robustness (OA.1--OA.7); Part~II develops the mechanism decomposition (OA.8--OA.12); Part~III documents policy-design extensions (OA.13--OA.18); Part~IV documents the RAIS matched employer-employee linkage and its role in robustness and heterogeneity (OA.19--OA.27); Part~V reports the synthetic control estimator (OA.28). Replication code and diagnostic files are deposited at the paper's online repository; every numerical claim in the main text and this appendix is traceable to a table or diagnostic file produced by \texttt{scripts/01\_clean.R} through \texttt{scripts/25\_bid\_moments.R}.

\part{Data and Identification Robustness}

\section{Data Cleaning and Construction}

The primary data source is the full-census microdata of \emph{Bolsa Eletr\^onica de Compras} (BEC), the Sao Paulo state procurement platform, covering January 2016 through December 2019. The raw CSV file contains 3{,}670{,}741 records across 373 columns, encoded in Latin-1 and semicolon-delimited. The cleaning pipeline (\texttt{scripts/01\_clean.R}) selects 76 variables needed for the empirical analysis, casts string-valued columns (CNPJ, municipio, UF) to character to preserve leading zeros and the ``Sem Vencedor'' sentinel, and derives derived variables including \texttt{g65} (indicator for product group~65), \texttt{Pre} (indicator for $t<$March~2018), the 6-, 12-, and 18-month window masks, item and PBU factor encodings, and the CNPJ raiz (first 8 digits of the winning firm's 14-digit CNPJ). The cleaned data is cached as Parquet (\texttt{data/processed/paper2\_me\_epp.parquet}, version~3, compressed Snappy) and as an R data.table RDS (\texttt{/tmp/p2\_prepared.rds}). Subsequent analyses consume this RDS for speed.

The Parquet cache is regenerated whenever \texttt{PARQUET\_VERSION} in \texttt{scripts/utils.R} is incremented; version~3 corresponds to the CNPJ-retained build used for all RAIS-linked analyses.

\section{Staggered-DiD Alternative Estimators}

This section reports the two staggered-DiD estimators that complement the item-level DiD in the main text: \citet{callaway2021} and \citet{sun2021}, together with the \citet{goodmanbacon2021} decomposition.

\subsection*{OA.6.1 Callaway-Sant'Anna (2021)}
Table~\ref{tab:cs2021-oa} reports the overall Average Treatment Effect on the Treated, estimated on a \texttt{codigogrupo}$\times$month panel with the 76 never-treated groups as the comparison set. The estimator uses \texttt{did::att\_gt} with outcome-regression and a multiplier-bootstrap variance (999 iterations). The dynamic event study (Figure~\ref{fig:cs2021-event}) traces the ATT from 18 months pre- to 17 months post-treatment and visualizes the convergence of Group-65 outcomes toward the never-treated baseline.

\subsection*{OA.6.2 Sun-Abraham (2021) Interaction-Weighted Estimator}
Sun-Abraham reduces to a cohort $\times$ event-time interaction specification for a single treated cohort. \subsection*{OA.6.3 Goodman-Bacon (2021) Decomposition}
With a single treated cohort and 76 never-treated controls within the sample window, the \citet{goodmanbacon2021} decomposition collapses to a single comparison type (``Treated vs Untreated''), which carries 100\% of the TWFE weight. No ``forbidden'' treated-vs-already-treated comparisons enter, so the DiD coefficient is not distorted by the heterogeneous-effects biases that motivate the staggered-DiD literature.

\section{Formal Cross-Group Spillover Test}

\IfFileExists{../output/tables/tab_spillover.tex}{\input{../output/tables/tab_spillover.tex}}{}

The spillover test regresses control-group outcomes on $\mathit{Post} \times \mathit{Exposure}$, where $\mathit{Exposure}_c$ is the pre-period share of control group~$c$'s winners whose CNPJ raiz also won at least one Group-65 item in the same pre-period. The coefficient on the interaction is $+0.04$ with a standard error of $0.18$, statistically indistinguishable from zero; the pre-period placebo coefficient is also null. Multiplied by the mean exposure across groups (3.1\%), the implied bias on the headline price coefficient is $+0.001$ log-points, or approximately 1\% of the $-0.133$ estimate. The full diagnostic is in \texttt{output/tables/diag\_spillover.txt}.

\section{Phase-2 Bid-Distribution Moments}

The central mechanism finding of the paper draws on the minimum, maximum, mean, and standard deviation of phase-2 electronic-auction bids. Phase~2 is the iterative bidding stage in which registered suppliers submit progressively lower offers in real time; phase-1 sealed bids are sparse in the Sao Paulo data (under 1\% non-zero in min\_bid\_ph1) and are not used. Phase-2 bid moments are extracted ad-hoc from the raw CSV by \texttt{scripts/25\_bid\_moments.R}; 74.8\% of CSV rows have valid phase-2 moments.

Table~\ref{tab:bid-moments-oa} reports the DiD on three bid-distribution outcomes: \textit{discount from reference} $=(p_\text{ref}-\min_\text{bid})/p_\text{ref}$, \textit{log SD of ph-2 bids}, and \textit{normalized bid range}. Each outcome is estimated in the full sample and in subsamples with exactly 2, exactly 3, or five-plus participating firms. The discount-from-reference coefficient is $+0.104$ in the full sample and remains at $+0.074$ to $+0.098$ when firm count is held fixed. The log SD coefficient is $-0.087$ full and grows monotonically to $-0.224$ at $N\!\geq\!5$.

\section{Value-Threshold Counterfactual}

Table~\ref{tab:cf-oa} reports the per-quartile DiD coefficients used in the value-threshold counterfactual simulation. The simulation splits Group-65 pre-period items into quartiles of reference value (cutoffs R\$500, R\$1{,}875, R\$7{,}623), estimates the DiD within each quartile, and computes the fiscal cost recovered by exempting the top~$k$ quartiles. Because 92\% of pre-period Group-65 procurement value concentrates in the top quartile, exempting Q4 alone recovers 90\% of the counterfactual baseline fiscal cost.

\part{RAIS Matched Employer-Employee Linkage}

Brazil's \emph{Rela\c{c}\~ao Anual de Informa\c{c}\~oes Sociais} (RAIS) is a matched employer-employee registry covering the universe of formal-sector employment relationships. This part links the winning firms in BEC to their RAIS profiles at the CNPJ-raiz (8-digit firm identifier) level, using the establishment-level (ESTB) files for 2011--2017. Of 17{,}857 distinct winning CNPJ raízes in the Paper-2 sample, 14{,}755 match RAIS 2017 nationwide (82.6\% coverage); within Sao Paulo state alone, 12{,}533 match (70.2\%). The link is produced by \texttt{scripts/05\_link\_rais.py}, a DuckDB-based ETL.

\section{Validation of the Self-Declared ME/EPP Label}

The BEC data includes a self-declared \texttt{fornec\_enquad} (firm-enquadramento) variable. Table~\ref{tab:rais-val} tests whether the headline DiD is sensitive to the label's classification noise by restricting the estimation sample to firms with (i) a RAIS match, (ii) active employment at most 49 (ME/EPP ceiling), (iii) active employment at most 9 (micro ceiling), and (iv) active employment strictly below 100 (excluding the top of the RAIS size distribution). The price coefficient is essentially invariant across these subsamples.

\IfFileExists{../output/tables/tab_rais_validation.tex}{\begin{table}[htbp]\centering\caption{RAIS-Validated Winner Subsamples}\label{tab:rais-val}\end{table}}{}

\section{Winner Composition: RAIS-Validated SME}

Table~\ref{tab:winner-rais-oa} uses a RAIS-validated SME flag (winner active employment $\leq 49$) as the dependent variable. The DiD on this outcome estimates how the SME-only rule shifts the probability of a genuinely small winner; the mechanism prediction is a positive post-policy shift.

\section{Enforcement Heterogeneity}

Table~\ref{tab:enforcement-oa} tests whether the price effect is larger in PBUs that were historically more exposed to non-SME winners. \section{Firm-Level Employment Pre-Trends (RAIS 2011--2017)}

Figure~\ref{fig:pretrends-oa} plots the event-study coefficients on $\log(1+\text{v\'inculos})$ for treated firms (ever won a group-65 item in the BEC pre-period) versus controls (won only non-group-65 items in the same period). Pre-reference joint $F$-test is 1.46 ($p=0.198$) in the balanced panel with zero-fill, failing to reject parallel pre-trends. The always-present subset (firms with $\geq 1$ employment link every year 2011--2017) rejects more strongly; this reflects the selection of procurement winners on firm productivity and growth rather than a violation of the paper's item-level identification.
