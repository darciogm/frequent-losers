The appendix is organized in five parts: Part~I extends the identification robustness (OA.1--OA.7); Part~II develops the mechanism decomposition (OA.8--OA.12); Part~III documents policy-design extensions (OA.13--OA.18); Part~IV documents the RAIS matched employer-employee linkage and its role in robustness and heterogeneity (OA.19--OA.27); Part~V reports the synthetic control estimator (OA.28). \part{Data and Identification Robustness}

\section{Data Cleaning and Construction}

The primary data source is the full-census microdata of \emph{Bolsa Eletr\^onica de Compras} (BEC), the Sao Paulo state procurement platform, covering January 2016 through December 2019. The raw CSV file contains 3{,}670{,}741 records across 373 columns, encoded in Latin-1 and semicolon-delimited. The cleaning pipeline (\texttt{scripts/01\_clean.R}) selects 76 variables needed for the empirical analysis, casts string-valued columns (CNPJ, municipio, UF) to character to preserve leading zeros and the ``Sem Vencedor'' sentinel, and derives derived variables including \texttt{g65} (indicator for product group~65), \texttt{Pre} (indicator for $t<$March~2018), the 6-, 12-, and 18-month window masks, item and PBU factor encodings, and the CNPJ raiz (first 8 digits of the winning firm's 14-digit CNPJ). The cleaned data is cached as Parquet (\texttt{data/processed/paper2\_me\_epp.parquet}, version~3, compressed Snappy) and as an R data.table RDS (\texttt{/tmp/p2\_prepared.rds}). Subsequent analyses consume this RDS for speed.

The Parquet cache is regenerated whenever \texttt{PARQUET\_VERSION} in \texttt{scripts/utils.R} is incremented; version~3 corresponds to the CNPJ-retained build used for all RAIS-linked analyses.

\subsection*{OA.6.3 Goodman-Bacon (2021) Decomposition}
With a single treated cohort and 76 never-treated controls within the sample window, the \citet{goodmanbacon2021} decomposition collapses to a single comparison type (``Treated vs Untreated''), which carries 100\% of the TWFE weight. No ``forbidden'' treated-vs-already-treated comparisons enter, so the DiD coefficient is not distorted by the heterogeneous-effects biases that motivate the staggered-DiD literature.

\section{Formal Cross-Group Spillover Test}

\IfFileExists{../output/tables/tab_spillover.tex}{\input{../output/tables/tab_spillover.tex}}{}

The spillover test regresses control-group outcomes on $\mathit{Post} \times \mathit{Exposure}$, where $\mathit{Exposure}_c$ is the pre-period share of control group~$c$'s winners whose CNPJ raiz also won at least one Group-65 item in the same pre-period. The coefficient on the interaction is $+0.04$ with a standard error of $0.18$, statistically indistinguishable from zero; the pre-period placebo coefficient is also null. Multiplied by the mean exposure across groups (3.1\%), the implied bias on the headline price coefficient is $+0.001$ log-points, or approximately 1\% of the $-0.109$ estimate. The full diagnostic is in \texttt{output/tables/diag\_spillover.txt}.

\section{Phase-2 Bid-Distribution Moments}

The paper's mechanism finding rests on the minimum, maximum, mean, and standard deviation of the bids submitted in phase~2 of each electronic auction---the iterative stage in which registered suppliers progressively lower their offers in real time. Phase-1 sealed bids are too sparse in the Sao Paulo data to be usable (fewer than 1\% of rows have a non-zero min\_bid\_ph1), so the paper relies on phase~2 throughout. The moments are extracted ad-hoc from the raw CSV by \texttt{scripts/25\_bid\_moments.R}; 74.8\% of rows carry valid phase-2 data.

Table~\ref{tab:bid-moments-oa} reports the DiD on three bid-distribution outcomes: \textit{discount from reference} $=(p_\text{ref}-\min_\text{bid})/p_\text{ref}$, \textit{log SD of ph-2 bids}, and \textit{normalized bid range}. Each is estimated on the full sample and on the 2-, 3-, and $\geq\!5$-firm subsamples. The discount coefficient is $+0.104$ in the full sample and stays between $+0.074$ and $+0.098$ when firm count is held fixed. The log-SD coefficient starts at $-0.087$ and deepens monotonically to $-0.224$ at $N\!\geq\!5$.

\section{Causal Forest}

The \citet{athey2019,wager2018} causal forest locates where treatment effects concentrate across the covariate space. I train the forest on the 18-month completed-items sample using log reference value, log quantity, class dummies, the sealed-bid indicator, and PBU type as inputs. Figure~\ref{fig:cforest-varimp} shows the variable-importance diagnostic: item quantity dominates with an importance of 0.486, roughly an order of magnitude above the next covariate. Figure~\ref{fig:cforest-gate} then plots the group-average treatment effect (GATE) across quartiles of the estimated conditional-average treatment effect (CATE). The monotone decline from Q1 to Q4 is the heterogeneity signal that motivates the value-threshold counterfactual in Section~4.6.

\section{Quantile Difference-in-Differences}

The \citet{canay2011} quantile DiD on the residualized log-price distribution delivers a parallel check. Figure~\ref{fig:quantile-did-oa} plots the coefficient at each decile. The effect is strongly negative at the lower quantiles ($\tau=0.10$: $-0.623$; $\tau=0.25$: $-0.543$) and turns positive at the upper tail ($\tau=0.90$: $+0.445$). At the lower quantiles---where items are standardized and multiple capable suppliers exist---open competition drives prices down sharply; at the upper tail, items tend to be specialized with thinner markets, and the pattern reverses. The curve is the quantile-regression twin of the causal-forest GATE in Figure~\ref{fig:cforest-gate}, and of the residualized-QR diagnostic in Figure~\ref{fig:qte}. Three different estimators deliver the same heterogeneity story.

\section{Value-Threshold Counterfactual}

Table~\ref{tab:cf-oa} reports the per-quartile DiD coefficients used in the value-threshold counterfactual simulation. The simulation splits Group-65 pre-period items into quartiles of reference value (cutoffs R\$500 / US\$143, R\$1{,}875 / US\$536, R\$7{,}623 / US\$2{,}180 (at R\$3.50 per US dollar)), estimates the DiD within each quartile, and computes the fiscal cost recovered by exempting the top~$k$ quartiles. Because 92\% of pre-period Group-65 procurement value concentrates in the top quartile, exempting Q4 alone recovers 90\% of the counterfactual baseline fiscal cost.

The pharma decomposition splits Group 65 into class~6531 (medications subject to CMED price ceilings) and the remaining classes (non-pharma medical supplies). \part{RAIS Matched Employer-Employee Linkage}

Brazil's \emph{Rela\c{c}\~ao Anual de Informa\c{c}\~oes Sociais} (RAIS) is a matched employer-employee registry covering the universe of formal-sector employment relationships. This part links the winning firms in BEC to their RAIS profiles at the CNPJ-raiz (8-digit firm identifier) level, using the establishment-level (ESTB) files for 2011--2017. Of 17{,}857 distinct winning CNPJ raízes in the Paper-2 sample, 14{,}755 match RAIS 2017 nationwide (82.6\% coverage); within Sao Paulo state alone, 12{,}533 match (70.2\%). The link is produced by \texttt{scripts/05\_link\_rais.py}, a DuckDB-based ETL.

\section{Validation of the Self-Declared ME/EPP Label}

The BEC data includes a self-declared \texttt{fornec\_enquad} (firm-enquadramento) variable. Table~\ref{tab:rais-val} tests whether the headline DiD is sensitive to the label's classification noise by restricting the estimation sample to firms with (i) a RAIS match, (ii) active employment at most 49 (ME/EPP ceiling), (iii) active employment at most 9 (micro ceiling), and (iv) active employment strictly below 100 (excluding the top of the RAIS size distribution). The price coefficient is essentially invariant across these subsamples.
