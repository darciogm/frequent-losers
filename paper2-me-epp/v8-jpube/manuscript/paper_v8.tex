\title{\textbf{The Price of Exclusion:\\ SME Set-Asides in Public Procurement}}

\author[INSPER]{Darcio Genicolo-Martins}\ead{darciogm1@insper.edu.br}
\address[INSPER]{INSPER, S\~ao Paulo}

\begin{abstract}
Set-asides expand SME access by removing their rivals from the auction. A
set-aside replaces competition with eligibility. When the bidders it excludes
are the price-forming ones, the replacement is partial, expensive, and
outperformed by a price preference in standardized markets. I test this on
S\~ao Paulo's centralized
electronic procurement platform, where a recent legal reinterpretation
expanded SME-only tendering into medical and hospital supplies. Its
reverse-auction format records drop-out prices that, under the maintained
independent-private-values interpretation, reveal type-specific willingness
to supply and let me decompose the price effect into a lost-discipline
channel and a protected-pool offset. Static welfare
loss reaches \welfLossPctLthirtyNp{} percent of the open-regime price at
$\lambda=\lambdaMain$ in standardized non-pharmaceutical procurement;
pharmaceutical procurement carries a larger and more model-sensitive loss. A
10 percent SME price preference keeps non-SMEs in the auction at near-zero
price cost in standardized markets, so the relevant policy frontier sits
between competition-preserving and exclusionary redistribution, not between
SME support and none.
\end{abstract}
