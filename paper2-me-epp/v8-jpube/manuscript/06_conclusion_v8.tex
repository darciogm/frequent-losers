\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

This paper shifts the evaluation of SME set-asides from access alone to
price formation. A set-aside does not merely direct contracts toward
protected firms; it changes the bidder pool from which the auction price
is formed. When the excluded non-SMEs are the bidders that discipline the
relevant order statistic, SME access is purchased by weakening the
competitive mechanism itself.

The evidence from S\~ao Paulo's procurement reform shows that the protected
pool responds, but incompletely. SME participation rises after the expansion
of SME-only tendering, yet the post-policy protected pool does not recreate
the price discipline supplied by excluded non-SMEs. In standardized
non-pharmaceutical procurement, this leaves a large static cost: the full
set-aside generates a welfare loss of \welfLossPctLthirtyNp{} percent of the
open-regime price at $\lambda=\lambdaMain$. Pharmaceutical procurement
exhibits the boundary of the exercise. There, the protected pool is thinner,
composition changes more, and the welfare ranking becomes model-sensitive.

The policy implication is not that SME support should be abandoned. It is
that the instrument matters. A full set-aside is a high-redistribution
instrument because it forces eligibility by removing rival bidders. A price
preference is a lower-redistribution benchmark, but it preserves the
non-SME bidders that discipline the price-forming order statistic. The
relevant comparison is therefore not SME support versus no support. It is
exclusionary redistribution versus support that keeps the price-forming
bidder pool inside the auction.

Full exclusion remains defensible when the planner places enough value on
SME surplus, market access, or dynamic capacity gains to justify the
missing competitive discipline. But that defense must be explicit. In
thick standardized markets, where a preference rule can tilt allocation
without deleting rival bidders, exclusion is an expensive way to
redistribute. Support for SMEs need not remove the bidders that discipline
the price.
