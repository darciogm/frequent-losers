\begin{letter}{The Editors\\Journal of Public Economics}

\opening{Dear Editors,}

I am submitting ``The Price of Exclusion: SME Set-Asides in Public
Procurement'' for consideration at the \emph{Journal of Public Economics}.
The paper documents that an SME set-aside replaces competition with
eligibility, and that where the bidders the rule excludes are the ones
forming the price, the replacement is partial and expensive.

The contribution sits between the welfare-policy literature on procurement
instruments---Marion (2007), Athey, Coey, and Levin (2013), Krasnokutskaya
and Seim (2011), Nakabayashi (2013)---and the public-finance treatment of
redistribution under a marginal cost of public funds. That literature has
established that SME-favoring rules can be costly. It has not separated how
much of the cost comes from removing rival bidders and how much comes from
the protected pool's response. I rebuild the comparison as a frontier
between support that preserves competitive discipline inside the auction and
support that achieves access by deleting rival bidders. Drop-out bids from
electronic reverse auctions, in a S\~ao Paulo set-aside expansion, identify
where on that frontier a 10 percent price preference and a full set-aside
sit.

The paper fits the welfare-anchored tradition of public-spending evaluation
that Marion (2007) launched in this journal and that connects to the broader
effectiveness-of-government agenda represented by work like Best, Hjort, and
Szakonyi (2023). I declare no conflicts of interest, and the paper has not
been submitted elsewhere.

Replication materials are available on request at \texttt{darciogm1@insper.edu.br}.
