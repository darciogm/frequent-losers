\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

A decade of empirical work has established that restricting a procurement auction to small firms raises winning prices. Two non-exclusive mechanisms operate behind that markup: the bidder pool shrinks, and the surviving firms bid differently. This paper estimates the total impact of S\~ao Paulo's \policyCutoffMonth{} SME-only extension and decomposes it into these two channels. The structural framework---asymmetric IPV with Krasnokutskaya UH cleaning, Haile--Tamer drop-out point-identification, and observed-equilibrium-entry treatment in the Athey--Seira spirit---is established in the literature; the contribution is to combine these conditions with a single clean legal trigger and to price the welfare consequence against a specific alternative instrument.

The decomposition delivers four findings. \emph{Sheltered bidding}---the within-auction price formation that survives when the bidder pool collapses---accounts for \bneShareIntensNp~percent of the simulated total effect in non-pharmaceuticals and \bneShareIntensPh~percent in pharmaceuticals; the surviving pool's bidding, not its size, is what the set-aside mostly buys. The participation response works in the opposite direction, attenuating a latent shock that would otherwise be \latentShockRatioNp~percent larger in non-pharma and \latentShockRatioPh~percent larger in pharma---the government's partial offset, not the source of the markup. Translating to welfare, the loss reaches \welfLossPctLthirtyNp~percent of the open-regime baseline price in non-pharma and \welfLossPctLthirtyPh~percent in pharma at $\lambda = \lambdaMain$---an annual cost of R\$\,\welfAnnualBRLLo~million on a single product group of S\~ao Paulo's R\$\,\becAnnualVolBRL~billion centralized platform. Set against an alternative instrument, a \optPrefThreshold{} SME price preference welfare-dominates the set-aside in thick standardized non-pharmaceutical markets across both the main and the strict-invariance specifications, at near-zero fiscal cost; in thin heterogeneous pharmaceutical markets the ranking depends on how the post-policy SME pool is modeled, with the implied welfare weight on SME surplus crossing the utilitarian benchmark only under strict invariance ($w^{\text{SME}}_\star = \welfWeightSiPh$).

The policy reading is conditional, not universal. A moderate price preference is recommended where the protected pool is thick enough and the good standardized enough to recover nearly the same distributive gain at a fraction of the welfare cost; bidder exclusion remains defensible only where the pool is thin, the good heterogeneous, and the planner's welfare weight on SME surplus high enough to absorb the larger allocative wedge. The structural exercise leaves three margins outside its scope---structural identification of entry costs, dynamic capacity accumulation, and the FPSB-with-preference equilibrium---each flagged in Section~\ref{sec:discussion}. The choice belongs at the level of the product class, not the level of the procurement code.
