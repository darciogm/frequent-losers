\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

A decade of empirical work has established that restricting a procurement auction to small firms raises winning prices. Two non-exclusive mechanisms operate behind that markup---the bidder pool shrinks, and the surviving firms bid differently---and this paper estimates the total impact of S\~ao Paulo's \policyCutoffMonth{} SME-only extension while decomposing it into these two channels. The structural framework (asymmetric IPV with Krasnokutskaya UH cleaning, Haile--Tamer drop-out point-identification, observed-equilibrium-entry \`a la Athey--Seira) is established in the literature; the contribution is to combine these conditions with a single clean legal trigger and to price the welfare consequence against a specific alternative instrument.

\emph{Sheltered bidding}---the within-auction price formation that survives when the bidder pool collapses---accounts for \bneShareIntensNp/\bneShareIntensPh~percent of the simulated total effect in non-pharma/pharma; the surviving pool's bidding, not its size, is what the set-aside mostly buys. The participation response attenuates a latent shock that would otherwise be \latentShockRatioNp/\latentShockRatioPh~percent larger---a partial offset, not the source of the markup. The implied welfare loss reaches \welfLossPctLthirtyNp/\welfLossPctLthirtyPh~percent of the open-regime baseline price at $\lambda = \lambdaMain$---an annual cost of R\$\,\welfAnnualBRLLo~million on a single product group of S\~ao Paulo's R\$\,\becAnnualVolBRL~billion platform. A \optPrefThreshold{} price preference welfare-dominates the set-aside in thick standardized non-pharma markets across both specifications at near-zero fiscal cost; pharma's ranking depends on how the post-policy SME pool is modeled, with $w^{\text{SME}}_\star = \welfWeightSiPh$ under strict invariance.

The policy reading is conditional, not universal: a moderate preference is recommended where the protected pool is thick enough and the good standardized enough to recover nearly the same distributive gain at a fraction of the welfare cost; bidder exclusion remains defensible only where the pool is thin, the good heterogeneous, and the planner's welfare weight on SME surplus high enough to absorb the larger allocative wedge. The framework identifies that band within one platform; cross-platform replication on federal ComprasNet, on municipal procurement, and on non-Brazilian set-aside reforms is the natural next step, with the structural objects developed here---within-auction component, participation-margin offset, welfare-weight identity---providing the comparable measurement layer. The choice belongs at the level of the product class, not the level of the procurement code.
