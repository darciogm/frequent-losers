\section{Robustness}\label{sec:robustness}

Nine dimensions of robustness discipline the headline: the strict-invariance limiting case; the cost-distribution estimator ($F_c^k$ regime); the kernel bandwidth used in the Convite GPV cross-check; the bid-level sample filter; the temporal window; the phased BEC enablement check on the cutoff date; the set of alternative policy variants considered; bid-coordination diagnostics; and the Maskin--Riley FPSB upper bound on the V3 expected price. Three claims survive each in turn. \emph{(a)} The within-auction component remains the larger of the two components in all six (class $\times$ window) cells; its share stays in the \withinShareLow--\withinShareHigh~percent range across cost-distribution estimators and across the two specifications of the post-policy SME pool. \emph{(b)} The welfare ranking V3 $\succ$ V0 in thick standardized non-pharmaceutical markets is preserved under cost-affiliation up to $\rho_c = \ipvRhoMax$, under the FPSB upper bound, and across both equilibrium-selection and strict-invariance treatments of the post-policy SME pool. \emph{(c)} The pharmaceutical bifurcation between the two specifications is itself the diagnostic of where the modeling treatment of the protected pool is first-order---a substantive result delineating where the policy ranking is robust and where it demands caution.

\subsection{Strict-primitive-invariance benchmark}

Table~\ref{tab:v3_strict_invariance} reports the limiting case in which $F_c^{\text{SME,Post}}$ is replaced by $F_c^{\text{SME,Pre}}$ while preserving the observed post-policy SME entry count. This is the strict-invariance bound on the equilibrium-selection main specification of Assumption~\ref{a:setaside}. The simulated total price effect is $+\siDeltaTotalNp$ in non-pharmaceuticals and $+\siDeltaTotalPh$ in pharmaceuticals, with within-auction shares of \strictShareNp{} and \strictSharePh{} percent, respectively. Welfare loss at $\lambda = \lambdaMain$ is \siWelfLossPctNp~percent of $p_{S_1}$ in non-pharmaceuticals and \siWelfLossPctPh~percent in pharmaceuticals.

What changes is the policy ranking in the thin heterogeneous pharma market. In non-pharma, the \optPrefThreshold{} price preference remains Pareto-superior under strict invariance, just as under the main specification. In pharma, the preference ranking reverses and the implied indifference weight (defined formally in equation~\eqref{eq:welfare_weight}, Section~\ref{sec:discussion}) falls to $w^{\text{SME}}_\star = \welfWeightSiPh$, versus \welfWeightMainPh{} under the main equilibrium-selection specification. The bifurcation is the diagnostic: it identifies precisely where equilibrium selection among protected bidders is first-order, agreeing across the two readings in thick standardized markets and separating them in thin heterogeneous ones. Read economically, strict invariance imposes that the SMEs entering under the protected regime draw from the same cost distribution as the SMEs that entered under the open regime---the most generous assumption to the policy, since it implies the protective regime selects entrants whose cost primitives match incumbents'. The realistic alternative is that protected entrants draw from the higher-cost tail (firms unprofitable under the open regime that become profitable under exclusion), which is what equilibrium selection in the main specification captures. Strict invariance therefore understates the welfare cost in pharma; the main-specification cost of \welfLossPctLthirtyPh~percent of $p_{S_1}$ is the welfare-conservative number relative to the equilibrium-selection mechanism, not an aggressive upper bound.

\subsection{Cost-distribution regime}

The baseline all-bidders estimator biases the upper tail of $F_c^k$ slightly downward; losers-only biases it materially upward; a Turnbull NPMLE that treats the winner as left-censored delivers point identification. Table~\ref{tab:v3_sensitivity_fc} runs the BNE decomposition under each. The three point estimates for $p_{S_3} - p_{S_1}$ are \costRegimeLosersNp/\costRegimeLosersPh{} (losers-only), \costRegimeAllNp/\costRegimeAllPh{} (all-bidders, baseline), and \costRegimeTurnNp/\costRegimeTurnPh{} (Turnbull) for non-pharma and pharma respectively. All cells lie within \turnbullDeviationPct~percent of the all-bidders point estimate (non-pharmaceutical losers-only $+\costRegimeDevLoNp$ percent; non-pharmaceutical Turnbull $-\costRegimeDevTurnNp$ percent; pharmaceutical losers-only $+\costRegimeDevLoPh$ percent; pharmaceutical Turnbull $+\costRegimeDevTurnPh$ percent). The bootstrap CIs (Table~\ref{tab:v3_bootstrap_ci}) overlap materially across regimes. Turnbull raises the within-auction share in pharmaceuticals from \bneShareIntensPh~percent (all-bidders) to \turnPhShareUp~percent---if anything strengthening the main interpretation. The all-bidders approximation used throughout the paper is defensible without the Turnbull machinery; readers who prefer point identification should see Turnbull and the same decomposition.

\paragraph{Sensitivity to cost affiliation.} A Gaussian-copula relaxation of the IPV restriction (Online Appendix, Table~OA.1) couples the type-specific cost marginals at within-auction correlation $\rho_c \in \ipvRhoGrid$. The within-auction share moves by less than \ipvShareDriftPp{} percentage points in either class, and the total simulated effect $p_{S_3} - p_{S_1}$ moves by less than \ipvTotalDriftPct~percent of the IPV baseline. The \withinShareLow--\withinShareHigh~percent within-auction share range remains valid for $\rho_c \le \ipvRhoMax$. Higher affiliation levels are inconsistent with the cross-modality consistency check of Table~\ref{tab:v3_cross_modality}, which would reject GPV-vs-drop-out convergence under strong common-values contamination.

\subsection{Kernel bandwidth}

The cross-modality check in Section~\ref{sec:identification} uses GPV inversion on Convite bids, which requires a kernel density estimate $g(b)$. Silverman's rule-of-thumb $h = 1.06 \hat\sigma n^{-1/5}$ is the baseline. Table~\ref{tab:v3_bandwidth_grid} runs the GPV under $h$-factors of \bandwidthGrid. The resulting range of $c_{0.5}$ (median cost) across the eight (period $\times$ pharma $\times$ type) Convite strata is \bandwidthLowPct--\bandwidthHighPct~percent of the baseline in seven strata; the thinnest cell (Convite pharma SME Pre, \bandwidthThinN{} auctions) reaches \bandwidthThinCellPct~percent and is the only cell where bandwidth dispersion exceeds the BNE Monte Carlo noise of \monteCarloNoiseLo--\monteCarloNoiseHi~percent. Bandwidth is not a first-order concern for the identification.

\subsection{Bid-level sample filter}

The baseline filter is $c_\epsilon \le \filterCepsUpper$ and $n \ge \filterMinBidders$. Table~\ref{tab:v3_filter_sensitivity} compares four alternatives.
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Tight} ($c_\epsilon \le 2$, $n \ge 3$): $p_{S_3} - p_{S_1}$ is \filterTightDropNp~percent lower in non-pharma (\filterTightNp{} vs.\ \filterBaseNp) and \filterTightDropPh~percent lower in pharma (\filterTightPh{} vs.\ \filterBasePh).
\item \emph{Very tight} ($c_\epsilon \le \filterVtightCeps$, $n \ge \filterVtightNbid$): \filterVtightDropNp{} and \filterVtightDropPh~percent lower respectively. The intensive share in pharma jumps to \filterVtightSharePh~percent---tightening the cost distribution to its central bulk effectively removes the tail where the entry response operates.
\item \emph{Loose} ($c_\epsilon \le 5$): \filterLooseUpNp~percent higher in non-pharma and \filterLooseUpPh~percent higher in pharma. The pharma move is concentrated in the extra observations with $c_\epsilon \in (\filterCepsUpper, 5]$---almost always outliers at the 500--1000 percent-of-reference band, probably data-entry errors at the hundred-times level. The baseline cut is designed to truncate these.
\end{itemize}
The baseline sits between tight and loose in both classes, is inside the bootstrap CI under any reasonable cut, and can be defended directly: the \filterCepsUpper{}x-reference upper bound catches plausible error patterns while preserving \filterPreservedPct~percent of the data.

\subsection{Temporal window}

Table~\ref{tab:v3_window_sensitivity} varies the symmetric window around the \policyCutoffMonth{} cutoff. ``18m'' is the baseline (\structuralWindowM{} months on each side, \nDataMonths{} months total); the 12m and 6m alternatives are 12 and 6 months on each side respectively. The total effect is $p_{S_3} - p_{S_1} = +\windowTotalEighteenNp$ (non-pharma 18m), $+\windowTotalTwelveNp$ (12m), and $+\windowTotalSixNp$ (6m); $+\windowTotalEighteenPh$ (pharma 18m), $+\windowTotalTwelvePh$ (12m), $+\windowTotalSixPh$ (6m). The non-pharmaceutical effect is essentially flat across windows; the pharmaceutical effect is monotonically increasing as the window shrinks, consistent with a transitional period in which the within-auction adjustment establishes more quickly than the participation-margin adjustment.

The within-auction share is comparably stable: \windowShareEighteenNp/\windowShareTwelveNp/\windowShareSixNp\% in non-pharma and \windowShareEighteenPh/\windowShareTwelvePh/\windowShareSixPh\% in pharma across the 18m/12m/6m windows. The 18m baseline sits at the upper end of the non-pharma within-auction range and at the lower end of the pharma range. The share varies by \windowShareDriftNp{} percentage points in non-pharma and \windowShareDriftPh{} percentage points in pharma across the three windows, and the within-auction component remains the larger of the two components in all six (class $\times$ window) cells. 18m is the mature, steady-state choice, with shorter windows providing a corroborating short-run check.

\subsection{Phased adoption}

The institutional timeline of \S\ref{sec:institutional} documents that BEC operationalized the SME-only functionality in two acts (COMUNICADO BEC \comunicadoBECone{} in July 2017 and COMUNICADO BEC \comunicadoBECtwo{} in November 2017) before the empirical cutoff of \policyCutoffMonth, which captures the moment of mass take-up by Group-65 PBUs rather than formal regulatory enablement. As a robustness check, Table~\ref{tab:phased_adoption} re-estimates the headline DiD coefficient on the sample with the eight months $m \in [\phasedExcludedFrom, \phasedExcludedTo]$ excluded---i.e., dropping the BEC enablement window between July 2017 and February 2018, the period during which the new functionality was technically available but not yet broadly used in Group 65.

The DiD coefficient on $g65 \times \text{Pre}$ is $\phasedDidTrunc$ (s.e.\ $\phasedDidSe$) on the truncated sample, versus $\phasedDidFull$ on the full sample, both significant at the 1 percent level on standard errors clustered at the item level. The \phasedReductionPct~percent reduction is consistent with the enablement window contributing to but not dominating the headline---and the structural counterfactual, identified from cross-regime cost-distribution differences pooled within the 18-month post window rather than from a single discrete event, is invariant to this temporal restriction by construction. The structural identification therefore inherits the cleaner subset of the variation while the DiD benchmark serves as a calibration anchor under either window.

\subsection{Alternative policy variants}

The \optPrefThreshold{} price preference in Section~\ref{sec:pareto} is informative partly because the alternative variants reveal what bidder exclusion is buying. Table~\ref{tab:v3_apv} also reports two intermediate variants that serve as policy-design sensitivities:

\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{V1} (partial set-aside, purely diagnostic): \vOneMidPct~percent of auctions run SME-only at the Post pool, \vOneMidPct~percent open at the Pre pool. V1 is not a policy proposal in current legislation; it serves to demonstrate the non-linearity of pool restriction. $\Delta p$ is \vOneGapLo--\vOneGapHi~percent of V0's---roughly a third, indicating that a partial rule buys roughly proportional fiscal savings at the cost of a more complex administrative rule.
\item \emph{V2} (entry-isolated counterfactual): full set-aside with the SME pool held at pre-period size (no participation response). $\Delta p$ is \latentShockTimesNp~percent of V0 in non-pharmaceuticals and \latentShockTimesPh~percent in pharmaceuticals. Without endogenous entry the simulated policy effect would be materially larger; the participation-margin component absorbs \latentShockRatioNp~percent of what would otherwise be a heavier within-auction shock in non-pharmaceuticals (and \latentShockRatioPh~percent in pharmaceuticals). This is why Section~\ref{sec:results} reads the participation response as a partial offset to a larger latent within-auction shock.
\end{itemize}

Taken together, V1--V3 span the policy design space between the status-quo full set-aside and the open regime. V2 isolates what the entry response is worth; V1 prices a middle-ground design; V3 shows that a well-targeted preference instrument captures the SME-protection value at essentially zero price cost in thick markets and gives a diagnostic benchmark in thin markets.

\subsection{Bid-coordination diagnostics}\label{sec:bid_coord}

The structural identification of $F_c^k$ from Preg\~ao drop-out bids holds under any bidding configuration in which exit-at-cost is the weakly dominant strategy for each individual bidder \citep{haile2003}---a property that survives within-coalition pricing schemes that only adjust the timing of withdrawal among coordinated bidders, since the recovered $F_c^k$ averages over both the coordinated and independent participants in each auction. The welfare comparison between V0 and V3 therefore inherits the same robustness: the cost primitives are recovered jointly across coordinated and independent bidders, and the ranking V3 $\succ$ V0 in thick standardized markets is preserved under the recovered primitives.

Four diagnostic screens, reported in the replication package, characterize the residual bidding patterns: (i) a Conley-style close-pair screen on UH-clean residuals; (ii) the same screen with the null distribution conditioned on the number of bidders and on intra-auction bid-range quartiles, absorbing clustering attributable to reference-price anchoring and bid-increment discreteness; (iii) the \citet{bajariye2003} persistent-pair test, which examines whether specific firm pairs co-bid more frequently than under firm-label independence; and (iv) the same test with the permutation null restricted within CADMAT product class. Bid-pair clustering exceeds independence baselines in all four strata; conditioning on product class closes roughly half the gap in non-pharmaceutical strata and a smaller share in pharmaceutical strata, where only two to three CADMAT classes are present. Modern references on pair-based collusion detection in procurement \citep{conley2016, kawainakabayashi2022} emphasize that screening evidence is suggestive rather than dispositive in the absence of independent confirmation, and the residual clustering observed here is consistent with several non-collusive mechanisms the screens cannot separate from coordination---subcontracting networks among SME suppliers, joint ventures formally registered with BEC, regional logistical specialization within product class, and sub-class specialization that the CADMAT level does not absorb. The cross-modality consistency check between Convite GPV inversion and Preg\~ao drop-out recovery (Section~\ref{sec:identification}) provides the natural discipline on this dimension in the stratum where it is least confounded by the policy reform.

\subsection{FPSB equivalent of V3 — Maskin-Riley upper bound}\label{sec:fpsb_bound}

Section~\ref{sec:results} computes the \optPrefThreshold{} SME price preference V3 under Vickrey equivalence---that is, treating the expected transaction price under V3 as identical to the expected transaction price under the corresponding ascending-clock translation. Under asymmetric IPV, \citet{maskinriley2000} show that revenue equivalence between FPSB and ascending-clock formats does not generally hold; the FPSB expected price differs from the Vickrey expected price by an amount bounded above by a function of the type-specific cost-distribution dispersion. This robustness reports that bound and shows that the welfare ranking V3 $\succ$ V0 is preserved under the FPSB upper bound.

Following the first-order argument in \citet{maskinriley2000} and the asymmetric-IPV bid-function structure formalized in \citet{lebrun1999}, the FPSB-Vickrey expected-price gap on V3 (with preference parameter $\rho = \prefRhoSmall$) is bounded above by $\rho \cdot \bar\sigma_c$, where $\bar\sigma_c$ is the SME-share-weighted average of the type-specific coefficient of variation of the UH-clean cost distributions. Computed from the data: $\bar\sigma_c$ is \fpsbCovNp{} in non-pharma and \fpsbCovPh{} in pharma, delivering an FPSB upper-bound gap of approximately \fpsbBoundLoPct--\fpsbBoundHiPct~percent of the reference price in either class.
