\section{Robustness}\label{sec:robustness}

Nine dimensions of robustness discipline the headline: the strict-invariance limiting case; the cost-distribution estimator ($F_c^k$ regime); the kernel bandwidth used in the Convite GPV cross-check; the bid-level sample filter; the temporal window; the phased BEC enablement check on the cutoff date; the set of alternative policy variants considered; bid-coordination diagnostics; and the Maskin--Riley FPSB upper bound on the V3 expected price. Three claims survive each in turn. \emph{(a)} The within-auction component remains the larger of the two components in all six (class $\times$ window) cells; its share stays in the \withinShareLow--\withinShareHigh~percent range across cost-distribution estimators and across the two specifications of the post-policy SME pool. \emph{(b)} The welfare ranking V3 $\succ$ V0 in thick standardized non-pharmaceutical markets is preserved under cost-affiliation up to $\rho_c = \ipvRhoMax$, under the FPSB upper bound, and across both the main (point-by-point estimation of $\hat F_c^{\text{SME,Post}}$) and strict-invariance ($F_c^{\text{SME,Post}} = F_c^{\text{SME,Pre}}$) specifications of the post-policy SME pool. \emph{(c)} The pharmaceutical bifurcation between the two specifications is itself the diagnostic of where the modeling treatment of the protected pool is first-order---a substantive result delineating where the policy ranking is robust and where it demands caution.

\subsection{Strict-primitive-invariance benchmark}

Table~\ref{tab:v3_strict_invariance} reports the limiting case in which $F_c^{\text{SME,Post}}$ is replaced by $F_c^{\text{SME,Pre}}$ while preserving the observed post-policy SME entry count. This is the nested benchmark to the main specification of Assumption~\ref{a:setaside} (Section~\ref{sec:model}), obtained by imposing the equality the main specification allows. The simulated total price effect is $+\siDeltaTotalNp$ in non-pharmaceuticals and $+\siDeltaTotalPh$ in pharmaceuticals, with within-auction shares of \strictShareNp{} and \strictSharePh{} percent, respectively. Welfare loss at $\lambda = \lambdaMain$ is \siWelfLossPctNp~percent of $p_{S_1}$ in non-pharmaceuticals and \siWelfLossPctPh~percent in pharmaceuticals.

What changes is the policy ranking in the thin heterogeneous pharma market. In non-pharma, the \optPrefThreshold{} price preference remains Pareto-superior under strict invariance, just as under the main specification. In pharma, the preference ranking reverses and the implied indifference weight (defined formally in equation~\eqref{eq:welfare_weight}, Section~\ref{sec:discussion}) falls to $w^{\text{SME}}_\star = \welfWeightSiPh$, versus \welfWeightMainPh{} under the main specification. The bifurcation is the substantive object the framework can bracket but not adjudicate without an additional input. Read empirically, the main specification absorbs the entire pre-vs-post empirical difference in $\hat F_c^{\text{SME}}$ into the SME cost primitive used in the counterfactual; strict invariance imposes that this difference is zero. If the empirical difference reflects equilibrium selection (firms previously unprofitable under the open regime that become profitable under exclusion drawing from a higher-cost tail), the main specification is correctly attributing the cost; if it reflects a within-window technology shift orthogonal to the policy, strict invariance is closer to correct. The paper does not adjudicate between these readings---neither is a tested model---and reports both as bounds on the welfare conclusion. In pharma, the bounds straddle the V0 vs.\ V3 ranking; in non-pharma, they agree.

\subsection{Pharma SME firm turnover: discriminating between selection and invariance}\label{sec:turnover}

The pair of bounds reported in Section~\ref{sec:robustness} brackets the welfare conclusion under two contrasting attributions of the same empirical fact---the post-policy SME distribution being measurably different from the pre-policy one. Adjudicating between the two readings requires an empirical signature that distinguishes selection (firms previously unprofitable under the open regime now profitable under exclusion) from a within-window technology shift among incumbents. The natural signature is firm turnover: under selection, post-period SME bids should come substantially from firms ABSENT in the pre-period pool; under strict invariance, post-period bids should be dominated by continuing firms with the same cost primitive.

Table~\ref{tab:pharma_firm_turnover} reports the test. The pharmaceutical SME pool grows from \turnoverPhNPre{} pre-period firms to \turnoverPhNPost{} post-period firms (\turnoverPhNNew{} new entrants, only one exit). \emph{\turnoverPhPctNewFirms~percent of post-period pharma SME firms are new (absent pre)}, contributing \turnoverPhPctNewBids~percent of post-period pharma SME bids. The non-pharma pool, by contrast, grows from \turnoverNpNPre{} to \turnoverNpNPost{} firms with only \turnoverNpPctNewFirms~percent new firms contributing \turnoverNpPctNewBids~percent of bids.

\input{../output/tables/tab_pharma_firm_turnover.tex}

The pharmaceutical composition shift is empirically large and runs in the direction selection predicts. The bifurcation reported in Section~\ref{sec:welfare} is therefore not a modeling artifact: the empirical difference between $\hat F_c^{\text{SME,Pre}}$ and $\hat F_c^{\text{SME,Post}}$ reflects a real change in who the active SME bidders are, with new pharmaceutical entrants supplying nearly two-fifths of post-cutoff bids. Strict invariance, which imposes that this composition shift carries no information about the cost primitive, is the welfare-conservative reading in pharma; the main specification is the welfare-correct reading if the new entrants draw from a higher-cost tail (consistent with their pre-policy non-participation), and is mismeasured upward only if the new firms happen to draw from the same distribution as continuing firms despite their distinct entry decision. The turnover evidence rules out the strongest version of the latter possibility.

\subsection{Cost-distribution regime}

The baseline all-bidders estimator biases the upper tail of $F_c^k$ slightly downward; losers-only biases it materially upward; a Turnbull NPMLE that treats the winner as left-censored delivers point identification. Table~\ref{tab:v3_sensitivity_fc} runs the BNE decomposition under each. The three point estimates for $p_{S_3} - p_{S_1}$ are \costRegimeLosersNp/\costRegimeLosersPh{} (losers-only), \costRegimeAllNp/\costRegimeAllPh{} (all-bidders, baseline), and \costRegimeTurnNp/\costRegimeTurnPh{} (Turnbull) for non-pharma and pharma respectively. All cells lie within \turnbullDeviationPct~percent of the all-bidders point estimate (non-pharmaceutical losers-only $+\costRegimeDevLoNp$ percent; non-pharmaceutical Turnbull $-\costRegimeDevTurnNp$ percent; pharmaceutical losers-only $+\costRegimeDevLoPh$ percent; pharmaceutical Turnbull $+\costRegimeDevTurnPh$ percent). The bootstrap CIs (Table~\ref{tab:v3_bootstrap_ci}) overlap materially across regimes. Turnbull raises the within-auction share in pharmaceuticals from \bneShareIntensPh~percent (all-bidders) to \turnPhShareUp~percent---if anything strengthening the main interpretation. The all-bidders approximation used throughout the paper is defensible without the Turnbull machinery; readers who prefer point identification should see Turnbull and the same decomposition.

\paragraph{Sensitivity to cost affiliation.} A Gaussian-copula relaxation of the IPV restriction (Online Appendix, Table~OA.1) couples the type-specific cost marginals at within-auction correlation $\rho_c \in \ipvRhoGrid$. The within-auction share moves by less than \ipvShareDriftPp{} percentage points in either class, and the total simulated effect $p_{S_3} - p_{S_1}$ moves by less than \ipvTotalDriftPct~percent of the IPV baseline. The \withinShareLow--\withinShareHigh~percent within-auction share range remains valid for $\rho_c \le \ipvRhoMax$. The upper bound $\rho_c = \ipvRhoMax$ is the largest affiliation level consistent with the cross-modality consistency check of Table~\ref{tab:v3_cross_modality}: under stronger common-values contamination ($\rho_c > \ipvRhoMax$), the Convite GPV and Preg\~ao drop-out recoveries of the same primitive would diverge beyond the bootstrap CI in pharma non-SME Pre, which the data does not deliver. The reported $\rho_c$ grid is therefore the empirically defensible band rather than an arbitrary range.

\paragraph{Kernel bandwidth.} The Convite GPV inversion uses Silverman's rule $h = 1.06 \hat\sigma n^{-1/5}$ as the baseline. Table~\ref{tab:v3_bandwidth_grid} runs the GPV under $h$-factors of \bandwidthGrid: median-cost $c_{0.5}$ moves \bandwidthLowPct--\bandwidthHighPct~percent of the baseline in seven of eight strata, with the thinnest cell (\bandwidthThinN{} auctions) reaching \bandwidthThinCellPct~percent---the only cell where bandwidth dispersion exceeds BNE Monte Carlo noise of \monteCarloNoiseLo--\monteCarloNoiseHi~percent.

\subsection{Bid-level sample filter}

The baseline filter is $c_\epsilon \le \filterCepsUpper$ and $n \ge \filterMinBidders$. Table~\ref{tab:v3_filter_sensitivity} compares four alternatives.
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Tight} ($c_\epsilon \le 2$, $n \ge 3$): $p_{S_3} - p_{S_1}$ is \filterTightDropNp~percent lower in non-pharma (\filterTightNp{} vs.\ \filterBaseNp) and \filterTightDropPh~percent lower in pharma (\filterTightPh{} vs.\ \filterBasePh).
\item \emph{Very tight} ($c_\epsilon \le \filterVtightCeps$, $n \ge \filterVtightNbid$): \filterVtightDropNp{} and \filterVtightDropPh~percent lower respectively. The intensive share in pharma jumps to \filterVtightSharePh~percent---tightening the cost distribution to its central bulk effectively removes the tail where the entry response operates.
\item \emph{Loose} ($c_\epsilon \le 5$): \filterLooseUpNp~percent higher in non-pharma and \filterLooseUpPh~percent higher in pharma. The pharma move is concentrated in the extra observations with $c_\epsilon \in (\filterCepsUpper, 5]$---almost always outliers at the 500--1000 percent-of-reference band, probably data-entry errors at the hundred-times level. The baseline cut is designed to truncate these.
\end{itemize}
The baseline sits between tight and loose in both classes, is inside the bootstrap CI under any reasonable cut, and can be defended directly: the \filterCepsUpper{}x-reference upper bound catches plausible error patterns while preserving \filterPreservedPct~percent of the data.

\subsection{Temporal window}

Table~\ref{tab:v3_window_sensitivity} varies the symmetric window around the \policyCutoffMonth{} cutoff. ``18m'' is the baseline (\structuralWindowM{} months on each side, \nDataMonths{} months total); the 12m and 6m alternatives are 12 and 6 months on each side respectively. The total effect is $p_{S_3} - p_{S_1} = +\windowTotalEighteenNp$ (non-pharma 18m), $+\windowTotalTwelveNp$ (12m), and $+\windowTotalSixNp$ (6m); $+\windowTotalEighteenPh$ (pharma 18m), $+\windowTotalTwelvePh$ (12m), $+\windowTotalSixPh$ (6m). The non-pharmaceutical effect is essentially flat across windows; the pharmaceutical effect is monotonically increasing as the window shrinks, consistent with a transitional period in which the within-auction adjustment establishes more quickly than the participation-margin adjustment.

The within-auction share is comparably stable: \windowShareEighteenNp/\windowShareTwelveNp/\windowShareSixNp\% in non-pharma and \windowShareEighteenPh/\windowShareTwelvePh/\windowShareSixPh\% in pharma across the 18m/12m/6m windows. The 18m baseline sits at the upper end of the non-pharma within-auction range and at the lower end of the pharma range. The share varies by \windowShareDriftNp{} percentage points in non-pharma and \windowShareDriftPh{} percentage points in pharma across the three windows, and the within-auction component remains the larger of the two components in all six (class $\times$ window) cells. 18m is the mature, steady-state choice, with shorter windows providing a corroborating short-run check.

\paragraph{Phased adoption.} BEC operationalized the SME-only functionality in two acts (Comunicados \comunicadoBECone{} and \comunicadoBECtwo, July and November 2017) before the empirical cutoff of \policyCutoffMonth, which captures mass take-up by Group-65 PBUs rather than formal enablement. Table~\ref{tab:phased_adoption} re-estimates the headline DiD on the sample with the BEC enablement window $m \in [\phasedExcludedFrom, \phasedExcludedTo]$ excluded: $\hat\beta = \phasedDidTrunc$ (s.e.\ $\phasedDidSe$) versus $\phasedDidFull$ on the full sample, both significant at 1 percent. The \phasedReductionPct~percent reduction is consistent with the enablement window contributing to but not dominating the headline; the structural counterfactual is invariant to the temporal restriction by construction since identification pools cross-regime cost-distribution differences within the 18-month post window rather than relying on a single discrete event.

\paragraph{Alternative policy variants.} Table~\ref{tab:v3_apv} reports two intermediate variants alongside V0 and V3. \emph{V1} (partial set-aside, purely diagnostic): \vOneMidPct~percent of auctions run SME-only at the Post pool, \vOneMidPct~percent open at the Pre pool---$\Delta p$ is \vOneGapLo--\vOneGapHi~percent of V0's, roughly a third, indicating proportional fiscal savings at the cost of administrative complexity. \emph{V2} (entry-isolated): full set-aside with the SME pool held at pre-period size, $\Delta p = $ \latentShockTimesNp{}/\latentShockTimesPh~percent of V0; without endogenous entry the participation margin absorbs \latentShockRatioNp/\latentShockRatioPh~percent of a heavier within-auction shock. Together V1--V3 span the policy-design space between the full set-aside and the open regime: V2 isolates entry's value, V1 prices a middle design, and V3 captures the SME-protection value at essentially zero price cost in thick markets while providing a diagnostic benchmark in thin ones.

\paragraph{Bid-coordination diagnostics.}\label{sec:bid_coord} Two concerns motivate this subsection. First, the structural identification of $F_c^k$ from drop-out bids holds under any bidding configuration in which exit-at-cost is weakly dominant for each individual bidder \citep{haile2003}---a property that survives within-coalition pricing schemes adjusting only the timing of withdrawal, since the recovered $F_c^k$ averages over coordinated and independent participants. Second, and more substantively for the sheltered-bidding interpretation, the post-restriction within-auction price shift could in principle reflect not only an order-statistic mechanism but also an intensification of coordination among the surviving SME pool---per \citet{kawainakabayashi2022}, IPV decompositions in environments where screens flag clustering may estimate bid-rigging rather than cost asymmetry. The empirical content of this concern is testable by comparing pre- and post-restriction clustering magnitudes.

Tables~\ref{tab:collusion_screen}--\ref{tab:collusion_screen_pair_classcond} report four screens on the structural sample, separately by class $\times$ period. The Conley-style close-pair statistic (Table~\ref{tab:collusion_screen}) flags pair clustering above the bootstrap null in all four (class $\times$ period) cells: \collConleyRealPreNp/\collConleyRealPostNp~percent realized vs.\ \collConleyNullPreNp/\collConleyNullPostNp~percent null in non-pharma Pre/Post, and \collConleyRealPrePh/\collConleyRealPostPh~percent realized vs.\ \collConleyNullPrePh/\collConleyNullPostPh~percent null in pharma Pre/Post. The \citet{bajariye2003} persistent-pair test (Table~\ref{tab:collusion_screen_bajariye}) likewise rejects independence everywhere, with $T_1$ obs/null ratios of \collBYRatioPreNp/\collBYRatioPostNp{} in non-pharma Pre/Post and \collBYRatioPrePh/\collBYRatioPostPh{} in pharma Pre/Post. The product-class-conditioned version (Table~\ref{tab:collusion_screen_pair_classcond}) closes a substantial portion of the gap in non-pharma, consistent with CADMAT product-class specialization the catalog-level classification does not fully absorb.

\emph{The substantive defense rests on the Pre-vs-Post comparison.} If sheltered bidding were primarily driven by an intensification of coordination among the surviving SME pool after the non-SMEs are removed, the Post clustering would exceed Pre. The data show the opposite direction: the Conley realized share falls or stays flat (\collConleyRealPreNp{}\,$\to$\,\collConleyRealPostNp{} in non-pharma, \collConleyRealPrePh{}\,$\to$\,\collConleyRealPostPh{} in pharma) and the Bajari--Ye $T_1$ ratio falls in both classes (\collBYRatioPreNp{}\,$\to$\,\collBYRatioPostNp{} in non-pharma, \collBYRatioPrePh{}\,$\to$\,\collBYRatioPostPh{} in pharma). Clustering does not intensify under the restriction; the sheltered-bidding decomposition therefore cannot be primarily attributed to a post-restriction coordination response, leaving the order-statistic interpretation as the parsimonious reading. The residual baseline clustering---present in both periods at similar magnitudes---is consistent with within-class specialization, registered joint ventures, and subcontracting networks that the screens cannot separate from coordination per \citet{conley2016}; an unobserved time-invariant component of coordination would attenuate to the FE-absorbed component of the DiD reduced form but would not be separately identified in the structural exercise. The V3 $\succ$ V0 ranking in thick standardized markets, which sits on top of this baseline, inherits the qualifier.

\paragraph{FPSB equivalent of V3 (Maskin--Riley upper bound).}\label{sec:fpsb_bound} Section~\ref{sec:results} computes V3 under Vickrey equivalence; under asymmetric IPV, \citet{maskinriley2000} show that revenue equivalence between FPSB and ascending-clock formats does not generally hold, with the FPSB expected price differing from the Vickrey by an amount bounded above by a function of the type-specific cost-distribution dispersion. Following \citet{maskinriley2000} and the bid-function structure of \citet{lebrun1999}, the FPSB--Vickrey gap on V3 (with $\rho = \prefRhoSmall$) is bounded above by $\rho \cdot \bar\sigma_c$, where $\bar\sigma_c$ is the SME-share-weighted average coefficient of variation of the UH-clean cost distributions ($\bar\sigma_c = \fpsbCovNp$ non-pharma, $\fpsbCovPh$ pharma; FPSB upper-bound gap $\fpsbBoundLoPct$--$\fpsbBoundHiPct$~percent of the reference price). Table~\ref{tab:maskin_riley_bound} reports the V0--V3 expected-price gap as \vZeroVThreeNp{}/\vZeroVThreePh{} in non-pharma/pharma---an order of magnitude larger than the FPSB upper-bound gap. The V3 $\succ$ V0 ranking is therefore preserved at the FPSB upper bound in both classes; auction format is not a margin on which the comparison turns.
