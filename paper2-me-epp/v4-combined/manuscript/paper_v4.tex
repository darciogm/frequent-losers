\title{\textbf{How Much of a Set-Aside's Price Is Bidding?\\ A Reduced-Form and Structural Decomposition of a Brazilian Procurement Reform\tnoteref{tfoot}}}
\tnotetext[tfoot]{\emph{This version: April 2026. I thank Paulo Furquim de Azevedo for extensive early discussions and the BEC/SEFAZ-SP team for data access. All remaining errors are my own.}}
\author[INSPER]{Darcio Genicolo-Martins}\ead{darciogm1@insper.edu.br}
\address[INSPER]{INSPER, S\~ao Paulo}

\begin{abstract}
Textbook intuition on procurement preferences holds that more bidders at the auction should sharpen competition. This paper shows that a rule that \emph{doubles} small-firm participation can still raise winning prices 10--11 percent. A March 2018 S\~ao Paulo reform extended a state-level SME-only rule to medical supplies; in a difference-in-differences against 76 never-treated product groups, SME entry nearly doubles yet prices rise sharply. A Gelbach decomposition assigns only 5 percent of the effect to the entry and winner-composition channels the reduced-form literature usually debates. Combining the reform with an asymmetric independent-private-values auction model, I show that 75--85 percent of the effect is \emph{sheltered bidding}---the within-auction response of the surviving pool to the removal of non-SMEs. Endogenous SME entry absorbs 60--70 percent of a larger latent shock. A 10 percent price preference Pareto-dominates the set-aside in thick markets at near-zero fiscal cost.
\end{abstract}
\end{frontmatter}

\textbf{Keywords:} public procurement, SME set-asides, asymmetric auctions, structural estimation, difference-in-differences, welfare.\\
