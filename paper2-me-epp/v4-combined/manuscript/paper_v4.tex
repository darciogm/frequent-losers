\title{\textbf{The Bidding Inside Exclusion: Reduced-Form and Structural Evidence on SME Procurement Set-Asides\tnoteref{tfoot}}}
\tnotetext[tfoot]{\emph{This version: April 2026. I thank Paulo Furquim de Azevedo for extensive early discussions and the BEC/SEFAZ-SP team for data access. All remaining errors are my own.}}
\author[INSPER]{Darcio Genicolo-Martins}\ead{darciogm1@insper.edu.br}
\address[INSPER]{INSPER, S\~ao Paulo}

\begin{abstract}
When a government reserves procurement for small firms, prices rise. Is it because fewer firms bid, or because each firm bids less aggressively? I combine a March 2018 S\~ao Paulo reform that extended a state-level SME-only rule to medical supplies with an asymmetric independent-private-values auction model. A difference-in-differences against 76 never-treated product groups places the winning-price effect at 10--11 percent, with 95 percent of that effect unexplained by entry and winner-composition channels. Structural recovery of type-specific cost distributions from Preg\~ao drop-out bids locates 75--85 percent of the effect in the intensive margin and shows that endogenous SME entry dampens 60--70 percent of a larger latent shock. Welfare cost is 29 percent of procurement value in non-specialty goods and 45 percent in price-regulated pharmaceuticals. A counterfactual 10 percent SME price preference Pareto-dominates the set-aside in thick markets.
\end{abstract}
\end{frontmatter}

\textbf{Keywords:} public procurement, SME set-asides, asymmetric auctions, structural estimation, difference-in-differences, welfare.\\
