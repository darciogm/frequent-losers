\begin{tablenotes}\footnotesize
\item Bayes-Nash equilibrium simulation, $B = 3{,}000$ MC draws per cell.
Preference rate $k$ scores SME bids by a factor $(1-k)$ for winner
selection while paying the actual winning bid (Vickrey-equivalent
translation). Baseline $S_1$ is the open regime at the Pre-period
pool; V0 is the full set-aside with endogenous Post-period SME pool.
$\Delta$ is the simulated price effect relative to $S_1$, in
reference-price units. The nonzero $\Delta$ at $k = 0$ reflects
Monte Carlo sampling noise relative to the $S_1$ baseline (true
$\Delta = 0$ by construction). Welfare loss = (DWL$_{\text{alloc}}$
$+ \lambda \cdot \Delta$)$/p_{S_1}$, $\lambda = 0.30$. The
qualitative pattern: as $k$ rises from 0 to 30 percent, the SME
win-rate climbs sharply (from 17.4 to 32.7 percent in non-pharma
and from 15.3 to 23.5 percent in pharma) while the welfare cost
rises slowly until around 15--20 percent, then accelerates. The
10 percent rate raises SME win rates modestly while keeping
welfare loss near zero, in contrast to the full set-aside, which
delivers a 100 percent SME win rate at a 28--44 percent welfare
loss.
