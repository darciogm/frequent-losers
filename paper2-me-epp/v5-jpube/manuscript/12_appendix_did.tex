\section{Appendix: Reduced-Form Benchmark}\label{app:did}

The structural exercise of Sections~\ref{sec:results}--\ref{sec:welfare} is anchored to a magnitude in the data: the absolute log-price effect of the March~2018 cutoff. This appendix reports that magnitude as a difference-in-differences against the 76 never-treated product groups, on the same sample window used by the structural exercise. The DiD is included for design validation rather than as a competing estimate; the structural exercise interprets the within-auction and participation-margin channels that this number aggregates.

The estimating equation is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:did}
\log p^{\mathrm{final}}_{i,j,t} \;=\; \alpha_i + \mu_t + \beta \, (\mathrm{g65}_j \cdot \text{Pre}_t) + X'_{i,j,t} \gamma + \varepsilon_{i,j,t},
\end{equation}
where $i$ indexes items, $j$ indexes product groups, $t$ indexes calendar months, $\alpha_i$ is an item fixed effect, $\mu_t$ is a calendar-month fixed effect, $X_{i,j,t}$ collects auction-format and quantity controls, and $\beta$ identifies the difference between the open-regime price level (Pre, $\mathrm{g65}=1$) and the post-cutoff level relative to the never-treated control set, in the difference-in-differences-in-reverse sense of \citet{kim2019}: a negative coefficient on the pre-period dummy is the open-regime discount that the SME-only rule undoes after March~2018.

\input{../output/tables/tab_prices.tex}

Table~\ref{tab:prices} reports $\hat\beta$ across the 6-, 12-, and 18-month windows on each side of the cutoff. The 18-month specification (the structural baseline) gives $\hat\beta = -0.109$ without and $-0.113$ with PBU controls, both significant at the 1 percent level on standard errors clustered at the item level. The interpretation is that absolute winning prices were about 11 percent lower under the open regime than they were after the SME-only extension. The 12-month and 6-month windows give similar estimates ($-0.108$ and $-0.142$ respectively); the 6-month coefficient captures the immediate post-cutoff price response before the participation margin has equilibrated, while the 12-month and 18-month coefficients reflect progressive absorption of the within-auction shock by SME entry, consistent with the structural-window reading in Section~\ref{sec:robustness}. The 10--11 percent magnitude that anchors the structural calibration is therefore the 18-month log-price coefficient.

\input{../output/tables/tab_efficiency.tex}

Table~\ref{tab:efficiency} reports the same estimating equation with the price-to-reference ratio $p^{\mathrm{final}} / p^{\mathrm{ref}}$ as the outcome on a level (not log) scale, so coefficients are interpretable as percentage-point shifts in the ratio. The 6-month window gives $-0.060$ (with PBU controls), consistent with the 18-month log-price effect of $-0.11$ once the average ratio of about $0.65$ in the pre-period is taken into account. The 12-month and 18-month coefficients on this level outcome are smaller in magnitude and statistically insignificant, reflecting that the price-to-reference ratio is bounded above by the buyer's reservation level and therefore averages over a compressed support; the log-price specification of Table~\ref{tab:prices} is the headline. The price-to-reference outcome is reported as a robustness check on the headline log-price magnitude rather than as a structural input.

\subsection{Event-study and placebo diagnostics}

The DiD specification is reinforced by an event-study around the March 2018 cutoff and three placebo treatment dates. Figure~\ref{fig:eventstudy_prices} plots $\hat\beta_s$ for six semesters around the cutoff (each semester is six calendar months) with item and semester fixed effects, on the same Group-65 vs.\ 76-control panel used by Table~\ref{tab:prices}; the semester immediately preceding the cutoff is the omitted reference. The semester binning aggregates the monthly noise that would otherwise dominate the visual reading. Pre-cutoff coefficients are small in magnitude relative to the post-cutoff coefficients; the post-cutoff coefficients are stably negative and consistent with the $-0.10$ to $-0.11$ DiD coefficient. The placebo evidence in Table~\ref{tab:placebo} adds discipline at fake cutoff dates and is read jointly with the event-study below.

Table~\ref{tab:placebo} reports placebo treatment dates at September 2017, March 2017, and June 2017. The price coefficients for the three placebos are $-0.013$ (statistically insignificant), $-0.030$ (significant at the 5 percent level), and $-0.034$ (significant at the 1 percent level). The placebo evidence does not establish absence of pre-period correlation in Group-65 prices: low-frequency variation in the treated group is detectable at fake cutoffs near the real one. The relevant test for whether the DiD coefficient anchors a structural input is therefore not absence of pre-trend, but magnitude separation. The actual March 2018 coefficient of $-0.108$ to $-0.142$ is three to eight times larger in absolute value than any of the three placebo coefficients and is significant at the 1 percent level in every specification. The structural calibration is anchored to a treatment effect of demonstrably larger magnitude than any pre-period drift the placebo design can detect; the residual concern that some of the real-cutoff coefficient reflects gradual anticipation rather than discrete treatment is a bound, not a refutation, on the structural input. Recent guidance on event-study contamination \citep{borusyak2024, sun2021} emphasizes that staggered or anticipated treatments produce coefficients that combine the discrete-treatment effect with anticipation dynamics; the magnitude-separation defense above bounds the anticipation contribution at the difference between the real-cutoff and placebo coefficients, which is at least 67 percent of the real-cutoff magnitude. Read jointly, the event-study and the placebo table support the interpretation of the DiD coefficient as a treatment effect with at most moderate anticipation contamination.

\subsection{Bridge to the structural counterfactual}

The structural exercise of the main text recovers the within-auction and participation-margin decomposition of the $-0.11$ DiD coefficient. Section~\ref{sec:results} translates the structural counterfactual into normalized units: $p_{S_3} - p_{S_1} = +0.259$ in non-pharma and $+0.308$ in pharma, of which the within-auction component accounts for 74.5 and 73.3 percent respectively. Expressed as a ratio to the open-regime baseline $p_{S_1}$, these are $0.259/0.759 \approx 34$ percent in non-pharma and $0.308/0.656 \approx 47$ percent in pharma; the corresponding DiD-implied price ratio is $\exp(0.11) - 1 \approx 12$ percent on absolute prices. The structural counterfactual is therefore three to four times the DiD coefficient, not coincident with it. The two exercises measure related but distinct objects: the DiD recovers the average log-price change in the treated set conditional on items completing, with item and month fixed effects absorbing the cross-sectional cost variation; the structural counterfactual recovers the simulated mean of the second-order statistic $c_{(2)}$ under the BNE-equivalent transaction price, on the cleaned cost residuals after the Krasnokutskaya auction-level scale is shrunk out, conditional on the bidder pool that endogenously enters under each regime. The DiD averages over the realized price distribution including item- and PBU-level shocks that the structural exercise normalizes away; the structural counterfactual averages over a counterfactual primitive distribution that the DiD never observes. The DiD therefore identifies the magnitude of the policy effect on a directly observable outcome; the structural exercise identifies the within-auction vs participation-margin decomposition of a transformed counterfactual object. The DiD is reported here for design validation; the gap between the two magnitudes is mechanical, not contradictory, and the welfare arithmetic of Section~\ref{sec:welfare} is anchored to the structural object on the structural normalization.
