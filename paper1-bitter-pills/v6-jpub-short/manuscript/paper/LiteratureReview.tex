\subsection*{Related Literature}

The paper speaks to three literatures. On passive waste in public spending \citep{bandiera2009active}: prior work has studied bureaucratic capacity \citep{best2023procurement}, management practices \citep{rasul2018management}, and procurement design \citep{lewisfaupel2016can, coviello2018bidding, decarolis2018buyer, baltrunaite2021discretion, szucs2024discretion}. We identify a new channel---judicial compression of planning time. Unlike \citet{coviello2018bidding}, where courts \emph{extend} procurement timelines, ours is a setting where courts \emph{compress} them. Second, on unintended costs of accountability: our ``under the gun'' effect shows that the threat of judicial punishment---intended to ensure compliance---distorts procurement incentives in ways that raise costs \citep{duflo2018obligations, williams2021bureaucratic}. Third, we add fiscal evidence to the legal and public health literatures on health litigation in Latin America \citep{wang2015right, ferraz2009right, biehl2009will}, which have focused on legal and health dimensions with limited attention to procurement costs.
