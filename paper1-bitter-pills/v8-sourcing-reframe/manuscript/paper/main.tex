\begin{abstract}
When mandates secure compliance, what efficiency does the state
forgo? Standard accountability models
\citep{prendergast2007bureaucrats} predict suppliers extract
delivery-risk premia; passive-waste accounts
\citep{bandiera2009active} predict the buyer's sourcing pattern
shifts. The two are observationally equivalent in cross-buyer data,
which conflate within-firm pricing with equilibrium supplier
composition. Using bid-level data on \BPnPOIfull{} pharmaceutical
purchases by the S\~ao Paulo State Department of Health
(\BPsampleStartYear--\BPsampleEndYear), we exploit a parallel
administrative channel sharing all planning constraints of
court-mandated procurement, without sanction exposure.
Selection-corrected Manski-Lee bounds place the under-the-gun gap
at $[\BPutgBoundLow, \BPutgBoundHigh]$. \emph{There is no
within-firm markup} in deep markets
($\hat\beta=\BPutgTripleCoefUTG$, $\mathrm{SE}=\BPutgTripleSEUTG$
within firm-buyer-item triples), with a sanction premium reappearing
on thin-supplier subsamples. In deep markets, the price margin
reflects demand fragmentation (admin orders \BPutgQtyAdminFoldPref$\times$ larger)
and a sourcing shift (modal winner differs across regimes in
\BPwinnerSwitchDiffModalPct{} of item-buyer pairs); a placebo on
never-litigated items returns zero. Bounded welfare cost:
\BPwelfareBound{} per year on \BPwelfareBoundAnnualSpend{} of
annual litigated spending in S\~ao Paulo. The policy lever is
demand aggregation, not contract design: delivery is guaranteed;
sourcing efficiency is the bill.
\end{abstract}
