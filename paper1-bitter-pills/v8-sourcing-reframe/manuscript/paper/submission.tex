\begin{abstract}
When mandates secure compliance, what efficiency does the state
forgo? Standard models of accountability
\citep{prendergast2007bureaucrats} predict suppliers extract
delivery-risk premia; passive-waste accounts
\citep{bandiera2009active} predict the buyer's sourcing pattern
shifts. The two are observationally equivalent in cross-buyer
data, which conflate within-firm pricing with equilibrium supplier
composition. Using bid-level data on \BPnPOIfull{} pharmaceutical
purchases by the S\~ao Paulo State Department of Health
(\BPsampleStartYear--\BPsampleEndYear), we exploit a parallel
administrative-request channel that shares all planning constraints
of court-mandated procurement but carries no penalty for officials.
Manski-Lee bounds place the selection-corrected ``under-the-gun''
gap at $[\BPutgBoundLow, \BPutgBoundHigh]$. \emph{There is no
within-firm markup} in deep markets
($\hat\beta=\BPutgTripleCoefUTG$, $\mathrm{SE}=\BPutgTripleSEUTG$
within firm-buyer-item triples), with a sanction premium reappearing
on thin-supplier subsamples consistent with delivery-risk pricing.
In deep markets, the price margin operates through demand
fragmentation (admin orders are roughly
\BPutgQtyAdminFoldPref$\times$ larger) and a sourcing shift (the
modal winner differs across regimes in
\BPwinnerSwitchDiffModalPct{} of item-buyer pairs); a placebo on
items never subject to litigation returns economically and
statistically zero. Bounded welfare cost: \BPwelfareBound{} per
year on \BPwelfareBoundAnnualSpend{} of annual litigated spending
in S\~ao Paulo. The policy lever is demand aggregation, not
contract design: delivery is guaranteed; sourcing efficiency is
the bill.
\end{abstract}
