\opening{Dear Editors,}

We are pleased to submit \emph{``Bitter Pills: Sourcing under Compressed Timelines and the Cost of Compliance in Brazilian Public Health Procurement''} for consideration as a \textbf{Short Paper} in the \emph{Journal of Public Economics}.

\medskip\noindent\textbf{What the paper does.}
We use bid-level administrative data on \BPnPOIfull{} pharmaceutical purchases by the S\~ao Paulo State Department of Health over \BPsampleStartYear--\BPsampleEndYear{} to ask how court-mandated procurement raises costs. Within the same item, urgent purchases carry negotiated prices \BPnegPctHeadline{} higher and attract \BPfirmsPctHeadline{} fewer bidders than ordinary purchases, yet succeed \BPsuccessPP{} more often. Within urgent purchases, an institutional contrast unique to S\~ao Paulo since \BPsampleStartYear---a parallel administrative-request channel that shares all planning constraints but carries no penalty for officials when delivery fails---offers an ``under the gun'' (UTG) comparison. The naive UTG gap is large but contaminated by selection: the SES/SP scientific committee admits cases on cost-effectiveness criteria. Manski-Lee bounds put the selection-corrected UTG at $[\BPutgBoundLow, \BPutgBoundHigh]$.

\medskip\noindent\textbf{The conceptual contribution.}
The economically interesting finding is what produces the gap. Within firm-buyer-item triples observed under both regimes, the same firm sells the same item to the same buyer at essentially the same per-unit price ($\BPutgTripleCoefUTG$, SE $\BPutgTripleSEUTG$, NULL). \emph{There is no within-firm markup.} The standard accountability-distortion intuition---that sanction-exposed officials face higher per-unit prices because suppliers extract a premium for delivery risk---is empirically absent in our setting. The UTG cost margin operates through demand fragmentation (admin orders are larger, so the bulk-discount technology mechanically pulls admin prices below lit) and through equilibrium changes in \emph{which} firm wins the contract. Accountability-driven sourcing constraints, not price discrimination, are what make compliance expensive. We see this distinction---between sourcing-channel and pricing-channel mechanisms of accountability-induced inefficiency---as the paper's main contribution.

\medskip\noindent\textbf{Identification discipline.}
The within-item, within-buyer FE design identifies the urgent-vs-ordinary premium. The Lee bound and a Heckman parametric correction discipline the UTG comparison; the Heckman is reported as a sensitivity diagnostic that flags identification fragility (the inverse Mills ratio is collinear with the second-stage FE), not as a competing point estimate. Wild cluster bootstrap inference on the preferred FE confirms the result ($p_{\mathrm{boot}}=\BPutgBoottestPval$). A Borusyak-Jaravel-Spiess imputation event-study with a Rambachan-Roth honest sensitivity overlay is reported in the Appendix; we honor its boundaries in the main text. Items never litigated (placebo) deliver economically and statistically zero coefficients.

\medskip\noindent\textbf{Welfare and policy.}
Applied to the \BPwelfareBoundAnnualSpend{} of annual litigated spending in S\~ao Paulo with an admissibility-share calibration of \BPwelfareBoundAdmissibility, the bounded UTG implies a per-unit-price cost of \BPwelfareBound{} per year, with the Lee-endpoint range delivering $[\BPwelfareBoundCIlow, \BPwelfareBoundCIhigh]$. We report a single bounded figure rather than the stacked ranges common in the policy literature. The mechanism the paper isolates---sourcing under fragmentation, not pricing under sanctions---points to demand-aggregation reforms rather than contract-design changes deterring opportunistic markup. Brazil's Lei~14.133/2021, which introduces framework agreements, sets up a natural test of this prediction in post-reform data.

\medskip\noindent\textbf{JPubE fit.}
The paper sits at the intersection of procurement design (Bandiera et al.\ 2009; Bosio et al.\ 2022; Best, Hjort \& Szakonyi 2023), judicial enforcement of public contracts (Coviello et al.\ 2018), and accountability-induced bureaucratic distortion (Prendergast 2007; Bandiera et al.\ 2021). The Short Paper track is the right venue: the body totals \BPbodyWordCount{} words (under the \BPbodyWordCap-word cap), uses \BPexhibitsTotal{} main-text exhibits, and is self-contained.
