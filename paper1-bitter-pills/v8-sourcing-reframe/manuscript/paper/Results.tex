\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Three motivating patterns}
\label{sec:main_results}

Within the same item, urgent purchases carry negotiated prices
\BPnegPctHeadline{} higher than ordinary purchases and attract
\BPfirmsPctHeadline{} fewer bidding firms; reference prices set
during the planning phase rise by \BPrefPctPreferred{} ($p<0.10$),
attenuating sharply once buyer fixed effects absorb buyer-level
heterogeneity. Despite worse conditions, urgent tenders \emph{succeed}
more often: \BPsuccessPP{} ($p<0.01$). Officials accept worse terms
to ensure compliance rather than cancel; the urgency premium does not
arise because urgency selects easier purchases.\footnote{The
\BPnegPctHeadline{} within-item premium sits at the lower end of
within-buyer procurement-cost dispersion documented in comparable
settings: Bandiera-Prat-Valletti 2009 \BPbpvBaseline; Best, Hjort \&
Szakonyi 2023 \BPbhsBaseline; Bosio et al.\ 2022 \BPbosioBaseline;
Coviello-Guglielmo-Spagnolo 2018 \BPcgsBaseline. Tighter within-buyer
within-item identification is consistent with a smaller headline
number.}

\subsection{Same firm, same buyer, same item: no within-firm markup}
\label{sec:within_firm_null}

Restricting to firm-buyer-item triples observed under both the
litigated and the administrative regime
(\BPutgTripleCountUTG{} triples covering \BPutgTripleNUTG{}
observations), the Admin coefficient is $\BPutgTripleCoefUTG$ (SE
$\BPutgTripleSEUTG$): statistically indistinguishable from zero
(95\% bootstrap CI [\BPutgWithinCIlow, \BPutgWithinCIhigh]). The
same firm prices the same item to the same buyer at the same level
under either regime; suppliers who already serve the buyer's urgent
procurement do not extract a markup when the official is
sanction-exposed.

This is the empirical pivot of the paper. The standard
accountability-distortion intuition
\citep{prendergast2007bureaucrats} predicts that sanction-exposed
officials face higher per-unit prices because suppliers extract a
delivery-risk premium; in the bulk of our data, that mechanism is
absent.

\paragraph{Robustness across subsamples.}
Table~\ref{tab:within_firm_robustness} re-fits the within firm-buyer-item
specification on five subsamples. The null is robust on the deepest
markets---above-median quantity and SUS-formulary items---and on the
later period (2014--2019), where the administrative-channel
infrastructure had matured. The null breaks on two thin-supplier
slices: below-median quantity items (+6.6\%, $p<0.05$) and the earlier
period (+11.7\%, $p<0.01$). The pattern is consistent with the
sourcing-vs-pricing reading: where the buyer has a deep supplier base
to switch toward, sanctions reorganize sourcing without distorting
the incumbent's price; where the buyer is constrained to a thin
supplier set, even the incumbent extracts a markup, exactly as
\citet{prendergast2007bureaucrats} predicts when the supplier has
outside-option leverage. The headline within-firm null is therefore a
deep-market result, not a universal one.

\subsection{The gap is sourcing, not pricing: decomposition and heterogeneity}
\label{sec:reconciliation}

Decompose the observed log-price gap (admin minus litigated,
\BPutgObsLogGap) into an arithmetic identity:
\begin{equation*}
\underbrace{\Delta y_{\text{obs}}}_{\BPutgObsLogGap}
\;=\;
\underbrace{\beta_{\text{bulk}} \cdot \Delta \ln Q_{\text{adm-lit}}}_{\text{mechanical: } \BPutgMechLogGap}
\;+\;
\underbrace{\beta_{\text{within-firm}}}_{\text{within-firm: } \BPutgWithinLogGap}
\;+\;
\underbrace{\beta_{\text{composition}}}_{\text{residual: } \BPutgCompLogGap}.
\end{equation*}

Three readings follow, with 95\% cluster-bootstrap CIs (PBU,
\BPutgReconBootB{} replicates). \emph{Bulk-discount channel
identified and large}: $\BPutgMechanicalCone$ ([\BPutgMechCIlow,
\BPutgMechCIhigh]). Order-size differences alone would make admin
cheaper by \BPutgMechPctAbs{}, more than the
\BPutgObsPctLitOverAdmin{} actually observed. \emph{Within-firm
channel identified and zero}: $\BPutgWithinFirmOffset$
([\BPutgWithinCIlow, \BPutgWithinCIhigh]). \emph{Residual channel
imprecisely identified}: $\BPutgCompositionResidual$ on the point
estimate, ([\BPutgCompCIlow, \BPutgCompCIhigh]) on the bootstrap, and
inherits variance from the other three bars by construction. The
residual loads what the identity attributes neither to scale nor to
within-firm pricing: equilibrium changes in \emph{which} firm wins,
match-quality shifts, and any other margin not separately identified
in this design.

\paragraph{Direct mechanism: winners shift, prices don't.}
The composition residual in the decomposition above is identified by
elimination; we now provide direct evidence that the sourcing
margin loads it. Among the \BPwinnerSwitchIBpairs{} item-buyer pairs
observed under both regimes, the winner-firm sets overlap only
modestly: mean Jaccard similarity is \BPwinnerSwitchJaccardMean,
\BPwinnerSwitchNoOverlapPct{} of pairs have \emph{no} winner in
common across regimes, and the modal winner differs across regimes
in \BPwinnerSwitchDiffModalPct{} of pairs. Conditional on the same
item and buyer, switching from administrative to litigated
procurement reorganizes the supplier set far more than it reorganizes
incumbent pricing. Table~\ref{tab:winner_switch} reports the full
distribution.

\paragraph{Where the cost bites confirms the sourcing reading.}
Splitting items by mean number of participating firms (median
\BPhetHHIMedianValue), competitive items show a
\BPhetHHIlowBaselinePct{} urgency premium with
\BPhetHHIlowFirmFEPct{} surviving firm fixed effects
(\BPhetHHIlowAttnPct\% attenuation). Concentrated items show no
detectable premium (\BPhetHHIhighBaselinePct, not significant).
Urgency erodes competitive pressure where there is competitive
pressure to erode---the heterogeneity matches the sourcing channel,
not a pricing channel.

\subsection{Selection bounds on the size of the gap}
\label{sec:utg_bounds}

The naive Admin coefficient on the urgent-only subsample is
$\BPutgCoef$ ($p<0.05$) under the preferred FE, implying
litigated purchases are \BPutgPointNaive{} more expensive than
administrative ones; wild cluster bootstrap (Rademacher,
$B=\BPutgBoottestB$) confirms with $p=\BPutgBoottestPval$ and 95\% CI
in log-points $[\BPutgBoottestCIlow, \BPutgBoottestCIhigh]$. This
comparison is contaminated by selection into the administrative
channel: the SES/SP scientific committee admits cases on
cost-effectiveness criteria. The committee's selection rule,
recovered from a probit on pre-period item characteristics
($N=\BPutgFirstStageN$), rejects higher pre-period log reference
prices (\BPutgProbitRefPriceCoef, \BPutgProbitRefPricePval) and
SUS-formulary status (\BPutgProbitSUSCoef, \BPutgProbitSUSPval); admin
over-represents items that would have been priced lower under any
regime.

We bound the selection wedge using Lee (2009) trimming within
item$\times$year$\times$PBU strata where the admin sample exceeds the
litigated count. Trimming admin from the upper tail of the
within-stratum price distribution gives the lower bound; trimming from
the lower tail gives the upper bound.

\input{../../output/tables/tab_utg_lee_bounds}

The bounded gap falls in $[\BPutgBoundLow, \BPutgBoundHigh]$, well
below the naive \BPutgPointNaive: selection on observables explains
approximately one-third of the headline gap. The within-cell variation
that identifies the tightest specification comes from a
non-representative subsample (Online Appendix
Table~\ref{tab:both_types_cells}): of \BPutgCellsItemYM{}
item$\times$month cells, only \BPbothCellN{} (about 11\%) carry
within-cell variation and they tilt toward higher-volume,
more-frequent SUS-formulary medications. The bounded estimate
therefore speaks to the more liquid items in the urgent panel, not to
the universe of court-mandated purchases.

\subsection{Falsification: items never litigated}
\label{sec:placebo}

Running the preferred specification on items with zero litigated
purchases across \BPsampleStartYear--\BPsampleEndYear{} (restricted to
those with both ordinary and administrative purchases) yields placebo
coefficients of $\BPplaceboNegCoef$ (SE $\BPplaceboNegSE$) for
negotiated prices and $\BPplaceboRefCoef$ (SE $\BPplaceboRefSE$) for
reference prices. Both are economically small and statistically
insignificant (Online Appendix Table~\ref{tab:placebo}). The urgency
premium exists only for items that are targets of litigation.
