\subsection*{A.2 Selection Bounds and Robustness}

\paragraph{Manski-Lee bounds.}
Table~\ref{tab:utg_lee_bounds} reports the bounded UTG coefficient
under monotone selection. Trimming admin observations within
item$\times$year$\times$PBU strata where the admin sample exceeds the
litigated count yields a bounded gap in
$[\BPutgBoundLow, \BPutgBoundHigh]$ against a naive coefficient of
\BPutgPointNaive. Average trimming intensity is \BPutgLeeTrimMean{}
of admin observations across \BPutgLeeStrataExcess{} of
\BPutgLeeStrataTotal{} strata.

\input{../../output/tables/tab_utg_lee_bounds}

\paragraph{Heckman selection model (non-identified).}
Table~\ref{tab:utg_heckman} reports a parametric Heckman correction
with the same first-stage probit (pre-period log reference price,
mean quantity, mean number of bidders, and order count as the
exclusion restriction; SUS-formulary indicator). The probit on
$N=\BPutgFirstStageN$ observations recovers negative loadings on
pre-period reference price and SUS status. The inverse Mills ratio
is collinear with the item, year, and PBU fixed effects in the
second stage; the resulting Admin coefficient ($\BPutgHeckmanCoef$,
SE $\BPutgHeckmanSE$) is uninformative. The Lee bound is the
primary selection-corrected object.

\input{../../output/tables/tab_utg_heckman}

\paragraph{Both-types-cell representativeness.}
Table~\ref{tab:both_types_cells} compares the \BPbothCellN{}
item$\times$year-month cells that contain both administrative and
litigated urgent purchases (the within-cell variation that
identifies the tightest specification) with the
\BPbothCellNSingleton{} singleton cells. Both-types cells
systematically have lower mean log reference and negotiated prices,
larger quantities, slightly fewer bidders, and a higher
SUS-formulary share. The bounded UTG estimate therefore speaks to
the more liquid, formulary-anchored items in the urgent panel.

\input{../../output/tables/tab_both_types_cells}

\paragraph{Within-firm null robustness.}
Table~\ref{tab:within_firm_robustness} re-fits the within
firm-buyer-item Admin specification on five subsamples constructed
from the triple sample (\BPutgTripleCountUTG{} triples). The null
is robust on above-median quantity, SUS-formulary, and the later
period; it breaks on below-median quantity (+6.6\%, $p<0.05$) and
the earlier period (+11.7\%, $p<0.01$). The pattern locates the
deep-market versus thin-market boundary between the BPV sourcing
reading and the Prendergast within-firm-pricing reading.

\input{../../output/tables/tab_within_firm_robustness}

\paragraph{Wild cluster bootstrap.}
Table~\ref{tab:utg_boottest} reports Rademacher wild cluster
bootstrap inference on the UTG coefficient (\BPutgBoottestB{}
replicates, PBU clustering, restricted-null residuals). The
preferred-FE Admin coefficient is significant under both asymptotic
and bootstrap inference ($p_{\text{boot}} = \BPutgBoottestPval$,
95\% CI in log-points $[\BPutgBoottestCIlow, \BPutgBoottestCIhigh]$);
the item$\times$year-month FE specification is borderline
($p_{\text{boot}} = \BPutgBoottestPvalIYM$), as expected given the
thin within-cell sample.

\input{../../output/tables/tab_utg_boottest}

\subsection*{A.3 Event Study with Honest Sensitivity}

We re-estimate the dynamic response of log negotiated price (and log
quantity) to the first court order for each item using the
imputation estimator of \citet{borusyak2024revisiting} (BJS) and the
ATT$(g,t)$ estimator of \citet{callaway2021did} (CS). The panel is
item-year, winner-only; treatment is the calendar year of the first
litigated purchase for each item; the never-litigated set is the CS
control group. BJS pre-period coefficients are economically small
($<\BPbjsPreMaxAbs$ in absolute value); post-period coefficients
rise from $+\BPbjsETzero$ at $t=0$ to $+\BPbjsETfive$ at $t=+5$.
Within the same item, order quantity drops sharply at the year of
the first court order (\BPeventQtyDropFirstOrder{} at $t=0$) and
recovers slowly (\BPeventQtyDropAtFive{} at $t=+5$), tracing the
fragmentation channel in real time.

\paragraph{Rambachan-Roth honest sensitivity.}
Figure~\ref{fig:event_study_honest_rr} reports the BJS event-study
coefficients with a Rambachan-Roth honest sensitivity overlay
\citep{rambachan2023more}. Pre-period maximum absolute coefficient
is $\BPbjsPreMaxAbs$; the $t=0$ effect is $\BPbjsETzero$ (SE
$\BPbjsETzeroSE$); the breakdown linear-extrapolation parameter at
which the $t=0$ confidence interval just contains zero is
$M=\BPrrBreakdownM$. The $t=0$ effect is significant under parallel
trends but does not survive the linear extrapolation of the observed
pre-period maximum---we honor this in the main text by treating the
cross-sectional fixed-effects estimate as primary and the BJS event
study as supportive, not as a stand-alone identification claim.

\subsection*{A.4 Falsification: Items Never Litigated}

Running the preferred specification on items with zero litigated
purchases across \BPsampleStartYear--\BPsampleEndYear{} (restricted
to those with both ordinary and administrative purchases) yields
placebo coefficients of $\BPplaceboNegCoef$ (SE $\BPplaceboNegSE$)
for negotiated prices and $\BPplaceboRefCoef$ (SE
$\BPplaceboRefSE$) for reference prices. Both are economically
small and statistically insignificant.

\subsection*{A.5 Regex Classifier Validation}
\label{sec:appendix_regex}

The purchase-type indicator is constructed by a regular-expression
algorithm applied to public tender notices, which by Brazilian
procurement law must reference any underlying court order. We draw a
stratified random sample of \BPregexNotices{} tender-notice subjects
($\sim$\BPregexPerClass{} per predicted class) and hand-label each
as ordinary, administrative, or litigated.
\BPregexValidationStatus{} The regex relies on textual markers
present or absent independently of the price outcomes we estimate,
so misclassification attenuates rather than biases our
estimates.\footnote{The stratified sample is reproduced from
\texttt{output/validation/validation\_sample.csv}, generated by
\texttt{analysis/33\_regex\_validation\_sample.R}; F1 statistics
are emitted by \texttt{34\_regex\_validation\_f1.R} when the
labeled CSV is finalized.}
