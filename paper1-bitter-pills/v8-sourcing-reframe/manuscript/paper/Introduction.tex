\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Standard models of bureaucratic accountability
\citep{prendergast2007bureaucrats} predict that sanction-exposed
officials face higher per-unit prices because suppliers extract a
delivery-risk premium. Passive-waste accounts
\citep{bandiera2009active} predict instead that distortion operates
through \emph{sourcing}---which firm wins the contract, what order
size is feasible---rather than through within-firm pricing. The two
predictions are observationally equivalent in cross-buyer data, where
buyer-supplier match heterogeneity confounds within-firm and
between-firm margins. We provide the first within firm-buyer-item
test of these competing predictions in a setting where accountability
pressure should bite hardest: court-mandated pharmaceutical
procurement under fines, personal liability, and fund seizure for
delivery failure. The within-firm prediction fails; the sourcing
prediction holds.

We use all pharmaceutical procurement by the S\~ao Paulo State
Department of Health (SES/SP) from \BPsampleStartYear{} through
\BPsampleEndYear: \BPnPOIfull{} purchase-offer-item observations on the
state's electronic procurement platform (BEC), which is the mandatory
channel for common goods in the state since~\BPbecMandatorySinceYear.
Court orders and administrative requests partition urgent purchases
into two types that share all planning constraints---compressed
timelines (\BPurgentPlanLow--\BPurgentPlanHigh{} days against the
\BPordPlanLow--\BPordPlanHigh{} days for ordinary purchases), small
quantities, diverted budget resources, and the same auction
procedures---but differ in penalty exposure. Litigated purchases expose
the official to fines, personal liability, and fund seizure if delivery
fails. Administrative requests, instituted by the SES/SP in
\BPsampleStartYear, carry no such penalties. This is the institutional
contrast we exploit, and it is, to our knowledge, unique in the
Brazilian and Latin American procurement record.

\paragraph{Selection bounds}
The administrative subsample is itself a non-random subset of urgent
demand: the SES/SP scientific committee admits cases on
cost-effectiveness criteria. A pre-period probit on item characteristics
recovers the committee's selection rule and confirms that admin
over-represents items that would have been priced lower under any
regime: the coefficient on pre-period log reference price is
\BPutgProbitRefPriceCoef{} (\BPutgProbitRefPricePval); on SUS-formulary
status, \BPutgProbitSUSCoef{} (\BPutgProbitSUSPval). The naive
under-the-gun (UTG) gap of \BPutgPointNaive{} therefore confounds the
sanction effect with selection on item economics. Under Manski-Lee
monotone restrictions on the committee rejection rule, the UTG gap
lies in $[\BPutgBoundLow, \BPutgBoundHigh]$. A parametric Heckman
correction is non-identified in this design (Online
Appendix~\ref{tab:utg_heckman}); the Lee bound is the primary
selection-corrected object.

\paragraph{Three findings}
\emph{First}, the headline urgent-vs-ordinary premium is
\BPnegPctHeadline{} across the full sample, with the same item under
compressed timelines attracting \BPfirmsPctHeadlineAbs{} fewer bidders;
urgent tenders nonetheless succeed \BPsuccessPP{} \emph{more} often than
their ordinary counterparts---a pattern that rules out an alternative in
which urgency simply selects easier purchases. The \BPnegPctHeadline{}
within-item premium sits at the lower end of plausible procurement-cost
dispersion in comparable bureaucratic settings
\citep[e.g.,][]{best2023procurement, bosio2022public}, consistent with
the tighter within-buyer within-item identification we use here.

\emph{Second, the price margin is not a within-firm markup.} Within
firm-buyer-item triples observed under both the litigated and the
administrative regime (\BPutgTripleCountUTG{} triples,
\BPutgTripleNUTG{} observations), the Admin coefficient is
$\hat\beta=\BPutgTripleCoefUTG$ ($\mathrm{SE}=\BPutgTripleSEUTG$),
statistically indistinguishable from zero. The same firm sells the same
item to the same buyer at essentially the same per-unit price under
either regime. Standard accountability-distortion intuition
\citep{prendergast2007bureaucrats} predicts that sanction-exposed
officials face higher per-unit prices because suppliers extract a
delivery-risk premium; in our data, that mechanism is empirically
absent. This is the empirical pivot of the paper.

\emph{Third}, the bounded UTG admits an arithmetic decomposition.
Administrative orders are roughly \BPutgQtyAdminFoldPref$\times$ larger
than litigated orders, and the bulk-discount elasticity implies a
mechanical price effect through the quantity channel of
$\BPutgMechanicalCone$ in the admin-minus-litigated log price. The
within-firm null contributes essentially zero. The composition
residual ($\BPutgCompositionResidual$ point estimate; bootstrap CI
[\BPutgCompCIlow, \BPutgCompCIhigh]) closes the identity by
elimination: it is wide and inherits the variance of the other
components, but its sign is consistent with the bulk-discount
disadvantage of small litigated orders dominating and producing the
net observed gap. Sourcing-and-aggregation channels, not within-firm
pricing, load the cost margin.
Figure~\ref{fig:cascade} reports the decomposition.

\paragraph{Inference, dynamics, and falsification}
Wild cluster bootstrap inference on the preferred-FE Admin coefficient
delivers $p_{\mathrm{boot}}=\BPutgBoottestPval$
($B=\BPutgBoottestB$, Rademacher), with a 95\% CI in log-points of
$[\BPutgBoottestCIlow, \BPutgBoottestCIhigh]$. A Borusyak-Jaravel-Spiess
imputation event-study around each item's first court order shows
post-period coefficients rising from $\BPbjsETzero$ at $t=0$ to
$\BPbjsETfive$ at $t=+5$ (Online Appendix), and a Rambachan-Roth
honest sensitivity test \citep{rambachan2023more} on those estimates is
honored in the main text: the $t=0$ effect is significant under
parallel trends but does not survive a linear extrapolation of the
observed pre-period maximum, so we treat the cross-sectional FE design
as primary and the event study as supportive. A placebo on items never
subject to litigation delivers economically and statistically zero
coefficients ($\BPplaceboNegCoef$, SE $\BPplaceboNegSE$ for negotiated
prices), confirming that the urgency premium does not exist outside
the litigation pool.

\paragraph{Contributions}
Our central contribution is conceptual: \emph{the sourcing-vs-pricing
distinction.} Standard intuition under accountability mechanisms relies
on within-firm pricing distortion; the within firm-buyer-item triple
evidence cleanly rejects that mechanism in this setting and isolates
sourcing under fragmentation as the empirical signature of
accountability-induced inefficiency. Two supporting contributions
follow: (i) we provide direct within-item evidence on the price impact
of court-mandated procurement, exploiting an institutional within-urgent
contrast unique to S\~ao Paulo; and (ii) in contrast to
\citet{coviello2018court}---where slow courts \emph{extend} procurement
timelines and raise prices through delay---we study judicial injunctions
that \emph{compress} timelines, with the cost concentrated in items
where demand aggregation matters most. The two papers identify
alternative institutional channels through which judicial intervention
raises procurement costs.

\paragraph{Welfare and policy}
Applying the bounded UTG to the \BPwelfareBoundAnnualSpend{} of annual
litigated spending in S\~ao Paulo, with an admissibility-share
calibration of \BPwelfareBoundAdmissibility, gives a per-unit-price
welfare bound of \BPwelfareBound{} per year, with the Lee-endpoint
range delivering $[\BPwelfareBoundCIlow, \BPwelfareBoundCIhigh]$. We
report a single bounded figure rather than the stacked ranges common in
the policy literature. The mechanism the paper isolates---sourcing
under fragmentation, not pricing under sanctions---points to
demand-aggregation reforms (framework agreements, expanded
administrative-channel capacity), not to contract-design changes
deterring opportunistic markup. Brazil's Lei~14.133/2021, which
introduces framework agreements, sets up a natural test of this
prediction in post-reform data.

\paragraph{Position vis-à-vis the literature}
The paper sits at the intersection of three strands. Procurement design
and frictions
\citep{bandiera2009active, best2023procurement, bosio2022public,
decarolis2018buyer, carril2026competition}: the within-firm-buyer-item
identification we use isolates the sourcing margin from the pricing
margin, a distinction the cross-buyer literature cannot make. Bureaucratic
distortion under accountability mechanisms
\citep{prendergast2007bureaucrats, bandiera2021allocation}: our
within-firm null delivers a direct test of the delivery-risk-premium
prediction, which we reject. The judicialization of health in Latin
America \citep{wang2015right, ferraz2009right, biehl2009will}: we
provide the first bid-level cost-channel decomposition of the
procurement consequences. Our methodological discipline honors
post-treatment-bias \citep{acharya2016explaining}: quantity- and
firm-fixed-effect controls are read as channel-isolating rather than
direct-effect estimators, and the within firm-buyer-item triple is the
mechanism's positive evidence.

Figure~\ref{fig:cascade} previews the central decomposition: the
within firm-buyer-item bar is statistically indistinguishable from
zero, while the mechanical bulk-discount channel and the supplier
composition residual jointly account for the observed gap.
