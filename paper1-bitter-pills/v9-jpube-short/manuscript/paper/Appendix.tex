\section{Data Construction and Classifier Validation}
\label{app:classifier}

The procurement data come from BEC pharmaceutical purchases in group \BPbecGroup{} during \BPsampleStartYear--\BPsampleEndYear{}, linked to litigation and administrative-process markers. The classifier operates at the purchase-order/tender-notice level; the empirical analysis is conducted at the purchase-offer-item (POI) level after classified regimes are linked to BEC item records and sample restrictions are applied. Price regressions use accepted winning bids. The classification rule combines machine-learning predictions, structured JSON flags, regex patterns, and position-based checks, weighted \BPclfWeightML{}, \BPclfWeightJSON{}, \BPclfWeightRegex{}, and \BPclfWeightPosition{}, and classifies a regime when the vote reaches \BPclfVoteThreshold{}; \BPclfNPatternsBoiler{} boilerplate patterns are excluded.

\input{../../output/tables/tab_classifier_validation_v9}

The classifier universe contains \BPregexNClassified{} classified purchase orders, larger than the \BPnPOIfull{} POI-level analysis file because classification is upstream of item-level linkage. Validation against \BPregexNGroundTruth{} ground-truth purchase orders gives \BPregexExactMatchPct{} exact agreement, with urgent-class F1 of \BPregexFoneJud{} (judicial) and \BPregexFoneAdm{} (administrative) and macro-F1 \BPregexFone{}. A purchase-order-level error-sensitivity diagnostic (not tabulated) implies contamination rates that are small relative to the regime differences studied here; remaining misclassification would attenuate, not inflate, the regime contrasts.

The analysis sample contains \BPnAnalysisSample{} POI observations; the winners-only price sample \BPnegNobs{} accepted winning bids; the urgent panel \BPnUTG{} winner observations; and the firm-buyer-item triple sample \BPutgTripleNUTG{} observations in \BPutgTripleCountUTG{} triples. Administrative and litigated urgent purchases are not balanced by design: administrative purchases are selected, larger, and differ in item composition. The design therefore combines selection bounds, within-firm pricing tests, and direct sourcing evidence rather than treating the administrative channel as randomly assigned.

\section{Selection Bounds}
\label{app:selection_bounds}

Administrative requests are screened before purchase, which likely makes administrative cases easier or cheaper to fulfill, so naive administrative-versus-litigated comparisons can overstate the sanction-related gap. The main paper's Table~1, Panel~B reports the resulting Lee bounds; this section documents the trimming and its robustness. All coefficients are administrative minus litigated log negotiated prices, so a negative coefficient means litigated purchases are more expensive; percentage gaps are reported in the reader-facing litigated-over-administrative direction. Trimming the high and low tails of the administrative price distribution within item~$\times$~year~$\times$~PBU strata gives the lower and upper bounds. Trimming affects \BPutgLeeStrataExcess{} of \BPutgLeeStrataTotal{} strata, with mean rate \BPutgLeeTrimMean{} and maximum \BPutgLeeTrimMax{}. The bounded interval is robust to violations of the trimming monotonicity assumption: trimming an extra share of the administrative tail within each stratum to absorb such violations leaves the litigated-over-administrative gap positive until the additional trim exceeds \BPwfLeeBreakdownDelta{}, far beyond what the data require. A parametric (Heckman-type) selection correction is non-informative in this design---the available exclusion is weak and the fixed effects absorb most identifying variation---so the bounds plus mechanism evidence are preferred to a point estimate.

\section{Robustness and Diagnostics}
\label{app:robustness}

\textit{Placebo.} The placebo uses items never observed in litigation, checking whether the urgent-procurement pattern appears outside the litigation margin rather than reflecting generic platform or time-trend dynamics.

\input{../../output/tables/tab_placebo}

\textit{Inference.} Because PBU clusters are few and uneven, Rademacher wild-cluster inference for the under-the-gun contrast gives $p=\BPutgBoottestPval{}$ for the preferred specification and $p=\BPutgBoottestPvalIYM{}$ for the tighter item-by-year-month specification; the contrast continues to reject a zero gap, though inference does not address the selection concern handled by the bounds.

\textit{Bounding the null.} The within firm-buyer-item null---administrative coefficient \BPutgTripleCoefUTG{} (SE \BPutgTripleSEUTG{})---could be a genuine null or merely underpowered. A two-one-sided-tests (TOST) equivalence test on the within-triple coefficient rejects broad same-firm markups above about \BPwfNullRuleOutPct{} (one-sided upper 95\% bound). At a \BPwfTostMarginPct{} equivalence margin the TOST $p$-value is \BPwfTostPten{}, and at a 5\% margin it is \BPwfTostPfive{}; the minimum detectable effect at 80\% power is \BPwfNullMdePct{}, and power against a 10\% markup is \BPwfNullPowerTen{}. The deep-market data therefore rule out broad markups but are underpowered against modest ones, which is why the result is reported as ``no broad same-firm markup'' rather than a zero.

\textit{Multiple testing.} The within firm-buyer-item heterogeneity splits (main paper Table~2) could be multiple-testing artifacts. They survive correction: under Holm the below-median-quantity coefficient has adjusted $p=\BPwfBelowQtyHolm{}$ and the earlier-period coefficient $p=\BPwfEarlyHolm{}$, and a Romano-Wolf free step-down (PBU-cluster bootstrap, 999 replications) gives $p=\BPwfBelowQtyRW{}$ and $p=\BPwfEarlyRW{}$.

\textit{Channel diagnostics.} Adding supplier fixed effects and log quantity attenuates the urgent price coefficient, consistent with sourcing and scale rather than a broad same-firm markup. Conditioning tightly on log quantity is a channel diagnostic, not a robustness test of the total effect: quantity is itself a margin of judicial urgency, so the coefficient can change sign once the scale channel is partialled out, and the preferred total object remains the no-quantity-control bounded estimate. A direct aggregation comparison within common buyer-item-month cells is mixed---administrative cells carry larger total quantity while litigated cells show more repeated purchase-offer-items---and is suggestive of fragmented repeated demand only in combination with the main-paper decomposition figure and winner-switching evidence. Heterogeneity of the within firm-buyer-item test across quantity, formulary, and period subsamples is reported in the main paper's Table~2; the deep-market null, the residual within-firm gap in the earlier period, and the finding that the quantity dimension instead tracks scale are all part of the mechanism evidence.

\textit{Supplier turnover benchmark.} The winner-switching evidence (main paper Table~3) could reflect ordinary supplier turnover rather than regime-driven reallocation. A within-regime placebo---randomly splitting each regime's purchases for the same item-buyer pair and computing the winner-set overlap between halves, averaged over 200 seeded random splits---gives a baseline Jaccard similarity of \BPwfChurnBaselineJaccard{}, against a cross-regime Jaccard of \BPwinnerSwitchJaccardMean{} (a gap of \BPwfChurnGap{}). The regime change pushes winner-set overlap below normal churn, so the reallocation is specific to the litigated-versus-administrative contrast rather than background turnover.

\textit{Dynamics.} The BJS event study assesses timing around item-level exposure and is diagnostic, not the main identification strategy. The first post-exposure estimate is \BPbjsETzero{} (SE \BPbjsETzeroSE{}) and the five-period estimate is \BPbjsETfive{} (SE \BPbjsETfiveSE{}). Honest-DiD sensitivity does not survive deviations at the observed maximum pre-period scale, so the main claims rest on the selection bounds, the within firm-buyer-item pricing test, and the sourcing evidence.

\section{Procurement-Cost Calculation}
\label{app:cost}

The fiscal procurement-cost calculation applies the Lee midpoint \BPprocCostBoundUTGmidPct{} to an admissibility calibration of \BPprocCostBoundAdmissibility{} and annual litigated spending of \BPprocCostBoundAnnualSpend{}, yielding \BPprocCostBound{} per year (Lee-bound range \BPprocCostBoundCIlow{}--\BPprocCostBoundCIhigh{}). This is a fiscal procurement-cost calculation, not a full welfare estimate: it excludes health benefits, search and compliance costs, and other welfare components, and it should not be read as the value of scaling back legal enforcement. Varying the admissible share of litigated spending or the annual-spending anchor moves the figure mechanically and leaves the bounded under-the-gun gap unchanged.
