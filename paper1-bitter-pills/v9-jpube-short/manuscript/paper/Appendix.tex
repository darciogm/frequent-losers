\section{Data Construction and Classifier Validation}
\label{app:classifier}

The procurement data come from BEC pharmaceutical purchases in group \BPbecGroup{} during \BPsampleStartYear--\BPsampleEndYear. The construction starts from \BPregexNClassified{} classified purchase orders and links procurement fields to litigation and administrative-process markers. Ordinary purchases are those without urgent judicial or administrative markers. Administrative urgent purchases are requests processed through the health agency's administrative channel. Litigated urgent purchases are purchases tied to court-mandated delivery.

The classifier combines four signals: machine-learning predictions, structured JSON flags, regex patterns, and position-based checks in the tender notice. The production rule uses weights \BPclfWeightML{}, \BPclfWeightJSON{}, \BPclfWeightRegex{}, and \BPclfWeightPosition{} for these signals and classifies a regime when the vote reaches \BPclfVoteThreshold{}. Boilerplate language is filtered using \BPclfNPatternsBoiler{} exclusion patterns.

\input{../../output/tables/tab_classifier_validation_v9}

Validation against \BPregexNGroundTruth{} ground-truth observations gives \BPregexExactMatchPct{} exact agreement. The urgent-class F1 scores are \BPregexFoneJud{} for judicial purchases and \BPregexFoneAdm{} for administrative purchases; the macro-F1 over these urgent classes is \BPregexFone{}. The held-out machine-learning test set contains \BPregexNHO{} observations and produces F1 scores of \BPregexFoneJudHO{} and \BPregexFoneAdmHO{}. Because the empirical comparisons use classified regimes, remaining false positives and false negatives would attenuate regime contrasts if they are not systematically correlated with residual prices within controls.

\section{Selection Bounds}
\label{app:selection_bounds}

The administrative channel screens requests before purchase. Let $A=1$ denote administrative urgent procurement and $A=0$ denote litigated urgent procurement. The main under-the-gun coefficient is the administrative-minus-litigated log price coefficient. A negative coefficient means litigated purchases are more expensive.

The Manski-Lee procedure trims the overrepresented administrative group within item-by-year-by-buyer strata. If administrative cases are selected among requests that would otherwise appear under litigation, monotone selection implies that trimming one tail of the administrative price distribution bounds the counterfactual litigated comparison. Trimming the top tail produces one bound; trimming the bottom tail produces the other. The preferred bounds imply a litigated-over-administrative price gap of $[\BPutgBoundLow,\BPutgBoundHigh]$.

\input{../../output/tables/tab_utg_lee_bounds}

The mean trimming rate is \BPutgLeeTrimMean{}, with a maximum of \BPutgLeeTrimMax{}. Administrative-excess trimming occurs in \BPutgLeeStrataExcess{} of \BPutgLeeStrataTotal{} strata. The sign convention in all tables is administrative minus litigated for coefficients and litigated over administrative for percentage gaps.

The Heckman-style correction is reported only as a diagnostic. The first-stage exclusions are weak for this institutional comparison, and the second-stage estimate is not informative. The main text therefore relies on trimming bounds and mechanism tests.

\section{Procurement-Cost Calculation}
\label{app:cost}

The procurement-cost calculation applies the Lee midpoint \BPwelfareBoundUTGmidPct{} to an admissibility share of \BPwelfareBoundAdmissibility{} and annual litigated spending of \BPwelfareBoundAnnualSpend{}. This yields \BPwelfareBound{} per year, with a Lee-bound range from \BPwelfareBoundCIlow{} to \BPwelfareBoundCIhigh{}. This is a fiscal procurement-cost calculation, not a welfare estimate.
