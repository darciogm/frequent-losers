\label{app:classifier}

The regime classifier operates at the purchase-order/tender-notice
level, before classified orders are linked to purchase-offer-item
(POI) records on BEC. This is the institutional level at which legal
status is observed; prices, quantities, winners, and bidder
participation are then measured at the POI level.

\section{Selection Bounds and Inference}
\label{app:selection_inference}

Administrative requests are screened before purchase, so the
administrative-versus-litigated coefficient is bounded rather than
point-identified. The main paper uses Lee trimming within item
$\times$ year $\times$ PBU strata. Trimming affects
\BPutgLeeStrataExcess{} of \BPutgLeeStrataTotal{} strata, with mean
rate \BPutgLeeTrimMean{} and maximum \BPutgLeeTrimMax{}. Coefficients
are estimated as administrative minus litigated; tables report the
reader-facing litigated-over-administrative gap.

\section{Identification Diagnostics}
\label{app:diagnostics}

The first diagnostic uses items never observed in litigation. If the
urgent-price pattern were a generic feature of BEC dynamics or
administrative urgency, the same pattern should appear in that sample.
It does not: the negotiated-price placebo coefficient is
\BPplaceboNegCoef{} with standard error \BPplaceboNegSE{}.

\section{Mechanism Diagnostics}
\label{app:mechanisms}

The main mechanism claim combines three pieces of evidence. First, the
same-firm pricing test conditions on firm-by-item-by-buyer triples.
The one-sided upper 95\% bound rules out broad same-firm markups above
about \BPwfNullRuleOutPct{} in this repeated urgent-market sample. At
a \BPwfTostMarginPct{} equivalence margin, the TOST $p$-value is
\BPwfTostPten{}; at a 5\% margin, it is \BPwfTostPfive{}. The minimum
detectable effect at 80\% power is \BPwfNullMdePct{}, and power
against a 10\% markup is \BPwfNullPowerTen{}. These diagnostics justify
the limited wording that the data do not show a broad same-firm markup
in deep repeated urgent markets.

Second, quantity is a mechanism, not a control for a preferred direct
effect. Administrative urgent orders are larger, and the main figure
uses the bulk-discount elasticity to show how scale loss contributes
to the administrative-minus-litigated price gap. Third, winner-set
evidence shows supplier-set reallocation. Randomly splitting purchases
within the same regime gives a baseline Jaccard similarity of
\BPwfChurnBaselineJaccard{}, while the cross-regime Jaccard is
\BPwinnerSwitchJaccardMean{}, a gap of \BPwfChurnGap{}. The
cross-regime difference is therefore larger than ordinary turnover
within a regime.

The heterogeneity evidence in the main table is interpreted as
mechanism evidence. The same-firm margin is weak in deeper repeated
urgent markets, while supplier leverage can reappear in thinner or
earlier markets. The mechanism claim is therefore conditional: the
core cost margin is fragmented sourcing, with same-firm pricing more
plausible where demand is small, irregular, or institutionally less
routinized.

\section{Fiscal Procurement-Cost Implication}
\label{app:cost}

The fiscal calculation translates the Lee-bounded sanction margin into
a procurement-cost magnitude. It applies the Lee midpoint
\BPprocCostBoundUTGmidPct{} to an admissibility calibration of
\BPprocCostBoundAdmissibility{} and annual litigated spending of
\BPprocCostBoundAnnualSpend{}, yielding \BPprocCostBound{} per year
(Lee-bound range \BPprocCostBoundCIlow{}--\BPprocCostBoundCIhigh{}).
This is not a full social valuation: it excludes health benefits,
search costs, compliance costs, and other relevant components.
