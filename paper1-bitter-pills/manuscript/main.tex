\title{\textbf{Bitter Pills to Swallow: The Enforcement Costs of Health Litigation\tnoteref{tfoot}}}
\tnotetext[tfoot]{\emph{This version: April 2025 (First version: January 2023)}}
\author[INSPER]{Darcio Genicolo-Martins} \ead{darciogm1@insper.edu.br} 
\author[INSPER]{Paulo Furquim de Azevedo} \ead{paulofa1@insper.edu.br}
\address[INSPER]{INSPER}

\begin{abstract}
Public health procurement is shaped not only by administrative decisions but also by judicial interventions that compel urgent purchases. These court-mandated procurements carry substantial fiscal costs for two main reasons: they undermine procurement planning and expose officials to sanctions that incentivize compliance over cost-efficiency—a mechanism we term the "under the gun" effect. Using matched administrative and procurement data from Brazil (2009–2019), we estimate that judicial enforcement increases negotiated prices by 30.8\% to 44.4\%, reduces bidder participation by up to 32.2\%, and lowers success rates by nearly 49\%. Isolating the sanction channel, we find judicial pressure alone raises prices by 8.8\% to 10\%. These findings highlight the economic burden of judicial enforcement and suggest that legal mandates should weigh procurement efficiency alongside individual rights.
\end{abstract}

\end{frontmatter}
