\item \textit{Notes.} Sensitivity of the population-weighted Sun--Abraham post-period

parallel trends. Inputs are the event-study coefficient vector (pre periods $e\in[-6,-2]$,

population-share aggregation weights that reproduce the headline ATT (suicide $+0.28$,

are reported. Under relative magnitude (RM), $M$ caps the post-period parallel-trends
violation at $M$ times the largest single pre-period deviation (suicide $1.05$, self-harm $1.29$, ICSAP $2.99$ per unit).
Under smoothness ($\Delta^{SD}$), $M$ caps how much the differential trend's slope may
change per period. $M=0$ is the original confidence interval.
The substantive threshold is a quarter of the pre-treatment treated baseline ($0.25\times5.01=1.25$ suicide; $0.25\times7.58=1.89$ self-harm).
At this per-100k noise level the RM extrapolation is uninformative beyond $M=0$ for both mortality outcomes (RM breakdown $M=0$ suicide, $M=0$ self-harm): one noisy pre-period jump dominates the bound.
The smoothness restriction is tighter but still crosses the substantive threshold immediately ($\Delta^{SD}$ breakdown $M=0$ suicide, $M=0$ self-harm), so the null is supported by the classical interval rather than by extrapolation.
The ICSAP placebo correctly fails: its classical CI already includes zero, so no bounded
extrapolation can rescue a causal effect (breakdown $=$ none).
