\subsection{Exposure-validation placebo: the disagreement set}
\label{app:disagreement-placebo}

The flow and distance rules disagree on most flagged pairs. That disagreement is
itself a test: if the flow rule captures revealed reliance, the first stage should
appear among municipalities flows flag, not among those distance flags but flows
miss. I split each municipality's earliest flagged closure into flow-only,
both-rules, and distance-only cohorts and estimate the headline event study for each
against never-flagged controls (Table~\ref{tab:disagreement-placebo},
Figure~\ref{fig:disagreement-first-stage}). The discriminating outcome is inpatient
psychiatric admissions: flow-flagged municipalities lose about $3.7$ per $1{,}000$
with flat pre-trends, roughly four times the movement among distance-only false
positives, whose smaller change also fails the pre-trend test. Travel burden, by
contrast, falls for any municipality near a closing hospital and does not separate
the groups. Read together, the first stage loads primarily on revealed use rather
than proximity, though distance-only municipalities are not pure controls: they sit
on differential pre-trends, the contamination the flow rule avoids. This corroborates
the exposure rule; it does not replace the travel-burden and utilization first stages
in the main text.

    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.92\textwidth]{../04_figures/fig_disagreement_first_stage.pdf}
    \caption{Exposure-validation placebo, PNASH psychiatric sample.

\subsection{Causal test of non-absorption}

The decomposition is descriptive. To net out trends, I re-estimate the headline
staggered Sun-Abraham specification with the outcome redefined as inpatient
psychiatric admissions at hospitals \emph{other} than the focal closing hospital,
per $1{,}000$ residents. The same specification applied to total admissions
reproduces the $69\%$ decline; admissions at other hospitals are statistically
unchanged (Table~\ref{tab:other-hosp-es}, Figure~\ref{fig:es-other-hosp}), so the
non-absorption is causal, not an artifact of pre-existing trends.

\section{Sensitivity to Pre-Trend Violations}

Table~\ref{tab:honestdid-sensitivity} and Figure~\ref{fig:honestdid} report
HonestDiD relative-magnitude sensitivity \citep{rambachanRoth2023} for the
mortality outcomes and for ICSAP. At the per-$100{,}000$ scale of small
municipalities the mortality series is noisy, so the robust set widens
rapidly once any pre-trend extrapolation is allowed: relative-magnitude
restrictions are uninformative for the mortality null, which therefore
rests on the classical interval and the synthetic design rather than on
trend extrapolation. The same machinery is informative against ICSAP, where
no bounded extrapolation can rescue the effect.

\subsection{Network-spillover contamination of controls}
\label{app:network-spillover}

A closure reroutes its catchment's patients onto substitute hospitals. If a
never-treated control municipality uses those same hospitals, the closure changes
its access as well, contaminating the control and biasing the estimate toward zero.
The main robustness battery drops controls that share a referral hub, a substitute
hospital, or a high flow-similarity link with treated catchments.
Table~\ref{tab:network-spillover} adds a single, transparent network definition.
Using the projected municipality co-patiency network---an edge joins two
municipalities in proportion to how much they co-use the same hospitals---I flag as
contaminated any never-treated municipality joined to an exposed catchment by a
co-patiency edge of weight at least $\theta$, and re-estimate the suicide event
study after dropping them. Because spillover attenuates a true effect toward zero,
removing contaminated controls would \emph{raise} the estimate if an effect were
hidden. Dropping up to $1{,}173$ network-linked controls leaves the suicide ATT a
bounded null ($+0.28$ to $+0.36$ per $100{,}000$, every interval covering zero), so
the null is not an artifact of spillover-polluted controls.

\paragraph{Triple-difference against a placebo cause.} A triple-difference
that nets the suicide estimate against a placebo cause of death---acute
myocardial infarction, which a psychiatric-bed closure should not move but
which tracks the same local economic and health-system shocks---does not
sharpen the null: at $\valNclosuresPnash$ closures the placebo cause is
itself imprecisely estimated, so the subtraction widens the interval rather
than tightening it. Identification therefore rests on the timing argument,
the flat event-study pre-trends, and the robustness battery, not on a
placebo-cause subtraction.

    \centering

    \caption{Spillover-robust controls. Population-weighted Sun-Abraham
    suicide event studies on the $48$ PNASH psychiatric closures, comparing
    the headline specification (all never-treated controls) against one that
    drops the $1{,}424$ never-treated municipalities whose own admissions to
