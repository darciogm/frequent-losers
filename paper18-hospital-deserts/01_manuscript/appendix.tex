\section{Closure filter cascade ($F1$--$F6$)}
\label{app:filter}

The closure sample is constructed from the universe of $3{,}518$ CNES
establishments that stop reporting any monthly competence between
$2010$ and $2023$. Most of these are not hospital closures in any
economically meaningful sense: small clinics, day units, and registry
artefacts where the same legal entity continues to operate under a
different CNES code. We apply five sequential statistical filters
($F1$ through $F5$) to obtain the F5 main sample of $\valFiltFive$ exogenous
closures, and a sixth NLP-documented filter ($F6$) to define the
robustness sub-sample of $\valFiltSix$ closures.

\paragraph{The five statistical filters.} Table~\ref{tab:filter} reports
the surviving $N$ at each step.

\paragraph{Filter $F1$.} Closure years are restricted to two windows that
together exclude both border-of-panel artefacts (closures in $2010$ or
$2011$ have no $\geq 5$-year pre-period) and the COVID-$19$ pandemic
disruption window ($2020$--$2021$, where admission patterns are
non-stationary). The two retained windows are $\{2012, \ldots, 2018\}$
(seven pre-pandemic cohorts) and $\{2022, 2023\}$ (two post-pandemic
cohorts). The filter discards roughly half the candidate set
($1{,}690$ events).

\paragraph{Filter $F2$.} CNES distinguishes establishment types via the
\texttt{tp\_unid} code; we retain those that the Brazilian Ministry of
Health classifies as inpatient-bed hospitals: $05$ (general hospital),
$07$ (specialized hospital), $15$ (mixed unit), and $62$ (day hospital).
The filter discards $465$ establishments that are CAPS, posto de saúde,
\textit{unidade básica}, or other non-hospital units.

\paragraph{Filter $F3$.} The bed register \texttt{cnes\_lt} reports
contracted bed counts by specialty type. We retain establishments with
average \texttt{QT\_SUS} $\geq 30$ in the year preceding closure. The
$30$-bed threshold corresponds approximately to the lower bound of
``small hospital'' as classified by the Brazilian National Quality
Assessment Program (PNASH); below this threshold the establishment is
typically an outpatient facility regardless of the \texttt{tp\_unid}
classification. This filter is severe ($1{,}243$ events discarded),
indicating the register is heavily populated by small or part-bed
units.

\paragraph{Filter $F4$.} We require at least $100$ AIH (admissions) in
the year preceding closure. This screens out registry entries that
formally classify as hospitals but are functionally inactive prior to
closure. The filter discards another $30$ events.

\paragraph{Filter $F5$.} We rule out gradual exit due to local demand
collapse by requiring that the admission count in the year preceding
closure (year $-1$) be at least $50\%$ of the average over years $-3$
and $-2$. Closures with admissions trending sharply downward already
in the pre-period are discarded ($30$ events). The remaining $\valFiltFive$ closures form the F5 main sample.

\paragraph{Distribution of the F5 main sample.} The geographic
distribution concentrates in the Southeast ($37$ events, $62\%$), with
meaningful presence in the Northeast ($15$, $25\%$) and smaller
representation in the Center-West ($3$), South ($3$), and North ($2$).
By facility type: $41$ specialized hospitals (\texttt{tp\_unid}=07),
$18$ general hospitals (\texttt{tp\_unid}=05), and one hospital-day
unit (\texttt{tp\_unid}=62). By closure year: $11$ events in $2012$
(the densest cohort, reflecting the early implementation of the
psychiatric reform audit cycles), $4$--$9$ events in each subsequent
pre-pandemic year, $11$ in $2022$, and $3$ in $2023$.

\paragraph{Filter $F6$.} The NLP classification of declared closure
motives (Appendix~\ref{app:nlp}) yields $\valFiltSix$ events whose declared
motive falls in \{administrativo, fiscal, fal\^encia\}. The $9$ events
dropped relative to F5 are those with motive \texttt{unknown} or where
Receita Federal cadastral data was not available. The F6 v2 sub-sample
serves as a motive-documented robustness check on the closure-exogeneity
filter; because its external validation is incomplete
(Appendix~\ref{app:nlp-validation}), it informs robustness only and not
the primary exogeneity argument, which rests on the PNASH calendar and
the reimbursement freeze (Section~\ref{sec:setting-psiquiatrica}).

\section{Cross-sectional diagnostic in detail}
\label{app:xs}

This appendix reports the $11$ cross-sectional ridge specifications
summarized in Figure~\ref{fig:cross-section-null}. They establish a
negative result that justifies two choices in the main text: the
embedding does not improve out-of-sample mortality prediction over simple
distance or travel-time baselines, which is why we keep it as a
descriptive diagnostic rather than a predictor; and the cross-section is
confounded in ways the within-event design is not, which is why we
identify the mortality effect only from closure shocks.

    \centering

    Filled markers: $\Delta R^2$ adding the embedding to a model that

    \label{fig:cross-section-null}

\paragraph{Specification.} For each outcome $Y_m$, we estimate
\begin{equation}
  Y_m \;=\; \alpha + \beta_{km} z(\log \mathrm{iso}^{km}_m) +
            \beta_{emb} z(\mathrm{iso}^{emb}_m) +
            X_m^\top \gamma + \mathrm{UF}_m + \varepsilon_m,
  \label{eq:xs}
\end{equation}
where the controls $X_m$ include population (log-mean) and GDP per
capita (log-mean), $\mathrm{UF}_m$ are Federation Unit fixed effects,
and the isolation measures $\mathrm{iso}^{km}_m$ and
$\mathrm{iso}^{emb}_m$ are the average kilometer and embedding distance
from~$m$ to its $K=5$ nearest hospitals respectively. We estimate
ridge regression with $\alpha = 1$ and report $5$-fold cross-validated
$R^2$ over a haversine-kilometer baseline. Four nested specifications
are estimated: $M_0$ with controls only, $M_{km}$ adding the kilometer
measure, $M_{emb}$ adding the embedding only, and $M_{both}$ adding
both.

the unrestricted universe of $n = \valNmunisDiverg$ municipalities; the
ten cause-specific and SIH-based regressions (Table~\ref{tab:xs-causes})
restrict to municipalities with mean population $\geq 10{,}000$
($n = 3{,}112$).

specification, and the within-municipality identification of
Section~\ref{sec:results-mortality} is the necessary correction.

\section{Patient-flow embedding: construction and measurement role}
\label{app:embedding}

The embedding is a descriptive summary of the referral network. It does
not enter the causal design---the treatment rule is the share-based
exposure of equation~\eqref{eq:share}, invariant to the embedding and its
seed---but it supplies the divergence measure mapped in
Figure~\ref{fig:map-divergence} and a check on whether realized flow
geography is recoverable from co-attendance alone.

\paragraph{TF-IDF projection.} Let $A \in \mathbb{R}_+^{|M| \times |H|}$
be the bipartite weight matrix, $A_{mh} = w(m,h)$. We form a
TF-IDF-projected municipality similarity
$S = \widetilde{A}\,\widetilde{A}^\top$ with
$\widetilde{A} = D_M^{-1} A\, D_H^{\mathrm{idf}}$, where $D_M$
row-normalizes so each municipality contributes equal mass and
$D_H^{\mathrm{idf}} = \mathrm{diag}(\log(|M|/\sum_m \mathbf{1}\{A_{mh}>0\}))$
down-weights hub hospitals serving many municipalities. We zero the
diagonal and keep the top-$30$ entries per row, yielding a sparse
symmetric graph $G^M$.

\paragraph{node2vec.} We embed $G^M$ into $\mathbb{R}^{128}$ with node2vec
\citep{grover2016} via PecanPy \citep{liu2021pecanpy}: unbiased walks
($p=q=1$), length $80$, $10$ walks per node, window $10$, $5$ epochs,
seed $42$. The embedding recovers Brazilian metropolitan and
\emph{ribeirinho} geography from flow alone (the cosine-nearest neighbors
of São Paulo capital are Greater São Paulo municipalities; those of
Manaus are river-accessible municipalities in its health region), and its
pair-distance correlation with haversine kilometers is only
$\rho = \valEmbKmCorr$, confirming it carries flow information distance does not.

\paragraph{Why it is not the treatment rule.} Two findings keep the
embedding in the appendix. Promoting the cosine geometry to the exposure
rule makes the treated set seed-dependent (Appendix~\ref{app:stability}),
whereas the share rule is exactly seed-invariant. And in a cross-section,
the embedding does \emph{not} improve out-of-sample mortality prediction
over simple distance or travel-time baselines (Appendix~\ref{app:xs}).
The embedding is therefore a measurement diagnostic, not a predictor or a
treatment assignment.

\section{Embedding stability across seeds}
\label{app:stability}

We probe the seed-to-seed stability of the patient-flow embedding in
two complementary ways. First, we audit the geometry itself by
re-training the node2vec model on the same pooled bipartite graph
under five seeds (\texttt{seeds} $\in \{42, 7, 13, 99, 2024\}$, with
$42$ as the baseline used throughout the main text). Second, we stress
test the embedding-driven exposure rule by re-training $20$ seeds and
rebuilding the alternative $E1_{emb}$ treatment assignment used in the
Phase B referee response. All other hyperparameters are fixed (dim
$= 128$, walks $= 10$, walk length $= 20$, window $= 10$, epochs $= 5$,
$p = q = 1$).

Two stability measures are reported. First, the Pearson correlation of
pair-wise embedding distances against the baseline seed,
computed on the same $5{,}000$ random municipality pairs across all
seeds (Table~\ref{tab:stab-pairs}). Second, the Jaccard overlap of
the cosine top-$10$ neighborhoods of two canonical municipalities
(São Paulo capital and Manaus) against the baseline
(Table~\ref{tab:stab-jaccard}).

\paragraph{Interpretation.} The pair-distance Pearson correlation of
$\rho \approx 0.77$ indicates that approximately $60\%$ of the
variance in pair-wise embedding distance is preserved across seeds;
the remaining variance is seed-specific noise driven by the
stochasticity of the random-walk and skip-gram training process. The
top-$10$ Jaccard values, while substantively non-trivial (chance under
a null of random selection from $5{,}588$ municipalities is
approximately $10/5{,}588 \approx 0.002$), do show meaningful
seed-to-seed variation. The implication is that individual
municipalities' exact top-$K$ neighbor lists should not be interpreted
as fixed properties of the embedding, but the substantive regional
clusters (the Greater São Paulo metropolitan area, the
\emph{ribeirinho} municipalities of Manaus, the sertão semi-arid
hinterland) are preserved across seeds. The divergence map of
Figure~\ref{fig:map-divergence} is read at the level of regional
patterns and signed magnitudes, not of any single municipality's exact
rank.

\paragraph{Implication for causal estimation.} The main share-based E1
design remains exactly invariant to the embedding seed because it is
constructed from raw bipartite admission counts $w(m, h)$ rather than
from cosine similarities. But that invariance disappears once the
cosine geometry is used directly to define treatment. The $20$-seed
rerun of the alternative $E1_{emb}$ rule yields a mean travel ATT of
$-1.50$~km (SD $0.36$; support $[-2.10, -0.77]$) and a mean treated
count of $700.5$, versus $-5.84$~km and $130$ treated municipalities
under the benchmark share-based E1. Table~\ref{tab:seed-att-distribution}
summarizes the distribution and Figure~\ref{fig:seed-att-distribution}
plots it. The seed-driven variation therefore matters for
geometry-derived exposure definitions, for the divergence map of
Figure~\ref{fig:map-divergence}, and for the cross-section diagnostic
outcome $\mathrm{iso}^{\mathrm{emb}}$ that enters the causal-forest
feature vector (Section~\ref{app:cf}), even though it does not
mechanically alter the baseline E1 design.

\section{NLP classification protocol}
\label{app:nlp}

This appendix documents the LLM-based classification of declared
closure motives that defines the F6 sub-sample.

\paragraph{Pipeline overview.} For each of the $90$ candidate closures
($60$ F5-survivors plus $30$ F5-recovery candidates from the
admission-decline filter), the pipeline assembles a context block
containing (i) closure metadata (CNES, year, municipality,
\texttt{tp\_unid}, pre-closure bed count and AIH count, ratio$_{\text{pre}}$),
(ii) Receita Federal corporate registry data retrieved via BrasilAPI
(legal name, trade name, CNAE, cadastral status \emph{ativa} /
\emph{baixada} / \emph{inapta}), and (iii) up to ten URL snippets
retrieved by two structured web searches. The context block is
submitted to a zero-shot Claude Sonnet 4.6 classifier under an
explicit anti-hallucination protocol that returns a JSON object with
fields \texttt{motivo}, \texttt{confidence} ($1$--$5$),
\texttt{reasoning} (Portuguese), \texttt{evidence\_flags}, and
\texttt{web\_source\_used}.

\paragraph{Eight-element schema.} The motive is one of:
\emph{administrativo} (de-accreditation under the psychiatric reform
or other federal administrative decision), \emph{fiscal} (Receita
Federal compliance suspension, INAPTA), \emph{fal\^encia} (declared
bankruptcy or BAIXADA), \emph{fus\~ao} (acquisition by another entity
operating under a new CNES code at the same location),
\emph{demanda} (genuine local demand collapse), \emph{pandemia}
(closure explicitly attributed to the COVID-$19$ disruption),
\emph{outros} (identifiable other cause), \emph{unknown} (insufficient
evidence to discriminate).

\paragraph{Cache schema.} Both BrasilAPI calls and Claude API calls
are cached in SQLite databases with deterministic keys
(\texttt{cnpj} for BrasilAPI; \texttt{(CNES, year, model\_id, prompt\_hash)}
for Claude). Re-running the pipeline with these caches in place skips
all external service calls and reproduces the published
classifications byte-for-byte.

\paragraph{Anti-hallucination protocol.} The system prompt instructs
the classifier explicitly:

\begin{quote}
\small\itshape
USE the web snippet when available. If it contradicts metadata
(e.g., Receita ATIVA but snippet documents fechamento real), trust the
snippet. DO NOT fabricate facts not present in metadata or snippets.
If snippet is missing AND metadata insufficient, return motivo$=$unknown
with confidence $\leq 2$. Specific patterns: ``Reforma Psiquiátrica''
$\to$ administrativo (conf $5$); ``descredenciamento SUS'' $\to$
administrativo (conf $4$--$5$); ``valor SUS sem reajuste /
estrangulamento financeiro'' $\to$ administrativo (conf $4$--$5$),
because this is institutional insolvency caused by federal policy, not
local demand. ``CNPJ BAIXADA'' alone $\to$ fal\^encia (conf $4$).
``CNPJ INAPTA'' alone $\to$ fiscal (conf $3$--$4$).
\end{quote}

The prompt's explicit categorization rules anchor the classifier
against the most common substantive misreading---treating
``estrangulamento financeiro'' (financial strangulation by federal
policy) as a demand-driven exit rather than as an administrative
closure. The full prompt and the per-CNES output (with retrieved
URLs and evidence flags) are in the replication package.

\paragraph{Human-in-the-loop validation.} We sampled $15$ closures
stratified by NLP-assigned motive and confidence (administrativo
high-conf, administrativo low-conf, fal\^encia, fiscal, unknown), and
the author reviewed the raw web snippets and Receita Federal data
independently before reading the LLM output. Inter-rater agreement
was full-class agreement on $14/15$ closures. The single disagreement
was a high-confidence \emph{administrativo} that the human reviewer
classified as \emph{fal\^encia} based on a court order document the
LLM had not weighted heavily (the Receita status was BAIXADA but the
LLM emphasized the de-accreditation news article). Cohen's $\kappa$
on the eight-element schema is approximately $0.86$, in the
near-perfect agreement range. The single disagreement is documented
in the replication notes; it does not affect the F6 sub-sample
membership for that closure (both motives map to the F6 inclusion set
\{administrativo, fiscal, fal\^encia\}). We acknowledge that the
human reviewer is the lead author of the manuscript, and that an
external (third-party) validation by a research assistant with no
stake in the manuscript's conclusions would strengthen the
exogeneity claim materially. We treat the present validation as
suggestive rather than dispositive. The replication package now
contains the operational next step: a blinded $10\%$ random sample of
the F5 universe ($6$ closures), exported for coding, plus a
two-coder/adjudication scaffold. Those human labels are still pending,
so all external-validation statistics remain unavailable at the time
of writing.

\paragraph{Selection in unknowns.} The $38$ \texttt{unknown}
classifications are not random: closures from $2022$--$2023$ are
over-represented because the Receita Federal cadastral status often
lags closure events by $1$--$2$ years, and the absence of a
documented motive trace correlates mechanically with closure recency.
The $9$ closures dropped relative to F5 (within F6) are skewed toward
post-pandemic events, which may differ on observable characteristics.
We re-ran the main F5 ATT estimates restricting to closures from
$2012$--$2018$ (the pre-pandemic cohorts) and obtain results
qualitatively unchanged from the full F5 main: travel burden under E1
is approximately $-5.2$~km versus the headline $-5.84$~km, and the
ICSAP under E1 is approximately $+0.95$~per~$1{,}000$ versus the
headline $+1.18$. The post-pandemic cohorts contribute marginally to
the average effect, and the F5/F6 comparison is therefore not
contaminated by the unknown-classification selection.

\section{Counterfactual graph perturbation}
\label{app:perturbation}

This appendix documents the counterfactual graph-perturbation
diagnostic---an auxiliary check on the embedding's measurement content,
bearing on neither the first stage nor the mortality result---and reports
the empirical distribution of the noise-corrected signal $z_m^{(h)}$
across all municipality--closure pairs.

\paragraph{Algorithm.} For each closing hospital $h$ with closure year
$t_h^{\star}$:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item Build the bipartite admission graph $G_{t_h^{\star} - 1}$ from
    the year preceding closure (year-aggregated SIH-RD admissions).
    \item Train four \emph{baseline} embeddings on $G_{t_h^{\star} - 1}$
    with seeds $\{42, 7, 13, 99\}$, holding all hyperparameters fixed.
    \item Compute the cosine top-$10$ neighborhood
    $\mathcal{N}_m^{(s)}$ for every municipality $m$ in each seed
    embedding $s$.
    \item For each pair of baseline seeds $(s, s')$, compute the
    Jaccard $J_m^{(s, s')} = |\mathcal{N}_m^{(s)} \cap
    \mathcal{N}_m^{(s')}| / |\mathcal{N}_m^{(s)} \cup
    \mathcal{N}_m^{(s')}|$. Average across all $\binom{4}{2} = 6$ pairs
    to obtain the intra-baseline Jaccard
    $\overline{J}_m^{\mathrm{intra}}$ and its standard deviation
    $\sigma_m^{\mathrm{intra}}$.
    \item Build the \emph{perturbed} graph $G_{t_h^{\star} - 1}^{\setminus h}$
    by removing all edges incident to $h$, and train one perturbed
    embedding on this graph with seed $2024$.
    \item Compute $\mathcal{N}_m^{\mathrm{pert}, \setminus h}$ and
    Jaccard $J_m^{(h, \mathrm{vs\,base})}$ against each of the four
    baselines, averaging.
    \item Form the noise-corrected signal $z_m^{(h)}$ as
\begin{equation}
  z_m^{(h)} \;=\;
  \frac{\overline{J}_m^{\mathrm{intra}} - J_m^{(h, \mathrm{vs\,base})}}{
        \sigma_m^{\mathrm{intra}}},
  \label{eq:z-perturbation}
\end{equation}
    where $J_m^{(h, \mathrm{vs\,base})}$ is the average Jaccard between
    the perturbed embedding (one seed) and the four baselines.
\end{enumerate}

The procedure runs in approximately one hour for $61$ closing
hospitals on the workstation described in Appendix~\ref{app:replication};
the total set of $61 \times 4 + 61 = 305$ embedding trainings is the
dominant compute cost.

\paragraph{Distribution of the signal.} Across $334{,}946$
municipality--closure pairs, the median $z_m^{(h)}$ is approximately
zero (centered, as expected under the null hypothesis of no
topological displacement), the inter-quartile range is $[-0.60,
+0.70]$, and the distribution carries a long right tail:
$8.2\%$ of pairs exceed $z = 1.96$, $5.9\%$ exceed $z = 2.58$,
$4.8\%$ exceed $z = 3$, and $2.7\%$ exceed $z = 5$
(Figure~\ref{fig:perturbation-zhist}). The empirical rate of
exceedance at $z = 1.96$ is $3.3\times$ the nominal $2.5\%$ rate, at
$z = 2.58$ is $11.7\times$ the nominal $0.5\%$ rate, and at $z = 5$ is
many orders of magnitude beyond the Gaussian nominal. The
distribution's right tail is thicker than a noise-only null can
generate.

\paragraph{Threshold sensitivity (re-confirming Section~\ref{sec:robustness}.7).}
Re-defining E1 as $z_m^{(h)} > z^\star$ at thresholds
$z^\star \in \{1.96, 2.58, 3, 5\}$ produces treated sets of size
$27{,}428$, $19{,}656$, $16{,}061$, and $8{,}950$ municipality--closure
pairs respectively (each pair where any hospital closes counts; some
pairs are sums across multiple closures). Reduced to unique
municipalities ever-treated under each threshold, the treated set
remains $> 4{,}500$ municipalities at $z^\star = 5$, confirming that
the perturbation diffuses too broadly to define a discriminating
binary exposure. The procedure's role in the analysis is therefore
the validation of the conceptual content of E1
(Section~\ref{app:stability}), not as an alternative
operationalization.

\section{Causal forest details}
\label{app:cf}

The causal forest is an auxiliary, cross-sectional first-difference
diagnostic of treatment-effect heterogeneity. It is not part of the
identified mortality result and we read it cautiously; we report it for
completeness and because it locates where the first-stage travel effect
concentrates.

\paragraph{Hyperparameters and configuration.} The causal forest is
trained with $4{,}000$ trees and honesty enabled (a $50/50$ split
between training and estimation samples within each tree, so leaf-level
treatment effect estimates are independent of the data used to grow
the tree). The propensity score $\widehat{e}(x)$ is estimated by a
parallel regression forest, and the forest applies the doubly-robust
transformation of \citet{wagerAthey2018} to construct the
orthogonalized score driving treatment-effect estimation. The seed is
$42$. All other hyperparameters use the \texttt{grf} package defaults
(\texttt{R} package \texttt{grf} version~$2.x$; minimum node size $5$,
\texttt{min.node.size = 5}; \texttt{alpha = 0.05}). The runtime is
approximately $25$~seconds per outcome on the workstation described in
Appendix~\ref{app:replication}.

\paragraph{Treatment, outcome, and feature vector.} Concretely:
$W_m = 1\{m \text{ is flow-exposed by any
closure with } g_m \in [2012, 2018]\}$; $\Delta Y_m = \overline{Y}_{m,
[2018, 2023]} - \overline{Y}_{m, [2010, 2015]}$ for the two outcomes
(travel burden in km, ICSAP rate per $1{,}000$); the feature vector
$X_m$ collects the standardized embedding-revealed isolation, the
standardized km-revealed isolation, the divergence
$\Delta_m = \mathrm{iso}^{\mathrm{emb}}_m -
\mathrm{iso}^{\mathrm{km}}_m$, log-population, log-GDP, the share of
deaths recorded with ill-defined cause (R96--R99 ICD-10 codes), and
$26$ Federation-Unit dummies.

\paragraph{Variable importance ranking.} The full ordered variable
importance for both outcomes is reported in
Table~\ref{tab:cf-vi-full}. The top three features for travel burden
(log-GDP, divergence, log-population) account for $66\%$ of decision
splits; for ICSAP, the same three features account for $58\%$.
Federation-Unit fixed effects collectively contribute the remainder.

\section{Additional sensitivity outcomes}
\label{app:sensitivity}

This appendix reports the AMI in-hospital mortality recipient-side
sensitivity analysis referenced in Section~\ref{sec:data-sih}.

\paragraph{Construction.} For each AIH (admission record) in SIH-RD
with primary diagnosis \texttt{DIAG\_PRINC} $\in$ \texttt{I21*} (acute
myocardial infarction), we record the in-hospital death indicator
\texttt{MORTE} and aggregate to the municipality-of-residence × year
panel: $Y_{m, t} = $ (deaths among AMI admissions of $m$-residents
in year $t$) / (AMI admissions of $m$-residents in year $t$). Note
that this is computed at the \emph{hospital-receiving end}---the
admission, regardless of where the hospital is located---which
sidesteps the SIM cross-border death-recording problem (Section~\ref{sec:data-sim}).
The outcome is a percentage (typical mean $\approx 11.8\%$).

\paragraph{Power considerations.} AMI in-hospital mortality is a
rate-of-rate outcome with a small denominator in many municipalities
(small towns may have $< 10$ AMI admissions per year). Power for
detecting a closure-induced change is limited; we report the
estimate primarily as a tertiary outcome and as a triangulation
device.

\paragraph{Estimation.} We re-run the staggered Sun-Abraham specification
of Section~\ref{sec:method-estimator} with $Y_{m, t}$ as the outcome on the
F5 main panel, using the flow exposure rule. The simple ATT is
approximately $+0.3$~percentage points ($\mathrm{SE} \approx 0.5$), not
statistically distinguishable from zero. We report it as an additional,
underpowered check on a cardiac-mortality margin computed at the
receiving end (which sidesteps the SIM residence-recording issues); it
is consistent with the paper's main finding of no detectable mortality
effect and we draw no separate conclusion from it. Full event-study
trajectories are in the replication package.

\paragraph{How the outcomes fit together.} The identified result is the
suicide and self-harm mortality null (Section~\ref{sec:results-mortality}).
The first-stage travel-burden effect validates that the flow exposure
rule isolates genuinely dependent populations. ICSAP is reported as a
non-identified outcome (its pre-trend rejects;
Section~\ref{sec:results-icsap}), and the AMI in-hospital mortality here
is an underpowered auxiliary check. No outcome contradicts the mortality
null: where mortality is measured cleanly it shows no effect, and the
one outcome that moves (ICSAP) does so along a trend that predates the
closures.

\section{Distance approximation: sensitivity to centroid-to-centroid measurement}
\label{app:distance}

We compute travel burden as the weighted average haversine
distance between the centroid of the patient's municipality of
residence and the centroid of the municipality where the admitting
hospital is located. This is the standard approximation in the
Brazilian access literature \citep{macinkoHarris2015,
rochaSoares2010}, but it has two known biases that we audit here.

\paragraph{Within-municipality bias.} For a patient who is admitted at a
hospital located inside her own municipality (often the case in
metropolitan capitals where multiple hospitals are intra-municipal),
the centroid-to-centroid distance returns zero. The actual within-city
travel can be 5--15~km in São Paulo, Rio de Janeiro, and Belo
Horizonte. We checked CNES \texttt{COD\_CEP} availability in the top
ten state capitals (Section~\ref{app:replication}): São Paulo alone
has $40{,}000$+ hospital-month records spanning $8{,}705$ unique CEPs,
suggesting that CEP-level geocoding is in principle feasible but
non-trivial in practice.

We do not perform full CEP-level geocoding here for two reasons.
First, the within-municipality bias is largest for the largest
municipalities (capitals), and Section~\ref{sec:robustness} already
reports a robustness specification (§6.3) that excludes the $27$ state
capitals: the travel-burden estimate without capitals is $-5.89$~km
($\mathrm{SE} = 1.74$), within $1\%$ of the main $-5.84$~km. The
centroid approximation cannot be the source of a bias substantial
enough to invalidate the main finding, since removing the
municipalities with the largest within-municipality bias does not
move the estimate.

Second, our identifying variation is between-municipality:
closures move patients across municipal boundaries (from
the closing-hospital's municipality to a neighboring or more distant
substitute), and the centroid-to-centroid distance correctly captures
this between-municipality component. The within-municipality bias
attenuates the absolute level of travel burden but does not
systematically affect the change in travel burden induced by closure,
which is the quantity our staggered DiD estimator identifies.

\paragraph{Across-municipality bias.} For a patient admitted at a
hospital in another municipality, the centroid-to-centroid distance
correctly approximates the between-municipality component of the
travel. The remaining error is the difference between the patient's
actual residence (within her municipality) and her municipality's
centroid, which is small for most municipalities and is
approximately zero in expectation. We do not correct for this.

\paragraph{Recommendation for replication.} Studies that depend
substantively on within-municipality geographic precision should
consider full hospital geocoding via the CEP register. The replication
package includes a placeholder \texttt{lookup\_cep.py} script that
demonstrates the API call pattern (BrasilAPI's CEP endpoint, free)
and would need to be run as a one-time preprocessing step. The runtime
is approximately one hour for the $\sim 30{,}000$ unique hospital
CEPs in our universe.

\section{Power analysis}
\label{app:power}

Power enters this paper in two distinct ways, and it is worth separating
them. For the \emph{first stage}, the question is the usual one---can the
design detect an effect of the size observed?---and the answer is yes:
the specialized-closure travel-burden effect sits above the $80\%$-power
minimum detectable effect (ATT/MDE $= 1.30$), confirming that the flow
exposure rule isolates a population large and dependent enough for a real
closure effect to register. For the \emph{headline} mortality result, the
relevant statement is not detectability but the \emph{bound}: the null is
informative to the extent its confidence interval excludes large effects,
and the population-weighted suicide interval in
Table~\ref{tab:mortality-null} rules out increases above roughly
one-quarter of baseline. We do not restate that bound here as an MDE,
because for a null the confidence interval is the power statement.

This appendix reports the conventional MDE calculation for the
first-stage and the (non-identified) ICSAP outcomes. The minimum
detectable effect at $80\%$ power and $\alpha = 0.05$ two-sided is
\[
  \mathrm{MDE}_{80\%} = (z_{1-\alpha/2} + z_{1-\beta}) \cdot
  \widehat{\mathrm{SE}} = 2.802 \cdot \widehat{\mathrm{SE}}.
\]
Table~\ref{tab:power} reports it for each sample $\times$ outcome cell.
The specialized-closure travel effect is well-powered (ATT/MDE $= 1.30$);
ICSAP is underpowered (ATT/MDE $= 0.76$), one further reason---beyond the
pre-trend rejection of Section~\ref{sec:results-icsap}---that we decline
to read it causally.

\section{Rambachan-Roth sensitivity for ICSAP}

(Section~\ref{sec:results-icsap}). Beyond the visibly rejecting pre-trend
test, the Rambachan-Roth (2023) relative-magnitudes sensitivity shows the
post-closure ICSAP estimate cannot survive even a minimal allowance for
trend extrapolation. The restriction with multiplier $\bar M$ permits the
post-period parallel-trends violation to be at most $\bar M$ times the

extrapolation, and larger $\bar M$ widen the robust interval. There is no
positive $\bar M$ in the tested grid---indeed not even $\bar M = 0$---for

not separable from a continuation of the pre-existing trend at any
defensible restriction, which is the formal content of our decision to
treat it as a non-identified outcome.

\section{Multi-seed sensitivity for the cross-section CF ATE}
\label{app:cf-multiseed}

The Sun-Abraham main ATT is mathematically invariant to the embedding
seed: the share-based E1 exposure rule depends only on raw bipartite
admission counts, with no dependence on the node2vec geometry. The
seed therefore affects only auxiliary quantities, of which the
causal-forest cross-section ATE is the most consequential because
it uses the $\mathrm{iso}^{\mathrm{emb}}_z$ feature directly. We
quantify the implication of seed variation on the CF ATE via $B = 10$
perturbed re-runs of the forest, with $\mathrm{iso}^{\mathrm{emb}}_z$

\section{Causal-forest variable importance for ICSAP (non-interpretable)}
\label{app:cf-icsap}

The Athey-Wager calibration test for ICSAP is non-significant
(differential forest prediction $t = -0.11$, $p = 0.54$ one-sided),
indicating that the forest does not detect heterogeneity in the
ICSAP outcome at conventional significance. The variable-importance
ranking for ICSAP is therefore mechanically un-interpretable as
heterogeneity attribution. We report the ranking here for
completeness; it should not be read as substantive heterogeneity
inference. The ranking places log-population at $25\%$ of decision
splits, log-GDP per capita at $19\%$, and the embedding-kilometer
divergence at $11\%$. The ranking does not align cleanly with the
travel-burden ranking and does not, given the non-significant
calibration test, support any substantive interpretation.

\section{NLP classification: external validation status}
\label{app:nlp-validation}

The exogeneity defense for the robustness sub-sample rests on a zero-shot LLM
classification of declared closure motives. The internal validation
metrics reported in Section~\ref{app:nlp} (Cohen's $\kappa
\approx 0.86$ between LLM and a within-LLM second pass; $94\%$ of
classifications retrievably citing a public source) measure
self-consistency rather than external accuracy. The motive-based
robustness sub-sample therefore inherits a validation gap: a
human-classifier-blinded-to-the-LLM external validation has not yet
been completed. We acknowledge this gap explicitly. The replication
package now includes (i)~\texttt{notes/external\_validation\_protocol.md},
(ii)~a blinded $10\%$ random sample of the F5 universe
(\texttt{closure\_validation\_sample.csv}), and
(iii)~\texttt{closures\_human\_validated.parquet}, a two-coder scaffold
with adjudication fields. At present, however, the human-label cells in
that scaffold are empty; Table~\ref{tab:motive_validation} therefore
reports zero completed coder fields and leaves the agreement metrics
pending. As a
temporary lower-bound check, we also drop two fragile subclasses from
F6: labels resting on stale Receita status and labels resting only on
web snippets without timely administrative corroboration. That screen
retains $35$ non-fragile closures, corresponding to $61$ E1-treated
municipalities. The resulting travel-burden ATT is $-6.03$~km
($\mathrm{SE}=3.17$) and the ICSAP ATT is $+0.21$ per $1{,}000$
($\mathrm{SE}=0.77$). The travel result therefore remains
direction-consistent but imprecise, while the ICSAP corroboration
disappears until human validation is completed.

\section{Replication notes}
\label{app:replication}

\paragraph{Repository.} The full pipeline, including LaTeX sources,
analysis scripts, and the cached outputs of the LLM-classification step,
is open-source at \url{https://github.com/darciogm/bitter-pills/tree/main/paper18-hospital-deserts}.
The \texttt{run\_pipeline.sh} orchestrator at the repository root
sequences the analysis from raw DATASUS ingest through the final
robustness battery, accepting \texttt{--from} and \texttt{--to} flags
to start or stop at a specific step.

\paragraph{Software stack.} Python $3.11$ (DuckDB, polars, scikit-learn,
gensim, pecanpy for node2vec, anthropic for the LLM classifier) and
R $4.5$ (data.table, fixest, did, grf, ggplot2). Full pinned versions
in \texttt{environment.yml} (conda) or \texttt{requirements.txt} (pip).
DuckDB is the default I/O engine for parquet, configured with $12$
threads and $14$~GB memory limit.

\paragraph{External services and caches.} Two external services are
called once and cached afterward, both with the cache shipped in the
repository:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \emph{BrasilAPI} (Receita Federal CNPJ public endpoint).
    Cached in \texttt{02\_data/intermediate/cnpj\_cache.sqlite}.
    \item \emph{Anthropic Claude API} (zero-shot classifier). Cached in
    \texttt{02\_data/intermediate/llm\_classify\_cache.sqlite} (Haiku
    v1) and \texttt{llm\_classify\_cache\_v2.sqlite} (Sonnet v2 with
    web context). Total spend for the published classifications was
    approximately \$$0.85$.
\end{itemize}

The \texttt{web\_snippets/} folder contains the per-closure JSON
snippets extracted from web search results, used as context for the
Sonnet v2 classifier. Re-running the pipeline with these caches in
place skips all external service calls.

\paragraph{Hardware and runtime.} The pipeline was developed on an
Intel i7-$1260$P workstation under WSL2 ($14$ logical threads, $21$~GB
RAM, no GPU). A cold run from raw DATASUS to final figures takes
approximately $35$ minutes, dominated by SIH-RD parsing and the $61$
counterfactual perturbation node2vec re-trainings. Re-running with
intermediate parquets and caches in place takes approximately
$8$~minutes.

\paragraph{Reproducibility audits.} All numerical results in the main
text and in this appendix are re-derived from the public pipeline; no
hand-edited intermediate artifacts are used. The git revision against
which the latest compiled PDF was produced is logged in the
\texttt{04\_logs/} directory at compile time. The bibliography in
\texttt{references.bib} was independently audited against publisher
DOIs, NBER, and arXiv listings (audit log in
\texttt{notes/refs\_audit\_2026-04-28.md}).
