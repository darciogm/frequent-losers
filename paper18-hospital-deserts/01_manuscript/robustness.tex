\section{Robustness and diagnostics}
\label{sec:robustness}

Figure~\ref{fig:spec-curve} puts the robustness battery in one picture. It
plots the population-weighted suicide ATT across $\valNspecCurve$
specifications that vary the sample, the estimator, the controls, the
inference, and the leading threats to the design. The point estimates fall
in a narrow band---$\valSpecBandLo$ to $\valSpecBandHi$ per $100{,}000$
across the design-based specifications---and every $95\%$ confidence
interval includes zero, with a single exception: the naive two-way
fixed-effects benchmark, the one estimator known to be biased under
staggered timing. The null is a feature of the data, not of a chosen
specification. The rest of this section walks through the individual checks
behind the curve; the full battery is in the online appendix.

\paragraph{Alternative psychiatric samples.} The headline does not hang on
the F5 pre-decline screen. The stricter sample of dedicated psychiatric
hospitals ($\valNtrSpec$ exposed municipalities) gives a
population-weighted suicide ATT of $\valSuicSpecAtt$ per $100{,}000$ (95\%
CI $[\valSuicSpecLo, \valSuicSpecHi]$); the broader psychiatric sample
that relaxes F5 ($\valNtrPsymax$ exposed) gives $\valSuicPsymaxAtt$
($[\valSuicPsymaxLo, \valSuicPsymaxHi]$). Both sit beside the primary
$\valSuicWAtt$ and exclude increases above roughly a quarter of baseline.

\paragraph{Alternative estimators.} The null reproduces under
\citet{cs2021} and \citet{borusyak2024}. The one estimate that drifts is
the naive two-way fixed-effects specification, $\valSuicTwfeAtt$ per
$100{,}000$---the upward pull expected when staggered treatment-effect
heterogeneity contaminates the TWFE comparison, and the reason the design
leans on \citet{sunAbraham2021} to begin with. A Goodman-Bacon
decomposition confirms the diagnosis \citep{goodmanBacon2021}:
$\valBaconNeverPct\%$ of the TWFE weight falls on clean never-treated
comparisons and only $\valBaconForbidPct\%$ on the forbidden
already-treated ones, so the drift is heterogeneity weighting, not
contaminated comparison groups. The null also holds under doubly-robust
Callaway--Sant'Anna estimation with a not-yet-treated control group
($\valCsNotYetAtt$ per $100{,}000$) and under covariate adjustment
($\valCsDrCovAtt$).

\paragraph{Pandemic years and capitals.} Dropping $2020$--$21$ leaves the
suicide ATT at $\valSuicDropPandAtt$ per $100{,}000$; the closures
themselves sit outside the pandemic window by construction, and removing
the pandemic from the control years does not move the estimate. Excluding
the $27$ state capitals---the main sites of within-municipality hospital
dispersion, where the centroid distance approximation is weakest---gives
$\valSuicNoCapAtt$ and shifts the first-stage travel ATT by less than
$1\%$.

\paragraph{Exposure-rule sensitivity.} The flow cutoff is not driving the
null. Sweeping $\theta$ across $[0.01, 0.20]$ keeps the negative
travel-burden first stage intact and never produces a positive mortality
response. Psychiatric-specific denominators flag many more municipalities,
since psychiatric admissions are sparse in the all-SUS denominator; these
broader rules are stress tests, not a substitute for the main
all-SUS reliance rule. The full set of exposure-variant estimates and an
alternative $z$-score threshold variant are in the online appendix.

\paragraph{Network contamination and influential closures.} A closure can
push patients onto a hospital that also serves a control municipality,
contaminating that control. Dropping controls that share a health-region
proxy, a referral hub, a substitute hospital, or a high flow-similarity
link with treated catchments leaves the suicide ATT close to the primary estimate.
Removing one closure catchment at a time yields suicide ATTs between
$\valLooLo$ and $\valLooHi$ per $100{,}000$, so no single event carries
the result. A sharper spillover test drops the $\valNrecipDropped$
never-treated municipalities whose own admissions to the substitute
hospitals rose after closure---the revealed recipients of redirected
flow---and re-estimates on the $\valNcleanCtrl$ clean controls. Because
spillover attenuates a true effect toward zero, dropping the
spillover-exposed controls should raise the estimate if an effect were
hidden; instead the suicide ATT is $\valSuicRecipAtt$ (95\% CI
$[\valSuicRecipLo, \valSuicRecipHi]$), if anything slightly below the
headline, and the two event-study trajectories nearly coincide (online
appendix). The self-harm point estimate does rise under this control set
but stays insignificant.

\paragraph{Mechanical displacement and outcome recording.} Two threats
specific to a mortality null get direct tests. When a psychiatric
hospital closes, in-facility deaths mechanically leave the catchment's
records, which could offset a true positive effect. Splitting deaths by
place of occurrence and re-estimating on out-of-hospital deaths
alone---the ones a closing hospital cannot mechanically absorb---leaves
the suicide ATT at $\valSuicOutHospAtt$ per $100{,}000$ (95\% CI
$[\valSuicOutHospLo, \valSuicOutHospHi]$), with the in-hospital component
only $\valInHospShare\%$ of pre-closure suicide deaths (online appendix). Differential
death-recording is the other threat: the ill-defined-cause rate (ICD-10
R$96$--R$99$) does not jump at closure ($\valIllDefAtt$ per $100{,}000$ on
a base of $\valIllDefBase$), so cause-coding error is a level the fixed
effects absorb rather than something closure manufactures.

\paragraph{Inference.} The null is not an artifact of understated standard
errors. Clustering at the Federation Unit ($\valNUFclusters$ clusters)
widens the suicide interval to $[\valSuicUFLo, \valSuicUFHi]$;
randomization inference over $500$ placebo reassignments returns a
two-sided $p$ of $\valSuicRIp$ and a placebo interval of
$[\valSuicRILo, \valSuicRIHi]$. Both widen the uncertainty and both stay
centered near zero. The data rule out large acute mortality increases of
the magnitude in Table~\ref{tab:main-mortality-scaled-bounds}; they do
not rule out smaller harms or non-mortality welfare losses.
