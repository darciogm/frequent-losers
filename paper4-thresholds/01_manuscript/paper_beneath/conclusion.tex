\section{Discussion and Conclusion}\label{concl}

This paper assembles the first conviction-anchored cartel-detection benchmark that integrates auction-level bid data, matched employer--employee records, and formal antitrust rulings. The benchmark links seven CADE-convicted procurement cartels in S\~{a}o Paulo to 3.8~million bids on the state's electronic procurement platform and to the universe of formal-sector employment relationships in RAIS. On a unified close-margin sample of roughly 2{,}300 pregão auctions, we document three main findings.

\emph{First}, canonical variance-based screens fire in the direction predicted by the bid-rotation model---bid dispersion, coefficient of variation, and bidder counts are weakly compressed at cartel-tainted auctions---but achieve AUCs of only $0.52$--$0.56$, far too low for investigative triage. The close-margin RD of \citet{kawai2023using} likewise produces a null on the cartel-active subsample. Neither result repudiates the underlying designs; both warn that screens validated in thick-labor environments lose operational discriminating power when redeployed to settings where cartel coordination relies on alternative technologies.

\emph{Second}, network-based screens constructed from inter-firm worker flows achieve AUCs of $0.75$ (shared workers) and $0.74$ (Jaccard similarity), outperforming the best classical screen by roughly 20~percentage points. A co-bidding density screen yields $0.70$. The worker-flow signal survives conditioning on sector and on co-bidding intensity, indicating that it carries detection information beyond what bid-pattern or co-participation data alone can reveal.

\emph{Third}, a specialization placebo decomposes the worker-flow signal into a market-specialization component (approximately 44\% of the total) and a cartel-specific coordination residual (approximately 56\%). Both components are consistent with an institutional-thinness mechanism: thin specialized-labor markets generate spontaneous inter-firm labor circulation, and cartels reinforce those flows to sustain coordination. This two-component structure implies that conditioning worker-flow screens on product-market identity sharpens the cartel signal at a modest cost in overall AUC.

\medskip

\noindent\textbf{Policy implications.} For competition authorities in middle-income economies with access to employer--employee microdata---increasingly common as countries digitize social-security and labor records---worker-flow screens offer a deployable complement to canonical bid-pattern tests. The marginal cost of constructing such screens is negligible once the data linkage exists. Our results suggest that enforcement strategies relying exclusively on classical screens may systematically under-detect cartels in precisely the institutional environments where deterrence capacity is weakest.

\noindent\textbf{Limitations and directions for future work.} Several caveats apply. The CADE convictions that anchor our benchmark are not a random sample of all cartels; they are the product of a selection process that may itself correlate with worker-flow intensity (Section~\ref{sec:model}). If CADE cases are more likely to be initiated or sustained when personnel connections between firms are visible to investigators, the worker-flow AUC may overstate the screen's performance on the universe of undetected cartels. Future work should investigate whether worker-flow screens maintain their discriminative power when evaluated against cartels detected through independent channels (e.g., leniency applications or dawn raids triggered by unrelated investigations). The strict pair-matched sample ($N = 10$) is too small to draw conclusions about head-to-head cartel coordination; larger ground-truth sets, potentially assembled from cross-state CADE linkages, would allow sharper tests. Finally, the simple model of Section~\ref{sec:model} treats bid-pattern and worker-flow coordination as substitutes; a richer framework allowing complementarity---where cartels use both channels simultaneously---could generate additional testable predictions and refine the screen-design implications.
