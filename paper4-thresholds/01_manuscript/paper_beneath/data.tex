\section{Data}\label{data}

The analysis rests on three administrative data sources---procurement bids, employment records, and antitrust rulings---which we link at the firm level to construct the conviction-anchored benchmark.

\subsection{BEC: bid-level procurement data}

We draw on the complete bid-level records from S\~{a}o Paulo's \emph{Bolsa Eletr\^{o}nica de Compras} (BEC) for January 2009 through December 2019. After restricting to preg\~{a}o and convite procedures with at least two participating firms, the data contain 2{,}613{,}010 competitive auctions, 13{,}957{,}419 firm-level bid participations, 40{,}273 distinct firms, and 153{,}048 unique item codes. Each record identifies the purchase offer, item code, participating firm (by CNPJ), bid amount, outcome, auction format, and purchasing agency.

For each auction we compute the screens described in Section~\ref{sec:classical}---coefficient of variation, bid spread, margin of victory, and bidder count---on firm-best bids (keeping each firm's lowest bid when multiple rounds exist). Markets are defined transitively following \citet{kawai2023using}: two firms share a market if they ever bid on the same item code in the same auction. This yields 7{,}581 distinct markets.

\subsection{RAIS: employer--employee matched panel}

The \emph{Rela\c{c}\~{a}o Anual de Informa\c{c}\~{o}es Sociais} (RAIS) is Brazil's compulsory annual census of formal employment. Every establishment with a CNPJ must report each employment bond active at any point during the year, identified by the worker's PIS/PASEP number. We use the 2009--2017 vintages, restricting to establishments whose 8-digit CNPJ root appears in the BEC firm universe---roughly 19{,}000 roots. The result is a nine-year panel of every formal-sector job at every BEC-active firm.

From this panel we build the worker-flow edge list. For each pair of BEC firms $(i, j)$, we count distinct PIS numbers that appear at both firms at any point during the nine years (the shared-worker count $W_{ij}$) and compute the Jaccard similarity $J_{ij}$. Pairs with no shared workers are zero-imputed when merged to the auction benchmark.

A temporal asymmetry is worth noting. The RAIS panel covers 2009--2017, while BEC auction data extend to 2019 and CADE judgment dates for most cartels fall after 2019 (Table~\ref{tab:cartels}). The ``post-conduct'' window we measure in the temporal decomposition (Section~\ref{sec:decomposition}) therefore captures the period between the end of cartel conduct and the end of the RAIS panel---typically the investigation and pre-judgment period, not the post-judgment period. This distinction matters for interpreting enforcement-driven labor reallocation: the outflows we document may reflect the anticipation of enforcement rather than its formal realization.

\subsection{CADE: cartel ground truth}

Our ground truth comes from CADE administrative proceedings with final Tribunal decisions convicting named firms of cartel conduct in S\~{a}o Paulo procurement markets (see Section~\ref{hist}). Table~\ref{tab:cartels} lists the seven cartels we identified---spanning pharmaceuticals, school meals, trash bags, solar water heaters, metropolitan rail, airport cafeterias, and school transport---together with their conduct periods and the 35 firms whose CNPJ roots match BEC records.

The key feature for detection is that each case file specifies not just the colluding firms but the product market and time window. We exploit this to label individual auctions as cartel-tainted: an auction earns the flag only if a convicted firm bid during the CADE-specified conduct period for the relevant product and year. This firm $\times$ period $\times$ product matching avoids the coarser alternative of treating every auction involving a flagged firm as suspicious.

\subsection{Sample construction}

All detection tests use the close-margin envelope $\lvert MV \rvert \le 0.10$. Within that envelope we define three samples:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Cartel-active benchmark} ($N \approx 2{,}300$ preg\~{a}o; $N = 263$ convite). At least one convicted firm bid during the active period of its cartel. This is the primary evaluation sample.

\item \textbf{Strict pair-matched sample} ($N = 10$ preg\~{a}o). Both the winner and the runner-up belong to the same convicted cartel during the same conduct period. The gold standard for head-to-head coordination, but power-limited.

\item \textbf{Anytime-listed sensitivity} ($N \approx 80$ preg\~{a}o). Both firms appear in a CADE conviction for the same cartel, but the auction year may fall outside the conduct window. Of the 107 pre-filtering auctions, 93 are out-of-window and 100 come from a single case (the medicamentos hub-and-spoke, CADE 08012.002222/2011-09). Reported as a sensitivity only.
\end{enumerate}

The \emph{clean control} is every close-margin auction with no cartel-listed firm ($N = 527{,}259$ preg\~{a}o; $519{,}557$ convite). All screens are evaluated against this common baseline.
