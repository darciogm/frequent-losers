\begin{abstract}
Brazil's antitrust authority secured administrative convictions against 35 firms in seven procurement cartels operating in the state of S\~{a}o Paulo between 2009 and 2019. We match these firms and the product markets delineated in the case files to 3.8~million bids on the state's electronic procurement platform and to the universe of matched employer--employee records, assembling the first conviction-anchored benchmark that integrates auction behavior, labor microdata, and antitrust rulings.

On a unified benchmark of roughly 2{,}300 close-margin pregão auctions (absolute bid margin $\lvert MV \rvert \le 0.10$) in which at least one convicted firm participated during the active period of its cartel, canonical variance-based screens fire in the direction predicted by the bid-rotation model---bid dispersion and the coefficient of variation are weakly compressed (Cohen's $d \approx -0.07$ to $-0.10$) and the number of active bidders is modestly lower ($d \approx -0.24$)---yet the corresponding areas under the ROC curve ($0.52$--$0.56$) remain far too low for investigative triage, indicating near-total distributional overlap between cartel and clean auctions. A close-margin regression discontinuity in the spirit of \citet{kawai2023using} likewise yields a null. By contrast, network screens constructed from the same benchmark achieve markedly higher discrimination: inter-firm worker flows from the matched employer--employee data yield AUCs of $0.75$ (shared workers) and $0.74$ (Jaccard similarity); a co-bidding density screen yields $0.70$. The worker-flow signal survives conditioning on sector and on co-bidding intensity. A specialization placebo restricted to non-cartel firm pairs bidding on the same product code in the same year absorbs approximately half of the worker-flow signal, leaving a cartel-specific residual---consistent with cartels both inheriting and reinforcing the personnel flows natural to thin specialized-labor markets.

These findings support a view in which collusion leaves institution-specific signatures: in markets with thin specialized-labor pools, the cheapest coordination technology operates through personnel flows rather than bid-pattern manipulation. For competition authorities with access to employer--employee microdata, worker-flow screens offer a deployable complement to canonical bid-pattern tests.
\end{abstract}
\end{frontmatter}

%In this study, we adopt a Regression Discontinuity design, following the methodology of \cite{kawai2023using}, to investigate 3.8~million public procurement auctions in São Paulo, Brazil. We aim to detect bid rotation and incumbency trends as indicators of anti-competitive behavior by analyzing auctions with narrowly winning and narrowly losing bids. Our findings reveal an incumbency advantage but no definitive evidence of bid rotation. This indicates that political favoritism or corruption, rather than firm collusion, may influence the auction outcomes.

\subsection{Bandwidth sensitivity}\label{app:bandwidth}

Table~\ref{tab:bw} reports AUCs for all screens across six bandwidth choices for the close-margin envelope. The benchmark bandwidth of $\lvert MV \rvert \le 0.10$ used throughout the paper is neither the most nor the least favorable for the worker-flow screens. At narrower bandwidths ($0.02$, $0.05$), worker-flow AUCs are marginally higher ($0.76$, $0.75$) but sample sizes are smaller; at wider bandwidths ($0.20$, $0.50$), AUCs decline modestly ($0.74$, $0.72$) as the sample dilutes toward the full population. Classical screen AUCs follow the same monotonic pattern. The gap between the best classical screen (number of bidders) and the worker-flow screens remains stable at approximately 18--20 percentage points across all bandwidths.
